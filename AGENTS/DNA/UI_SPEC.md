# UI Spec

How the title and the two modes are laid out and behave. Visual language is in [[DESIGN]]; rules are in [[PRODUCT]].

## Frame

- The game is full-screen: one `<canvas>` filling the window, a DOM overlay (`#ui`) on top. The overlay is `pointer-events: none` except for its children, so the canvas keeps focus.
- Minimum supported viewport is a laptop at 1280×720 and a tablet in landscape at 1024×768 (and 1180×820); every screen holds at both, with the touch controls on at the tablet sizes (§ Pointer and touch). A touch screen held upright (taller than wide) shows "Turn your screen sideways to play!" over everything, with a tablet outline turning onto its side (with reduced motion, lying still on its side), and takes no taps until it is turned. Phones are not a supported size in general: a screen is laid out for a phone held sideways only where its own section says so. A phone held sideways is one line for the whole game, a screen under 560 px tall (`SHORT_SCREEN`), except the animal book's, which is 500. The battle's screen (wild battles and friendly matches) holds on one at 844×390 with an iPhone's notch and home indicator, 740×360 and 667×375, in both languages (§ Battle mode, "On a phone held sideways"), and so does the explore screen (§ Explore mode, "The corners"); the animal book (§ Pause menu) has rules of its own for one.
- The camera never zooms or rotates. Resizing the window changes how much world is visible horizontally; vertical extent stays 14 tiles.
- **The safe area.** Where a notch, rounded corners or a home indicator take a strip of the screen, the world still draws under them, and everything a kid reads or taps keeps inside the safe area the browser reports (`--safe-*` in `styles.css`): the explore HUD, the touch controls and the battle's status boxes keep their usual distance from its edges, the battle panel and the doctor's card stand on its bottom edge with the world showing under them, and the pause menu and the cards over everything centre in it while their dim covers the whole screen. An iPad's home indicator lifts everything at the bottom by 20 px; an iPhone held sideways keeps the notch clear on both sides. The title keeps its own margins, which clear an iPad's home indicator but not a phone's notch.
- **When the game cannot start**: where the browser cannot draw the world (no WebGL 2), a card on the sky says so in the language on screen instead of the game: the fox, "This browser can't draw Animath's world.", "Ask a grown-up for help!", and a smaller line for the grown-up. A browser running no JavaScript shows the same kind of card in English and Danish at once.
- **On an old address** (the tunnel's, where the game was shared before it had its own; any address that is neither the game's domain nor a developer's machine, [[DECISIONS]] § Deployment): before anything else, every time the page opens, a card on the sky instead of the game, in the language the game would start in: the fox, "Animath has moved!", and one big accent button, "Play at animath.xyz", which opens the game's address in the same tab (Enter or Space press it from anywhere on the card but the small link; its Enter cap shows unless the main pointer is a finger). Under it, small and quiet, "Keep playing here": the game starts at the old address, the title first, as on any other page. Only a click, a tap, or Tab to it and then Enter or Space presses it; Escape and the game's other keys do nothing, so a kid meets the move before anything else. The next page load shows the card again.

## Pointer and touch

A mouse and a finger work everything a keyboard works, and a tablet plays with no keyboard at all.

- **A click or a tap is a key press** — the one path to every action. Every row, button and card a key can reach is a button, and pressing it sends the key the keyboard would (`input/press.ts`): Enter, Escape, a digit, an arrow held down, or one of the pointer's own keys for what it touched — a row, an option of the animal picked in the pause menu, a battle level button, a language, a card of the party column, an animal on an open card, or a card dropped at another place. They go through the same screen routing and the same guards as keys: the quiet moment before a new choice takes a pick (§ Battle mode, "A quiet moment"), a turn playing. A button never keeps the focus, so Enter or Space can't press it a second time.
- **A tap begins and ends on one button, on one screen**: the finger (or the mouse button) goes down on the button and comes up on it, or hardly moved (20 px), while the same screen is up, and the button presses as it lifts. **A tap has no time limit**: young kids tap slowly, so a finger that rests on a button however long, and lifts where it landed, taps it; nothing in the game acts on a long press alone. So a finger that slides off cancels, and a button that changes under a still finger (the first touch after the keyboard brings the touch controls, and Go! loses its key cap) still takes the tap. A finger lifted over something it did not go down on presses nothing, nor does one that went down before the screen changed (a row touched while the turn played, lifted once the menu is back; one animal's option in the pause menu, lifted after a tap on the team put up another's; a button held while the tablet was turned upright), nor one on a row that moved while it rested there (the team re-sorted by another finger's Move up). **A double click or tap counts once when its first half moved what it pressed**: a second press within 0.7 s and 20 px of a tap, on a button with another key, while the button tapped no longer stands where it was tapped (a party card or an open card's animal re-sorted to the top, the screen changed), presses nothing, since what slid into its place is not what the kid aimed at; a double click on a button that stayed put presses it twice, as before. Each finger counts on its own, so a tap on Talk or Menu works while the other thumb walks on the D-pad. The number pad's keys and the D-pad act as the finger lands instead. The browser's own click from a finger or a mouse is never used; a click with no pointer behind it (a screen reader's) still presses.
- **What a tap does**: on the doctor's list, the pause menu, the party cards and the title's menu and confirm, a tap does the row at once (a card of several animals opens to show them, and an animal there goes first): nothing is spent there, every move can be moved back, and the confirm's "Yes" is a deliberate choice; on the doctor's list, its confirm and the title's confirm it waits their quiet moment first, as Enter does (picking one animal to go home, which can always be unpicked, does not wait; a whole kind's row waits, so a mash never picks a stack). A tap on one of the doctor's tabs goes there at once. In a battle, where a pick spends the turn, a tap on a row only highlights it and a tap on a level button only sets that attack's level: **Go!** (Enter) does the highlighted row. On the starters a tap on a name tag, or on the animal above it, lights that one, and the card's "Pick the Rabbit!" (Enter) picks. The result card is one big button: a tap anywhere on the screen goes on, and waits as Enter waits.
- **Buttons for keys**, shown with a mouse too: Go! (and Back on the switch list the kid opened) in the battle's right card; Back beside a doctor's puzzle; Save and Back under the pause menu's name box; Back and "Pick the Rabbit!" or "Let's go!" on the title's starter card; "Esc Menu" under the party cards. Without the touch controls the battle's buttons show their keys' caps ("Go! Enter"); elsewhere the key reminder under the buttons names the keys. A mouse over something it can press lights it faintly; nothing is only on hover.
- **The touch controls** show where the main pointer is a finger (`pointer: coarse`: a tablet), and after that follow the latest input: a touch or a pen shows them, a key pressed on a real keyboard hides them (typing in a name box and the page's own presses don't count), a mouse changes nothing. With them on:
  - **Explore**: a D-pad in the bottom-left corner (four arms of 64 × 64 px round an empty centre) walks as the arrow keys do: a finger held on an arm keeps walking, a tap is one step, sliding the thumb onto another arm turns, and lifting it stops; a finger resting in the centre presses nothing. In the bottom-right corner, **Talk** (96 px, Enter), named for what Enter does in front of the player — **Talk** at a tent, **Chop** at a tree with the axe, **Break** at a rock with the pickaxe ("Snak", "Fæld", "Knus") — and lit orange and glowing while it does something (the glow pulses out, or with reduced motion stays lit), and **Menu** (72 px, Escape) beside it. Once the paraglider is owned, **Fly** (77 px, "Flyv") stands over Talk, under the same thumb: a finger held on it holds Space down, lit orange while it does, so it flies after the wind-up and comes down when the finger lifts (or the finger is lost to the page, as the D-pad's is); a quick tap is a tap of Space. The hints name them: "Hold an arrow to walk", "Tap Talk to talk to the witch doctor", "Tap Chop to chop the tree", "Tap Break to break the rock", and under the party cards "Tap an animal to pick who goes first". The message line narrows to stay between the D-pad and the buttons.
  - **Puzzles**, in battle and at the doctor: a number pad beside the prompt and the answer box, in the right half of the puzzle card — 1 2 3 / 4 5 6 / 7 8 9 / − 0 ⌫, and a tall OK down the right edge — under the right thumb. Its keys are the keys (a digit, the minus, Backspace, Enter for OK) and act as the finger lands; the pad dims while the screen takes no typing. Beside it the prompt is smaller (`clamp(32px, 6vh, 52px)`, and `clamp(32px, 4.5vh, 40px)` for a prompt over 11 characters) and wraps between numbers, never inside one. "Tap the answer, then OK" replaces the key reminder. The answer box is not a text field, so a tablet's own keyboard never comes up over a puzzle.
  - **Sizes**: every row and button is at least 48 px tall (`--tap`). The battle panel and the doctor's card are `clamp(364px, 48vh, 400px)`, so a bear's four attack tiles and the row of Leash, Switch and Run fit in battle, each a finger tall (on a phone held sideways, a battle's cards are 220 px and its moves stand beside the tiles: § Battle mode); at the doctor the doctor's line and the tokens move over the puzzle and the left card runs the card's full height: the tabs, the list, which a finger scrolls, and the rows under it. The battle's level buttons, on the preview card, are at least 50 px tall and 84 px wide, with no row under them to press by mistake; the languages on a Language row are a finger tall too.
  - **Reminders**: key reminders become tap hints ("Tap an attack, then Go!", "Tap an animal to help it"), or go where every row is plainly a button (the pause menu, the title); key caps inside buttons go.
  - **Name boxes** (the pause menu's, the title's two) use the tablet's own keyboard. Tapping New name, New game (or Yes, or Continue for a game saved without a name) or the starter's Pick opens the box and focuses it inside the tap, as a tablet needs to bring its keyboard up; while it is open the menu, or the starter card, moves to the top of the screen, clear of the keyboard rising from the bottom; its Enter (labelled "done" or "go") saves; closing the box puts back any scroll the keyboard caused.
- **A game, not a page**: no pinch or double-tap zoom, no pull-to-refresh or rubber-band scroll, no text selection or long-press menu under a finger (the name boxes select and edit as usual), and no delay before a tap counts. Only lists scroll: the pause menu on a screen too short for it, a long team wherever it is listed (the party column, a card's animals, the switch list, the doctor's list), and the animal book's cards.
- **Across screens**: a finger still on the D-pad when a battle, the doctor or the menu takes the screen lets go there, presses nothing on the new screen when it lifts (not the battle row under it), and walks nothing when explore comes back until it is lifted and pressed again, as a held key does (§ Battle mode, "Keys across modes"). Away from the page it lets go the same way: when the window loses the focus or the page is hidden, where explore drops its held keys, and when the browser cancels the finger, the D-pad's arrow goes out and the finger is forgotten, so a finger still resting there walks nothing until it is lifted and pressed again, and any other finger can steer.

## Title

The first screen, every time the page opens (except the throwaway switches `?new`, `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop`, which go straight into explore, and a page that reloaded itself mid-game to catch up with another window, which goes straight back into play), and the one Quit to title returns to. Nothing is started, walked, rolled or saved behind it.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      A n i m a t h                       │
│                                                          │
│  ┌──────────────────────────────┐                        │
│  │ ▸ Continue     Pip 3 animals │     [trainer + team]   │
│  │   New game                   │      (the world,       │
│  │   Language  (English) Dansk  │       drifting)        │
│  │   Sound             (●) On   │                        │
│  │    ↑ ↓ choose · Enter pick   │                        │
│  └──────────────────────────────┘                        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Backdrop**: the world where the game stands (the saved spot, or the spawn tile with no save), seen through the explore camera pointed a little aside so the trainer stands right of centre, clear of the menu: the same angle, no zoom, no turn, drifting slowly (a sway of about a tile, over half a minute; still with reduced motion). The trainer faces the saved way, the boat on their back when the game has one (or under them, when it was saved out on the water); one animal of each kind in the team stands on the walkable tiles nearest it (none, far out on a lake), and each sea animal swims, low in the water, on the water tiles nearest it instead (none, with no water near),
 in the cards' order (with no save, the starters do), breathing, so a team of a hundred is still a handful of figures. They are scenery and go when the game starts.
- **Name**: "Animath" at the top, each letter in a palette colour (coral, orange, green, blue, amber) with a cream rim, bobbing gently out of step; still with reduced motion.
- **Menu** (a card at the left): **Continue** (only with a saved game; beside it the name of the one that goes first where the game stands (out on the water, the first standing that swims, else, riding in the boat, the first standing) and the team's size, "Pip 3 animals", or under it when a long name leaves no room beside it, so twelve of the widest letters show whole), **New game**, **I have an account → Log in** (for a guest, and for a player whose session has ended: the account card, to log in; § Accounts; never on a throwaway page, `?new` and the like, which keeps nothing), **Language** (every language in its own words, the one on screen lit), **Sound** (the pause menu's switch, and its state in words). Up/down or W/S move the cursor, wrapping; Enter or Space pick, at once (Continue carries on and New game asks first, so the menu needs no quiet moment); left/right or A/D change the setting on its row (the next language; sound off on the left, on on the right; Enter changes it too), and do nothing on the others. M switches the sound too, as on every screen. Menu moves and picks make the menus' soft blips (§ Sound and juice). Escape does nothing. The cursor starts on the first row: Continue when there is one. Under the rows, for a player logged in, "Playing as Nini"; opened as a web app from the Home Screen with nothing saved there, a soft blue hint that a game played in Safari comes over with an account ("Played in Safari before? Make an account there (Menu → Make an account), then log in here.", #90); a key reminder (on a setting's row, how to change it), and, on every title of a page that cannot keep the game or has a newer build's game waiting, the line that says so: no storage at all, no room left after a write failed, a newer build's save in the key or on the server. Start screen's title says it too, Continue or not.
- **Confirm** (New game with a saved game): a card over a dim backdrop that puts the menu out of reach (a screen reader's too), "Start a new game?", "Your game with Pip is put away safely." (on a page that keeps nothing, "Your game with Pip can't be kept on this page."), "You start again with a new animal.", then **No, go back** (lit first) and **Yes, new game**. Up/down pick, without wrapping; Enter or Space do it, after a quiet moment (§ Battle mode, "A quiet moment"); Escape goes back. So however Enter is mashed on New game, the confirm takes none of it, and starting over takes a deliberate Down first. Back returns to the menu on New game.
- **Your name** (New game without a save, or Yes; and Continue for a game saved before players had names, once): a card over the title's world, the menu dimmed and out of reach under it, as under the confirm. "What's your name?" over a name box (on Continue, "Your animals are waiting for you!" above it), and under the box the rule, "2 to 16 letters or numbers.", or, after an Enter the name would not do for, why, kindly, in ink on a soft tint of the "not quite" red: "Type your name first.", "A name needs at least 2 letters.", "That's a long one! 16 letters at most.", "Only letters and numbers, please.", "Let's pick another name." (the engine's `checkName`), with the soft bonk of a wrong answer. It works like the starter's name box: every key but Enter, Escape and Tab types, and typing again clears the reason; Enter goes on after a quiet moment (§ Battle mode, "A quiet moment"), counted from the box coming up and the last Enter, never on auto-repeat: to the starters with the name tidied (trimmed, single spaces), or, on Continue, into the game, which is saved with the name at once. Escape goes back to the menu, on New game or Continue. Back (Escape) and Next (Enter; "Let's go!" on Continue) under the box. The box holds the name given already this visit, else the saved game's (a new game over it keeps or changes it), else nothing; it takes up to 32 characters, so a name too long is typed out and told, never cut off unseen. With the touch controls on the card is at the top of the screen, clear of the tablet's keyboard.
- **Starters** (after your name): the 3D starter stage replaces the world: every starter in catalog order side by side on a round meadow with tufts, flowers and bushes, big (scaled towards a common size, so a squat frog is sized by its width), the lit one on a warm ring, facing the camera and bouncing (a third as high with reduced motion), the others turned towards it. "Pick your first animal!" across the top; each animal's name in a tag under its feet (the lit tag orange); a card at the bottom with the lit one's name and every kind of sum its attacks can ask, at any level ("The Rabbit loves adding, taking away, missing numbers, times tables, and number patterns!") and the keys. Left/right (A/D, and up/down) move, wrapping; Enter or Space pick, after a quiet moment; Escape goes back to the player's name box, the name as it was. The row is as long as the starter list.
- **Name box** (after a pick; the picked one hops for joy, twice, or once and small with reduced motion): the card asks "What will you call your Rabbit?" over a name box with the species as its placeholder, the name rule, and "Leave it empty and it's just called Rabbit." (or, when the typed text will not be stored as shown, what it will be called). It works like the pause menu's name box: every key but Enter, Escape and Tab types; Enter starts the game (an empty box starts it without a name), after a quiet moment, counted from the box coming up and the last Enter (Space and the numbers are letters here), and never on auto-repeat; Escape goes back to the starters with the same one lit. The card grows for the box, and the row of animals slides up just far enough that their name tags stay clear of it (with the touch controls on, where the card is at the top, down just far enough that their heads do), and back when the box closes; at once with reduced motion. It moves no further than it must, and never when the card already leaves it room (#78).
- **Starting**: the game begins at the spawn tile of its home world (a random one: the backdrop behind the title was World 1's) with the explore HUD and the controls hint, the message line saying what start-up found about the save when there was something (the save that didn't load); the first Continue after the page opens says "Welcome back!" (a Continue after Quit to title says nothing).
- **Pointer**: a menu row or a language does its thing at once, a confirm choice once the confirm's quiet moment has passed; a starter's tag, or the animal above it, lights it; the name card's Next (or "Let's go!"), the starter card's "Pick the Rabbit!" and the name box's "Let's go!" are Enter and their Back is Escape (§ Pointer and touch).
- It holds at 1280×720 and 1024×768 in both languages, and with the touch controls on at 1180×820, the name tags clear of the card on every screen of it.


- **The corners** (#120, #121): every piece of the explore HUD has its place, and none stands on another, with every piece on (a party of several cards, all four tools, the glider, a friend's arrow at each corner), in both languages, at 1024×768 and on a phone held sideways at 844×390 (an iPhone's notch and home indicator), 740×360 and 667×375.
  - **Top left, the party**: the cards, and the hint under them. **Top right, what the kid has**: the tokens, the puzzles solved, the tools and the world. **Bottom left, the D-pad**, with the touch controls on. **Bottom right, the right thumb's**: Talk, Menu and Fly, and the coordinates (without the touch controls, the coordinates alone). **Top middle, the notes** ("Ada is here!"); **bottom middle, the message line**, with the Challenge button over it. **Between them, the edges**: the arrows to the players out of sight.
  - **On a phone held sideways** (under 560 px tall), three things give way, and the thumbs' buttons stay where a tablet has them. The top right corner packs into two rows, 72 px tall, so it ends above Fly (Fly's top is 117 px down at 740×360): the tokens and the puzzles side by side, and under them the tools as pictures alone, side by side (their names only for a screen reader), with the world at the end of that row, under the puzzles. With the touch controls on, the hint under the party cards goes, so the room over the D-pad is the cards' (116 px at 740×360: a card and a half, and the column scrolls). And the notes stand over the message line, since the two top corners leave no room between them. The coordinates stand beside Fly (below).
  - **The arrows' track**: a mark at the edge of the screen (a friend's arrow) rides a rectangle 44 px inside the safe area's sides and top and 96 px inside its bottom (clear of a message line of one or two lines), and goes round every piece above (the party column and a stack's open card, the top right corner, the coordinates, the message line, the Challenge button, the note while it shows, the D-pad, Talk, Menu and Fly), each grown by 24 px. It stands on the line from the player to the friend, where that line first leaves the track: on the track's edge, or just before the first piece in the way, pointing past it, so it is never under one (a friend down and to the left, beyond the D-pad, has an arrow over or beside the D-pad, where it used to hide under it). A piece the player's spot is inside (a message line of many lines on a phone) is not in the way.
- **Tokens, puzzles solved and the world** (top-right): a pill with the gold coin and "23 tokens", right under it a pill of the same size with a green tick and "312 puzzles", one above the other so the corner stays narrow (side by side, on a tablet they would reach the note at the top of the screen, "Ada is here!"). Under them one chip per tool the kid owns, its picture and its name ("Axe"), one under the other (on a phone held sideways, the pictures alone, side by side: § "The corners"), and under those a pill with the world the kid is in ("World 42"): the number a kid reads out to a friend, a glance away without opening a menu. Nothing to press, and nothing moves: a count that went up in a battle or at the doctor is already higher when the HUD comes back. With `?debug` they sit under the position and the cues.
- **Coordinates** (bottom-right): a pill like the world's with where the kid stands, "x 40 · y 1000": the steps to the right (x) and up the screen (y) from the world's spawn, which reads "x 0 · y 0" in every world ([[DECISIONS]] § Client), below zero the other way with a real minus sign ("x −3 · y −12"), and nothing between the thousands. It changes as each step begins, and tile by tile in the boat and up in the air with the glider; a trip to a new world starts it at 0, 0. Nothing to press. It keeps 16 px from the safe area's edges, the arrows keep clear of it (§ "The corners"), and the message line keeps clear of it: the line stays narrower than the screen by the room "x −9999 · y −9999" takes (160 px) and a gap, on each side, as it is centred. With the touch controls on, that corner is the right thumb's, under Talk and Menu, so the pill stands 12 px over Talk, or over Fly with the glider, lined up with the pills of the top-right corner, where the thumb does not cover it. On a phone held sideways (under 560 px tall) with the glider there is no room over Fly under the top right corner, so the pill stands on Fly's left, level with its middle. Explore only: never in a battle, at the doctor, in the menu or on the title.
- **Party HUD** (top-left): one card per kind in the party, in battle order (the party is in bundles, [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Party", so the cards top to bottom are the order the animals fight in). A card of one animal shows it: its name (nickname if set) and its HP bar with `hp/max` text. A card of several is a stack, with two cards peeking out behind it: the kind's name, how many ("Rabbit ×4"), how they are, and a slim bar of all their HP together (colour as an HP bar's, no numbers). How they are counts three kinds of animal: ready, tired soon (still playing, but a fifth of its HP or less: its own bar would be red) and tired; "All ready", "All tired soon" or "All tired" when they are all one kind, else each count that isn't zero ("2 ready · 3 tired soon · 1 tired"), a line breaking between the counts, never inside one. So a stack never says "All ready" while one of them is nearly out, and the words say it without colour. The cards are all as wide as the longest name needs, from 260 px, so twelve of the widest letters show whole, and a tag that no longer fits beside the name goes under it. A card whose animals are all tired is greyed with a "tired" tag. The lead's card ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Party") is outlined in the accent colour, its key cap filled, and tagged "goes first", so it reads without colour too. With two or more cards, the first nine show the number key that picks them (as a key cap). With one card there is nobody to choose between: no key caps, no tag, nothing to drag. Under the cards, a small panel shows the keys: "1–n Pick who goes first", "Esc Menu", and, with two or more cards, "Drag a card to move it"; with the touch controls on, "Tap a card to open it, then tap who goes first" (or, with no card of several, "Tap an animal to pick who goes first") and "Hold a card, then drag it" (§ Pointer and touch). The panel stands clear of the cards' scroll box, so a card the column cuts off at its bottom is never under it. On a phone held sideways with the touch controls on, the panel gives way to the cards (§ "The corners").
- **A stack's animals**: pointing at a card of several with the mouse shows its animals in a card of their own beside the column, top-aligned with it (lower when the card is near the bottom), one row each in party order: name, HP bar with numbers, and "goes first" on the lead or "tired" at 0 HP, the rows lined up in columns. They stay while the pointer is on the card or on them. A click or a tap on the stack opens it and keeps it open, a second closes it, and so does a step, choosing who goes first from it, or leaving explore. A click or a tap on an animal there makes it go first (its card moves to the top, and it to the front of its card); on a tired one, the message line says it is tired, and on one that can't go first where the trainer stands, why (below, "Who goes first out on the water"). The list scrolls when it is taller than the screen allows. A click or a tap on a card of one animal makes it go first. A double click on either counts once (§ Pointer and touch): the second click lands where the first one's card or animal has just left, on what slid into its place, and presses nothing.
- **Moving a card**: a card is dragged up or down the column to another place, with a mouse once it has moved (8 px), with a finger after holding it still for half a second (a finger that moves first scrolls the column): then the card lifts, and it moves once the finger does, past a tap's 20 px. A press that never moved is a tap, however long it rested: a card lifted and let go where it was does what a tap on it does (a slow tap, the way young kids tap). The card lifts with a deeper shadow, follows the pointer (never past the column's first or last card), and the others slide aside to show where it will land: the place nearest where it is drawn, so a card carried as far as it goes lands first or last, whatever the sizes of the cards it passed. Let go, it takes that place, and the party is put in that order (the card at the top leads; one whose animals can't go first where the trainer stands, a sea animal's on land or one that can't swim out on the water, takes the top and leaves the lead where it was, and the message line says why, as its number key would). A drag is never also a tap. The column scrolls, with a finger or a wheel, when it is taller than the room it has (above the D-pad with the touch controls on). A card carried to within 40 px of its top or bottom edge keeps it scrolling that way, faster the nearer the edge, for as long as it is held there, moving or not, until the column's end shows; a card lifted at an edge and carried away from it scrolls nothing.
- **Message line** (bottom-centre): up to two lines in one box. ## Battle mode

Borrowed composition from the Game Boy games: your animal from behind at bottom-left, the opponent from the front at top-right, status boxes opposite each sprite, and a panel across the bottom. Our addition is a **puzzle area** that takes real space, because solving is the whole game.

## Doctor

Talking to a doctor (facing a tent, Enter/Space) opens a dialogue card in explore mode (no mode switch): the doctor's line and the kid's tokens across the top, three tabs over a list on the left, and on the right what the highlighted row does, the confirm before a hand-over, or a puzzle in the same puzzle component as battle. **Heal** (the tab the card opens on): the team's hurt animals; solve → healed, a whole species at once, with a cheer; miss → "Not quite! Nothing is written over, in the browser or on the server. A save no version could read is saved past once the kid has played, and kept aside on the server.
- **Only while the server can keep one**: the hourly card, "Make an account" and "Log in" in the pause menu, "I have an account → Log in" on the title and the Home Screen hint show only while the server says it can make and keep an account (the page asks as it starts and every half minute on screen; no answer is a no). When it stops saying yes, the rows go with the page's next answer, each menu's highlight staying on its row (when the lit row itself goes: Keep playing in the pause menu, never "Log out", which acts at once; the next row on the title), and an hourly card that is up goes without an answer; an account card already open stays, with what the kid typed, and says "We can't reach the game's home right now. ## Sound and juice

The rules are in [[DESIGN]] § Sound and § Aesthetic direction; this is where each sound and flourish goes.

**Cues.** Each plays with the thing on screen it goes with, from the screen that shows it; nothing plays for a moment the screen does not show.

| Cue         | Plays                                                                                                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `encounter` | A wild animal jumps out, as the iris starts to close.                                                         |
| `move`      | The cursor moves on any list (battle menu, switch list, doctor, pause menu, the title's menu, confirm and starters), a doctor's tab changes, a doctor's puzzle swaps, an animal or a whole kind unpicked to go home, the doctor's confirm's choice moves or its No by Escape, Escape back from the switch list. On an attack row, left/right blip at the level's pitch: higher for medium and hard. |
| `confirm`   | A choice is made: an attack (at its level's pitch), Leash, Switch, Run, an animal sent in, the result card left, the doctor's card opening, an animal picked (to heal or to go home) or a whole kind picked to go home, Set them free, a confirm choice, a tool picked or Bye at the doctor, the pause menu opening, an animal, a card or an option picked in the pause menu (Go first plays `lead` instead, Go and Go home on the Worlds screen `travel`), the Worlds screen opened, a name saved, the language changed, Sound turned on; on the title, Continue, New game, a confirm choice, a starter picked and the name box's Enter. |
| `correct`   | "Correct!", in battle and at the doctor.                                                                     |
| `wrong`     | "Not quite!", in battle and at the doctor.                                                                   |
| `hit`       | An attack lands: with the shake and the burst beside the status box ("−N").                                  |
| `faint`     | An animal lies down, tired.                                                                                    |
| `throw`, `wobble` | The leash flies; tick-tock while it wobbles on the animal.                                              |
| `caught`, `boing` | "Caught!" with the cheer, the stars and both bursts of confetti, all on the same frame; or "It broke free!". |
| `heal`      | The doctor's heal: the HP bars filling, the sparkles; and the animals going home, made better, as they slide away. |
| `coins`     | Tokens change hands at the doctor: counted in after a hand-over with the "+N", or out for a tool with the "−N". |
| `lead`      | A new animal goes first ("Fox goes first!"), from a number key, a card clicked, tapped or dropped at the top, or the pause menu.                             |
| `won`       | The result card says "You won!", with the winner's hop and stars.                                             |
| `chop`      | The axe lands in a tree: a woody knock with the blade's click, and a thud and a rustle as the tree comes down. |
| `crack`     | The pickaxe lands on a rock: a crack over a thump, and the pebbles ticking down.                              |
| `travel`    | A trip to another world: a whoosh rising as the world closes round the trainer, and a four-note chime climbing as the new one opens. |

Silent on purpose: typing an answer, a name or a world's number, a key that does nothing (a greyed row, a tired animal picked), a miss's puff, running away, "Good try!".

**M, anywhere.** M turns the sound off and on over any screen — the title, explore, a battle, the doctor's card, the pause menu — except while an answer or a name is being typed, where it is a letter. A held M flips once. A chip at the top centre (on the title, under its name) says what happened for three seconds, fading over the last half-second: "Sound is on", or "Sound is off" with "Press M to turn it back on" under it. The chip takes no keys.

**Flourishes.** Small, made of what the game already has, and always inside the beats that already exist: none adds a wait or takes a key.

- The trainer walks: arms and legs swing from shoulder and hip, one step leading with each foot, the body rocking onto the landing foot and stretching a little in the hop.
- The lead follows the trainer, bouncing on each step, and when who goes first changes it shrinks away and the new lead grows in (§ Explore mode).
- A chop or a break: the trainer's swing, the tree tipping over with chips flying, the rock shaking and crumbling into pebbles (§ Explore mode).
- At every tent: the witch doctor's hop and wave as the trainer comes near, and his staff's knock (§ Explore mode). Silent: there is no cue for them.
- Into a battle: the iris (§ Battle mode, Entering).
- In a battle: the lunge, shake, puff, hop, recall and appearance (§ Battle mode); a hit's burst beside the status box of the animal hit, popping in, holding and fading within the hit's beat; the glow on the status box of the animal whose turn it is, and the lighter segment breathing on the wild animal's HP bar while an attack is highlighted; the ring of dust round a tired animal as it lies down, and the z's that rise over it while it rests; a winner's hop with a few stars; a caught animal's cheer (two hops, the first with a turn) with stars, the confetti round it, in the palette's colours, falling back to the ground within about 1.3 s, and the poppers' bigger confetti, up the sides of the scene and down over it within about 2 s.
- On the result card: a new friend's name in big letters over a burst of rays, with stars; a few stars off "You won!" (§ Battle mode, End).
- At the doctor: stars that sparkle along each healed animal's HP bar as it fills, with the "+N"; the animals going home sliding off their rows as they fade; a "+N" or "−N" over the tokens; a tool bought lighting up and hopping on its row; a row that can't be picked giving a little shake.

**Reduced motion.** When the system asks for less motion (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`), everything that moves moves less, and what happened still shows: the iris becomes a dim that comes and goes on the same clock; the trainer's hop is a third as high and its limbs swing less, with no rocking; the lunge, shake and hop travel about a third as far, and so does the follower's bounce; a caught animal's cheer is one small hop with no turn; a miss's puff swells less and stays where it is; the ring of dust round a tired animal rolls out a third as far; confetti, the poppers' too, is fewer and drifts down without tumbling; stars twinkle in place, in the scene and on the result card; the card's rays stand still and the name's letters fade in; the leash flies in a flatter arc and wobbles less, and one that pops off rises a third as high; a hit's burst and the doctor's "+N" and "−N" fade in place, and the whose-turn glow and the HP bar's preview hold still; the answer box's shake, and a refused row's on the switch list, are 2 px; the doctor's row doesn't hop and its sparkles twinkle in place, the animals going home fade where they are, and a row that can't be picked shakes 2 px; a resting animal's z's grow and fade near where they start instead of rising; the Sound switch's knob doesn't slide; the trainer's swing is half as big, a tree shrinks away where it stands instead of tipping over, a rock crumbles without shaking, and three chips or pebbles fade in place instead of flying; a trip to another world fades the sky in and out instead of closing a circle, and the world's number fades in without popping or bobbing. The faint (lying down), the recall and the appearance keep their shape, the follower's swap too, since they say what happened: the one called back still shrinks away, rising a third as high, and the one sent in grows to its size without the bounce past it. Other screens say what reduced motion does to them where they are described: the title's drift and letters, the starters, the butterflies, the witch doctor, Talk's glow, the turn-sideways tablet, the pause menu's re-sort. What stays as it is: an HP bar sliding to its new length (its number changes at once), a pressed button giving way under a finger, the figures' breathing (about a pixel), and every fade.

**`?debug`** shows the last four cues asked for, newest last, under the position (with ✕ while sound is off), so a screenshot shows which sound would have played.

## While the game does not answer

On the game's own address, when the server does not answer (the first start, or the server down), nginx shows a page of its own (`nginx/502.html`) in place of the game: the sky, one cream card with three dots bobbing in the title's colours (still with reduced motion), "Back in a moment!" and "The game is getting ready." It speaks the language the game would start in (`?lang=`, the one picked on this device, the browser's), and English with no script. It takes no keys and reloads itself every 5 seconds, so the game is back at the first load after the server answers. A deploy swaps the server without a gap, so a deploy alone never shows it.

## Component reuse

One `PuzzlePanel` component serves battle and doctor, with one `NumberPad` in it on touch (at the doctor it also carries a token sum's story over the prompt, and Back), and one helper (`input/answer.ts`) turns keys into the typed answer for both. One `BundleCard` draws a card of the party (one animal, or a stack of its kind) and one `BundleAnimals` a card's animals, wherever the team is shown as cards: the HUD's column and its open card, and the pause menu's card; one `stackSummary` (`hp.ts`) words how a card of several is, on the HUD's card and the pause menu's row alike. The doctor's list is not cards: its tabs share one grid of rows, the kinds' rows and check boxes on Set free among them, so it draws its own rows with the same `HpBar`. One `HpBar`, which can write a word in its empty track (the doctor's "tired", on Heal and Set free), draws slim without numbers for all of a stack's HP together (`thin`), draws chunky with its numbers big (`thick`: the battle's status boxes) and previews a hit (`preview`: the HP it would leave, a lighter segment), with the colour bands of `hpBand`. Every card — the HUD's, the battle panel's, the doctor's, the pause menu, the title's — has one look, from the tokens `--panel-bg`, `--radius` and `--hud-shadow` in `styles.css`, not from a shared component. New UI reuses these before inventing.

**The battle's pieces** take their data as props and assume nothing about a wild opponent, so any fight between two animals can be drawn with them: `StatusBox` (a name and a chunky `HpBar`; the paw of the other side, `opponent`; the turn's glow, `acting`; the preview; a hit's `HitBurst` beside it, on the side it is told), `AttackTile` (a name, a level's word and a `HitBadge`), `MoveButton` (a `MoveIcon` — the leash, switch, run, and a door for a match's Leave — its word, an optional hint in words and colour, greyed with `off`), `LevelPicker` (the three levels, each with its word and badge), `TopicChips` (a puzzle's kinds as their glyphs, `kindGlyph`) and `ActionPreview` (the right card before a pick, from an attack's or a move's words and numbers). `menu.ts` takes the row of moves after the attacks as a list (`WILD_MOVES`: Leash, Switch, Run). **A friendly match's screen is the battle's own** (§ Friendly matches): `MatchController` fills the same `battle` view, with `battle.vs` (the other player) and `battle.moves` (`MATCH_MOVES`, Switch and Leave, through the same `menuKey`, `actionAt` and `rowOf`), so `BattlePanel` draws both: its two status boxes from the view's teams, `acting` from whose turn it is, the attack tiles, the level picker, the badges and the preview (`attackDamage`, `landHit`) as a wild battle's, the other's animal named with its owner, the door for Leave, a `waiting` screen with the other's puzzle (a `ShownPuzzle`, in `PuzzlePanel`'s `watch`) where the preview would be, and the match's own result (`MatchResult`) and notes (`MatchNotes`). The chunky toy button is a look from tokens, not a component: `--press`, `--accent-edge` and `--edge` in `styles.css`, a darker edge under the face that squashes flat when pressed. A list whose rows line up in columns (the switch list, the doctor's list) is a grid whose rows are subgrids, sized by its longest name; its edge columns are never a fixed width, because a subgrid's padding is a margin on the items at its edges, and a fixed-width column can't make room for it (the caret slid into the gap against the name).

A component that shows one thing — an animal's HP bar, its name — is keyed by that thing's id wherever a different thing can take its place on screen, so a transition never runs from one animal's value to another's (a newcomer's bar must not drain from the full bar of the animal that left).
