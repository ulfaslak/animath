# UI Spec

How the title and the two modes are laid out and behave. Visual language is in [[DESIGN]]; rules are in [[PRODUCT]].

## Frame

- The game is full-screen: one `<canvas>` filling the window, a DOM overlay (`#ui`) on top. The overlay is `pointer-events: none` except for its children, so the canvas keeps focus.
- Minimum supported viewport is a laptop at 1280×720 and a tablet in landscape at 1024×768 (and 1180×820); every screen holds at both, with the touch controls on at the tablet sizes (§ Pointer and touch). A touch screen held upright (taller than wide) shows "Turn your tablet sideways to play!" over everything, with a tablet outline turning onto its side (with reduced motion, lying still on its side), and takes no taps until it is turned. Phones are out of scope.
- The camera never zooms or rotates. Resizing the window changes how much world is visible horizontally; vertical extent stays 14 tiles.

## Pointer and touch

A mouse and a finger work everything a keyboard works, and a tablet plays with no keyboard at all.

- **A click or a tap is a key press** — the one path to every action. Every row, button and card a key can reach is a button, and pressing it sends the key the keyboard would (`input/press.ts`): Enter, Escape, a digit, an arrow held down, or one of the pointer's own keys for what it touched — a row, an option of the animal picked in the pause menu, a battle level button, a language, a card of the party column, an animal on an open card, or a card dropped at another place. They go through the same screen routing and the same guards as keys: the quiet moment before a new choice takes a pick (§ Battle mode, "A quiet moment"), a turn playing. A button never keeps the focus, so Enter or Space can't press it a second time.
- **A tap begins and ends on one button, on one screen**: the finger (or the mouse button) goes down on the button and comes up on it, or hardly moved (20 px), while the same screen is up, and the button presses as it lifts. So a finger that slides off cancels, and a button that changes under a still finger (the first touch after the keyboard brings the touch controls, and Go! loses its key cap) still takes the tap. A finger lifted over something it did not go down on presses nothing, nor does one that went down before the screen changed (a row touched while the turn played, lifted once the menu is back; one animal's option in the pause menu, lifted after a tap on the team put up another's; a button held while the tablet was turned upright), nor one on a row that moved while it rested there (the team re-sorted by another finger's Move up). Each finger counts on its own, so a tap on Talk or Menu works while the other thumb walks on the D-pad. The number pad's keys and the D-pad act as the finger lands instead. The browser's own click from a finger or a mouse is never used; a click with no pointer behind it (a screen reader's) still presses.
- **What a tap does**: on the doctor's list, the pause menu, the party cards and the title's menu and confirm, a tap does the row at once (a card of several animals opens to show them, and an animal there goes first): nothing is spent there, every move can be moved back, and the confirm's "Yes" is a deliberate choice; on the doctor's list, its confirm and the title's confirm it waits their quiet moment first, as Enter does (picking an animal to go home, which can always be unpicked, does not wait). A tap on one of the doctor's tabs goes there at once. In a battle, where a pick spends the turn, a tap on a row only highlights it and a tap on a level button only sets that attack's level: **Go!** (Enter) does the highlighted row. On the starters a tap on a name tag, or on the animal above it, lights that one, and the card's "Pick the Rabbit!" (Enter) picks. The result card is one big button: a tap anywhere on the screen goes on, and waits as Enter waits.
- **Buttons for keys**, shown with a mouse too: Go! (and Back on the switch list the kid opened) in the battle's right card; Back beside a doctor's puzzle; Save and Back under the pause menu's name box; Back and "Pick the Rabbit!" or "Let's go!" on the title's starter card; "Esc Menu" under the party cards. Without the touch controls the battle's buttons show their keys' caps ("Go! Enter"); elsewhere the key reminder under the buttons names the keys. A mouse over something it can press lights it faintly; nothing is only on hover.
- **The touch controls** show where the main pointer is a finger (`pointer: coarse`: a tablet), and after that follow the latest input: a touch or a pen shows them, a key pressed on a real keyboard hides them (typing in a name box and the page's own presses don't count), a mouse changes nothing. With them on:
  - **Explore**: a D-pad in the bottom-left corner (four arms of 64 × 64 px round an empty centre) walks as the arrow keys do: a finger held on an arm keeps walking, a tap is one step, sliding the thumb onto another arm turns, and lifting it stops; a finger resting in the centre presses nothing. In the bottom-right corner, **Talk** (96 px, Enter), lit orange and glowing while the player faces a tent (the glow pulses out, or with reduced motion stays lit), and **Menu** (72 px, Escape) beside it. The hints name them: "Hold an arrow to walk", "Tap Talk to talk to the doctor", and under the party cards "Tap an animal to pick who goes first". The message line narrows to stay between the D-pad and the buttons.
  - **Puzzles**, in battle and at the doctor: a number pad beside the prompt and the answer box, in the right half of the puzzle card — 1 2 3 / 4 5 6 / 7 8 9 / − 0 ⌫, and a tall OK down the right edge — under the right thumb. Its keys are the keys (a digit, the minus, Backspace, Enter for OK) and act as the finger lands; the pad dims while the screen takes no typing. Beside it the prompt is smaller (`clamp(32px, 6vh, 52px)`, and `clamp(32px, 4.5vh, 40px)` for a prompt over 11 characters) and wraps between numbers, never inside one. "Tap the answer, then OK" replaces the key reminder. The answer box is not a text field, so a tablet's own keyboard never comes up over a puzzle.
  - **Sizes**: every row and button is at least 48 px tall (`--tap`). The battle panel and the doctor's card are `clamp(364px, 48vh, 400px)`, so seven rows fit in battle (a bear's four attacks and Leash, Switch, Run); at the doctor the doctor's line and the tokens move over the puzzle and the left card runs the card's full height: the tabs, the list, which a finger scrolls, and the rows under it. A level button's box runs its row's height and meets its neighbours, so a finger that lands near one presses it, not the row; the languages on a Language row are a finger tall too.
  - **Reminders**: key reminders become tap hints ("Tap an attack, then Go!", "Tap an animal to help it"), or go where every row is plainly a button (the pause menu, the title); key caps inside buttons go.
  - **Name boxes** (the pause menu's, the title's) use the tablet's own keyboard. Tapping New name or the starter's Pick opens the box and focuses it inside the tap, as a tablet needs to bring its keyboard up; while it is open the menu, or the starter card, moves to the top of the screen, clear of the keyboard rising from the bottom; its Enter (labelled "done" or "go") saves; closing the box puts back any scroll the keyboard caused.
- **A game, not a page**: no pinch or double-tap zoom, no pull-to-refresh or rubber-band scroll, no text selection or long-press menu under a finger (the name boxes select and edit as usual), and no delay before a tap counts. Only lists scroll: the pause menu on a screen too short for it, and a long team wherever it is listed (the party column, a card's animals, the switch list, the doctor's list).
- **Across screens**: a finger still on the D-pad when a battle, the doctor or the menu takes the screen lets go there, presses nothing on the new screen when it lifts (not the battle row under it), and walks nothing when explore comes back until it is lifted and pressed again, as a held key does (§ Battle mode, "Keys across modes"). Away from the page it lets go the same way: when the window loses the focus or the page is hidden, where explore drops its held keys, and when the browser cancels the finger, the D-pad's arrow goes out and the finger is forgotten, so a finger still resting there walks nothing until it is lifted and pressed again, and any other finger can steer.

## Title

The first screen, every time the page opens (except the throwaway switches `?new`, `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop`, which go straight into explore, and a page that reloaded itself mid-game to catch up with another window, which goes straight back into play), and the one Quit to title returns to. Nothing is started, walked, rolled or saved behind it.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      A n i m a t h                       │
│                                                          │
│  ┌──────────────────────────────┐                        │
│  │ ▸ Continue     Pip 3 animals │     [trainer + team]   │
│  │   New game                   │      (the world,       │
│  │   Language  (English) Dansk  │       drifting)        │
│  │   Sound             (●) On   │                        │
│  │    ↑ ↓ choose · Enter pick   │                        │
│  └──────────────────────────────┘                        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Backdrop**: the world where the game stands (the saved spot, or the spawn tile with no save), seen through the explore camera pointed a little aside so the trainer stands right of centre, clear of the menu: the same angle, no zoom, no turn, drifting slowly (a sway of about a tile, over half a minute; still with reduced motion). The trainer faces the saved way, the boat on their back when the game has one (or under them, when it was saved out on the water); one animal of each kind in the team stands on the walkable tiles nearest it (none, far out on a lake),
 in the cards' order (with no save, the starters do), breathing, so a team of a hundred is still a handful of figures. They are scenery and go when the game starts.
- **Name**: "Animath" at the top, each letter in a palette colour (coral, orange, green, blue, amber) with a cream rim, bobbing gently out of step; still with reduced motion.
- **Menu** (a card at the left): **Continue** (only with a saved game; beside it the name of the team's first animal that is standing and the team's size, "Pip 3 animals", or under it when a long name leaves no room beside it, so twelve of the widest letters show whole), **New game**, **Language** (every language in its own words, the one on screen lit), **Sound** (the pause menu's switch, and its state in words). Up/down or W/S move the cursor, wrapping; Enter or Space pick, at once (Continue carries on and New game asks first, so the menu needs no quiet moment); left/right or A/D change the setting on its row (the next language; sound off on the left, on on the right; Enter changes it too), and do nothing on the others. M switches the sound too, as on every screen. Menu moves and picks make the menus' soft blips (§ Sound and juice). Escape does nothing. The cursor starts on the first row: Continue when there is one. Under the rows, a key reminder (on a setting's row, how to change it), and, on every title of a page that cannot keep the game or has a newer build's game waiting, the line that says so: no storage at all, no room left after a write failed, a newer build's save in the key or on the server. Start screen's title says it too, Continue or not.
- **Confirm** (New game with a saved game): a card over a dim backdrop that puts the menu out of reach (a screen reader's too), "Start a new game?", "Your game with Pip is put away safely." (on a page that keeps nothing, "Your game with Pip can't be kept on this page."), "You start again with a new animal.", then **No, go back** (lit first) and **Yes, new game**. Up/down pick, without wrapping; Enter or Space do it, after a quiet moment (§ Battle mode, "A quiet moment"); Escape goes back. So however Enter is mashed on New game, the confirm takes none of it, and starting over takes a deliberate Down first. Back returns to the menu on New game.
- **Starters** (New game without a save, or Yes): the 3D starter stage replaces the world: every starter in catalog order side by side on a round meadow with tufts, flowers and bushes, big (scaled towards a common size, so a squat frog is sized by its width), the lit one on a warm ring, facing the camera and bouncing (a third as high with reduced motion), the others turned towards it. "Pick your first animal!" across the top; each animal's name in a tag under its feet (the lit tag orange); a card at the bottom with the lit one's name and every kind of sum its attacks can ask, at any level ("The Rabbit loves adding, taking away, missing numbers, times tables, and number patterns!") and the keys. Left/right (A/D, and up/down) move, wrapping; Enter or Space pick, after a quiet moment; Escape goes back to the menu on New game. The row is as long as the starter list.
- **Name box** (after a pick; the picked one hops for joy, twice, or once and small with reduced motion): the card asks "What will you call your Rabbit?" over a name box with the species as its placeholder, the name rule, and "Leave it empty and it's just called Rabbit." (or, when the typed text will not be stored as shown, what it will be called). It works like the pause menu's name box: every key but Enter, Escape and Tab types; Enter starts the game (an empty box starts it without a name), after a quiet moment, counted from the box coming up and the last Enter (Space and the numbers are letters here), and never on auto-repeat; Escape goes back to the starters with the same one lit.
- **Starting**: the game begins at the spawn tile with the explore HUD and the controls hint, the message line saying what start-up found about the save when there was something (the save that didn't load); the first Continue after the page opens says "Welcome back!" (a Continue after Quit to title says nothing).
- **Pointer**: a menu row or a language does its thing at once, a confirm choice once the confirm's quiet moment has passed; a starter's tag, or the animal above it, lights it; the starter card's "Pick the Rabbit!" and the name box's "Let's go!" are Enter and its Back is Escape (§ Pointer and touch).
- It holds at 1024×768 in both languages.

## Explore mode

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ┌────────────┐                                         │
│ │ Party HUD  │              (3D world)                 │
│ │ name + HP  │                                         │
│ └────────────┘                                         │
│                                                        │
│                          [player]                      │
│                                                        │
│                                                        │
│               ┌──────────────────────────┐             │
│               │ hint / message line      │             │
│               └──────────────────────────┘             │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Tokens** (top-right): a pill with the gold coin and "23 tokens", and under it one chip per tool the kid owns, its picture and its name ("Axe"). Nothing to press. With `?debug` they sit under the position and the cues.
- **Party HUD** (top-left): one card per kind in the party, in battle order (the party is in bundles, [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Party", so the cards top to bottom are the order the animals fight in). A card of one animal shows it: its name (nickname if set) and its HP bar with `hp/max` text. A card of several is a stack, with two cards peeking out behind it: the kind's name, how many ("Rabbit ×4") and how many can play ("3 ready · 1 tired", "All ready", "All tired"). The cards are all as wide as the longest name needs, from 260 px, so twelve of the widest letters show whole, and a tag that no longer fits beside the name goes under it. A card whose animals are all tired is greyed with a "tired" tag. The lead's card ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Party") is outlined in the accent colour, its key cap filled, and tagged "goes first", so it reads without colour too. With two or more cards, the first nine show the number key that picks them (as a key cap). With one card there is nobody to choose between: no key caps, no tag, nothing to drag. Under the cards, a small panel shows the keys: "1–n Pick who goes first", "Esc Menu", and, with two or more cards, "Drag a card to move it"; with the touch controls on, "Tap a card to open it, then tap who goes first" (or, with no card of several, "Tap an animal to pick who goes first") and "Hold a card, then drag it" (§ Pointer and touch).
- **A stack's animals**: pointing at a card of several with the mouse shows its animals in a card of their own beside the column, top-aligned with it (lower when the card is near the bottom), one row each in party order: name, HP bar with numbers, and "goes first" on the lead or "tired" at 0 HP, the rows lined up in columns. They stay while the pointer is on the card or on them. A click or a tap on the stack opens it and keeps it open, a second closes it, and so does a step, choosing who goes first from it, or leaving explore. A click or a tap on an animal there makes it go first (its card moves to the top, and it to the front of its card); on a tired one, the message line says it is tired. The list scrolls when it is taller than the screen allows. A click or a tap on a card of one animal makes it go first.
- **Moving a card**: a card is dragged up or down the column to another place, with a mouse as soon as it moves, with a finger after holding it still for a moment (a finger that moves first scrolls the column). The card lifts, with a deeper shadow, follows the pointer (never past the column's first or last card), and the others slide aside to show where it will land; let go, it takes that place, and the party is put in that order (the card at the top leads). A drag is never also a tap. The column scrolls, with a finger or a wheel, when it is taller than the room it has (above the D-pad with the touch controls on), and a card carried near its top or bottom scrolls it.
- **Message line** (bottom-centre): up to two lines in one box. Above, the latest thing said — a `message` event, a line the client words from an event that carries no words (the doctor's lines; `nothing-to-interact`, Enter away from a tent), or, when the game starts, what it found about the save ("Welcome back!" when a saved game was picked up, or why a new game started: "Your saved game didn't load, so here is a new one.") — for 5 seconds, then it fades; the seconds count only while the explore HUD is on screen, so a line said during a battle or a doctor visit is still there to read afterwards. Below, the controls hint ("Arrows / WASD to walk") until the player has walked 5 steps; bumps don't count, since a key held against a wall bumps every frame. With neither, the box fades away. After a party edit that changes who goes first, the line names the new lead ("Fox goes first!"), or says why an animal can't be chosen; a line about the lead goes at once when a battle or the doctor changes the party.
- **Interaction prompt**: when the player faces a doctor's tent, the lower line shows "Press Enter to talk to the doctor", in place of the controls hint. Enter anywhere else says "Walk up to a tent to talk to the doctor.", which gives way to the prompt as soon as the player faces a tent.
- **Saving shows nothing**: no icon, no "Saved", and nothing when the server is out of reach, because the game is always saved in the browser. The one exception is a page that is behind the save (below).
- **Behind another window**: a window of the game that another window has played past takes no keys. On screen while the kid plays in the other window, it shows a card over every screen, battle included, on a dimmed backdrop: "You kept playing in another window." and a pill button "Get your newest game [Enter]" (Enter under a click or a tap, § Pointer and touch; no key cap with the touch controls on). Enter, Space (not while an answer or a name is being typed), the button, or coming to the window loads the newest game, straight back into play without the title (a window that was on the title opens the title again), and the message line then says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!". The window the kid is using never shows the card, except after it has reloaded itself 3 times in a minute. A page that is behind for another reason reloads by itself as soon as it is on screen; after 3 of those in a minute it shows the same card, titled "Your newest game is ready." when another game has taken its place (one taken from the server backup) and "A new game is ready." when the save was cleared.
- **Loading**: "Loading…" over an empty screen until the title (or a throwaway game) is up, which is at once when the browser holds the save; only a browser that has lost its save but still knows the player waits, at most a few seconds, for the server's copy.
- **Controls**: arrows/WASD to walk, Enter/Space to interact (after a quiet moment, counted from explore taking the screen back and from the last Enter, Space or number: an Enter mashed on Continue, through a result card or after Bye talks to nobody), number keys 1–9 choose the card in that place to go first (its first animal standing leads, and the card moves to the top; a card whose animals are all tired stays put and the message line says they are tired and need the doctor), Escape opens the pause menu. Holding a key keeps walking; a tap moves one tile; letting go stops, however the key comes up (with Shift down, say). Pressing Ctrl, Cmd or Alt stops the walk too, and the page hidden or out of focus lets go of every key. On a tablet the D-pad, Talk and Menu are those keys (§ Pointer and touch).
- **The lead walks behind the trainer**, as in the Game Boy Color sequels: the animal that goes first (the first one not tired) follows one tile behind, on the tile the trainer just left, at the trainer's pace, bouncing a little with each step (the small ones bound, the big ones plod) and turning the way it walks. It is a view of the party and the trainer's steps and nothing more: it blocks nothing, meets nothing, and the authority never hears of it. Stepping back onto its tile swaps the two, and it steps round the trainer, on the side away from the camera, rather than through them, so even a bear never hides the trainer. When the trainer is put somewhere without walking — a new game, Continue or a reload, the trip to the tent after a lost battle — it is put beside the trainer at once, never walked across the map: behind, else to a side, else in front, on the first of those a trainer could stand on (so never water, rock, a tree or a tent); with none, it turns up on the tile the trainer's first step leaves. When who goes first changes (a number key, the pause menu, a knock-out, the doctor, a catch), the one following shrinks away and the new one grows in with a little bounce: the battle's recall and appearance. **When every animal is tired, nobody follows**: the team rests, and the first animal the doctor makes fit comes out behind the trainer as the card plays its heal. On the title the team gathers round the trainer instead (§ Title); Quit to title takes the follower away and Continue puts it back. **Out on the water**, the one following is the first animal standing that swims: it swims behind the boat, low in the water with a gentle bob, on the tile the boat left (the shore tile first, as the trainer hops in), and walks up onto land behind the trainer when they land. With no swimmer standing, the lead on land hops into the boat once the trainer is in it (it shrinks away on the shore and grows in at the bow, small enough to fit) and rides there until the trainer lands, when it hops out onto the ground beside them. An animal that can't swim never stands on the water, and one that lives only in the water never on land.

- The grid position is not shown to players. `?debug` in the URL puts the position and facing in the top-right corner.
- The player stays centred; the world scrolls under them. Chunks are built 2 chunks out in every direction so nothing pops in at the edge.
- **Butterflies**: six little butterflies (two with reduced motion, flapping slower) flutter over the land round the middle of the screen, on the title's drifting world too (far out on a lake, with no land near, over the water). Decoration only: not an animal of the catalog (nothing a kid would expect to battle or catch), never on a tile, never in the way, and the authority never hears of them. None ever appears or vanishes in view, whatever the window's shape: they start round the middle as the world appears (the first frame, Continue, the trip to the tent), one left behind a walking trainer comes back from just off screen ahead of it, and when reduced motion comes on the spare ones fly off screen before they go.
- **Biomes read at a glance** ([[DESIGN]] § Palette): the meadow's bright grass with flowers, the forest's darker floor crowded with dark pines, young trees and bushes, the river's sand with reed beds, the mountain's grey-green turf with pebbles, boulder fields and snow on the boulders of its highest rocks. Tall grass reads as "animals here" in every biome: a patch darker and greener than the ground round it, with blades standing in it; at the river, a yellow-green reed bed with tall reeds and brown heads. Out in a lake, the deep water, the sea, is a deeper blue than the two tiles of paler shallows along every shore.
- **The boat**: once the kid owns it, the trainer carries it on their back like a shell, standing on its bow, its brown bottom out and its coral rim round the edge; it peeks over the shoulders from the front. Bought at the doctor, it grows onto their back with a little bounce. A step onto the water takes a little longer than a step (0.55 s): the trainer hops in while the boat swings off their back, out to their side, turns over as it grows to full size and drops under them, right side up, a coral pennant rising at its stern. Out on the water they stand in it, their legs hidden by its sides, and glide from tile to tile without walking or hopping, the two of them rocking gently. A step back onto walkable ground plays the same swing backwards. With reduced motion, the step takes its usual time, the boat snaps from the back to the water (or back) half way through it, and nothing rocks. A game picked up out on the water (Continue, the title behind it) has the trainer in the boat already.
- **Who goes first out on the water**: the lead there, the first animal standing that swims (the otter, the frog): its card is the one tagged "goes first", and the one that follows. A number key or a card for one that can't swim says so on the message line ("Squirrel can't swim! On the water, an animal that swims goes first."), with no ding, and changes nothing. Back on land the lead is the land's again.

## Battle mode

Borrowed composition from the Game Boy games: your animal from behind at bottom-left, the opponent from the front at top-right, status boxes opposite each sprite, and a panel across the bottom. Our addition is a **puzzle area** that takes real space, because solving is the whole game.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ┌──────────────────┐                                     │
│ │ Wild Otter       │                      [opponent]     │
│ │ ▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░ 18/32 │                       (front)       │
│ └──────────────────┘                                     │
│                                   ┌──────────────────┐   │
│     [your animal]                 │ Squirrel         │   │
│        (back)                     │ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░  15/20 │   │
│                                   └──────────────────┘   │
│   ( Wild Otter used Splash! It hits Squirrel for 5. )    │
├──────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│ ▸ Nut Toss   (easy) medium  hard │                       │
│   Scurry Kick               hard │       7 × 8 = ?       │
│   Leash                strong ●  │         [ 56 ]        │
│   Switch                         │    Type the answer,   │
│   Run                            │    then press Enter   │
└──────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

- **Entering**: the encounter jingle plays and an **iris** closes on the player: a circle of world rimmed in the accent colour, with the ink colour outside it, shrinking over 0.4 s. It starts slowly, so the step into the grass lands in plain view. The screen stays closed for 0.1 s. Then the battle scene and the panel appear under an iris that opens on the wild animal over 0.45 s, while "A wild … appears!" is read. The explore HUD goes at once. With reduced motion the screen dims to the ink colour and clears instead, on the same clock. The iris is drawn over everything and takes no keys; the battle's keys wait for the opening lines as before.
- **Scene** (above the panel): a second Three.js scene with its own fixed perspective camera, low behind the player's animal. The image is shifted up (a lens shift, so verticals stay vertical) until the scene is centred in the part of the canvas the panel leaves free. The two animals stand three-quarters on, each turned to face the other, and are scaled towards a common height (by the square root of the ratio, clamped to 0.8–1.6) so an otter and a deer both read at battle size while a bear still looks bigger than a squirrel. Both idle; an attacker lunges; a hit shakes the target; a miss puffs beside it; a tired animal lies down to rest — on its belly, legs tucked out of sight, head a little low, a tail held up laid down behind — in a ring of dust that rolls out from round it and fades, and stays down, with little z's rising over its head once it is down, never over it; an animal called back shrinks away and the one sent in grows into its place with a little bounce; the winner hops, with a few stars twinkling round it. Background: the biome's own ground ([[DESIGN]] § Palette) with tufts of its tall grass (short reeds with brown heads at the river), plus trees (forest), boulders, the biggest capped with snow (mountain), or water behind (river). Out on the sea (deep water) the ground is the deep water's blue with its surface over both animals' feet, so they sit low in the water, a few paler crests of waves, and far behind a sandy shore with grass beyond it. Nothing stands between the camera and the player's animal, and no reed on the line from the camera to either animal.
- **Status boxes**: name, HP bar with numbers. No level (there are none). A nickname of twelve of the widest letters ("WWWWWWWWWWWW") shows whole at 1024 px. Opponent box top-left, named "Wild …" so a squirrel meeting a squirrel can tell them apart; player box on the right just above the narration line. A hit pops the damage ("−14") over the box of the animal that took it. When another animal steps in, the player box shows its HP at once; the bar never slides from the last animal's.
- **Bottom panel**: `clamp(260px, 40vh, 360px)` tall (`clamp(364px, 48vh, 400px)` with the touch controls on, rows a finger tall), two columns at 2 : 3 (1 : 1 below 900 px wide, where the attack names need the room), and 3 : 2 while the party list is up, whose rows hold a twelve-letter name beside the HP bar and the tag.
  - **Actions** (left): one row per attack, then Leash, Switch and Run. Every attack row has its **own level**, and the three levels are called **easy, medium and hard**: the highlighted row shows three level buttons with those words, its level lit; every other attack row shows its level's word. The word names the level, not the puzzle's place on the engine's 1–10 scale: a bear's "easy" is still a bear-hard puzzle ([[PRODUCT]] §4). Levels are remembered per attack of each species for as long as the page is open (a squirrel's Nut Toss keeps its level from battle to battle; a reload starts every attack at easy). While a puzzle is up, the menu beside it lights the attack it belongs to at its level, also in a battle picked up from a save. Keys: up/down or W/S move the cursor (wrapping round); left/right or A/D change the highlighted attack's level, and only its own; 1/2/3 set the highlighted attack to that level and attack at once; Enter or Space do the highlighted action. Switch is greyed when nobody can step in, and Enter on it does nothing. While a turn plays the rows dim and keys do nothing. Each time the narration brings the menu back (after the opening lines, after every turn) it takes a pick — Enter, Space, 1, 2 or 3 — only after a quiet moment (below), so a key mashed through the battle text, at any pace, never chooses the next action; a pick it ignores changes nothing, not even a level. The arrows move at once. Go! stays dimmed until a pick would count. After a switch the menu starts on the new animal's first attack. A click or a tap on a row only highlights it and one on a level button only sets that attack's level, at once, as the arrows do; **Go!** in the right card is Enter (§ Pointer and touch).
  - **Puzzle** (right): until something is picked, a title for what the highlighted row does — "Pick an attack" on an attack, "Throw the leash", "Switch animals", "Run away" — one sentence about it ("Scurry Kick on hard: adding, taking away, missing numbers, or times tables. It hits the wild Rabbit for 14.": what a puzzle at that level can actually be — the engine's `puzzleTopics`, so a hard missing number that sits in a times table is named as times tables — joined as the language lists them; for Leash "Try to catch the wild Rabbit! It works best when its HP is low."; for Switch "Send in a different animal. It uses up your turn." — "You need a second animal to switch. Catch one with the leash!" with a party of one, "Your other animals are tired." when nobody else is standing; for Run "You always get away. The wild Rabbit stays in the grass."), **Go!**, dimmed when it would do nothing (a greyed Switch, a turn playing), and a key reminder that fits the row: "↑ ↓ choose · ← → how hard · Enter go" on an attack, the only row with a level, "↑ ↓ choose · Enter go" on the others and "↑ ↓ choose" alone on a greyed Switch; with the touch controls on, "Tap an attack, then Go!" on an attack or a greyed Switch and "Tap Go!" on the others. Once chosen: the prompt in very large type and an answer box, and with the touch controls on the number pad beside them. Number keys type, a minus only as the first character, Backspace deletes, at most seven characters; Enter submits, and does nothing until a digit has been typed. **There is no way back**: once a puzzle is shown the turn is committed ([[PRODUCT]] §4), so Escape does nothing. Correct → the card flashes green and says "Correct!". Wrong → the answer box shakes red and says "Not quite!"; the correct answer is **not** shown (they will meet the puzzle again), and the narration line says "Missed!".
  - **Party list** (left, in place of the actions): Switch opens it. One row per party member in party order — name (nickname if set), HP bar with numbers, and a tag: "in battle" on the one in front, "tired" on a knocked-out one, and out on the water "can't swim" on one that can't swim (it sits the battle out in the boat); all of them are greyed and can't be picked, and the sentence for one that can't swim says so ("Squirrel can't swim. It stays in the boat."). When nobody else standing can swim, the Switch row is greyed with "Your other animals can't swim."
 The rows line up in columns sized by the longest name, which shows whole (twelve of the widest letters at 1024 px, on touch too), and the bars start together. Several animals of one kind stand under a quiet heading with the kind's name and how many ("Rabbit ×12"), as their card reads in the HUD; one alone needs none. A list longer than the card scrolls, and the highlighted row is kept in view. The right card says "Pick an animal", one sentence about the highlighted animal ("Send in Rabbit. Then the wild animal gets a turn.", "Rabbit is tired and needs a rest.", "Squirrel is already in the battle."), Back (Escape; not after a knock-out) and Go! (Enter), and the keys. The cursor starts on the first animal who can step in and visits every row; up/down or W/S move it (wrapping round), and left/right or A/D jump to the first animal of the previous or next kind (wrapping round); Enter or Space send the highlighted animal in, after the list's quiet moment (so a second Enter on the heels of the one on Switch sends nobody), and on a greyed one the row gives a little shake instead; Escape goes back to the menu, on the Switch row, at once.
  - **After a knock-out**, when someone else is still standing, the same list comes up by itself: the narration says "Squirrel is tired. Who goes next?", the sentence ends "Then it's your turn." (the pick is free), and there is no way back: Escape does nothing until an animal is picked. The list takes a pick only after its quiet moment, like the menu, so an Enter mashed through the knock-out can't pick for the kid.
- **Narration line**: one line above the panel, one beat per event with a hold of about a second each ("Wild Otter used Splash! It hits Squirrel for 5.", "Wild Otter used Splash! It missed.", "Missed! The wild Otter shrugs it off."); a beat too long for one line (a long nickname) breaks between its sentences. A switch reads "Come back, Squirrel!" then "Go, Rabbit!" before the wild animal's reply; picking who goes next after a knock-out reads only "Go, Rabbit!". Turn order and the opponent's action are visible, never instant.
- **Leash**: a loop on a rope flies in from the trainer's side (off-screen, lower left) in an arc only as high as the picture has room for, and the wild animal's status box is no room: the whole loop stays in view, under the top edge and clear of the status box, so a tall animal's bigger loop arcs lower than a frog's. The throw makes that room, not the layout (the status boxes and the camera stay as they are): the loop leaves the trainer's hand low down and comes in under the status box, with room to arc once past it, while the rope stays in the hand higher up, so that once the loop lands the rope runs over the player's animal to it. The rope never crosses the status box either. It wobbles on the animal through a suspense pause ("You throw the leash…"), then holds ("Caught!": the battle's biggest moment — the animal hops twice for joy, the first time with a whole turn, the doctor's stars twinkle round it, a small burst of confetti flies up round it, and two poppers go off in the scene's lower corners, close to the camera, throwing bigger confetti up the sides of the scene, clear of the caught animal, that flutters down over it all, all with the `caught` fanfare) or pops off ("It broke free!"). The odds are never shown as a number (kids should feel it, not compute it): the Leash row hints at the real chance — the engine's `catchProbability` for this animal at the HP on screen, with this leash — as a colour and the same thing in words, because colour is never the only signal: green "Good chance!" at one in two or better, amber "Maybe" at one in five or better, red "Hard to catch" below that. A fierce animal can stay "Hard to catch" all the way down (a bear never reaches one in five).
- **End**: a result card — "You won!", "You caught a Fox!" (a caught animal always joins the team), "Good try!" (the whole party is tired) or "You got away!" — over the authority's closing message (none after "Good try!": the doctor has the next word, in the world; see § Doctor), with one big button back to explore; to a click or a tap the whole screen is that button. Enter or Space press it, after the card's quiet moment, so an Enter mashed through the last beats cannot skip it; the button stays dimmed until then. A new friend on the team makes the card a celebration: above "You caught a Fox!", its name in big letters (the title's colours and cream rim) pops in letter by letter over a slowly turning burst of warm rays that spills past the card's top edge, and the doctor's stars fly out from behind the letters. "You won!" gets a few stars popping up off the top of the headline. No star crosses a word, and "Good try!" and "You got away!" get nothing.
- **Keys across modes**: keys go to one screen at a time — the title, else the battle, else the doctor's card, else the pause menu, else explore — and each press goes to the one that is up when it goes down, chosen before any of them acts on it. So a key that closes a screen is used up there: the Enter on Continue, on the result card or on the doctor's Bye is never also a step or a word with the doctor. A key already held down when the screen changes does nothing in the new one until it is pressed again: the title, battle, the doctor's card and the pause menu ignore auto-repeat, and explore drops held keys, queued taps and a pressed number (or a card clicked or dropped) while any of them is up. The one key outside that order is M, which turns the sound off and on over any screen (§ Sound and juice) and reaches none of them, except while an answer or a name is being typed.
- **A quiet moment**: every screen that puts up a choice takes a pick only after a quiet moment — 0.8 s of game time since the choice came up and since the last Enter, Space or number key, whether that press counted or not. Every press of those keys starts the moment again, so a mash never picks, at any pace and however long it goes on; stop for a moment, press once, and it picks. Auto-repeat never picks and never starts the moment again. It holds for the battle's menu, both switch lists and the result card, the doctor's list (when the card opens and each time it comes back after a beat) and its confirm before a hand-over, the title's confirm, starters and name box (there only Enter counts: Space and the numbers are letters), and Talk in explore (from when explore takes the screen back). The arrows, Escape, and a tap that only highlights move at once; a tap that picks (a row at the doctor or on the confirm) waits the moment as Enter does, without starting it again. The title's menu and the pause menu, where every choice can be taken back, pick at once.
- **Letters and shortcuts**, on every screen: W A S D (and any other letter a screen reads) mean the same with Caps Lock on or Shift down, and on a keyboard that types another alphabet W A S D are the keys where a QWERTY keyboard has them. A key pressed with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt is the browser's (bookmark, save, select all, back): no screen acts on it or keeps it from the browser. The name box is the exception to the first rule only: it types letters as they come.

## Doctor

Talking to a doctor (facing a tent, Enter/Space) opens a dialogue card in explore mode (no mode switch): the doctor's line and the kid's tokens across the top, three tabs over a list on the left, and on the right what the highlighted row does, the confirm before a hand-over, or a puzzle in the same puzzle component as battle. **Heal** (the tab the card opens on): the team's hurt animals; solve → healed, a whole species at once, with a cheer; miss → "Not quite! Let's try another one." with a new puzzle, no limit. **Help home**: pick animals to go home to the wild for tokens; a gentle confirm, then the sum of the tokens the kid will have. **Shop**: the tools on sale; the sum of the tokens left, then the tool is the kid's. A token sum missed says "Not quite! Try again." and asks the same sum. Escape goes back a step; Bye leaves at any time. Walking waits until the card closes.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     (3D world, player                    │
│                      facing the tent)                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ (Doctor) Let's help Rabbit and 1 more!    (●) 23 tokens  │
├────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┤
│ (Heal) Help home  Shop │                                 │
│   Squirrel  ▓▓░░  5/20 │                                 │
│ ▸ Rabbit   (tired) 0/22│        10, 15, 20, 25, ?        │
│   Rabbit    ▓░░░  3/22 │             [ 30 ]              │
│ ────────────────────── │  (Back Esc)  Type the answer…   │
│   Fox       ▓▓▓▓ 35/35 │     ↑ ↓ help another animal     │
│   Bye             Esc  │                                 │
└────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Layout**: one card across the bottom, `clamp(300px, 44vh, 400px)` tall, leaving the player and the tent in view above it. The doctor's line runs across its top, with the kid's tokens at its right (a gold coin with a heart, "23 tokens"; a "+N" or "−N" pops over them when tokens change hands). Below, the left card (2 of 5, or as much more as its longest name needs, twelve of the widest letters at 1024 px) beside the right-hand side (3), which keeps the rest, never less than the number pad beside the widest prompt on one line. The left card is the tabs, then the tab's list, which scrolls (a finger may scroll it), then the rows that stay put under it: Help home's button on that tab, and Bye on every tab. A list edge with more rows past it fades out, and the highlighted row keeps clear of the fade. The explore HUD (party cards, message line, tokens) is replaced by the card while it is open. With the touch controls on the card is `clamp(364px, 48vh, 400px)`, the doctor's line and the tokens sit over the right-hand side, and the left card runs the card's full height, tabs and rows a finger tall; the player still shows above it at 1024×768.
- **Tabs**: Heal · Help home · Shop ("Gør rask · Hjælp hjem · Butik"), the one on screen filled in the accent colour. Left/right (A/D) move between them, round from the last to the first, with the list's cursor on the tab's first stop and the doctor's line for the tab; a click or a tap on a tab goes there, and from a puzzle puts the puzzle away first.
- **Lists**: every tab's list is the team grouped by species (each species in the order it first comes in the team, then team order within it), with a thin line between the groups, so the animals one healing puzzle helps sit together; the rows line up in columns sized by the longest name, which shows whole (twelve of the widest letters at 1024 px, on touch too, in both languages). **Heal**: name and the one HP bar, "tired" written in the empty bar at 0 HP; healthy animals are greyed and the cursor skips them. **Help home**: a round check box, the name, and what the animal brings ("+6" and a coin); every animal is a stop; a picked one is ticked and green. **Shop**: the tool's picture, name and price, or a tick once owned; a tool the kid can't buy (owned, or too dear) is greyed but still a stop, so its card can say why. Up/down (W/S) move the cursor, wrapping round. The cursor starts on the first animal who needs the doctor (or Bye when nobody does), the first animal, the first tool (or Bye when nothing is on sale).
- **Picks** (Enter, Space, or a click or a tap on the row): on **Heal** an animal who needs the doctor opens its puzzle; on **Help home** an animal is picked or unpicked, at once (it can always be taken back), except one whose going would leave nobody standing, which gives a little shake and no sound: one animal that isn't tired always stays; **Help them home** (a stop once something is picked) opens the confirm; on **Shop** a tool the kid can buy opens its sum, and one they can't gives a little shake; **Bye** leaves. Everything but picking an animal to go home waits the list's quiet moment (§ Battle mode, "A quiet moment"), when the card opens and each time the list comes back after a beat; the arrows and the tabs move at once.
- **Right-hand side, before a pick**: on **Heal** "Pick an animal", what the highlighted animal's puzzle does ("Solve one puzzle and Rabbit and 1 more feel all better!" when more of its kind are hurt) and the keys; with nobody hurt, "Time to explore!" and how to say bye. On **Help home** "Help animals home", "Pick animals to go home. I make them all better, and you get tokens as a thank-you!", then what the picked ones bring ("2 animals picked: 4 tokens.") or the highlighted one ("Fox brings 6 tokens."), "One animal that isn't tired always stays with you." once only one animal left unpicked is standing, and the keys. On **Shop** the highlighted tool big, with its picture, name, what it does ("Chops down trees."), its price and, when it can't be bought, why ("You have the axe!", "You need 5 more tokens."); with nothing on sale, "Nothing for sale yet" and "New things are coming soon. Keep your tokens!".
- **The confirm** (Help them home): the right-hand side asks "Say bye bye to Squirrel and Rabbit?" (the names for up to three, "Say bye bye to 5 animals?" for more; in Danish the animals with "the": "Sig farvel til egernet og kaninen?"), "They go home to the wild, all better. You get 4 tokens.", and two choices side by side, **No, not now** lit first and **Yes, bye bye!**; the doctor says "Are you sure? They will be happy in the wild!". Left/right or up/down move between them without wrapping; Enter or Space do the lit one, and a click or a tap does its choice, only after the confirm's quiet moment, so however Enter is mashed it answers nothing; Escape is No. No goes back to the list with the animals still picked. The left card waits under it, dimmed, and takes no taps; the picked animals are scrolled into view.
- **Puzzles**: the same `PuzzlePanel` as battle, with a **Back** button (Escape; its key cap with a keyboard) beside the key reminder or the judgement. A heal's panel says "↑ ↓ help another animal" under it when another species is hurt too (on touch, "Tap another animal to help it instead", beside the number pad); up/down (W/S) swap the puzzle for the previous or next species that needs the doctor, a tap on an animal of another hurt species swaps to it, and the rows of the species being healed are lit together. A token sum has a story over the sum: "You have 23 tokens. You get 4 more. How many will you have?" (`23 + 4 = ?`), or "You have 27 tokens. The axe costs 8. How many will you have left?" (`27 − 8 = ?`); the doctor says "Thank you! Can you count your tokens?" or "Good choice! Can you count your tokens?"; up/down do nothing there. The right answer is never shown. Number keys, minus and Backspace type as in battle; Enter answers, only once a digit is typed.
- **Keys**: Escape goes back a step: from a puzzle to the list (nothing happens that was not answered), from the confirm to the list, and from the list — or while a beat plays — it says bye. Bye, clicked or tapped, leaves at any time, from a puzzle too. Keys other than Escape wait while a beat plays.
- **Beats**: a miss shakes the answer box and says "Not quite!" for about a second (the doctor "Not quite! Let's try another one.", or "Not quite! Try again." for a token sum), then the next puzzle — or the same sum — appears with an empty box. A right answer flashes the card green with "Correct!" for about a second; then:
  - a **heal**: every healed animal's bar fills, its row lights up green and hops, a green "+N" pops over it and little stars sparkle along its bar, all at once, and the doctor cheers ("Well done! Rabbit and 1 more feel all better! Who is next?", or "Everyone is fit and happy!" when nobody is left);
  - a **hand-over**: the animals going home slide off their rows and fade, with the heal's sparkle sound, as the doctor waves ("Bye bye, Fox! It feels much better now.", "Bye bye, Squirrel and Rabbit! They feel much better now.", "Bye bye, all 5! They feel much better now."); then they are gone from the list, the coins clink, the tokens count up with a "+N" ("Thank you! Here are 4 tokens. Now you have 27 tokens."), and the cursor is back at the top of the list, never on Bye;
  - a **purchase**: the coins clink, the tokens count down with a "−N", the tool's row lights up green and hops with its tick, and the doctor says "Here is your axe! You have 19 tokens left.".
  A moment later the list takes keys again, after its quiet moment.
- **Words**: everything the doctor says is chosen by the client from the doctor's events, like the battle narration, kept as data and worded from the copy files (`doctor.*`, `items.*`) when shown, so a language switch re-words an open card; the engine decides only what happened.
- **Leaving**: the card closes at once and the doctor's goodbye ("Bye! Come back any time.") is on the message line; the prompt shows under it while the player still faces the tent.

After a lost battle, the result card says "Good try!" and nothing more, and its button returns the player to explore standing beside the nearest tent, facing it, with the party healed and the doctor's line on the message line ("The doctor looked after your animals. Everyone feels better!", or "A doctor came by and looked after your animals…" when no tent was near).

## Pause menu

Escape in explore opens it; never in a battle (Escape there does nothing) or at the doctor (Escape there leaves). An overlay, not a mode: the world keeps drawing under a dim backdrop, and walking waits until it closes (a step already started lands).

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Paused                                                       │
│                                                              │
│   Your team                                ┌───────────────┐ │
│   1 Pip  Squirrel  ▓▓▓▓░ 20/20 goes first  │ Fox           │ │
│   2 Rabbit         ░░░░░  0/22      tired  │ ▸ Go first    │ │
│  [3 Fox            ▓▓░░░ 12/35           ] │   Move up     │ │
│   Language            [English]  Dansk     │  (Move down)  │ │
│   Sound                         (  ●) On   │   New name    │ │
│   ( Keep playing )                         │   Back        │ │
│                                            └───────────────┘ │
│                                                              │
│              ↑ ↓ choose · Enter pick · Esc back              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Left**: "Your team", one row per card in battle order, as the HUD's cards: slot number, then for a card of one animal its name (with the species beside a nickname, or under one that leaves it no room; twelve of the widest letters show whole at 1024 px, here and over the picked one's options) and HP bar with numbers, for a card of several the kind's name, how many ("×12") and how many can play ("9 ready · 3 tired"), and "tired" (all of them) or "goes first" — then the menu rows: the settings (Language, Sound), then "Keep playing" and "Start screen" (Quit to title: the game is saved as it stands and the title opens, with Continue on it; no confirm, since nothing is lost; a quieter pill than Keep playing). A setting's row is its name on the left and its value on the right. Up/down or W/S move the cursor, wrapping round; Enter or Space pick; Escape closes the menu. On a setting, Enter or Space change it and left/right (A/D) set it, and the menu stays open; left/right mean nothing on any other row. A click or a tap on any row does it at once, as the cursor and Enter would (a card opens its options, a setting changes, Keep playing closes); on a language, it switches to that one, and to none on the one already on screen. The rows stay live while a card or an animal's options are open beside them: a tap there puts them away and does the row (another card opens in their place, a setting changes with the cursor on it). While the name box is open they do nothing, and the name being typed stays.
- **Language**: the row lists every language in its own words ("English", "Dansk"), the one on screen lit, so a kid finds theirs whatever language the screen is in. Enter or Space, or left/right (A/D) on the row, switch to the next language: every word on screen changes at once, the menu stays open, and the choice is remembered on this device.
- **Sound**: a switch, green with the knob to the right when on, and the same in words ("On" or "Off"). Enter or Space flip it; left turns it off and right on. Turned on, it says so with a blip; turned off, only the switch and the word say so. While the cursor is on the row, the right side says what the row does and that M does it too; with the touch controls on, that a tap on Sound does it, and no keys.
- **A card of several** (Enter on it): on the right, the kind's name and how many, then what can be done with the card — **Go first** (its first animal standing leads; not when all of them are tired or one of them already goes first, nor out on the water for a kind that can't swim), **Move up**, **Move down** (the whole card, among the cards), **Back** — then a line ("Pick one of them to move it or give it a name.", or that they are all tired) and its animals, one row each as on the HUD's open card, in a box that scrolls. One cursor walks the options and the animals, skipping greyed options, and the lit animal stays in view; Enter or a tap on an animal opens its options. The card stays outlined on the left. Go first goes back to the team, the cursor on the card, now at the top; Escape or Back goes back to the list, on the card.
- **Right**: what can be done with the picked animal, which stays outlined in the list (its card): **Go first** (not for a tired animal or the lead, nor out on the water for one that can't swim, where the lead is the first swimmer standing;
 its card goes to the top with it in front), **Move up**, **Move down** (within its card when others of its kind are on it, else the card itself among the cards), **New name**, **Back**. Options that can't be done are greyed and the cursor skips them (a tired animal's panel says it is tired, so it can't go first); one that becomes impossible under the cursor (the animal reached the top) does nothing, so mashing Enter never overshoots. Moves happen at once and the list re-sorts with a short slide (none with reduced motion) of the animals that change place, and of one still on its way from the move before; the rest of the team never slides, and moves with the menu when the menu moves (a tablet's Save takes the menu from the top back to the middle). Go first also goes back to the team, the cursor on the card at the top. Escape and Back go back to the animal's card when it came from a card of several (the cursor on the animal, wherever it now is), else to the list, with the cursor on its card wherever it now is. Saving a name goes back the same way. A click or a tap on an option does it at once; on a greyed one, nothing. Before anything is picked, the panel says what picking does.
- **Name box**: New name opens a text box with the current nickname selected, the species' name as the placeholder, a line saying what a name may hold, and — when the typed text will not be stored as shown — what the animal will be called ("It will be called Pip."). Every key but Enter, Escape and Tab types: W A S D and Space are letters here, and the arrows move the caret. Enter saves (an empty name gives the species' name back), Escape goes back to the options without saving, and the box keeps the focus while it is open. An input method's Enter and Escape are its own. Under the box, **Back** (Escape) and **Save** (Enter) buttons.
- The key reminder at the bottom follows the screen (none with the touch controls on, where every row is plainly a button): "↑ ↓ choose · Enter pick · Esc close" ("↑ ↓ choose · ← → or Enter change the language · Esc close" on the Language row, "↑ ↓ choose · ← → or Enter sound off or on · Esc close" on the Sound row), "↑ ↓ choose · Enter pick · Esc back", "Enter save · Esc back".
- It holds at 1024×768 with eight cards, a card of 120 animals and a 12-character name, in either language.

## Sound and juice

The rules are in [[DESIGN]] § Sound and § Aesthetic direction; this is where each sound and flourish goes.

**Cues.** Each plays with the thing on screen it goes with, from the screen that shows it; nothing plays for a moment the screen does not show.

| Cue         | Plays                                                                                                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `encounter` | A wild animal jumps out, as the iris starts to close.                                                         |
| `move`      | The cursor moves on any list (battle menu, switch list, doctor, pause menu, the title's menu, confirm and starters), a doctor's tab changes, a doctor's puzzle swaps, an animal unpicked to go home, the doctor's confirm's choice moves or its No by Escape, Escape back from the switch list. On an attack row, left/right blip at the level's pitch: higher for medium and hard. |
| `confirm`   | A choice is made: an attack (at its level's pitch), Leash, Switch, Run, an animal sent in, the result card left, the doctor's card opening, an animal picked (to heal or to go home), Help them home, a confirm choice, a tool picked or Bye at the doctor, the pause menu opening, an animal, a card or an option picked in the pause menu (Go first plays `lead` instead), a name saved, the language changed, Sound turned on; on the title, Continue, New game, a confirm choice, a starter picked and the name box's Enter. |
| `correct`   | "Correct!", in battle and at the doctor.                                                                     |
| `wrong`     | "Not quite!", in battle and at the doctor.                                                                   |
| `hit`       | An attack lands: with the shake and the "−N".                                                                 |
| `faint`     | An animal lies down, tired.                                                                                    |
| `throw`, `wobble` | The leash flies; tick-tock while it wobbles on the animal.                                              |
| `caught`, `boing` | "Caught!" with the cheer, the stars and both bursts of confetti, all on the same frame; or "It broke free!". |
| `heal`      | The doctor's heal: the HP bars filling, the sparkles; and the animals going home, made better, as they slide away. |
| `coins`     | Tokens change hands at the doctor: counted in after a hand-over with the "+N", or out for a tool with the "−N". |
| `lead`      | A new animal goes first ("Fox goes first!"), from a number key, a card clicked, tapped or dropped at the top, or the pause menu.                             |
| `won`       | The result card says "You won!", with the winner's hop and stars.                                             |

Silent on purpose: typing an answer or a name, a key that does nothing (a greyed row, a tired animal picked), a miss's puff, running away, "Good try!".

**M, anywhere.** M turns the sound off and on over any screen — the title, explore, a battle, the doctor's card, the pause menu — except while an answer or a name is being typed, where it is a letter. A held M flips once. A chip at the top centre (on the title, under its name) says what happened for three seconds, fading over the last half-second: "Sound is on", or "Sound is off" with "Press M to turn it back on" under it. The chip takes no keys.

**Flourishes.** Small, made of what the game already has, and always inside the beats that already exist: none adds a wait or takes a key.

- The trainer walks: arms and legs swing from shoulder and hip, one step leading with each foot, the body rocking onto the landing foot and stretching a little in the hop.
- The lead follows the trainer, bouncing on each step, and when who goes first changes it shrinks away and the new lead grows in (§ Explore mode).
- Into a battle: the iris (§ Battle mode, Entering).
- In a battle: the lunge, shake, puff, hop, recall and appearance (§ Battle mode); the ring of dust round a tired animal as it lies down, and the z's that rise over it while it rests; a winner's hop with a few stars; a caught animal's cheer (two hops, the first with a turn) with stars, the confetti round it, in the palette's colours, falling back to the ground within about 1.3 s, and the poppers' bigger confetti, up the sides of the scene and down over it within about 2 s.
- On the result card: a new friend's name in big letters over a burst of rays, with stars; a few stars off "You won!" (§ Battle mode, End).
- At the doctor: stars that sparkle along each healed animal's HP bar as it fills, with the "+N"; the animals going home sliding off their rows as they fade; a "+N" or "−N" over the tokens; a tool bought lighting up and hopping on its row; a row that can't be picked giving a little shake.

**Reduced motion.** When the system asks for less motion (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`), everything that moves moves less, and what happened still shows: the iris becomes a dim that comes and goes on the same clock; the trainer's hop is a third as high and its limbs swing less, with no rocking; the lunge, shake and hop travel about a third as far, and so does the follower's bounce; a caught animal's cheer is one small hop with no turn; a miss's puff swells less and stays where it is; the ring of dust round a tired animal rolls out a third as far; confetti, the poppers' too, is fewer and drifts down without tumbling; stars twinkle in place, in the scene and on the result card; the card's rays stand still and the name's letters fade in; the leash flies in a flatter arc and wobbles less, and one that pops off rises a third as high; the "−N" and "+N" fade in place; the answer box's shake, and a refused row's on the switch list, are 2 px; the doctor's row doesn't hop and its sparkles twinkle in place, the animals going home fade where they are, and a row that can't be picked shakes 2 px; a resting animal's z's grow and fade near where they start instead of rising; the Sound switch's knob doesn't slide. The faint (lying down), the recall and the appearance keep their shape, the follower's swap too, since they say what happened: the one called back still shrinks away, rising a third as high, and the one sent in grows to its size without the bounce past it. Other screens say what reduced motion does to them where they are described: the title's drift and letters, the starters, the butterflies, Talk's glow, the turn-sideways tablet, the pause menu's re-sort. What stays as it is: an HP bar sliding to its new length (its number changes at once), a pressed button giving way under a finger, the figures' breathing (about a pixel), and every fade.

**`?debug`** shows the last four cues asked for, newest last, under the position (with ✕ while sound is off), so a screenshot shows which sound would have played.

## Component reuse
