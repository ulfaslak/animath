# Cheatsheet

Everything a player can do in **today's build**, and how: every key, every hidden behaviour, every exploit. This file is a record of what the game actually does. It is not a plan: intended controls live in [[UI_SPEC]], the list of features in [[PRODUCT]] §5, and the rules in [[PRODUCT]] §4.

**Keep it true.** Any PR that adds, removes or changes an input, a way to reach something, or a behaviour a player could stumble on updates this file in the same PR. If you find an exploit, record it here, and if a kid could use it to break the game, also file it as a `bug`.

Escape opens the pause menu in explore and leaves the doctor's card; in a battle it only backs out of the switch list — not even in a battle puzzle: once a battle puzzle is up, the only way on is to answer it. M is the one key that works the same on every screen, except in a window that another window has played past, which takes no key but Enter and Space (§ Saving). W A S D work the same with Caps Lock on or Shift held, in every menu too, and on a Russian or Greek keyboard they are the keys where W A S D sit on an English one. A key pressed with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt belongs to the browser everywhere: Cmd+D bookmarks, Ctrl+S saves the page, Alt+← goes back, and none of them moves the trainer. A mouse or a finger works every row, button and card too (§ Touch and mouse); walking takes the keys on a laptop and the D-pad on a tablet. The page has no debug keys or console hooks; its URL parameters are `?zoo`, `?lang`, `?debug`, `?party=`, `?new`, `?tokens=` and `?shop` (see § Hidden behaviour and § Saving). `?new`, `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop` skip the title: the game starts at once, and is saved nowhere.

## Touch and mouse

Everything below is a click with a mouse or a tap with a finger, and each is the key beside it: the same thing happens, behind the same waits. A tap counts as the finger lifts, and only on what it went down on, on the same screen, and not if that moved meanwhile (a team row another finger moved up); the number pad and the D-pad count as the finger lands. Each finger counts on its own: tap Talk or Menu with one thumb while the other walks. The **touch controls** are the D-pad, Talk, Menu and the number pad: they are there from the start on a tablet (a screen whose main pointer is a finger); anywhere, the first touch brings them and the first key pressed on a real keyboard puts them away. A mouse changes nothing.

## The world

- **Every world has a number**, from 1 to 9999, and the same number is the same world every time and on every machine. A new game from the title starts in a world of its own, your home, a number picked at random from 2 to 9999, so each new game is somewhere new. **World 1** is the world every game was played in before worlds had numbers (the seed `'prototype'`): a game saved before then carries on there, and `?new` (like `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop`) plays there. There is no way yet to see your world's number or go to another one on screen.
- A new game starts at its world's **spawn**: a grass tile with a doctor's tent at most 12 steps away and room to roam. In World 1 that is **(-2, 6)**, on grass next to a stretch of water, the tent at (5, 7) 7 steps away; after that, the game starts where you left it (§ Saving). Pressing Right adds 1 to x, and pressing Down adds 1 to y. The screen doesn't show the position unless the URL has `?debug`.
- The world never ends. It is generated in 16×16 chunks as you walk, in every direction.
- **You are a kid in a blue cap** (coral shirt, blue shorts). The figure turns to face the way you last walked or bumped, swings its arms and legs and hops a little with every step, stands on top of hills rather than sinking into them, and breathes gently while you stand still.
- **The animal that goes first walks behind you**, one tile back, on the tile you just left: it can never stand in water (unless it swims, out in the boat: § The boat), on a rock or in a tent, it never blocks you, and it never starts a battle. Walk back onto its tile and you swap places; it steps round you. Press another card's number (or pick "Go first" in the menu) and it shrinks away as the new one pops up in its place; the same happens after a battle when someone else ends up first. After Continue, a reload or the trip to the tent it is simply there beside you, behind you if there is room. When every animal is tired nobody follows (only `?party=` with every HP at 0 does that today, since a lost battle heals everyone): heal one at the doctor and it pops out behind you as its HP bar fills.
- **Butterflies** flutter round you wherever there is land, and behind the title. They are only for looking at: you can't catch, battle or bump into them, and walking never meets one. None pops up out of nowhere: walk away from them and new ones fly in from the edge of the screen ahead of you.
- **Each area looks like itself**: the meadow is bright green with little flowers; the forest has a darker floor, crowded dark pines, young trees and bushes; river banks are sand with reed beds; the mountains have grey-green turf, pebbles, fields of boulders and snow on the boulders of the highest rocks.

## Trees and rocks

- **Trees grow only in the forest, rocks only in the mountains** (the highest ones capped with snow). The nearest tree to the start is at (-15, 9), in the woods up the river bank to the left: stand on (-16, 9) and face right, or on (-15, 10) and face up. The nearest rock is at (-1, 19), south of the start: stand on (-2, 19) and face right. The nearest snow-capped peak is at (-28, -24): stand on (-29, -24) and face right.
- **The axe chops a tree down, the pickaxe breaks a rock** (8 and 13 tokens at the doctor's shop). Stand next to it, face it (walking into it turns you), and press Enter or tap Chop/Break. The trainer swings the tool over the shoulder, the tree tips over away from you with chips flying (the rock shakes and crumbles into pebbles), and a stump (or gravel) is left. You can walk into the gap at once.
- **It stays cleared.** Every tree and rock you clear is saved with your game: reload, walk far away and come back, and the stump is still there. A new game starts with every tree back.
- **The wrong tool does nothing**: the axe can't break a rock, the pickaxe can't chop a tree. Without the tool, the first bump into a tree or a rock in a game says the doctor sells one; after that only Enter says it.
- **Only trees and rocks.** Tall grass, reeds, sand, water and tents never go, whatever you own.
- **Clearing changes where you walk, never who comes out of the grass**: the animals in the woods are the woods' whether you chopped them bare or not.

## The boat

- **Buy it at the doctor** (the Shop tab, 21 tokens: `?tokens=40` in a throwaway game is enough). Leaving the card, it is on your back: a brown shell with a coral rim, bottom out.
- **Walk into the water** (shallow or deep) and you sail: the step takes a little longer (about half a second) while you hop in and the boat swings off your back, turns over, grows and drops under you. From then on every arrow sails a tile, in any water; rock, trees and tents still stop you. Step onto sand, grass or reeds to land, and the boat swings back onto your back. With "reduce motion" on it just snaps into place halfway through the step.
- **The quickest water from the start**: straight up. (-2, 5), (-2, 4) and (-2, 3) are shallows, and from (-2, 2) on it is deep water, the darker blue: the big lake north of the start.
- **Out on the water only animals that swim fight** (the otter, the frog, and the sea animals you caught). The first one standing that swims goes first there: its card gets the "goes first" tag and it swims behind the boat, low in the water. A number for one that can't swim says "Squirrel can't swim! …" and changes nothing, and so does its card dropped at the top of the column (it stays on top, and the lead stays the swimmer); in the pause menu its Go first is greyed, with "Squirrel can't swim, so it can only go first on land." under it. On land, who goes first is as before.
- **With nobody standing who swims**, your animal that goes first on land hops into the boat once you are in it and stands on the little deck at the front, facing where you sail (a big one made smaller to fit), turning with the boat when you bump into a rock or a tree; it hops out beside you when you land.
- **Only the deep water has animals.** The shallows along every shore never start a battle, so crossing a river is always peaceful; out on the deep water, the darker blue, the sea animals come out (§ The sea animals).
- **A reload out on the water** (Continue) puts you back in the boat where you were, the swimmer in the water behind you (beside you when there is no water behind; never on a beach, even right beside the boat) or the rider in the boat. A save with you on water and no boat (only by editing the save by hand) starts you on the spawn tile instead.
- **Chopping from the boat**: with the axe too, a tree right beside the water can be chopped from the boat, and its stump is somewhere to land. The nearest is 50 steps from the start: sail to (21, 33) and face down, at the tree on (21, 34).
- **The doctor from the water**: no tent stands right beside water (a sand bank is always between), so land first and talk from the ground.

## The sea animals

- **Six of them, only at sea**: the Crab and the Starfish (tier 1), the Turtle (2), the Dolphin (3), the Octopus (4) and the Whale (5). They come out only on the deep water, from the boat, on the same 1 step in 10 as tall grass, and only while an animal that swims (the otter, the frog, or a sea animal you caught) is standing to go first; with nobody standing who swims, nothing comes out. Never a frog or an otter out there, never a sea animal in the grass.
- **Meeting one fast**: with the boat and a frog or an otter, walk straight up from the start: (-2, 5), (-2, 4) and (-2, 3) are shallows, (-2, 2) is deep. Then step left and right between (-2, 2) and (-3, 2): about 1 step in 10 starts a battle, half the time within 7 steps. A saved game at (-2, 2) with no steps walked yet, the otter in front, meets a Turtle on the 11th step of left, right, left, right… (the sixth Left).
- **Who comes out**: near home with a frog in front, crabs and starfish 44.5% each, turtles 9%, dolphins 2%; with an otter, turtles 69%, dolphins 14%, crabs and starfish 7% each, octopuses 3%, a whale now and then (0.6%). Far from home, every sea animal from your lead's size up is equally likely. The sea is never quiet: even a whale in front meets octopuses and whales.
- **Catching one** is the same as on land (the leash, best when its HP is low); a caught sea animal joins your team, and caught ones go home at the doctor for tokens like any other (a whale brings 30).
- **At sea a sea animal is a swimmer like the otter**: it can go first, fight, be switched in, and it swims behind your boat when it leads.
- **On land a sea animal can't go first or fight**: its number or its card says "Crab lives in the sea! On land, an animal that walks goes first." and changes nothing (its card dropped at the top stays there and says the same); the pause menu greys its Go first, with "Crab lives in the sea, so it can only go first on the water." under it; in a land battle the switch list tags it "lives in the sea" ("Crab lives in the sea. It only fights out on the water."), and with only sea animals standing besides the one in front, Switch says "Your other animals live in the sea.". A team whose only animals standing are sea animals meets nothing in the grass and has nobody following on land: walk to a doctor.
- **The doctor keeps a walker**: help animals home all you like, but one that isn't tired and walks on land always stays ("Keep one animal that can walk with you!"), so helping animals home never leaves you with only sea animals standing; the last walker's check box is dashed before you try, and picking it gives a little shake. With no walker standing at all, nobody can go home until one is healed ("No animal can walk with you now. Heal one first!"). A battle can: see § Exploits and quirks.
- **A battle at sea**: both animals swim with the lower part of them under the surface, whatever their size, and at the end the wild one "swims home to rest" or "stays in the water" (the Run row says the same), never "in the grass".
- **They are no starters**: a new game starts on land, with the squirrel, the rabbit or the frog.

## Finding a battle fast

In World 1 the tile straight left of the start, (-3, 6), is a river reed with the lake all round it: tall grass on the river bank. Frogs live in the reeds and love the water, so most of what comes out there is a frog; near home squirrels and rabbits come down to the water too, and now and then an otter, about 1 battle in 16 (the odds are in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters"). In a new game there (`?new`, or a game saved before numbered worlds, with any starter, since they are all the same size; New game on the title now starts in another world), press Left and Right in turn (Left, Right, Left, …): every Left lands on the reed. **The 11th step (the sixth Left) always meets a Frog**, the 15th a Rabbit, the first Squirrel comes on the 69th step and the first Otter on the 97th. The steps are counted over the whole game and saved with it, so this only works from the start of a game, not after a reload. The nearest meadow tall grass is three steps straight down from the start, (-3, 9) to (-1, 9), and there is a strip at (2, 7)–(4, 7), beside the tent: open meadow, so about 7 battles in 10 are rabbits and most of the rest squirrels, with a fox 1 time in 11 and a deer 1 time in 55. For foxes and the odd deer, go to the forest: the nearest forest tall grass on foot is (14, 8), 18 steps east of the start, where a fox comes out 1 time in 6.

**Finding frogs**: any reed on a river bank, near home or far out, and the more water round the reed, the more of them. They live nowhere else, though near home, while a squirrel, a rabbit or a frog goes first, they also hop up into the mountain grass. With anything bigger than a fox or an otter in front, a frog never comes out.

**Where each animal lives.** Every animal favours one kind of ground near the tall grass it comes out of, and with plenty of that ground within 3 tiles it comes out four times as often as an animal of its size with none ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters"):

- **By the water**: frogs and otters (at the river's reeds).
- **By the trees**: squirrels, foxes and deer (the forest, and the meadow along its edge).
- **By the rocks**: wolves and bears (the mountains, and the forest beside them).
- **In open grass**, away from water, trees and rocks: rabbits (the open meadow).

Near home the ground only picks among animals of the same size, so it never makes the start harder: the frogs take the reeds by the water, the rabbits the open meadow, the squirrels the edge of the woods, while foxes, otters and the big animals stay as rare as ever. The further out you go, the more it sorts the sizes too: far from home the wolves and bears keep to the rocks, and the woods among the trees are squirrels, foxes and deer. The ground only changes which animal comes out, never whether one does: the same steps start battles wherever you walk. Where every animal that could come out likes the same ground, it changes nothing: far from home a river's reeds are half frogs, half otters, wet or dry.

Who comes out depends on who leads your party (§ Your party). With a frog or a rabbit in front, the same steps meet the same animals as with the squirrel. With a fox or an otter in front, the same steps on the reed meet an Otter each time until the 147th, which brings the first Frog; after that a frog now and then. With a deer in front the reed meets only otters, and with a wolf or a bear, the reed never starts a battle.

## Battles

- **A battle opens with a circle.** A little tune plays, a circle closes on you (the step into the grass still lands) and opens again on the wild animal.
- **You always go first.** Pick an attack and a level, answer the puzzle, then the wild animal answers with one of its attacks. Facing an animal its own size or bigger it sometimes misses ("It missed."); a smaller one it never misses.
- **Harder sum, bigger hit.** Every attack has its own level, easy, medium or hard, shown on its tile with a star badge of the damage it does there; the card on the right shows what kind of maths it asks (+ − × ÷ √, ? for a missing number, 2 4 6 for a pattern, and in words), what each of its three levels hits for, and "That would tire it out!" when that hit would win the battle, and the wild animal's HP bar shows, lighter, the part it would take off. "Correct!" shows the badge again, and the hit lands with a burst beside the wild animal's box: the harder the level, the bigger the burst. The words are the attack's own three steps, not a scale shared by every animal: a bear's easy Crush is still a very hard sum. Each level asks one step harder than the one below it ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Animals" has the ladder). A bear's Roar, Maul and Crush ask at the same three difficulties, and Crush hits hardest.
- **A wrong answer misses** ("Not quite!", then "Missed! The wild Rabbit shrugs it off."). The right answer is not shown.
- **Picking an attack can't be undone.** Leaving the puzzle is not possible; typing anything wrong counts as a miss.
- **Leash**: the round Leash button says how likely a catch is right now, with a ring of the same colour round it: "Good chance!" (green, one in two or better), "Maybe" (amber, one in five or better) or "Hard to catch" (red). It follows the real odds, so it changes as the animal's HP drops and depends on the species: a rabbit at full HP is "Hard to catch" and at 2 HP a "Good chance!"; a frog is slippery, "Maybe" from 8 HP down and a "Good chance!" only at 2 HP or less; a fox turns "Maybe" below about a third of its HP and "Good chance!" only at 1 HP; a bear is "Hard to catch" all the way down. A throw that breaks free costs your turn. One that holds is a party: the animal hops for joy with a spin, stars twinkle round it, confetti bursts round it and two poppers shoot confetti up the sides of the scene; then the card shows its name in big coloured letters over a burst of rays. A win gets a hop, a few stars, and stars round "You won!". There is no limit to your team: every animal you catch joins it.
- **Run** always works and costs nothing.
- **Switch** sends in another animal ("Come back, Squirrel!", "Go, Rabbit!"). It costs your turn: the wild animal has a go at the newcomer straight away. It is greyed with only one animal in your party ("You need a second animal to switch. Catch one with the leash!") and when nobody else can step in: everyone else is tired ("Your other animals are tired."), or can't fight where the battle is (out on the water "Your other animals can't swim.", on land "Your other animals live in the sea.").
- **When your animal is tired** (0 HP) and someone else who can fight there is still standing, the party list comes up: "Squirrel is tired. Who goes next?" Pick anyone who isn't tired and can fight there (out on the water, one that swims; on land, not a sea animal). That pick is free (the wild animal doesn't get a turn), and then you choose what to do.
- **Win, catch, or run**: a card says what happened; press Enter to walk on from the same tile. HP you lost stays lost.
- **Lose** (all your animals that can fight there are tired; out on the water, all that swim, whoever still stands in the boat): "Good try!" — press Enter and you are standing beside the nearest doctor's tent (nearest the way you get about: on foot, round the water and through the trees you chopped, or with the boat across the water too), facing it, with the whole party at full HP, and the bottom line says "The doctor looked after your animals. Everyone feels better!". From the reed by the start that is (4, 7), just left of the tent at (5, 7). If no tent is within 200 steps, you stay where you were and it says "A doctor came by…" instead.

## The doctor

- **Finding one**: the nearest tent to the start is at (5, 7). Walk 7 tiles right, all on grass, to (5, 6), then press Down: you bump into the tent and face it. (From the left, (4, 7) is tall grass.) Standing next to a tent without facing it is not enough: press the arrow toward it first.
- **Talking**: press Enter while facing the tent. A card opens at the bottom of the screen with the doctor's line and your tokens at the top, three tabs (Heal, Help home, Shop) over a list on the left, and a puzzle area on the right. It opens on Heal. Left and Right change the tab. Your party cards in the corner go away while it is open, and walking waits. The lists are grouped by kind of animal and scroll when they are long; an edge with more past it fades out.
- **Healing**: pick an animal who is hurt or tired and solve its puzzle: it, and every other hurt animal of its kind, is back to full HP ("Well done! Rabbit and 1 more feel all better!", their rows light up green, a green "+N" pops over each and stars sparkle along the bars). One puzzle heals one kind of animal, however many of it are hurt. The puzzle is a kind the animal's own attacks ask (so a frog gets a number pattern or a times table), and harder for fiercer animals (each species' difficulty is in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Knock-out and healing").
- **A wrong answer costs nothing**: "Not quite! Let's try another one." and a different puzzle, as many times as it takes. The right answer is not shown.
- **Fit animals** are shown greyed and can't be picked. With nobody hurt the doctor says "Hello! Your animals are all fit and happy." and Enter just says bye.
- **Helping animals home**: on Help home, pick the animals to go home (tired ones too: the doctor makes them better first). Each row shows the animal's HP bar, with "tired" in it at 0 HP. An animal brings 2 tokens for a squirrel, a rabbit or a frog, 6 for a fox or an otter, 12 for a deer, 20 for a wolf, 30 for a bear, and a sea animal as much as a land animal of its size (2 for a crab or a starfish, 6 for a turtle, 12 for a dolphin, 20 for an octopus, 30 for a whale): the right-hand side says it for the highlighted one, and once something is picked it keeps the running total ("40 animals picked: 240 tokens."). A kind of several has its own row on top ("Fox ×40 +240"), which picks the whole kind in one go. You always keep one animal that isn't tired and walks on land (§ The sea animals, "The doctor keeps a walker"): the last one won't pick, and its check box is dashed and faded so you see it first ("Fox stays with you.", "Keep one animal that can walk with you!"); picking a whole kind that holds the last walkers keeps its first one, and its row counts only the ones who go ("Fox ×40 +234", "39 of them bring 234 tokens.") (keep a tired one and it stays tired; heal it first if you want it to go and the other to stay). "Help them home" asks "Say bye bye to Fox and Rabbit?" with "No, not now" lit first; "Yes, bye bye!" asks the sum: "You have 12 tokens. You get 8 more. How many will you have?". The right answer waves them goodbye ("Bye bye, Fox! It feels much better now.") and counts the tokens in; a wrong one says "Not quite! Try again." with the same sum. Back, Escape or another tab puts the sum away, and nobody leaves.
- **The shop**: the tools on sale, with their pictures, prices and what they do: the axe (8 tokens) chops trees, the pickaxe (13) breaks rocks, the boat (21) sails on water. **Today all three are on sale**: the axe and the pickaxe (see § Trees and rocks) and the boat (see § The boat). A boat bought grows onto your back as you leave. Buying asks "You have 23 tokens. The axe costs 8. How many will you have left?"; the right answer hands it over ("Here is your axe! You have 15 tokens left."), a wrong one asks the same sum again. A tool you own, or can't pay for yet, gives a little shake, and the card says why. One of each is all you can have.
- **Your tokens** show beside the doctor's line, and in the world's top right corner with the tools you own under them. They are saved with the game.
- **Leaving**: Bye (Enter on it, or a tap on it, even from a puzzle) or Escape from the list, at any time. The doctor says "Bye! Come back any time." on the bottom line.

## Your party

The cards in the top-left corner are your party, one card per kind, in battle order. You start with the animal you picked on the title, at full HP (a Squirrel 20 of 20, a Rabbit 22 of 22, a Frog 21 of 21), named or not; a throwaway game (`?new`) starts with a Squirrel. Every animal you catch joins your party, with the HP it had when the leash landed, however many you have: behind the others of its kind, or on a new card at the bottom when it is the first of its kind. A card of one animal shows its name and HP; a card of several is a stack, "Rabbit ×4", with how many are ready, how many are tired soon (a fifth of their HP or less left, their bar red) and how many are tired ("2 ready · 1 tired soon · 1 tired", or "All ready", "All tired soon", "All tired"), and a slim bar of all their HP together. The pause menu's rows say the same. Point at a stack with the mouse, or tap it, and its animals show beside it with their names and HP (tap it again to close it; walking closes it too). An animal at 0 HP is greyed out with a "tired" tag and sits out battles, and a card whose animals are all tired is greyed too. Animals heal at a doctor's tent (one puzzle for each kind of animal hurt), and all at once for free when you lose a battle. The doctor also takes the animals you don't keep home to the wild, for tokens (§ The doctor). Reloading changes nothing: the party comes back exactly as it was, in the same order, with the same names and the same animal first.

- **Who goes first**: the first animal that isn't tired and can fight where you are (on land not a sea animal, out on the water one that swims), in the first card that has one; that card is outlined in orange and tagged "goes first" (with two or more cards), and on the open card the animal itself is. It steps into the next battle. If it gets tired in a battle you pick who steps in, and afterwards the first animal down that isn't tired goes first. Press a card's number (1–9), click a card of one animal, or pick "Go first" in the pause menu, to move that card to the top: its first animal standing goes first. Click or tap an animal on an open card, or pick it in the pause menu and "Go first", and its card goes to the top with that animal at its front. The numbers on the cards follow the order, so the card you picked becomes 1 and the others move down one.
- **Moving and naming**: drag a card up or down the column to put your kinds in another order (with a mouse, just drag; on a tablet, hold the card still for half a second until it lifts, then drag, since a quick swipe scrolls the column instead; let go of a lifted card without moving it and it counts as a tap). To reach a place out of view, hold the card at the column's top or bottom edge: the column keeps scrolling under it. The card at the top leads, unless all its animals are tired: then the first animal standing below it does. In the pause menu (Escape) a card moves up or down too, and a card of several opens to list its animals, each of which can move up or down within its card, a tired one too — a tired animal on top is skipped, and the tag stays with the first one standing. "New name" gives an animal a nickname, which the cards, the menu and battles all use ("Go, Pip!").
- **A big team**: the column scrolls with a finger or the mouse wheel when the cards don't fit (with the touch controls on, above the D-pad), an open card's list scrolls, and so do the battle's switch list and the doctor's list, which keep the highlighted animal in view.
- **What a name can be**: up to 12 letters (any alphabet, so "Søren" and "राम" work; an accent Unicode keeps separate from its letter, like a Hindi vowel sign, counts as a letter, and a few rarer alphabets keep their letters but lose their accents), digits, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and dots. Emoji and other symbols are left out when you press Enter, the text-generator tricks (underlines, strike-throughs, circled or fancy letters) come out as plain letters, extra spaces are squeezed, and a name with no letter or digit left — empty, spaces, only emoji — means no nickname: the animal is called by its species again. The name box shows "It will be called …" before you press Enter whenever the name will come out different from what you typed.

**Your lead decides what comes out of the grass.** Wild animals size up the animal that goes first:

- Nothing two or more tiers smaller than the lead ever comes out. One tier smaller comes out now and then where animals the lead's size or bigger live too: near home 1 battle in 7 to 1 in 13 on any ground (a fox in the meadow meets a squirrel or a rabbit 1 time in 7), and far out, where the ground sorts the sizes, from 1 in 5 (a fox in the open meadow, where the rabbits are) down to 1 in 100 (in the forest by the rocks).
- Everything else is the mix the starter meets, moved up to the lead's size ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters" has the shares for a fox or an otter in front, and a bear).
- It still starts a battle on 1 grass step in 10 wherever anything could, whoever leads: the same steps as with the starter, only the animal differs.
- Where nothing is big enough, the grass is quiet, and where only one-tier-smaller animals live, every battle is one of them, still on 1 grass step in 10 ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters" says where; the quiet grass is also § Exploits and quirks).

To meet smaller animals again, put a smaller animal in front: press its card's number, drag its card to the top, or pick "Go first" in the pause menu. A lost battle heals everyone and keeps the order, so the first card leads again.

## Hidden behaviour

- **A tap is always one step.** A key press shorter than a frame still moves you one tile.
- **Mashing is capped.** At most two taps are queued, so mashing a key does not queue a long walk.
- **Two keys held: the newest wins.** Hold Right, then press Up as well, and you walk up. Let go of Up and you walk right again. There is no diagonal movement.
- **Walking into something turns you to face it** without moving you.
- **Switching windows stops you.** If you tab away with a key held, or the page is hidden, you stop walking and any queued taps are dropped. So does pressing Ctrl, Cmd or Alt mid-walk; press the arrow again to walk on. A finger on the D-pad lets go the same way when you switch to another app or window: its arrow goes out, and a finger still resting there when you come back walks nothing until you lift it and press again.
- **Letting go always stops you.** A key let go with Shift held down stops the walk like any other.
- **Keys don't leak between walking and battling.** An arrow still held down when a battle starts does nothing in the battle until you press it again, and keys pressed in a battle never become steps.
- **One press, one screen.** A key counts on the screen that is up when you press it, and only there: the Enter on Continue, on the result card or on the doctor's Bye never also talks to the doctor, however slowly the game starts.
- **Nor does the D-pad.** A finger still on the D-pad when a battle, the doctor or the menu takes the screen walks nothing there, lifted over the new screen it presses nothing (not the battle row under your thumb), and it walks nothing when you are back until you lift it and press again.
- **Taps wait where keys wait.** A quiet moment, a turn playing: a tap does nothing there, as Enter does nothing. A tap counts only on what the finger went down on, on the screen it went down on: touch a row while the turn plays and lift once the menu is back, and nothing happens.
- **Two thumbs at once.** Each finger counts on its own: walk with the D-pad and tap Talk or Menu with the other thumb.
- **Slow taps count; a double tap on something that moves counts once.** A tap has no time limit: a finger resting on a button for seconds still taps it when it lifts where it landed. A second click or tap within 0.7 s on the spot the first one's button just left (a card sent to the top, a menu closed) presses nothing there, so a double click on an animal never picks the one that slid into its row. A double tap on a button that stays put (Move down) still presses it twice.
- **A key hides the touch controls; a touch brings them back.** Typing a name with the tablet's own keyboard hides nothing.
- **A tablet or a phone held upright** shows "Turn your screen sideways to play!" over everything, and takes no taps until it is turned.
- **No zooming or scrolling on a tablet**: pinching, double-tapping, pulling down or pressing long on the game does nothing (the name boxes still select and edit).
- **Nothing hides under the notch.** On an iPad the D-pad, Talk, Menu, the message line and the battle's cards sit above the home indicator; on a phone held sideways they keep clear of the notch too, while the world fills the whole screen.
- **No WebGL, no game, but a kind card.** A browser that cannot draw the world (WebGL switched off, hardware acceleration off, an iPhone or iPad in Lockdown Mode) shows "This browser can't draw Animath's world." in the language the game would start in; with JavaScript off, a card says so in English and Danish.
- **Attack levels are remembered, per attack and per kind of animal, until you reload.** Set a squirrel's Nut Toss to hard and it is hard in the next battle too, and for every squirrel you have; a rabbit's attacks keep their own. After a switch the menu starts on the new animal's first attack.
- **Switching can't be used to stall.** Every switch you choose gives the wild animal a turn, so switching back and forth only wears your party down.
- **Keys wait while a battle turn plays.** From your answer until the menu comes back, every key is ignored.
- **A new choice waits for a quiet moment.** Every menu, list or card that asks you to choose — the battle menu (after the opening lines and after every turn), both switch lists, the result card, the doctor's list (when it opens and after every heal), "Start a new game?", the starters, the starter's name box, and Talk in the world — takes a pick only once it has been up 0.8 s with no Enter, Space or number pressed. Every one of those presses starts the moment again, so mashing never picks, at any pace, however long you keep it up: mashing through the text can't pick the next attack, skip "You won!" or talk to the doctor by accident. Stop for a moment, press once, and it picks. Go! and the result card's button stay dimmed until then. The arrows and Escape work at once. The title's own menu and the pause menu take a press at once: everything there can be undone.
- **The doctor's card waits too.** It ignores every key but Escape while "Correct!" or "Not quite!" is showing. A key held down when it opens does nothing until pressed again, and keys pressed at the doctor never become steps.
- **Messages fade.** A line on the bottom of the screen stays for 5 seconds of walking about, then fades. A line said during a battle or at the doctor waits for you: after a battle the result's line is still there when you are back in the world. "Walk up to a tent…" goes as soon as you face a tent, where the prompt takes over. A line about who goes first also goes at once when a battle or the doctor changes your party.
- **The controls hint goes away** after your first 5 steps. Bumping into things doesn't count, however long you hold the key. It comes back for 5 steps after every reload.
- **The same walk meets the same animals.** Encounters are decided by how many steps you have taken in this game, so two new games walked the same way meet the same animals at the same steps (see § Finding a battle fast). The count is saved, so a reload carries on with the next step: it never replays the animals behind you. The doctor's puzzles work the same way.
- **Leading zeros are fine**: "09" is the same answer as "9".
- **Sound waits for your first key.** The game is silent until you press a key or touch the screen, because browsers allow sound only after one. Escape and Shift don't count; the first arrow, letter, number or Enter wakes it, and so does the first tap or click.
- **Sound off is remembered** on this device, across reloads. The first time, it is on.
- **Less motion if your computer asks for it.** With "reduce motion" turned on in the system's settings, the game moves less: the world behind the title holds still, a soft dim instead of the circle that closes into a battle, a lower hop and smaller arm swings when walking, two butterflies instead of six, smaller lunges and shakes, fewer confetti pieces, numbers and sparkles that fade in place, and Talk glows without pulsing. Turning it on or off while playing works at once; the spare butterflies fly away first.
- **Walking waits in the pause menu.** Opening it mid-step lets that step land, then nothing moves until the menu closes. An arrow still held when the menu opens or closes does nothing until you press it again, and a battle can never start while the menu is open. A reload with the menu open comes back to the world, the menu closed; a name typed but not yet saved with Enter is gone.
- **Danish or English, everywhere.** The game starts in Danish if your browser prefers Danish, else in English. Language in the pause menu, or on the title, switches every word at once — the animals' and attacks' names too ("Ræv", "Nøddekast") — even with a line still on the bottom of the screen, which changes language with the rest, and the game remembers the choice on this computer. `?lang=da` or `?lang=en` in the address picks one for that visit without changing what is remembered. Puzzles are the same sums in both languages, and so is typing the answer. A name you give an animal is never translated: "Nøddi" is Nøddi in both.
- **The title waits for you.** Nothing happens behind it: no step, no battle, no save. The world drifts past and one of each kind in your team (or, the first time, the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog) breathes beside the trainer, but they are scenery.
- **Mashing Enter never starts over.** On the title with a game saved, the cursor starts on Continue, so a mashed Enter carries on your game. "Start a new game?" starts on "No, go back" and takes no Enter until a quiet moment, so a mashed Enter leaves it up, answering nothing; starting over takes Down, then Enter. The starters and the name box wait for a quiet moment too, so an Enter mashed on New game can't pick a starter or skip its name unseen.
- **`?zoo` shows every animal.** Open `http://localhost:5180/?zoo` and one of each species stands in a row two or three tiles from the start, in catalog order — squirrel, rabbit, frog, fox, otter, deer, wolf, bear, then the sea animals, crab, starfish, turtle, dolphin, octopus, whale, standing on the grass like the rest (only the zoo puts them on land) — facing you. They are scenery: you walk straight through them and nothing else changes. It skips the title and plays a throwaway game, like `?new`: nothing is saved. Start screen, then Continue or a new game, finds the same line-up standing where it was, and no second one. It exists to check the figures, not to play with. `?zoo=tired` lays the same line-up down to rest, as a tired animal lies in a battle, with its z's.
- **`?debug` shows where you are**: the grid position and the way you face (`5, 6 · down`) in the top-right corner, and under it, once anything has made a sound, the last four sounds the game asked for, newest last (`♪ move · confirm · correct · hit`, with ✕ while sound is off).
- **`?tokens=` and `?shop` are for looking at the shop.** `?tokens=40` starts with 40 tokens (a whole number up to 9999; anything else is ignored), and `?shop` puts every tool on sale, on sale for real or not. Both play a throwaway game like `?new`: nothing is loaded or saved, and the saved game is left alone. An axe or a pickaxe bought there chops and breaks as in a real game (the trees come back with the next page), and a boat sails (`?tokens=40` is enough to buy it and try it). They combine with the others: `?debug&party=squirrel:5,rabbit:0,fox&tokens=23&shop`.
- **`?party=` picks your starting party**, for looking at screens that need one: `?party=squirrel:5,rabbit:0,fox` starts with a squirrel at 5 HP, a tired rabbit and a fox at full HP, in a throwaway game like `?new`: nothing is loaded or saved, and the saved game is left alone. Species are the catalog ids (`squirrel`, `rabbit`, `frog`, `fox`, `otter`, `deer`, `wolf`, `bear`, and the sea animals `crab`, `starfish`, `turtle`, `dolphin`, `octopus`, `whale`), HP is optional (full by default) and clamped to the species, and `*` and a number asks for that many (`rabbit*30`, `rabbit:0*5`), up to 1,000 animals in all. The party starts in bundles, each kind behind its first animal (`rabbit,fox,rabbit` starts with the two rabbits together). Anything misspelt, a count of 0, or more than 1,000 animals, and the game starts as usual. Switches combine: `?debug&party=bear`.

## Saving

- **Everything is saved as you go**, in this browser: after every step, every battle turn, every catch and every tree chopped or rock broken, and when you close the tab. There is no save button and nothing to wait for. Reload, or come back another day, and the title's Continue puts you where you were, facing the way you were, with the same animals and HP. The message line says "Welcome back!".
- **Start screen** (the pause menu's last row) saves the game as it stands and goes back to the title, with no question asked: Continue there carries on from the same step, and the same animals are waiting in the grass.
- **A reload in a battle picks the battle up** (Continue on the title): the same wild animal, the same HP, and the same puzzle if one was up, with its attack lit at its level in the menu beside it, or the list of who steps in after a knock-out (what you had typed is gone, and so are the levels you had picked for the other attacks). The narration starts again with "A wild … appears!". A reload after you answered, while the turn is still being told, skips to what happened: the answer already counted.
- **The saved game belongs to the browser and the address.** Another browser, a private window, `localhost` instead of the shared link, or a different link is a different game. A kid who comes back through the same link on the same browser finds their game.
- **A Home Screen icon can be a different game on an iPhone or iPad.** Share → Add to Home Screen gives the game's fox as an icon. On iOS and iPadOS 26 the icon opens as a web app unless "Open as Web App" is switched off as it is added, and a web app keeps its own storage apart from Safari's: it opens on the title with New game only, while the animals are still in Safari. Switched off, the icon opens the game in Safari with everything there.
- **Two tabs or windows of the game.** Both start as the same game. When one meets a wild animal, catches an animal, wins, loses, plays a battle turn, heals or changes the team, the other is behind: it ignores every key. A hidden tab loads the newer game when you switch to it. A window on screen next to the one you play in shows a card, "You kept playing in another window. Get your newest game [Enter]", and loads it when you click into that window or on the button, or press Enter or Space. Either way it goes straight back into play, without the title, and says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!" (a tab that was on the title opens the title again, with Continue on the newer game). A window you are using loads by itself at most 3 times a minute; after that it shows the card and waits for Enter, which always works. While a window is behind, typing in its name box shows no letters and a Space there does nothing; Enter still loads the newest game. When one tab starts a new game, the other goes to the title as soon as you look at it, with Continue on the new game. Walking in either is fine, as long as it meets nothing: the one you use carries on from where it is. Nothing a tab did is ever lost to the other one: a game one tab left for a new one is put away with the rest, and a tab that is behind never lets you play on in it.
- **Start a new game**: New game on the title, then your name and a first animal; the game starts in a new home world. With a game saved it asks "Start a new game?" first, and "Yes, new game" puts the saved game away: it is kept in this browser (`animath.save.previous`, then `.2`, `.3`, …) and on the server, and never deleted, but there is no way in the game to go back to it (the human can: [[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database). Nothing inside the game deletes a save; clearing this site's data in the browser does. `http://…/?new` plays a new game in that tab that is saved nowhere; drop `?new` and the saved game is back, untouched. A private window is also a new game, kept only while the window is open.
- **A browser that blocks site storage** (or has run out of room for the save, or a page with a newer version's save waiting) plays, but keeps nothing. Every title it opens says so, the one Start screen opens too: "This browser can't keep your game, so it starts new every time." (or "This browser has no room left to keep your game.", or "Your game was saved by a newer Animath. Reload the page to play it!", which also shows when the newer game is on the server). There, New game asks "Start a new game?" with "Your game with the Squirrel can't be kept on this page." instead of "is put away safely".
- **A save that won't load** (after an update the game cannot read it) leaves the title with New game only; once you have picked a starter the message line says "Your saved game didn't load, so here is a new one." The old save stays where it was until then, and is then kept aside, not deleted. A save written by a newer version of the game is not touched: the title says to reload the page, and a new game started there is not saved.
- **No server, no problem.** The game saves in the browser whether or not the server is running. It also keeps a backup on the server when it can reach it; if the server is down it tries a few times, then again after the next battle.
- **After an update that changes how games are saved** (numbered worlds did), Continue carries on exactly where you were, a game from before numbered worlds in World 1; a game with no name asks for yours first, once. The old save is kept as it was too, in `animath.save.upgraded` (and `.2`, `.3`, …) in this browser and on the server.

## Exploits and quirks

- **The boat is a way round the wild.** The shallows never start a battle, and nothing on the water challenges a team with nobody standing who swims, so a kid with the boat can cross a river, or sail round a lake's shore, without a battle; only the deep water has animals, and only for a team with a swimmer. After a lost battle the trip to the nearest tent counts the water too, so with the boat you may wake up beside a tent across a lake.
- **A quiet grass after a sea win.** End a battle at sea with a sea animal standing (win it, run from it or catch the wild one) while every animal of yours that walks is tired, and back on land nothing comes out of the grass and nobody follows, until a doctor makes one of them fit: a free repel. A reload doesn't heal them (someone is standing).
- **Losing is a free full heal.** Healing at the doctor costs one puzzle per kind of animal, but losing a battle on purpose (answer wrong) heals the whole party for nothing and puts you by the nearest tent. It earns no tokens and costs none. Reloading heals nothing and forgets nothing.
- **Farming the small ones.** Walking Left and Right on the reed by the start and throwing the leash at every squirrel, rabbit and frog brings about 1.2–1.4 tokens a minute once they go home (2 each): about twice ordinary play near home, and less than a fox in front brings (§4 of [[PRODUCT]] has the model). By design: every catch is still battles and sums.
- **A tired animal brings the same tokens** as a fit one: the doctor makes it better before it goes. By design.
- **A token sum can't be skipped, only put away**: a wrong answer asks the same sum again (unlike a healing puzzle, which changes), and Back, Escape, another tab or Bye puts it away with nothing done. The right answer is never shown.
- **A hard doctor's puzzle can be skipped**: answer anything (a wrong answer costs nothing and brings another puzzle), swap to another animal and back, or say bye and talk again. By design: at the doctor, nothing is lost by missing. A reload while the doctor's card is open closes it; anything healed stays healed, animals sent home stay home and a tool bought stays yours, and a sum not yet answered never happened.
- **The save is plain text in the browser.** Anyone who opens the browser's developer tools can read and edit `animath.save` in the site's local storage: change HP, add animals, move anywhere, set the tokens, add a tool, clear any tree or rock (`edits`; water, tents and tall grass stay what they are whatever it says). In the middle of a puzzle it also holds the puzzle's answer. The server backup takes whatever the browser sends. Harmless in a single-player game; it matters once games are shared ([[DEFERRED]]).
- **A bear in front walks through the meadow and the river in peace.** Nothing there is big enough to challenge it, so no battle ever starts; the same goes for a wolf at the river. With a bear in the team, its number key turns this on and off as you walk: put the bear first to cross the meadow without a battle, then a squirrel first to meet rabbits again.
- **A small lead with a big fighter.** The animal that goes first decides how fierce the animals you meet are, but anyone can fight them. Put the Squirrel first, switch to a Bear on the first turn of every battle, and the Squirrel never gets tired, so it stays first: you keep meeting squirrels and rabbits and beat them with Bear-sized hits (and Bear-hard sums). Without switching, the Squirrel would soon be tired and the Bear would go first.
- **`?party=`, `?tokens=` and `?shop` are cheats**: anyone who edits the URL can start with a bear (and so walk the meadow in peace), with thousands of tokens, or with every tool for sale. They are there for testing, and the game they start is never saved.
- **A nickname can be anything made of letters**, including another animal's name or "Wild Fox": a squirrel called "Bear" is still a squirrel, and one called "Wild Fox" shows "Wild Fox" in its own status box in battle.
- **The rude-word check on names is short and simple.** It catches the obvious English and Danish words, spelled plainly, in capitals, with digits for letters ("sh1t"), drawn out ("shiiit") or split by a space or a hyphen ("Fu Ck"), and lets through real names with a word inside (Scunthorpe, Hassan, Pikachu). A kid who spells a rude word with look-alike letters from another alphabet gets past it.
- **A path straight through a forest.** With the axe a kid can chop a line through any forest, or clear one bare, and walk where no one could; with the pickaxe, cut a gravel road over the mountains and wander the snowy peaks, which were all rock. The forest's and the mountain's tall grass stays, and so do their animals: who comes out is decided by the world as it was made. By design (the human's son asked for it).
- **A tent walled in by trees can be opened**, and a knock-out then takes the kid there along the path they cut: the trip to the nearest tent walks the world as the kid left it.
- **Thousands of chops later, the farthest grow back.** A game keeps about 24 KB of cleared tiles; past that, the ones farthest from the kid grow back a chunk at a time, never the ones on screen. A kid would have to clear thousands of tiles for this, fewer only if they were spread out one to a chunk all over the world.
