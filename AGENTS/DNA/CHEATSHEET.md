# Cheatsheet

Everything a player can do in **today's build**, and how: every key, every hidden behaviour, every exploit. This file is a record of what the game actually does. It is not a plan: intended controls live in [[UI_SPEC]], the list of features in [[PRODUCT]] §5, and the rules in [[PRODUCT]] §4.

**Keep it true.** Any PR that adds, removes or changes an input, a way to reach something, or a behaviour a player could stumble on updates this file in the same PR. If you find an exploit, record it here, and if a kid could use it to break the game, also file it as a `bug`.

Escape opens the pause menu in explore and leaves the witch doctor's card; in a friendly match it asks "Leave the match?"; in a battle it only backs out of the switch list — not even in a battle puzzle: once a battle puzzle is up, the only way on is to answer it. M is the one key that works the same on every screen, except where you type, and in a window that another window has played past, which takes no key but Enter and Space (and Escape for its Log out, when it has one: § Saving). W A S D work the same with Caps Lock on or Shift held, in every menu too, and on a Russian or Greek keyboard they are the keys where W A S D sit on an English one. A key pressed with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt belongs to the browser everywhere: Cmd+D bookmarks, Ctrl+S saves the page, Alt+← goes back, and none of them moves the trainer. A mouse or a finger works every row, button and card too (§ Touch and mouse); walking takes the keys on a laptop and the D-pad on a tablet. The page has no debug keys or console hooks; its URL parameters are `?zoo`, `?lang`, `?debug`, `?steps=`, `?party=`, `?new`, `?tokens=`, `?shop`, `?items=` and a welcome link's `#welcome=` (or `?welcome=`) (see § Hidden behaviour and § Saving). The throwaway switches among them ([[ARCHITECTURE]], the `flags.ts` row) skip the title: the game starts at once, and is saved nowhere.

## Touch and mouse

Everything below is a click with a mouse or a tap with a finger, and each is the key beside it: the same thing happens, behind the same waits. A tap counts as the finger lifts, and only on what it went down on, on the same screen, and not if that moved meanwhile (a team row another finger moved up); the number pad and the D-pad count as the finger lands. Each finger counts on its own: tap Talk or Menu with one thumb while the other walks. The **touch controls** are the D-pad, Talk, Menu, Fly (once the paraglider is owned) and the number pad: they are there from the start on a tablet (a screen whose main pointer is a finger); anywhere, the first touch (or pen) brings them and the first key pressed on a real keyboard puts them away. A mouse changes nothing.

## The world

- **Every world has a number**, from 1 to 9999, and the same number is the same world every time and on every machine. A new game from the title starts in a world of its own, your home, a number picked at random from 2 to 9999, so each new game is somewhere new. **World 1** is the world every game was played in before worlds had numbers (the seed `'prototype'`): a game saved before then carries on there, and `?new` (like every throwaway switch, § Hidden behaviour) plays there. Your world's number is in the top right corner, under your tokens and tools ("World 42").
- **Going to another world**: Escape, then Worlds (the row shows the world you are in), type a number on the pad or the keyboard, and Go; or Go home. The world closes into a circle of sky round you, the new one opens out of it, and its number pops up big for about two and a half seconds ("World 42!", with "A new world to explore!", "Back where you left off." or "Home again!"). No key does anything while the circle is closed or opening (M still does), and the D-pad is away: a thumb left where it was walks nothing until you lift it and press again. The world you left is kept as you left it (where you stood, the way you faced, the trees and rocks you cleared), for the last 100 worlds you left, your home always among them; a first visit starts at the world's spawn, facing down. Your animals, tokens, tools and name go with you. Only while exploring: the menu is closed in a battle and at the witch doctor. Opening the menu (or the witch doctor's card) while the number is up puts it away. With "reduce motion" on, the sky fades in and out instead.
- A new game starts at its world's **spawn**: a grass tile with a witch doctor's tent at most 12 steps away on foot and room to roam. In World 1 that is **(-2, 6)** on the engine's grid, on grass next to a stretch of water, the tent at (5, 7) 7 steps away; after that, the game starts where you left it (§ Saving). On the grid, pressing Right adds 1 to x and pressing Down adds 1 to y; the screen shows the grid only with `?debug` in the URL (§ Hidden behaviour). Positions written like **(-2, 6)** in these docs are the grid's.
- **Your coordinates are in the bottom-right corner** ("x 40 · y 1000"), counted in steps from your world's spawn, so the spawn of every world reads **x 0 · y 0**, as a maths book draws a graph: Right adds 1 to x, **Up adds 1 to y**, and Left and Down take 1 away, below zero too ("x −3 · y −12"). So the grid's (5, 7), World 1's tent, is x 7 · y −1 there. They change with every step, in the boat, and tile by tile while you glide; a first visit to a world starts at 0, 0. Everyone in a world has the same spawn, so a friend standing on your tile reads the same numbers. Where they sit on a tablet and a phone: [[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode. They are not shown in a battle, at the witch doctor or in the menu.
- The world never ends. It is generated in 16×16 chunks as you walk, in every direction.
- **You are a kid in a blue cap** (coral shirt, blue shorts). The figure turns to face the way you last walked or bumped, swings its arms and legs and hops a little with every step, stands on top of hills rather than sinking into them, and breathes gently while you stand still.
- **The animal that goes first walks behind you**, one tile back, on the tile you just left: it can never stand in water (unless it swims, out in the boat: § The boat), on a rock or in a tent, it never blocks you, and it never starts a battle. Walk back onto its tile and you swap places; it steps round you. Press another card's number (or pick "Go first" in the menu) and it shrinks away as the new one pops up in its place; the same happens after a battle when someone else ends up first. After Continue, a reload, a go-to or another world it is simply there beside you, behind you if there is room. When every animal that walks is tired nobody follows (after a lost battle it shrinks away as you are back in the world; `?party=squirrel:0` starts that way): heal one at the witch doctor and it pops out behind you as its HP bar fills.
- **Butterflies** flutter round you wherever there is land, and behind the title. They are only for looking at: you can't catch, battle or bump into them, and walking never meets one. None pops up out of nowhere: walk away from them and new ones fly in from the edge of the screen ahead of you.
- **Each area looks like itself**: the meadow is bright green with little flowers; the forest has a darker floor, crowded dark pines, young trees and bushes; river banks are sand with reed beds; the mountains have grey-green turf, pebbles, fields of boulders and snow on the boulders of the highest rocks.

## Trees and rocks

- **Trees grow only in the forest, rocks only in the mountains** (the highest ones capped with snow). The nearest tree to the start is at (-15, 9), in the woods up the river bank to the left: stand on (-16, 9) and face right, or on (-15, 10) and face up. The nearest rock is at (-1, 19), south of the start: stand on (-2, 19) and face right. The nearest snow-capped peak is at (-20, 37), 48 steps from the start: stand on (-20, 36) and face down, or on (-19, 37) and face left.
- **The axe chops a tree down, the pickaxe breaks a rock** (at the witch doctor's shop; prices in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Tokens and the witch doctor's shop"). Stand next to it, face it (walking into it turns you), and press Enter or tap Chop/Break. The trainer swings the tool over the shoulder, the tree tips over away from you with chips flying (the rock shakes and crumbles into pebbles), and a stump (or gravel) is left. You can walk into the gap at once.
- **It stays cleared.** Every tree and rock you clear is saved with your game: reload, walk far away and come back, and the stump is still there (only thousands of chops later do the farthest grow back: § Exploits and quirks). A new game starts with every tree back.
- **The wrong tool does nothing**: the axe can't break a rock, the pickaxe can't chop a tree. Without the tool, the first bump into a tree or a rock in a game says the witch doctor sells one; after that only Enter says it.
- **Only trees and rocks.** Tall grass, reeds, sand, water and tents never go, whatever you own.
- **Clearing changes where you walk, never who comes out of the grass**: the animals in the woods are the woods' whether you chopped them bare or not.

## The boat

- **Buy it at the witch doctor** (the Shop tab: `?tokens=40` in a throwaway game is enough). Leaving the card, it is on your back: a brown shell with a coral rim, bottom out.
- **Walk into the water** (shallow or deep) and you sail: the step takes a little longer (about half a second) while you hop in and the boat swings off your back, turns over, grows and drops under you. From then on every arrow sails a tile, in any water; rock, trees and tents still stop you. Step onto sand, grass or reeds to land, and the boat swings back onto your back. With "reduce motion" on it just snaps into place halfway through the step.
- **The quickest water from the start**: straight up. (-2, 5), (-2, 4) and (-2, 3) are shallows, and from (-2, 2) on it is deep water, the darker blue: the big lake north of the start.
- **Out on the water only animals that swim fight** (the frog, the otter, the toad, the beaver, the mute swan, and the sea animals you caught). The first one standing that swims goes first there: its card gets the "goes first" tag and it swims behind the boat, low in the water. A number for one that can't swim says "Squirrel can't swim! …" and changes nothing, and so does its card dropped at the top of the column (it stays on top, and the lead stays the swimmer); in the pause menu its Go first is greyed, with "Squirrel can't swim, so it can only go first on land." under it. On land, who goes first is as before.
- **With nobody standing who swims**, your animal that goes first on land hops into the boat once you are in it and stands on the little deck at the front, facing where you sail (a big one made smaller to fit), turning with the boat when you bump into a rock or a tree; it hops out beside you when you land.
- **Only the deep water has animals.** The shallows along every shore never start a battle, so crossing a river is always peaceful; out on the deep water, the darker blue, the sea animals come out (§ The sea animals).
- **A reload out on the water** (Continue) puts you back in the boat where you were, the swimmer in the water behind you (beside you when there is no water behind; never on a beach, even right beside the boat) or the rider in the boat. A save with you on water and no boat (only by editing the save by hand) starts you on the spawn tile instead.
- **Chopping from the boat**: with the axe too, a tree right beside the water can be chopped from the boat, and its stump is somewhere to land. The nearest is 50 steps from the start: sail to (21, 33) and face down, at the tree on (21, 34).
- **The witch doctor from the water**: nearly every tent has ground between it and the water, so land first and talk from the ground. A rare few stand right beside the water (in World 1 the nearest is far out, at (97, 349), with water below it): face one from the boat, and Enter talks to the witch doctor from there.

## The harness

- **Buy it at the witch doctor** (the Shop tab). Leaving the card, the message line says "Put a big animal first and hop on!", and the HUD lists it with your tools (a saddle).
- **Ride**: put a red deer, a wild boar, a wolf, a bear, a moose or a European bison first (a number key, a card, the menu) and you sit on its back at once; nothing to press. Any other animal first walks behind you as before, the lynx and the wolverine too.
- **Only a look**: you walk as fast as on foot, the grass is the same grass, and the same animals jump out. Your animal is under you, so nobody walks behind.
- **Water and air**: step onto the water with the boat and your big animal shrinks away and rides in the boat (as it always did without a swimmer); take off with the paraglider and you rise off its back, and it waits on the ground. Step back onto land, or come down, and you are on its back again.
- **Friends** see you riding too, and you see them ride theirs.
- **Quick look**: `?party=moose,bear,deer,wolf,wild-boar,european-bison&items=harness`, then 1 to 6 to change mounts.

## The paraglider

- **Buy it at the witch doctor** (the Shop tab: `?tokens=40` in a throwaway game is enough). Leaving the card, the message line says "Hold Space to fly!" ("Hold Fly to fly!" on a tablet), a little coral-and-cream roll sits high on your back (over the boat's shell when you carry the boat too), and the HUD lists it with your tools.
- **Hold Space a quarter of a second** (Fly on a tablet): you crouch, jump and rise as the canopy opens over you, and glide straight ahead the way you face, about 7 tiles a second, over trees, rocks, tents and water. A dark shadow under you shows where you are over. No steering: arrows do nothing up there.
- **The ring** on the ground is where you land if you let go now. Over water without the boat it sits on the far shore, and letting go anywhere over the water lands you there. Over a tree with the axe (or a rock with the pickaxe) the ring sits on it with the tool turning over it: land there and you chop it (break it) as you come down, and stand on the stump (the gravel), saved like any chop.
- **Let go** of Space (or lift your finger, press Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, or switch windows) and you glide on to the ring and come down there. **Keep holding** and you come down by yourself at the farthest tile of the 20 ahead you can land on.
- **Where you can land**: grass, tall grass, sand, stumps and gravel, always; water only with the boat (you land in it); a tree only with the axe; a rock only with the pickaxe; a tent never. The axe never lets you land on a rock, nor the pickaxe on a tree.
- **Too far**: with nowhere to land in the 20 tiles ahead you don't take off: a little hop, and "Too far! There's nowhere to land that way." Turn another way, or bring the boat. A lake more than 19 tiles across needs the boat.
- **The first flight**: from the start (-2, 6), bump into the lake above you to face up, and hold Space: however early you let go, you come down on the sand of the far shore at (-2, -8), 14 tiles up. With the boat, let go over the lake and you land in it right there.
- **Your bird flies with you**: with a bird in the team (a robin, a heron, an owl, a buzzard, a swan, an eagle), the first one standing takes off with you and flies behind you, its wings beating, and lands beside you when you land; it is the one that goes first up there ("goes first" moves to it for the flight). With no bird, your lead shrinks away as you take off and pops back beside you when you land (in the boat on water, the rider or the swimmer as usual).
- **Nothing on the ground happens up there**: no battle starts in the grass or at sea, over tall grass or deep water, nor on the tile you land on. Arrows, Enter, the number keys, the party cards and Escape do nothing; an arrow still held when you land walks on from there. Each tile flown counts as a step. Only a bird can notice you (§ Birds in the air).
- **From the boat**: take off from the water and the boat swings onto your back as you rise.
- **A reload in the air** (closing the page mid-flight) puts you where letting go would have: the game is always saved as if you had just let go.
- **Friends see you fly**: in their window you rise, glide over the tiles you fly and come down, your canopy in your shirt's colour; Who's here says "flying". Nobody follows you in their window until you are down, not your bird, nor a wild one chasing you.

## The Arctic

Only in a throwaway game for now (`?lands`, § Hidden behaviour): walk to the tent at (5, 7), Enter, L, answer the fare, and you come down beside the same tent in The Arctic. Its animals, shop and plane come in #191 steps 5 to 7.

- **Two poles.** A band of open sea runs east to west for ever just south of where you arrive (rows 11 to 22 on the grid, at least 10 wide, darker water, deep in the middle): north of it is the Arctic (tundra, taiga, fell, bird cliffs, frozen lakes, sea ice), south of it the Antarctic (the rookery coast, the white ice sheet, sea ice). Cross it only in the boat. Ice blocks float in it; the boat stops at them as at a rock.
- **The spawn** of every Arctic world is (5, 9), on the north shore, two steps below the tent at (5, 7), where the tundra meets the bird cliffs; the band's water is a step or two further down.
- **A tent on every spot** of the tents' lattice (x mod 23 = 5, y mod 19 = 7), each in a 5×5 clearing of snow, even out on the sea ice, where it stands on a little snowy island you reach by sliding.
- **Far north is mostly sea ice**: from about 100 rows north of the band the frozen Arctic Ocean takes over (in World 1 try around (-57, -420)); far south the ice sheet's coast is cut by bays of sea ice (around (-19, 157)).
- **You wear a warm hat** there: a knitted hat in your cap's blue, a white cuff, ear flaps and a bobble. Every other player there wears one too, in their own cap's colour. No butterflies.

| Tile | Walk on it? |
| ---- | ----------- |
| Snow | Yes. |
| Deep snow (a bluer patch heaped with white drifts) | Yes. The Arctic's tall grass: a step onto it rolls a battle, once the land has animals (until then it is quiet). On the sea ice its drifts are deep snow too. |
| Ice (pale cyan with white streaks of shine: frozen lakes and the sea ice) | Yes, but you **slide** (below). |
| Fishing hole (a dark round in the ice, a rim of snow) | No. Walking or sliding into it stops you in front of it, facing it (fishing comes with the rod, #191 step 6). |
| Ice block (a glassy blue block, on snow, on the ice or afloat) | No, not even in the boat. |
| Rock (a dark boulder capped with snow), spruce (taiga) | No. |
| Water, deep water | Only in the boat, as in Nordland. |

**Sliding.** A step onto the ice slides you on the way you stepped, tile after tile at a smooth even pace, feet together, until something stops you, with or without anything you own:
- ground that isn't ice (snow, deep snow, a snowdrift, the water in the boat) stops you **on it**: a slide onto deep snow is a step onto deep snow, and is the only step of the slide that can start a battle;
- the ice never touches anything you can't step onto: next to an ice block, a rock, a tree, a fishing hole or open water the ice is a **bank of snow**, so every slide ends on ground, and the same arrow the other way slides you straight back to where you started. No slide can leave you somewhere you can't get out of. (Were the ice ever to run into something with no bank, the rule would stop you on the ice in front of it, facing it.)
- every straight run of ice in The Arctic ends within 22 tiles (each row and column of the ice has a snowdrift or an ice block at least every 23 tiles), so no slide is ever longer than 23 tiles.
- **How to reach a fishing hole**: every hole on the ice has a bank of snow on its four sides. Slide straight at it along its row or column and you stop on its bank, facing it; or walk onto a bank from the snow. In World 1 one is at (4, -456): from the snow at (4, -451) (`x −1 · y 460`) press Up and you slide four tiles, to stop on its bank facing it.
- A slide is one move: the arrow you hold sends the next only once you are down, every tile counts as a step, and a reload puts you where the slide ended (the game is never saved in the middle of one). Friends see you slide the same tiles.
- From a standstill on the ice, a step onto the ice slides again; a step onto snow is an ordinary step. A step into the boat from the ice ends the slide on the water.
- **The paraglider** comes down on the ice and you stand there, no slide; never on an ice block or into a hole.

## Birds in the air

- **Only with a bird in the team.** With a bird standing (the first bird in your team order is your lead in the air), every tile you fly over has a 1-in-20 chance that a wild bird notices you: about once in 20 tiles, at most once a flight. With no bird standing (a squirrel and an otter, say, or a tired robin), nothing up there ever notices you.
- **It follows you down**: it comes from behind with a "!" over it and a squawk, the message line says "A grumpy Robin is following you!", and it chases you. Let go or hold on as ever: you land, the bird swoops in to hover in front of you, and the battle's circle closes on a **battle in the air**.
- **Only birds fight in the air**, wherever you landed (in tall grass, on sand, in the boat): your first bird standing steps in, only another bird can be switched in (the others "can't fly" and sit it out on the ground), and when every bird is tired the battle is lost, whoever still stands. Won, the wild bird flies home; run from, it stays up in the sky; lost, you stay where you came down, nobody healed, as after any lost battle (§ Battles, **Lose**): with a walker still standing (the squirrel that sat it out) the grass is its as ever, and with nobody who walks standing it is quiet, the bottom line says your animals are tired, and the disc points to the nearest tent on foot or by boat, never flown. A tired bird can't lead in the air either, so nothing up there notices you until a witch doctor has helped.
- **Which bird**: the sky over the tile it noticed you from decides, by the grass's rules. With a robin near home it is nearly always a robin (now and then a buzzard over the meadow, a tawny owl over the forest or a heron over the river; the odds are in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters"). Far out, bigger birds: eagle-owls and golden eagles over the mountains, herons, swans and sea eagles over the river, and **only sea eagles over the sea**. A bigger bird in front meets bigger birds.
- **Where to meet the buzzard**: in the meadow's tall grass, or in the air over the meadow, most often by the woods. A tawny owl, a heron or a buzzard in front makes most of the meadow's birds near home buzzards.
- **A reload is no escape**: close the page while a bird follows you, and Continue puts you down with its battle under way, the same bird with the same HP.
- **Quick look**: `?party=robin&tokens=40`, buy the paraglider, and fly from the start (-2, 6) up over the lake: 17 tiles to the reach, and a bird follows about 6 flights in 10.

## The sea animals

- **Fourteen of them, only at sea**: the Crab, the Starfish, the Moon jellyfish and the Plaice (tier 1), the Turtle, the Lion's mane and the Lobster (2), the Dolphin, the Harbour seal and the Porpoise (3), the Octopus and the Grey seal (4), and the Humpback whale and the Orca (5). The whale was called just "Whale" before the orca and the porpoise, whales too, came. They come out only on the deep water, from the boat, on the same 1 step in 10 as tall grass, and only while an animal that swims (the frog, the otter, the toad, the beaver, the mute swan, or a sea animal you caught) is standing to go first; with nobody standing who swims, nothing comes out. Never a frog or an otter out there, never a sea animal in the grass.
- **Meeting one fast**: with the boat and a frog or an otter, walk straight up from the start: (-2, 5), (-2, 4) and (-2, 3) are shallows, (-2, 2) is deep. Then step left and right between (-2, 2) and (-3, 2): about 1 step in 10 starts a battle, half the time within 7 steps. A saved game at (-2, 2) with no steps walked yet, the otter in front, meets a Moon jellyfish on the 11th step of left, right, left, right… (the sixth Left), a Turtle on the 15th, a Lobster on the 28th, a Lion's mane on the 71st and a Harbour seal on the 97th.
- **Who comes out**: mostly the sea animals of your lead's size: with a frog in front the four small ones, with an otter the turtles, lion's manes and lobsters, with a whale or an orca the whales and orcas. Far from home the bigger ones come out more. The sea is never quiet, whoever leads (the odds are in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters", "At sea").
- **Catching one** is the same as on land (the leash, best when its HP is low); a caught sea animal joins your team, and caught ones are set free at the witch doctor for tokens like any other (a whale or an orca brings 30).
- **At sea a sea animal is a swimmer like the otter**: it can go first, fight, be switched in, and it swims behind your boat when it leads.
- **On land a sea animal can't go first or fight**: its number or its card says "Crab lives in the sea! On land, an animal that walks goes first." and changes nothing (its card dropped at the top stays there and says the same); the pause menu greys its Go first, with "Crab lives in the sea, so it can only go first on the water." under it; in a land battle the switch list tags it "lives in the sea" ("Crab lives in the sea. It only fights out on the water."), and with only sea animals standing besides the one in front, Switch says "Your other animals live in the sea.". A team whose only animals standing are sea animals meets nothing in the grass and has nobody following on land: walk to a witch doctor.
- **The witch doctor keeps a walker**: set animals free all you like, but one that isn't tired and walks on land always stays ("Keep one animal that can walk with you!"), so setting animals free never leaves you with only sea animals standing; the last walker's check box is dashed before you try, and picking it gives a little shake. With no walker standing at all, nobody can be set free until one is healed ("No animal can walk with you now. Heal one first!"). A battle can: see § Exploits and quirks.
- **A battle at sea**: both animals swim with the lower part of them under the surface, whatever their size, and at the end the wild one "swims home to rest" or "stays in the water" (the Run row says the same), never "in the grass".
- **They are no starters**: a new game starts on land, with the squirrel, the rabbit or the frog.

## Finding a battle fast

In World 1 the tile straight left of the start, (-3, 6), is a river reed with the lake all round it: tall grass on the river bank. Frogs, brown rats and toads live in the reeds and love the water, so a little over half of what comes out there is one of them; near home the other small animals come down to the water too, and once in a while one of the river's tier-2 animals, an otter, a heron, a raccoon or a beaver, about 1 battle in 37 together; its big ones, the mute swan, the sea eagle and the moose, hardly ever come out near home for a small animal (the odds are in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters"). In a new game there (`?new`, or a game saved before numbered worlds, with any starter, since they are all the same size; New game on the title now starts in another world), press Left and Right in turn (Left, Right, Left, …): every Left lands on the reed. **The 11th step (the sixth Left) always meets a Brown rat**, the 15th a Frog, the first Common toad comes on the 71st step, a Stag beetle on the 97th, the first Wood mouse on the 103rd and the first Otter on the 117th. The steps are counted over the whole game and saved with it, so this only works from the start of a game, not after a reload. The nearest meadow tall grass is three steps straight down from the start, (-3, 9) to (-1, 9), and there is a strip at (2, 7)–(4, 7), beside the tent: open meadow, so about 7 battles in 10 are rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs and moles (17% each), and most of the rest the meadow's other small animals, with one of its six tier-2 animals (a fox, a roe deer, a badger, a stoat, an adder or a buzzard) 1 time in 10 and a red deer hardly ever (1 time in 7,000). The nearest forest tall grass on foot is (14, 8), 18 steps east of the start, by the lake: 30% toads there, the forest's small animals of the trees 11% each, and a raccoon 1 time in 29.

**Finding frogs**: any reed on a river bank, near home or far out, and the more water round the reed, the more of them. They live nowhere else, though near home, while a small animal goes first, they also hop up into the mountain grass. The bigger the animal in front, the rarer they get at the reeds: with a fox about 1 battle in 23 is a frog, with a bear 1 in 16,000 (the river has animals of every size since #89's big ones, and a bear meets mostly moose there). The brown rats live at the river and in the meadow, the toads at the river and in the forest, both by the water.

**Where each animal lives.** Every animal favours one kind of ground near the tall grass it comes out of, and with plenty of that ground within 3 tiles it comes out four times as often as an animal of its size with none ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters"):

- **By the water**: frogs, otters, herons, beavers, mute swans and sea eagles (at the river's reeds), brown rats (the river and the meadow), toads (the river and the forest), and raccoons and moose (the forest and the river).
- **By the trees**: squirrels, foxes, deer, wood mice, robins, roe deer and badgers (the forest, and the meadow along its edge), buzzards (the meadow, by the woods), stag beetles, pine martens, tawny owls and wild boars (the forest), and lynxes (the forest and the mountains).
- **By the rocks**: wolves, bears and eagle-owls (the mountains, and the forest beside them), wolverines and golden eagles (the mountains), common lizards, stoats and adders (the mountains, and the meadow by its stones).
- **In open grass**, away from water, trees and rocks: rabbits and moles (the open meadow), shrews and hedgehogs (the open meadow and the forest's glades), and European bison (the forest's glades).

Near home the ground only picks among animals of the same size, so it never makes the start harder: the frogs, rats and toads take the reeds by the water, the rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs and moles the open meadow, the squirrels, wood mice and robins the edge of the woods, while the tier-2 animals and the big ones stay as rare as ever. The further out you go, the more it sorts the sizes too: far from home the wolves, bears and eagle-owls keep to the rocks, and the woods among the trees are squirrels, foxes, deer, wild boars, lynxes and the other animals of the trees. The ground only changes which animal comes out, never whether one does: the same steps start battles wherever you walk. Where every animal that could come out likes the same ground, it changes nothing: far from home, with a small animal in front, a river's reeds are about a fifth each frogs, brown rats and toads, a tenth each otters, herons, raccoons and beavers, and 1 in 13 a mute swan, wet or dry.

Who comes out depends on who leads your party (§ Your party). With a frog or a rabbit in front, the same steps meet the same animals as with the squirrel. With a tier-2 animal in front (a fox, an otter, a heron…), the same steps on the reed meet tier-2 animals: the river's own four and, since bigger animals live at the river, the eight that come down to the water near home (foxes, roe deer, badgers, pine martens, stoats, adders, tawny owls and buzzards), each of the river's own four times as often as a visitor there, by the water; its small ones come about 1 battle in 8, and a mute swan 1 in 40: an Adder on the 11th step, a Brown rat on the 15th, a Common toad on the 53rd, an Otter on the 69th, a Raccoon on the 71st and a Mute swan on the 97th. With a bigger animal in front the reed still starts a battle on the same steps: with a red deer, mostly mute swans and the red deer, wild boars and eagle-owls that come down to the water (a Mute swan on the 11th step, a Raccoon on the 15th, a Wild boar on the 53rd); with a wolf, sea eagles and the wolves, lynxes, wolverines and golden eagles that come down to it (a Golden eagle on the 11th, a Mute swan on the 15th, a Lynx on the 53rd); with a bear, the moose, its size (a Moose on the 11th), sea eagles (the 15th) and now and then a mute swan (the first on the 147th).

## Battles

- **A battle opens with a circle.** A little tune plays, a circle closes on you (the step into the grass still lands) and opens again on the wild animal.
- **You always go first.** Pick an attack and a level, answer the puzzle, then the wild animal answers with one of its attacks. Facing an animal its own size or bigger it sometimes misses ("It missed."); a smaller one it never misses.
- **Harder sum, bigger hit.** Every attack has its own level, easy, medium or hard, shown on its tile with a star badge of the damage it does there; the card on the right shows what kind of maths it asks (+ − × ÷ √, ? for a missing number, 2 4 6 for a pattern, and in words), what each of its three levels hits for, and "That would tire it out!" when that hit would win the battle, and the wild animal's HP bar shows, lighter, the part it would take off. "Correct!" shows the badge again, and the hit lands with a burst beside the wild animal's box: the harder the level, the bigger the burst. The words are the attack's own three steps, not a scale shared by every animal: a bear's easy Crush is still a very hard sum. Each level asks one step harder than the one below it ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Animals" has the ladder). A bear's Roar, Maul and Crush ask at the same three difficulties, and Crush hits hardest.
- **A wrong answer misses** ("Not quite!", then "Missed! The wild Rabbit shrugs it off."). The right answer is not shown.
- **Picking an attack can't be undone.** Leaving the puzzle is not possible; typing anything wrong counts as a miss.
- **Leash**: the round Leash button says how likely a catch is right now, with a ring of the same colour round it: "Good chance!" (green, one in two or better), "Maybe" (amber, one in five or better) or "Hard to catch" (red). It follows the real odds, so it changes as the animal's HP drops and depends on the species: a rabbit at full HP is "Hard to catch" and at 2 HP a "Good chance!"; a frog is slippery, "Maybe" from 8 HP down and a "Good chance!" only at 2 HP or less; a fox turns "Maybe" below about a third of its HP and "Good chance!" only at 1 HP; a bear is "Hard to catch" all the way down. A throw that breaks free costs your turn. One that holds is a party: the animal hops for joy with a spin, stars twinkle round it, confetti bursts round it and two poppers shoot confetti up the sides of the scene; then the card shows its name in big coloured letters over a burst of rays. A win gets a hop, a few stars, and stars round "You won!". There is no limit to your team: every animal you catch joins it.
- **Run** always works and costs nothing.
- **Switch** sends in another animal ("Come back, Squirrel!", "Go, Rabbit!"). It costs your turn: the wild animal has a go at the newcomer straight away. It is greyed with only one animal in your party ("You need a second animal to switch. Catch one with the leash!") and when nobody else can step in: everyone else is tired ("Your other animals are tired."), or can't fight where the battle is (out on the water "Your other animals can't swim.", on land "Your other animals live in the sea.").
- **When your animal is tired** (0 HP) and someone else who can fight there is still standing, the party list comes up: "Squirrel is tired. Who goes next?" Pick anyone who isn't tired and can fight there (out on the water, one that swims; on land, not a sea animal). That pick is free (the wild animal doesn't get a turn), and then you choose what to do.
- **Win, catch, or run**: a card says what happened; press Enter to walk on from the same tile. HP you lost stays lost.
- **Lose** (all your animals that can fight there are tired; out on the water, all that swim, whoever still stands in the boat; up in the air, all your birds): "Good try!" and "Your animals need a rest. The witch doctor will make them feel all better!" — press Enter and you are back where the battle was, facing the same way, and nobody is healed. Walk (or sail) to a witch doctor's tent: the bottom line says "Your animals are tired. Walk to the witch doctor's tent!" ("Sail to the witch doctor's tent!" out on the water) for as long as no animal of yours can fight, nothing jumps out of the grass meanwhile, nobody walks behind you, and a cream disc with a little tent and a coral tip sits at the edge of the screen, pointing to the nearest tent you can get to (on foot, round the water and through the trees you chopped, or with the boat across the water too) until the tent is in plain sight (a tent half off the screen, or behind the bottom line or your cards, keeps its disc, a little way before it). The disc never goes under the bottom line, your cards or the touch controls: it moves in along its line, towards you, until they are clear of it, so right after a loss, with two lines at the bottom, it stands a little higher. Up on the glider it keeps pointing at the tent it had when you took off. In World 1, from the reed by the start the tent at (5, 7) is 8 steps away: Right eight times to (5, 6), then Down. The first heal ends it all: the line and the disc go, and the healed animal comes out behind you. If no tent is within 200 steps where you lose and you have no paraglider, a witch doctor comes instead: "A witch doctor came by and looked after your animals. Everyone feels better!", and everyone is fit where you stand.
- **Lost at sea with a walker in the boat** (a squirrel sat it out while the otter tired): you stay out on the water, nothing more comes out of the deep water (nobody who swims is standing), and no bottom line or disc, since the squirrel can still battle on land: land and the grass is the squirrel's again.
- **On a phone held sideways** a battle fits the short screen: Leash, Switch and Run stand in a column beside the attacks, each attack's level word sits under its name, the story line is at the top right over your animal's box, and when you tap Go! the sum and its number pad take the whole bottom of the screen until the turn is over. An attack's card leaves out its sentence there; the pictures say it.

## The witch doctor

- **Finding one**: every world's start has a tent at most 12 steps away (§ The world). In World 1 (`?new`) it is at (5, 7): walk 7 tiles right, all on grass, to (5, 6), then press Down: you bump into the tent and face it. (From the left, (4, 7) is tall grass.) Standing next to a tent without facing it is not enough: press the arrow toward it first.
- **He waves**: the witch doctor stands in front of every tent in a floppy purple hat, his staff in his hand and his pot bubbling on the fire beside him, never quite still. Come within two tiles of him and he hops and waves, turned your way (not when the tent is between you). He does it once, and again only after you have been more than three tiles away: walk off and back to see it again. With reduced motion he moves a third as much, and still waves. He is scenery: only the tent blocks the way.
- **Talking**: press Enter while facing the tent. A card opens at the bottom of the screen with the witch doctor's line and your tokens at the top, three tabs (Heal, Set free, Shop) over a list on the left, and a puzzle area on the right. It opens on Heal. Left and Right change the tab. Your party cards in the corner go away while it is open, and walking waits. The lists are grouped by kind of animal and scroll when they are long; an edge with more past it fades out.
- **Healing**: pick an animal who is hurt or tired and solve its puzzle: it, and every other hurt animal of its kind, is back to full HP ("Well done! Rabbit and 1 more feel all better!", their rows light up green, a green "+N" pops over each and stars sparkle along the bars). One puzzle heals one kind of animal, however many of it are hurt. The puzzle is a kind the animal's own attacks ask (so a frog gets a number pattern or a times table), and harder for fiercer animals (each species' difficulty is in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Knock-out and healing").
- **A wrong answer costs nothing**: "Not quite! Let's try another one." and a different puzzle, as many times as it takes. The right answer is not shown.
- **Fit animals** are shown greyed and can't be picked. With nobody hurt the witch doctor says "Hello! Your animals are all fit and happy." and Enter just says bye.
- **Setting animals free**: on Set free, pick the animals to set free (tired ones too: the witch doctor makes them better first). Each row shows the animal's HP bar, with "tired" in it at 0 HP. An animal brings tokens by its size, a sea animal as much as a land animal of its size ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Tokens and the witch doctor's shop" has the amounts): the right-hand side says it for the highlighted one, and once something is picked it keeps the running total ("40 animals picked: 240 tokens."). A kind of several has its own row on top ("Fox ×40 +240"), which picks the whole kind in one go. You always keep one animal that isn't tired and walks on land (§ The sea animals, "The witch doctor keeps a walker"): the last one won't pick, and its check box is dashed and faded so you see it first ("Fox stays with you.", "Keep one animal that can walk with you!"); picking a whole kind that holds the last walkers keeps its first one, and its row counts only the ones who go ("Fox ×40 +234", "39 of them bring 234 tokens.") (keep a tired one and it stays tired; heal it first if you want it to go and the other to stay). "Set them free" asks "Say bye bye to Fox and Rabbit?" with "No, not now" lit first; "Yes, bye bye!" asks the sum: "You have 12 tokens. You get 8 more. How many will you have?". The right answer waves them goodbye ("Bye bye, Fox! It feels much better now.") and counts the tokens in; a wrong one says "Not quite! Try again." with the same sum. Back, Escape or another tab puts the sum away, and nobody leaves.
- **The shop**: the tools on sale, with their pictures, prices ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Tokens and the witch doctor's shop") and what they do: the axe chops trees, the pickaxe breaks rocks, the boat sails on water, the paraglider glides over trees, rocks and water, the harness rides on big animals. **Today all five are on sale**: the axe and the pickaxe (see § Trees and rocks), the boat (see § The boat), the paraglider (see § The paraglider) and the harness (see § The harness). A boat or a paraglider bought grows onto your back as you leave. Buying asks "You have 23 tokens. The axe costs 8. How many will you have left?"; the right answer hands it over ("Here is your axe! You have 15 tokens left."), a wrong one asks the same sum again. A tool you own, or can't pay for yet, gives a little shake, and the card says why. One of each is all you can have.
- **Your tokens** show beside the witch doctor's line, and in the world's top right corner, with the puzzles you have solved, the tools you own and the world you are in under them. They are saved with the game.
- **Leaving**: Bye (Enter on it from the list, or a tap on it from the list or a puzzle; never while a beat plays or the confirm is up) or Escape, which works at any time: it goes back a step, and from the list or during a beat it says bye. The witch doctor says "Bye! Come back any time." on the bottom line.
- **On a phone held sideways** the card takes nearly all of the screen, and a puzzle and its number pad take the whole card: the sum, the answer, Back and every key in view, the list, the tabs and Bye out of the way until you tap Back. Answer right and the list comes back for the heal's stars (or the goodbye, or the tool's tick), with the sum, your answer and "Correct!" beside it.
- **A long nickname** (twelve W's) in the witch doctor's line goes on under the badge and the tokens when it doesn't fit between them, never under the tokens, and so does any line that would take more than two lines between them. A very long one (three animals named with twelve W's going home) gets a row of its own under them, in smaller letters.
- **Answer right** and, while the reward plays (the heal's stars, the goodbye, the tokens, the tool's tick), the puzzle shows only the sum, your answer and "Correct!": the number pad, the story and Back are gone until the list is back (Escape still says bye meanwhile).
- **A crowded card packs itself**: when what the right-hand side says would not fit (a long line from the witch doctor over it, twelve-W names, a five-digit sum), its lines move closer, then its words get a size smaller, then its hints (the keys, "Tap the answer, then OK", "Tap another animal to help it instead") go, and last the sum gets a little smaller. Back, OK and the tabs are always whole, at every size, in both languages.

## Puzzles solved

- **The count** shows in the world's top right corner, right under your tokens (beside them on a phone held sideways, where the tools show as pictures alone): a green tick and "312 puzzles" ("312 opgaver" in Danish). Every right answer adds one, wherever it was: an attack in a battle or a friendly match, a heal at the witch doctor, the sum when you set animals free or buy a tool. A wrong answer adds nothing, and the count never goes down. It is saved with the game, goes with you to every world, and a new game starts at 0.
- **It shows only while you explore.** In a battle or at the witch doctor the corner is hidden; the count is already higher when you come back out.
- **One sum, one puzzle.** Setting a whole group of animals free is one sum, so it counts one, however many go.

## Your party

The cards in the top-left corner are your party, one card per kind, in battle order. You start with the animal you picked on the title, at full HP (a Squirrel 20 of 20, a Rabbit 22 of 22, a Frog 21 of 21), named or not; a throwaway game (`?new`) starts with a Squirrel. Every animal you catch joins your party, with the HP it had when the leash landed, however many you have: behind the others of its kind, or on a new card at the bottom when it is the first of its kind. A card of one animal shows its name and HP; a card of several is a stack, "Rabbit ×4", with how many are ready, how many are tired soon (a fifth of their HP or less left, their bar red) and how many are tired ("2 ready · 1 tired soon · 1 tired", or "All ready", "All tired soon", "All tired"), and a slim bar of all their HP together. The pause menu's rows say the same. Point at a stack with the mouse, or tap it, and its animals show beside it with their names and HP (tap it again to close it; walking closes it too). An animal at 0 HP is greyed out with a "tired" tag and sits out battles, and a card whose animals are all tired is greyed too. Animals heal at a witch doctor's tent (one puzzle for each kind of animal hurt); a lost battle heals nobody, unless no tent is near (§ Battles). The witch doctor also sets free the animals you don't keep, for tokens (§ The witch doctor). Reloading changes nothing: the party comes back exactly as it was, in the same order, with the same names and the same animal first.

- **Who goes first**: the first animal that isn't tired and can fight where you are (on land not a sea animal, out on the water one that swims), in the first card that has one; that card is outlined in orange and tagged "goes first" (with two or more cards), and on the open card the animal itself is. It steps into the next battle. If it gets tired in a battle you pick who steps in, and afterwards the first animal down that isn't tired goes first. Press a card's number (1–9), click a card of one animal, or pick "Go first" in the pause menu, to move that card to the top: its first animal standing goes first. Click or tap an animal on an open card, or pick it in the pause menu and "Go first", and its card goes to the top with that animal at its front. The numbers on the cards follow the order, so the card you picked becomes 1 and the others move down one.
- **Moving and naming**: drag a card up or down the column to put your kinds in another order (with a mouse, just drag; on a tablet, hold the card still for half a second until it lifts, then drag, since a quick swipe scrolls the column instead; let go of a lifted card without moving it and it counts as a tap). To reach a place out of view, hold the card at the column's top or bottom edge: the column keeps scrolling under it. The card at the top leads, unless all its animals are tired: then the first animal standing below it does. In the pause menu (Escape) a card moves up or down too, and a card of several opens to list its animals, each of which can move up or down within its card, a tired one too — a tired animal on top is skipped, and the tag stays with the first one standing. "New name" gives an animal a nickname, which the cards, the menu and battles all use ("Go, Pip!").
- **A big team**: the column scrolls with a finger or the mouse wheel when the cards don't fit (with the touch controls on, above the D-pad), an open card's list scrolls, and so do the battle's switch list and the witch doctor's list, which keep the highlighted animal in view.
- **What a name can be**: up to 12 letters (any alphabet, so "Søren" and "राम" work; an accent Unicode keeps separate from its letter, like a Hindi vowel sign, counts as a letter, and a few rarer alphabets keep their letters but lose their accents), digits, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and dots. Emoji and other symbols are left out when you press Enter, the text-generator tricks (underlines, strike-throughs, circled or fancy letters) come out as plain letters, extra spaces are squeezed, and a name with no letter or digit left — empty, spaces, only emoji — means no nickname: the animal is called by its species again. The name box shows "It will be called …" before you press Enter whenever the name will come out different from what you typed.

**Your lead decides what comes out of the grass.** Wild animals size up the animal that goes first:

- Its own size comes out most, the sizes next to it less, and every size living there can come out, bar the very biggest near home (next bullet). The animals of each size weigh a bell around your lead's ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters" has the rule and the shares). With a fox in front near home in the meadow or the forest, about 1 battle in 3 is a small animal and 1 in 15 a tier-3 one (a red deer, or in the forest a wild boar or an eagle-owl too).
- Near home the bigger sizes are held back: one size bigger than your lead now and then, two sizes bigger hardly ever, and four sizes bigger never ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters" has the numbers): within 32 tiles of the start (as the crow flies) a small animal in front never meets a bear, a moose, a bison, a whale or an orca. They grow commoner the further out you go, until a size bigger is as likely as a size smaller.
- It starts a battle as often whoever leads ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters" has the chance): the same steps as with the starter, only the animal differs. No grass is ever quiet.
- Where nothing of your lead's size lives, the nearest sizes come out: with a wolf or a bear in front the meadow, whose biggest animal is the red deer, is mostly red deer. Everywhere else every size lives since the big animals came (#89): with a bear the river is mostly moose, the forest bears, moose and bison, the mountains bears.
- A visitor is never commoner than an animal of its size that lives where it visits, but for the ground: with woods all round a reed, the squirrels that visit it outnumber the river's own frogs. Near home, with a wolf in front, the sea eagle comes up the hills, the one animal its size that doesn't live there, about as often as each of the wolves, lynxes, wolverines and golden eagles that do: 1 battle in 6 each (#136; it used to be 6 battles in 10). With a red deer in front the mute swan comes up the hills the same way, 1 battle in 5, as often as the eagle-owl living there.

To meet smaller animals more often, put a smaller animal in front: press its card's number, drag its card to the top, or pick "Go first" in the pause menu. A lost battle heals nobody and keeps the order, so once the witch doctor has healed the first card, it leads again.

## The animal book

- **Opening it**: Escape (or Menu), then Up once, or a tap on "Animal book" at the top right of the menu ("9/50": the kinds caught, of all there are in the land you are in). Every kind of the land has a card, small animals first.
- **A page per land** (#191): once you can fly to another land (today only in a `?lands` or `?land=arctic` game), a tab per land sits over the cards: Up from the top row of cards onto the tabs, then Left and Right flip the page, Down goes back to the cards; or tap a tab. The book opens on the page of the land you are in, and its counts, "set free" among them, are that page's land's.
- **What goes in**: a kind is seen the moment a battle with it starts: run away at once and it is in the book with its name and figure, faded. It is caught when your leash catches one, and stays caught for good: set free at the witch doctor's, in another world, after a reload. It is **set free** (a rose heart on the card's top left, and one more in "3 / 50 set free" beside the title) the moment the witch doctor's sum for a hand-over is answered right: one animal of a kind is enough, and the rest of the kind can stay on the team. A wrong answer, Back or Bye sets nothing free. Your starter and every animal in your team count as caught. A friendly match adds nothing to it, and nor does watching a friend's battle from nearby.
- **A game from before the book** gets back every kind in its team as caught, and the animal of a battle it was saved in as seen. Kinds set free or run from before then are a "?" until you meet one again.
- **A game from before the hearts** (saved before #191's first step) counts every kind in its book that is not on its team as set free, run-from ones included: a free head start. A kind you meet only later has to be set free the usual way.
- **Exploit: a tab left open from before the hearts.** Until it meets a save of the new version, an old tab keeps saving the old way, and every such save counts its kinds met and not on the team as set free when the new version reads it: running from a wolf there earns the wolf's heart. Setting one fox of two free there earns nothing (a fox is still on the team).
- **Filling the hearts fast, to look at them**: `?party=fox,fox,rabbit,wolf&tokens=0` (a throwaway game), walk to the witch doctor's tent, and on Set free hand one fox over: the fox gets its heart at the right answer, and stays on the team with its other fox.
- **Filling it fast, to look at it**: `?party=` with many kinds (`?party=squirrel,rabbit,frog,fox,otter,deer,wolf,bear,crab,whale`, a throwaway game saved nowhere) starts with every one of them caught. For a kind seen and not caught, walk the reed (§ Finding a battle fast) and run from what comes out.
- **Hidden**: Enter, Space or a tap on the lit card makes its animal hop. The figures are drawn the first time the book opens in a page, one a frame, so they pop in.

## Other players

- **Who you see**: everyone in your world who is playing, not at the title. Within 24 tiles you see them walk, with their name over them and their animal behind; everyone further is on the pause menu's Who's here list (the 50 nearest). Everyone you can't see is pointed to by an arrow at the edge of the screen, for the four nearest ways: friends who are the same way share one arrow, with their names beside it, nearest first (four, or three and "+2 more"; on a phone held sideways two, or one and "+2 more"). The arrows go round the corners' panels and the touch controls, so none is ever under one: a friend down and to the left, past the D-pad, has an arrow just over or beside it. Nobody in another world, ever.
- **What you see them doing**: a bubble over the name while they battle a wild animal (an amber "!"), are at the witch doctor (a heart), have the menu open (two bars: the pause menu, and the account card or the save card too) or play a friendly match (a star). No bubble: walking about, or up in the air with the paraglider, where you see them glide (their canopy in their shirt's colour, high over the tiles they fly, never walking on water) and Who's here says "flying".
- **Watching a friend's battle**: stand near someone who steps onto tall grass (or meets a sea animal) and their battle pops out beside them: their animal and the wild one face each other in front of them, with their kinds' names ("Rabbit", never a nickname) and small HP bars. Over their name a thought bubble shows the sum they are working out ("7 × 8 = ?", never the answer), dots while they choose; a right answer pops it with a green tick, a wrong one wobbles it. Every hit floats its damage up ("−10") and makes the friend do a little double jump; a miss puffs; a leash's rope flies and holds with sparkles or is shaken off; a switch puffs; a tired animal lies down. At the end the winner jumps with a spin, then the scene clears and their animal follows them again. Walk up in the middle of it and you see it at once. A friendly match between two others shows the same way, the two animals in front of the two players, the bubble over whoever's turn it is. No name covers an animal's tag: with a lynx, a moose or a bison in front, the player's name (and bubble) sits higher, and two tags side by side move apart; a friend walking past a battle has their name float up over its tags as they pass. Nothing of this is on your own battle screen, and nobody watching changes anything in the battle.
- **Where a battle is drawn**: across the screen from the friend first (left or right of them), on ground an animal could stand on (on the water at sea); only when there is no room that way does it go up or down the screen. At most six battles show at once, the nearest; a seventh shows when one of those ends or you walk nearer it.
- **Go to**: Escape, Who's here, a name, Enter: you are next to them in a poof, facing them, however far away they were; "You're next to Ada!". No step is taken, so nothing jumps out even on tall grass, and the next walk meets what it would have. Out at sea or on an island without a boat it can't be done ("There's no room next to Ada right now."): you are never put where you couldn't walk away from. Only from the menu, so only while exploring.
- **Notes at the top**: "Ada is here!" when someone comes into your world, "Bo went home" ten seconds after someone leaves it; nothing about the players already there when you come, and nothing when someone reloads and is back within ten seconds.
- **Players walk through each other**, and through trees and rocks they cleared and you did not: what a kid clears is their own.
- **Two windows of the same game**: the one you play in last is the one the others see; the other says "You're playing in another window." and comes back when you play in it again.
- **Friendly matches**: stand within two steps of someone exploring on land (not in the boat), and "Challenge Bo" shows above the message line: C or a tap asks. They have 20 seconds; walking off, a wild animal, the witch doctor or the menu is a no. Up in the air with the paraglider there is no button, and a friend in the air can't be asked (their invite is declined at once). Two players asking each other at once start at once. In the match their puzzle shows on your screen while they think (never its answer), and their animals are "Bo's Rabbit". Nothing of your game changes but the puzzles you solved, which count.
- **A match waits 30 seconds** for a player whose page dropped out (closed, reloaded, the connection lost): back in time (Continue on the title after a reload), it goes on where it was; else the other wins. A kid who sits on their turn is asked "Still there?" after 90 seconds and loses after 3 minutes without a key.
- **A new version mid-match ends it** with no winner ("The game is updating."): Play again, once the friend is back, asks them again, and their Play again is the yes. A server that crashes mid-match ends it the same way, and the card says "The game restarted." (the page knows by the server's run it finds on coming back); "It ended while you were away." is only for a page that was gone longer than the 30 seconds. On a result whose friend already left (or went back), either kind of restart leaves the result as it is.
- **Free at once after a match**: leave it (or go back to exploring from its end) and a friend can challenge you straight away, and you them, even while the other kid still looks at their result. A Rematch? waiting for you is answered no the moment you ask or are asked by someone.
- **Back to exploring sticks, even offline**: pressed while "Getting the match back…" is up, the result doesn't come back with the connection, not after a reload and not in another tab of the game. Your friend reads "Ada went back to exploring." once you are back online, or when your 30 seconds run out.
- **Back to exploring beats a Rematch? at the same moment**: ask for a rematch, then go back just as your friend presses Rematch? too, and no rematch starts for you. If it had just begun on their screen, it flicks straight back to their result, with "Ada went back to exploring.".
- **Where to see it**: two browsers (or a browser and a private window, which is another player) on the same address, each with a named game in the same world. With the game on this machine: `pnpm players` ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Looking at the game).

## Hidden behaviour

- **A tap is always one step.** A key press shorter than a frame still moves you one tile.
- **Mashing is capped.** At most two taps are queued, so mashing a key does not queue a long walk.
- **Two keys held: the newest wins.** Hold Right, then press Up as well, and you walk up. Let go of Up and you walk right again. There is no diagonal movement.
- **Walking into something turns you to face it** without moving you.
- **Switching windows stops you.** If you tab away with a key held, or the page is hidden, you stop walking and any queued taps are dropped. So does pressing Ctrl, Cmd or Alt mid-walk; press the arrow again to walk on. A finger on the D-pad lets go the same way when you switch to another app or window: its arrow goes out, and a finger still resting there when you come back walks nothing until you lift it and press again.
- **`?puzzle=clock&d=5`: the puzzle preview** (#191). A page of its own, no game behind it: a puzzle of any kind at any difficulty in a card the battle panel's size, the Arctic's kinds (`thermometer`, `kroner`, `fraction`, `shape`, `barchart`, `clock`, `balance`) above all, which no animal asks yet. Type and Enter to answer; Enter or Space again for the next; ← → the difficulty, ↑ ↓ the kind, Escape another puzzle (the arrows on top do the same with a finger). The address follows, so a reload keeps the kind and the difficulty. `?lang=da` and the touch controls work there too.
- **A clock takes either reading**: for a quarter past three, 3:15, 15:15, 3.15 and 315 are all right.
- **Letting go always stops you.** A key let go with Shift held down stops the walk like any other.
- **Keys don't leak between walking and battling.** An arrow still held down when a battle starts does nothing in the battle until you press it again, and keys pressed in a battle never become steps.
- **The message line forgets what you read before a battle.** A line up when a battle or a friendly match starts ("Welcome back!", say) is gone when you are back; one said during it (the witch doctor's after a lost battle) waits for you and shows its full 5 seconds.
- **One press, one screen.** A key counts on the screen that is up when you press it, and only there: the Enter on Continue, on the result card or on the witch doctor's Bye never also talks to the witch doctor, however slowly the game starts.
- **Nor does the D-pad.** A finger still on the D-pad when a battle, the witch doctor or the menu takes the screen walks nothing there, lifted over the new screen it presses nothing (not the battle row under your thumb), and it walks nothing when you are back until you lift it and press again.
- **Taps wait where keys wait.** A quiet moment, a turn playing: a tap does nothing there, as Enter does nothing. A tap counts only on what the finger went down on, on the screen it went down on: touch a row while the turn plays and lift once the menu is back, and nothing happens.
- **Two thumbs at once.** Each finger counts on its own: walk with the D-pad and tap Talk or Menu with the other thumb.
- **Space is a tap or a hold, with the paraglider.** A press shorter than a quarter of a second is a tap, and talks (chops, breaks) as the key comes up rather than as it goes down; a longer one flies. Without the paraglider Space talks as it goes down, exactly as Enter. Holding Space through a tent flies over it; tap it to talk.
- **Letting go always lands.** Space up, Fly lifted, Ctrl, Cmd or Alt pressed, another window, the page hidden: each lets go of the glider, and you come down on the ring. Pressing Space again in the air changes nothing: once let go, you are coming down.
- **Slow taps count; a double tap on something that moves counts once.** A tap has no time limit: a finger resting on a button for seconds still taps it when it lifts where it landed. A second click or tap within 0.7 s on the spot the first one's button just left (a card sent to the top, a menu closed) presses nothing there, so a double click on an animal never picks the one that slid into its row. A double tap on a button that stays put (Move down) still presses it twice.
- **A key hides the touch controls; a touch brings them back.** Typing a name with the tablet's own keyboard hides nothing.
- **A tablet or a phone held upright**, once the touch controls are on, shows "Turn your screen sideways to play!" over everything, and takes no taps until it is turned.
- **No zooming or scrolling on a tablet**: pinching, double-tapping, pulling down or pressing long on the game does nothing (the name boxes still select and edit).
- **Nothing hides under the notch.** On an iPad the D-pad, Talk, Menu, the message line and the battle's cards sit above the home indicator; on a phone held sideways they keep clear of the notch too, while the world fills the whole screen.
- **No WebGL, no game, but a kind card.** A browser that cannot draw the world (WebGL switched off, hardware acceleration off, an iPhone or iPad in Lockdown Mode) shows "This browser can't draw Animath's world." in the language the game would start in; with JavaScript off, a card says so in English and Danish.
- **An iPad needs iPadOS 15 or newer.** An older iPad, iPhone or browser (Safari before 15, say an iPad on iPadOS 14) shows "This iPad is a bit too old for Animath." in English and Danish at once, instead of the game. Updating it, where it can be updated, is the way in.
- **Attack levels are remembered, per attack and per kind of animal, until you reload.** Set a squirrel's Nut Toss to hard and it is hard in the next battle too, and for every squirrel you have; a rabbit's attacks keep their own. After a switch the menu starts on the new animal's first attack.
- **Switching can't be used to stall.** Every switch you choose gives the wild animal a turn, so switching back and forth only wears your party down.
- **Keys wait while a battle turn plays.** From your answer until the menu comes back, every key is ignored.
- **A new choice waits for a quiet moment.** Every menu, list or card that asks you to choose — the battle menu (after the opening lines and after every turn), both switch lists, the result card, the witch doctor's list (when it opens and after every heal) and his confirm, "Start a new game?", the title's name box, the starters, the starter's name box, the save card and the account card, a friendly match's invite, its menus, "Leave the match?" and its result card, and Talk in the world — takes a pick only once it has been up a quiet moment ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode, "A quiet moment", has its length) with no Enter, Space or number pressed. Every one of those presses starts the moment again, so mashing never picks, at any pace, however long you keep it up: mashing through the text can't pick the next attack, skip "You won!" or talk to the witch doctor by accident. Stop for a moment, press once, and it picks. Go! and the result card's button stay dimmed until then. The arrows and Escape work at once. The title's own menu and the pause menu take a press at once: everything there can be undone.
- **The witch doctor's card waits too.** It ignores every key but Escape while "Correct!" or "Not quite!" is showing. A key held down when it opens does nothing until pressed again, and keys pressed at the witch doctor never become steps.
- **Messages fade.** A line on the bottom of the screen stays for 5 seconds of walking about, then fades. A line said during a battle or at the witch doctor waits for you: after a battle the result's line is still there when you are back in the world. "Walk up to a tent…" goes as soon as you face a tent, where the prompt takes over. A line about who goes first also goes at once when a battle or the witch doctor changes your party.
- **The controls hint goes away** after your first 5 steps. Bumping into things doesn't count, however long you hold the key. It comes back for 5 steps after every reload.
- **The same walk meets the same animals.** Encounters are decided by how many steps you have taken in this game, so two new games walked the same way meet the same animals at the same steps (see § Finding a battle fast). The count is saved, so a reload carries on with the next step: it never replays the animals behind you. The witch doctor's puzzles work the same way.
- **Leading zeros are fine**: "09" is the same answer as "9".
- **Sound waits for your first key.** The game is silent until you press a key or touch the screen, because browsers allow sound only after one. Escape and Shift don't count; the first arrow, letter, number or Enter wakes it, and so does the first tap or click.
- **Sound off is remembered** on this device, across reloads. The first time, it is on.
- **Less motion if your computer asks for it.** With "reduce motion" turned on in the system's settings, the game moves less: the world behind the title holds still, a soft dim instead of the circle that closes into a battle, a lower hop and smaller arm swings when walking, two butterflies instead of six, smaller lunges and shakes, fewer confetti pieces, numbers and sparkles that fade in place, and Talk glows without pulsing. Turning it on or off while playing works at once; the spare butterflies fly away first.
- **Walking waits in the pause menu.** Opening it mid-step lets that step land, then nothing moves until the menu closes. An arrow still held when the menu opens or closes does nothing until you press it again, and a battle can never start while the menu is open. A reload with the menu open comes back to the world, the menu closed; a name typed but not yet saved with Enter is gone.
- **Danish or English, everywhere.** The game starts in Danish if your browser prefers Danish, else in English. Language in the pause menu, or on the title, switches every word at once — the animals' and attacks' names too ("Ræv", "Nøddekast") — even with a line still on the bottom of the screen, which changes language with the rest, and the game remembers the choice on this computer. `?lang=da` or `?lang=en` in the address picks one for that visit without changing what is remembered. Puzzles are the same sums in both languages, and so is typing the answer. A name you give an animal is never translated: "Nøddi" is Nøddi in both.
- **The title waits for you.** Nothing happens behind it: no step, no battle, no save. The world drifts past and one of each kind in your team (or, the first time, the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog) breathes beside the trainer, but they are scenery.
- **Mashing Enter never starts over.** On the title with a game saved, the cursor starts on Continue, so a mashed Enter carries on your game. "Start a new game?" starts on "No, go back" and takes no Enter until a quiet moment, so a mashed Enter leaves it up, answering nothing; starting over takes Down, then Enter. The starters and the name box wait for a quiet moment too, so an Enter mashed on New game can't pick a starter or skip its name unseen.
- **`?zoo` shows every animal.** Open `http://localhost:5180/?zoo` and one of each species stands in a grid round the start, in catalog order, row by row: squirrel, rabbit, frog, fox, otter, red deer, wolf, bear, then the small animals of the countryside, shrew, wood mouse, brown rat, hedgehog, mole, common lizard, common toad, robin, stag beetle, roe deer, badger, pine marten, stoat, adder, grey heron, tawny owl, raccoon, beaver, buzzard, then the big ones, wild boar, mute swan, eagle-owl, lynx, wolverine, golden eagle, sea eagle, moose, European bison, then the sea animals, crab, starfish, turtle, dolphin, octopus, humpback whale, moon jellyfish, plaice, lion's mane, lobster, harbour seal, porpoise, grey seal and orca, standing on the ground like the rest (only the zoo puts them on land; the whole first row and four of the second stand out on the lake, and the last row is the octopus, the humpback whale and the eight sea animals after it) — facing you. The rows are two tiles apart, ten animals to a row, with your own row free between the second and the third, so the whole grid fits a 1024×768 screen. They are scenery: you walk straight through them and nothing else changes. It skips the title and plays a throwaway game, like `?new`: nothing is saved. Start screen, then Continue or a new game, finds the same line-up standing where it was, and no second one. It exists to check the figures, not to play with. `?zoo=tired` lays the same line-up down to rest, as a tired animal lies in a battle, with its z's.
- **`?steps=20` brings the save card every 20 steps** (5 to 1000) instead of every 1,000, for the "Keep your animals safe!" card a guest's game shows (§ Saving). The game is an ordinary saved one, not a throwaway: the card is only ever for a game that is saved. A game already played in this browser gets its next card within 20 steps too (#156).
- **`?debug` shows where you are on the grid**: the engine's grid position and the way you face (`5, 6 · down`) in the top-right corner, `y` growing down the screen and counted from the grid's own origin, not your spawn (the bottom-right corner's coordinates are x 7 · y 0 there, in World 1), and under it, once anything has made a sound, the last four sounds the game asked for, newest last (`♪ move · confirm · correct · hit`, with ✕ while sound is off).
- **`?items=` starts owning tools**, in a throwaway game: `?items=harness,boat` (item ids, comma-separated; a typo ignores the switch). It combines with the others, e.g. `?party=moose&items=harness,glider`.
- **`?tokens=` and `?shop` are for looking at the shop.** `?tokens=40` starts with 40 tokens (a whole number up to 9999; anything else is ignored), and `?shop` puts every tool on sale, on sale for real or not (today all five are on sale anyway). Both play a throwaway game like `?new`: nothing is loaded or saved, and the saved game is left alone. An axe or a pickaxe bought there chops and breaks as in a real game (the trees come back with the next page), a boat sails and a paraglider flies (`?tokens=40` is enough to buy either and try it). They combine with the others: `?debug&party=squirrel:5,rabbit:0,fox&tokens=23&shop`.
- **`?lands` opens every land**, in a throwaway game (#191): every land is unlocked and open, built or not. At a witch doctor's, L on his list asks to fly to the next land; the fare is a puzzle of that land's kinds (The Arctic's seven new ones), answered as any puzzle there, and the kid comes down beside a tent of that land in the same world number. The Arctic has its own map (§ The Arctic) and its first animals (#192 wave 1, its small land animals, met in its deep snow) but no shop yet. A first arrival there waits for one of its three starters (the Arctic fox, the Arctic hare, the puffin) with no screen to pick one on yet (step 7): the kid stands by the witch doctor, can't walk, and flies home from him. Without the switch nobody can fly: The Arctic is closed, and the card has no travel option yet (#191 step 7).
- **`?land=arctic` starts in The Arctic**, in a throwaway game, every land open and unlocked as with `?lands`: at its spawn (a tent a few steps away), with its first starter, the Arctic fox, or with `?party=` a party of its animals (`?land=arctic&party=puffin,raven:10`), to look at them at home: its deep snow holds its animals, its witch doctor heals them with the Arctic's picture puzzles, and the animal book opens on its page. Nordland, never visited there, has no party of the kid's. Without the switch nobody can fly: The Arctic is closed, and the card has no travel option yet (#191 step 7).
- **`?party=` picks your starting party**, for looking at screens that need one: `?party=squirrel:5,rabbit:0,fox` starts with a squirrel at 5 HP, a tired rabbit and a fox at full HP, in a throwaway game like `?new`: nothing is loaded or saved, and the saved game is left alone. Species are the catalog ids (`squirrel`, `rabbit`, `frog`, `fox`, `otter`, `deer` for the red deer, `wolf`, `bear`, the small animals of the countryside `shrew`, `wood-mouse`, `brown-rat`, `hedgehog`, `mole`, `common-lizard`, `common-toad`, `robin`, `stag-beetle`, `roe-deer`, `badger`, `pine-marten`, `stoat`, `adder`, `grey-heron`, `tawny-owl`, `raccoon`, `beaver`, `buzzard`, the big ones `wild-boar`, `mute-swan`, `eagle-owl`, `lynx`, `wolverine`, `golden-eagle`, `white-tailed-eagle` for the sea eagle, `moose`, `european-bison`, and the sea animals `crab`, `starfish`, `turtle`, `dolphin`, `octopus`, `whale` for the humpback whale, `moon-jellyfish`, `plaice`, `lions-mane-jellyfish` for the lion's mane, `lobster`, `harbour-seal`, `harbour-porpoise` for the porpoise, `grey-seal`, `orca`; The Arctic's, #192 wave 1, `arctic-fox`, `arctic-hare`, `puffin`, `arctic-lemming`, `snow-bunting`, `rock-ptarmigan`, `waxwing`, `adelie-penguin`, `snow-petrel`, `arctic-tern`, `king-eider`, `raven`, `barnacle-goose`, `gentoo-penguin`, `chinstrap`, and #192 wave 2, `reindeer`, `snowy-owl`, `great-grey-owl`, `glaucous-gull`, `giant-petrel`, `king-penguin`, `arctic-wolf`, `gyrfalcon`, `emperor-penguin`, `albatross`, `polar-bear`, `musk-ox`), HP is optional (full by default) and clamped to the species, and `*` and a number asks for that many (`rabbit*30`, `rabbit:0*5`), up to 1,000 animals in all. The party starts in bundles, each kind behind its first animal (`rabbit,fox,rabbit` starts with the two rabbits together). Only the animals of the land the game starts in come: Nordland's, or with `?land=arctic` The Arctic's (`?party=fox,puffin` starts with the fox alone). ## Saving

- **Everything is saved as you go**, in this browser: after every step, every battle turn, every catch and every tree chopped or rock broken, and when you close the tab. There is no save button and nothing to wait for. Reload, or come back another day, and the title's Continue puts you where you were, facing the way you were, with the same animals and HP. The message line says "Welcome back!".
- **A reload in the air lands you.** A game saved mid-flight (every tile flown saves, and so does closing the page) is saved as if you had let go right then: Continue puts you on the tile the ring was on, never over water or in a tree, with the tree there chopped if the ring carried the axe.
- **Start screen** (the pause menu's last row) saves the game as it stands and goes back to the title, with no question asked: Continue there carries on from the same step, and the same animals are waiting in the grass.
- **A reload in a battle picks the battle up** (Continue on the title): the same wild animal, the same HP, and the same puzzle if one was up, with its attack lit at its level in the menu beside it, or the list of who steps in after a knock-out (what you had typed is gone, and so are the levels you had picked for the other attacks). The narration starts again with "A wild … appears!", the menu already lighting that attack at its level (#155). A reload after you answered, while the turn is still being told, skips to what happened: the answer already counted.
- **The saved game belongs to the browser and the address.** Another browser, a private window, `localhost` instead of the shared link, or a different link is a different game. A kid who comes back through the same link on the same browser finds their game.
- **The old shared link says the game has moved.** Opened anywhere but https://animath.xyz (or `www.`) and a developer's own machine (`localhost`, a home network's address), the page shows "Animath has moved!" before anything else, every time: the big "Play at animath.xyz" button, Enter or Space goes there in the same tab, where the old address's game is not (another address, another `localStorage`: a game comes over only as an account a grown-up makes). The small "Keep playing here" under it (a click, a tap, or Tab to it and then Enter or Space; Escape does nothing) starts the game at the old address as before, the title first. A developer's machine reached by a name rather than an address (`mac.lan`, a Tailscale name) counts as an old address too: one click on "Keep playing here".
- **A Home Screen icon can be a different game on an iPhone or iPad.** Share → Add to Home Screen gives the game's fox as an icon. On iOS and iPadOS 26 the icon opens as a web app unless "Open as Web App" is switched off as it is added, and a web app keeps its own storage apart from Safari's: it opens on the title with New game only, while the animals are still in Safari. Switched off, the icon opens the game in Safari with everything there. With an account the game comes over: make one in Safari (Menu → Make an account), then log in in the web app; opened there with nothing saved, the title says so.
- **Two tabs or windows of the game.** Both start as the same game. When one meets a wild animal, catches an animal, wins, loses, plays a battle turn, heals or changes the team, the other is behind: it ignores every key. A hidden tab loads the newer game when you switch to it. A window on screen next to the one you play in shows a card, "You kept playing in another window. Let's play! [Enter]", and loads it when you click into that window or on the button, or press Enter or Space. Either way it goes straight back into play, without the title, and says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!" (a tab that was on the title opens the title again, with Continue on the newer game). A window you are using loads by itself at most 3 times a minute; after that it shows the card and waits for Enter, which always works. While a window is behind, typing in its name box shows no letters and a Space there does nothing; Enter still loads the newest game. When one tab starts a new game, the other goes to the title as soon as you look at it, with Continue on the new game. Walking in either is fine, as long as it meets nothing: the one you use carries on from where it is. Nothing a tab did is ever lost to the other one: a game one tab left for a new one is put away with the rest, and a tab that is behind never lets you play on in it.
- **Start a new game**: New game on the title, then your name and a first animal; the game starts in a new home world. With a game saved it asks "Start a new game?" first, and "Yes, new game" puts the saved game away: it is kept in this browser (`animath.save.previous`, then `.2`, `.3`, …; an account's under `animath.account.<name>.save.previous`) and, for an account, on the server, and never deleted, but there is no way in the game to go back to it (the human can: [[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database). Nothing inside the game deletes a save; clearing this site's data in the browser does. `http://…/?new` plays a new game in that tab that is saved nowhere; drop `?new` and the saved game is back, untouched. A private window is also a new game, kept only while the window is open.
- **A browser that blocks site storage** (or has run out of room for the save) plays, but keeps nothing. Every title it opens says so, the one Start screen opens too: "This browser can't keep your game, so it starts new every time." (or "This browser has no room left to keep your game."). There, New game asks "Start a new game?" with "Your game with the Squirrel can't be kept on this page." instead of "is put away safely".
- **A save that won't load** (after an update the game cannot read it) leaves the title with New game only; once you have picked a starter the message line says "Your saved game didn't load, so here is a new one." The old save stays where it was until then, and is then kept aside, not deleted.
- **A save written by a newer version of the game** (a later save format, or an animal this version does not have yet, in the team or in a battle: a tab left open from before an update meets one; a tool it does not have yet is only kept, and the save plays on) is never touched. A page of the old version that finds one, at start, from another tab or on the server, ignores every key and reloads as soon as it is on screen, which fetches the new version. It reloads by itself at most 3 times a minute; after that it shows a card, "A new version of the game is ready." with "Update! [Enter]" ("Tap to update!" on a touch screen), and reloads on the button, Enter or Space. If the page it reloads into is still the old version (an update half done, or rolled back), the card comes back, and nothing plays until the new version is there.
- **An account keeps your game on the server and brings it anywhere.** A guest's game offers one after every 1,000 steps ("Keep your animals safe!": "Save my game" or "Not now"), and the pause menu has "Make an account" and "Log in" (the title has "I have an account → Log in"). Your character's name is the username; a taken one is picked again, and the character takes it. The password needs 4 letters or numbers; "Show" shows it. Making an account takes the game on screen along, and the page starts again, back in it: "Your game is saved! Log in as Nini on any tablet or computer." Logging in on another browser or device brings the game there ("Welcome back, Nini!"), and the guest game in that browser stays where it was. Tab goes between the name and the password; Enter in the name box goes to the password while it is empty, and with a password typed it goes ahead (§ Controls).
- **Log out** (the pause menu, for a player logged in) saves, sends the newest save to the server, and starts again on the title, which says "Your animals are safe!" and "You're logged out. Your game waits in your account." over its first row, **Log in** with the account's name, lit: Enter opens the login card with the name already in it, and the password is all there is to type. The guest game is gone from that browser after making an account (it went into the account), so under Log in the title has New game only, unless there was another guest game (Continue). Logging in again finds the account's game, in this browser too. The next title, after a game, is the usual one; a reload of the title just after the logout is too (the note is read once).
- **A welcome link** (`…/#welcome=…`, from a grown-up who brought a game over from another computer: [[DEVELOPMENT]] § Moving a kid's game to production; `?welcome=` works too) opens on the title with "Welcome back, Aslak! Your animals are waiting. Pick a secret password." over it. The name is the account's and can't be changed there; Tab stays in the password. "Let's play!" (Enter) goes straight into the game where it was, logged in; the name and that password log in anywhere from then on. The link leaves the address as the page opens, and the tab keeps it: a reload, or a tablet bringing the tab back, opens the card again, until "Not now" (Esc) puts it away (open the link again to bring it back). It works once, for 14 days: used, it says "This link has been used already" with "Log in" (Enter) and "Play as a guest" (Esc), "Log in" only while the server can keep an account; too old, "This link is too old now". Opened with `?new` (or another throwaway switch) it does nothing and is not used up. A guest game in the browser is never touched, and comes back after logging out. A grown-up who sets a password with the admin command uses the link up too.
- **Two devices, one account.** Play on both and the one that played more wins: the other takes its game at its next save and says "You played on somewhere else. Here's your newest game!"; what it had played is kept aside in the browser (`animath.account.<name>.save.replaced`), not deleted. A game a newer version of Animath saved on another device is never touched: this one goes to the new version, as any page does that finds one (above). Logged in, its card also has "Log out" (Esc): the guest game, while the new version is not there yet.
- **Wrong password, many times**: after 10 wrong tries from one place in a quarter of an hour, that name waits there ("Lots of tries! Take a little break and try again in 3 minutes."); the kid's own device elsewhere still logs in. A forgotten password is set again by a grown-up with the admin command ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Accounts): there is no email.
- **No server, no problem.** The game saves in the browser whether or not the server is running. An account's game also goes to the server when it can reach it (a guest's never: it lives in the browser alone); if the server is down it tries a few times, then again after the next battle, and never says a word about it. When the server says an account is logged out (a new password, a year away), the game says so once and saves only in the browser until you log in again. With the server down, or on a server whose database has no account tables yet, nothing offers an account: no "Keep your animals safe!" card, no "Make an account" or "Log in" in the menu, no "I have an account" on the title. They appear within about 40 seconds of the server being able to keep accounts, and go as quickly when it stops ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Accounts).
- **After an update that changes how games are saved** (numbered worlds did), Continue carries on exactly where you were, a game from before numbered worlds in World 1; a game with no name asks for yours first, once. The old save is kept as it was too, in `animath.save.upgraded` (and `.2`, `.3`, …; an account's under `animath.account.<name>.save.upgraded`) in this browser and, for an account, on the server.

## Exploits and quirks

- **A friendly match can't be won by cheating, and nothing is won.** The server never sends a puzzle's answer, but every puzzle is plain arithmetic a changed page could work out by itself, and the team a page brings is its own word (a page can bring three bears). A match changes nothing but the kid's own count of puzzles solved, which a page could raise by itself anyway.
- **Asking and taking it back** pops the card up on the other player's screen each time: after Never mind (or a reload while asking), the same player can be asked again 10 seconds later (30 after a no, or no answer).
- **A match can't start from the water**: out in the boat the button says "You're out on the water. Matches are on land."; step ashore first. A nickname with a rude word in it shows to the other player as the animal's kind.
- **Go to is a free trip.** Going to a friend skips the walk however far they are, and lands you wherever they are: out in the wild, next to the biggest animals, or right beside a witch doctor's tent (a tired team goes along as tired, and the disc points from there). A kid who has wandered hundreds of tiles out can go straight back to a friend at home, and a small team can land far out, where fierce animals come out. Nothing is rolled on arrival, so the first danger is the first step onto tall grass.
- **A name is all the others go by.** Two guests can take the same name, and a guest can take the name of someone they know (accounts' names are unique; a guest's is not): the colour of the shirt and cap comes from the name, so two with one name even look alike.
- **Positions are the players' own word.** Anyone who edits their save, or makes their page say so, can stand anywhere within 100,000 tiles of the middle of a world (further out they are simply not seen), and the others will see them there. Nothing is gained by it but a surprise, but a friend who goes to them lands beside them, however far from home that is (on a tile they can walk away from, never inside rock or out at sea). The list says how many steps away they are first.
- **A wild battle seen from outside is its player's own word, too.** The fighter's page tells the server how its battle goes, so a changed page can show the players near it a made-up fight: any animals, any HP within theirs, any sum within five digits, hits that never happened. It can't write words: the bubble shows only a sum the game itself writes from numbers, and the animals go by their kinds (a nickname never shows to anyone watching). It is shown only while that page says it is in a battle, a few steps a second at most. A friendly match seen from outside is the server's own, and can't be faked.
- **A battle beside a friend is laid out on the world's own ground**, as it grew: it keeps off the trees and rocks there even where you (or they) cleared one, since what each kid clears is their own.
- **The boat is a way round the wild.** The shallows never start a battle, and nothing on the water challenges a team with nobody standing who swims, so a kid with the boat can cross a river, or sail round a lake's shore, without a battle; only the deep water has animals, and only for a team with a swimmer. After a lost battle the way to the nearest tent counts the water too, so with the boat the disc may point across a lake.
- **A quiet grass after a sea win.** End a battle at sea with a sea animal standing (win it, run from it or catch the wild one) while every animal of yours that walks is tired, and back on land nothing comes out of the grass and nobody follows, the bottom line says your animals are tired and the disc points to a tent, until a witch doctor makes one of them fit: a free repel. A reload doesn't heal them.
- **A tired team walks in peace.** After a lost battle nothing jumps out of the grass until a witch doctor has helped, so a kid can cross the wildest grass with a tired team and meet nothing. Nothing is gained: a tired team catches nothing either. Reloading heals nothing and forgets nothing.
- **A new world is a witch doctor nearby.** Tired far from a tent? Escape, Worlds, a number you have never been to: a first visit starts at that world's spawn, a witch doctor's tent at most 12 steps away (the disc points to it). Going home, or to a world you have been to, takes you back where you left it, as tired. By design: travel is always free.
- **Lost where no tent is near, a witch doctor comes, unless you can fly.** With no tent within 200 steps of where a battle is lost (walled in by trees, or a stretch of land with no tent that close), or of where a go-to or a trip to another world puts you with a tired team, a witch doctor comes and heals everyone there, if you don't own the paraglider. Walking far from every tent on purpose and then losing is a long way round a short walk (within 700 tiles of World 1's start, no spot you can walk to from there is more than 156 steps from a tent). With the paraglider no witch doctor ever comes: a spot walled in by trees is reached by gliding in, and you glide back out. A reload never heals, a glide never heals, and a trip out of such a spot and back heals nobody either: the team stays tired there, with no disc (no tent is a walk away) and the bottom line saying "Your animals are tired. Fly out to find the witch doctor's tent!" until you glide out.
- **Farming the small ones.** Walking Left and Right on the reed by the start and throwing the leash at every squirrel, rabbit and frog brings about 1.2–1.4 tokens a minute once they are set free (2 each): about twice ordinary play near home, about what a fox in front brings played the ordinary way, and less than half of what farming with the fox brings ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Tokens and the witch doctor's shop" has the model). By design: every catch is still battles and sums.
- **A tired animal brings the same tokens** as a fit one: the witch doctor makes it better before it goes. By design.
- **Small sums count as much as big ones.** The count of puzzles solved goes up by one for every right answer, whatever the puzzle: a squirrel's easy attack counts the same as a bear's hardest one, and an easy heal the same as a big token sum. Easy levels on small animals are the quickest way to a big number. By design: it counts puzzles, not points.
- **A token sum can't be skipped, only put away**: a wrong answer asks the same sum again (unlike a healing puzzle, which changes), and Back, Escape, another tab or Bye puts it away with nothing done. The right answer is never shown.
- **A hard witch doctor's puzzle can be skipped**: answer anything (a wrong answer costs nothing and brings another puzzle), swap to another animal and back, or say bye and talk again. By design: at the witch doctor, nothing is lost by missing. A reload while the witch doctor's card is open closes it; anything healed stays healed, animals set free stay free and a tool bought stays yours, and a sum not yet answered never happened.
- **The save is plain text in the browser.** Anyone who opens the browser's developer tools can read and edit `animath.save` (an account's game: `animath.account.<name>.save`) in the site's local storage: change HP (up to the animal's most: more is cut back when the game loads), add animals, move to any tile you could walk on, set the tokens or the puzzles solved, add a tool, clear any tree or rock (`edits`; water, tents and tall grass stay what they are whatever it says). In the middle of a battle's puzzle it also holds the puzzle's answer. An account's save goes to the server as the browser has it: the server checks that it is a save the game can read, not that it could have been played, so an edited game is kept in the account too. No other player gets anything from it: a friendly match takes the team a page sends, never the saved one, and changes nothing (the first bullet here; [[DEFERRED]] § The server stores whatever save the client sends).
- **A small lead with a big fighter.** The animal that goes first decides how fierce the animals you meet are, but anyone can fight them. Put the Squirrel first, switch to a Bear on the first turn of every battle, and the Squirrel never gets tired, so it stays first: you keep meeting squirrels and rabbits and beat them with Bear-sized hits (and Bear-hard sums). Without switching, the Squirrel would soon be tired and the Bear would go first. Kept on purpose: the Bear's hits need the Bear's sums ([[DECISIONS]] § Gameplay).
- **`?party=`, `?tokens=` and `?items=` are cheats**: anyone who edits the URL can start with a bear, with enough tokens for every tool, or owning every tool. They are there for testing, and the game they start is never saved.
- **A nickname can be anything made of letters**, including another animal's name or "Wild Fox": a squirrel called "Bear" is still a squirrel, and one called "Wild Fox" shows "Wild Fox" in its own status box in battle.
- **The rude-word check on names is short and simple.** It catches the obvious English and Danish words, spelled plainly, in capitals, with digits for letters ("sh1t"), drawn out ("shiiit") or split by a space or a hyphen ("Fu Ck"), and lets through real names with a word inside (Scunthorpe, Hassan, Pikachu). A kid who spells a rude word with look-alike letters from another alphabet gets past it.
- **A path straight through a forest.** With the axe a kid can chop a line through any forest, or clear one bare, and walk where no one could; with the pickaxe, cut a gravel road over the mountains and wander the snowy peaks, which were all rock. The forest's and the mountain's tall grass stays, and so do their animals: who comes out is decided by the world as it was made. By design (the human's son asked for it).
- **A tent walled in by trees can be opened**, and after a lost battle the disc then points there along the path they cut: the way to the nearest tent walks the world as the kid left it.
- **The paraglider is a peaceful way round the wild, for a team with no bird.** No flight starts a battle in the grass or at sea: it skips every tall grass and deep-water tile under it and the one it lands on, so a kid can hop over a reed bed or a stretch of sea, 20 tiles at a time, as often as they like. With no bird standing that is always in peace; with a bird, a wild bird may follow (§ Birds in the air), so leading with a tired bird or none keeps the sky quiet. Each tile flown still counts as a step, so the animals ahead on a walk come in the same order afterwards. By design (#91).
- **The glider can't be stretched.** Letting go late over water or trees carries you only to the first tile you can land on, never past the farthest of the 20 ahead you could land on: a lake of 20 water tiles can't be crossed without the boat however you time it.
- **It lands on trees without the axe's walk.** With the axe (or the pickaxe) a flight can come down in the middle of a forest (a mountain), chopping the tree it lands on, and a kid can hop from stump to stump to where no path reaches, as long as the next landing is within 20 tiles.
- **The way to the witch doctor never uses the glider**: after a lost battle the disc points to the nearest tent on foot, or by boat, as if the kid had no glider, so a tent only reachable by gliding is never the one it points to, and a witch doctor comes only when no tent is a walk (or a sail) away, even if one is a glide away. Gliding there anyway is fine: the witch doctor heals as always.
- **Thousands of chops later, the farthest grow back.** A game keeps about 24 KB of cleared tiles; past that, the ones farthest from the kid grow back a chunk at a time, never the ones on screen. A kid would have to clear thousands of tiles for this, fewer only if they were spread out one to a chunk all over the world.
