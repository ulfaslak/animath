# Invariants

What must stay true at runtime, and the failure that taught it.

**Admission test: could you write a test that fails when this stops being true?** If yes, it belongs here. A choice among alternatives goes in [[DECISIONS]]. Where code lives goes in [[ARCHITECTURE]]. A rule or number a player meets is stated in [[PRODUCT]] §4; an entry here names it and says what enforces it, without restating the formula. A fact that is here is not in those files — a second copy drifts, and the two disagree silently.

**One entry per invariant.** The `###` heading states the rule, phrased so it can be violated. Under it: the mechanism that enforces it, and the incident that made it necessary (or "design-time" when it was set before any incident).

An entry that no longer matches the code is a bug in one of them. Find out which before you trust it.

## Engine purity

### The engine imports nothing from outside its own package

No DOM, no Three.js, no Node built-ins, no npm packages. It must run byte-for-byte identically in a browser and on the server, because it runs in both: the browser's authority runs every single-player rule, and the server's runs friendly matches and reads what a page sends ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). Enforced twice: `packages/engine/tsconfig.json` has `lib: ["ES2022"]` and `types: []`, so a reference to `window` or `process` fails to compile; `test/purity.test.ts` scans every source file and fails on a non-relative `from` import (a bare `import 'x'` or an `import('x')` would pass it) and on any `/// <reference` directive, which would bring Node's types (the tests' `@types/node` sits in the engine's `node_modules`) or the DOM's lib past `types: []` and `lib`. Design-time.

### The engine never reads ambient randomness or the clock

Every random draw goes through an `Rng` the caller constructed from a seed, and nothing reads `Date.now`. A battle, a chunk, a puzzle can then be replayed exactly from `(seed, intents)`, which is what lets a client predict and a server verify without disagreeing. Enforced by `test/purity.test.ts` (comment-stripped source grep) and by `rng.test.ts` / `world.test.ts` / `puzzles.test.ts` asserting equal output for equal seeds. Design-time.

### The engine holds no player-facing words

Species and attacks are ids; events say what happened; a refusal is a code; a `message` line is a copy key from `LINES` with numbers and species by id. A sentence written in the engine would reach a Danish kid in English, and would have to be shipped twice once a server authority sends it. Enforced by `test/no-words.test.ts` (TypeScript's parser over every source file but `save.ts`, whose validator messages are for developers: no literal that reads as a sentence outside `new Error`), by the battle, doctor and match sweeps (`words.ts`: no worded string in any state, view or event, nicknames aside), by the types (`LINES` gives each param a kind, a species or a number, and `Line` is typed from it), and on the client by `messageWords`, which takes nothing else from a `message` line. Design-time.

### A chunk is a pure function of `(seed, cx, cy)`

Generating chunk `(4, −2)` alone yields the same tiles as generating it after `(3, −2)`, and tile `(x, y)` is the same whether asked via its chunk or via `tileAtWorld`. Per-tile randomness is derived from `hashInts(seed, x, y, …)`, never from a shared generator whose state depends on call order. Enforced by `world.test.ts` ("pure function", "no seams"). Design-time.

## Puzzles

### Every puzzle's answer is a whole number and matches its prompt

For every kind, at every difficulty in the kind's declared range, `answer` is an integer and an independent re-solve of `prompt` yields it. Division builds the dividend from the answer; square roots square the answer; sequences are generated from a closed form. A picture kind's prompt is its face (`kroner(1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 37, 0)`), which the solver reads as a kid reads the picture: the mercury walked a degree at a time, the coins added up one by one, the amount dealt into piles, a shape's squares and fence pieces counted cell by cell, a clock's minute hand turned; the balance is solved by trying every box. Enforced by `puzzles.test.ts`, which re-parses each prompt with its own solver over 200 puzzles per (kind, difficulty), each difficulty from its own fixed seed. Design-time.

### Operands climb with difficulty: every band has a floor, and both ends are non-decreasing

For the sums' kinds (`add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div`, `missing`, `sqrt`), the smallest and the largest number shown in a prompt are non-decreasing in difficulty (per operation, since `missing` mixes "+ ?" and "× ?"), nothing above difficulty 1 is "+ 1" / "× 1", ten is never a factor, and a puzzle at difficulty `d` shows a bigger number than any puzzle at `d − 2`. For sequences, each pattern's first term has both ends non-decreasing in difficulty and a floor above its ceiling two difficulties down (so no sequence is asked at `d` and at `d − 2`), each pattern's smallest answer never falls, and only difficulty 1 counts by ones. Operands, steps and first terms are drawn from `[lo, hi]` bands (`arithmetic.ts`, `sqrt.ts`, `sequence.ts`), never from `[1, max]` or `[0, max]`. Enforced by `puzzles.test.ts` § difficulty ladder, sampled over 300–900 puzzles per (kind, difficulty) from fixed seeds. The Arctic's kinds (#191) climb by skills, not by numbers alone (a kroner puzzle that asks for change is harder than counting 500 kroner, with smaller numbers): each has a table of steps (`THERMOMETER_LADDER`, `KRONER_LADDER`, …), one per difficulty, every number drawn from a `[lo, hi]` band of its step, and within one skill (counting, change from one thing, change from two; one piece of a fraction, several) neither end of any band ever falls. Enforced by `puzzle-kinds.test.ts` § the ladders climb, on the tables, and § what each kind promises, on 400 puzzles per (kind, difficulty). Incident: #7 — operands had ceilings only, so a difficulty-3 attack asked "4 + 1", "16 − 15" and "8 + ? = 9" three puzzles in a row, and difficulty 10 could ask "√9", "1, 1, 2, 3, ?" or "0, 10, 20, 30, ?".

### A sequence prompt has exactly one right answer

Every sequence prompt fits exactly one of the patterns a kid is taught — equal gaps, a constant ratio, adding the last two, gaps that grow by a constant — and its last three numbers are evenly spaced only when the whole prompt counts, so the generator's answer is the only right one. Add-the-last-two never starts with a pair `a, b` where `2b = 3a` (its gaps then also grow by a constant) or `b = a` (it then ends in a counting run). Enforced by `puzzles.test.ts` ("no prompt fits two patterns"), which reads every prompt with each pattern, and with "count on from the last three", independently of the generator; sampled over 900 puzzles per difficulty from fixed seeds. Incident: PR #10 — "2, 3, 5, 8, ?" was asked at difficulty 7–10 expecting 13, while "the gaps grow by one" answers 12; "4, 4, 8, 12, ?" expected 20 where counting on says 16.

### A clock's answer is right morning or afternoon, and a time that could mean two others is wrong

`checkAnswer` takes a time as hours (0 to 23), a colon, a dot or nothing, and exactly two digits of minutes (0 to 59), and it is right when it is the clock's time on a twelve-hour face: 3:15, 15:15, 03.15 and 315 for a quarter past three, 12:20 and 0:20 for twenty past twelve. "3:5" is wrong, never read as 3:05 or 3:50, and a bare number of minutes ("195") is no time. Every answer a puzzle asks is typed in a form it takes (`answerText`). Enforced by `puzzles.test.ts` § checkAnswer. Design-time: #191.

### An attack's description names exactly what its puzzles can be

The sentence about the highlighted attack lists the engine's `puzzleTopics(kinds, difficulty)` for that attack at its level, and every topic listed must be one a puzzle there can have, and every topic a puzzle there can have must be listed. The starter's Scurry Kick on hard said "adding, taking away or missing numbers" and asked "7 × ? = 56": a missing number sat in a times table, and the words came from the attack's kinds, not from what the generator produces at that difficulty (QA, the i18n PR). Enforced by `puzzles.test.ts` ("what an attack says it asks": every species × attack × level and every kind × difficulty, the topics met over 300 seeds, read from the prompts by an independent classifier, equal the topics promised) and by `copy-files.test.ts` (every topic has its words). Design-time.

### Answers are judged in the engine, never in the UI

`checkAnswer(puzzle, input)` is the only place a submitted answer is compared. The UI passes the raw string through; the authority calls `checkAnswer` and emits the result. So wherever the authority runs (a friendly match's runs on the server), a client cannot claim a hit it did not earn. The engine half is enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts` ("a wrong answer never damages the opponent, whatever the input"), `doctor.test.ts` ("judges only with checkAnswer, whatever the input") and `match.test.ts` § an attack (twelve inputs, right and wrong, judged as `checkAnswer` judges them); the client half only by review (grep the client for `=== puzzle.answer` / `.answer ===`), which Phase 2.5 skips for a client-only diff. Design-time.

### Every right answer adds exactly one to the puzzles solved, and nothing else moves it

Every reducer that judges an answer (a wild battle, a doctor visit, a friendly match) says so in exactly one `answer-judged` event, and the engine's `countSolved` reads the count from those events: one more for each that was right, and in a match only for the answers of the side it is asked about. `LocalAuthority` applies it to every battle and doctor step and says `solved-changed` (the whole count) right after the `battle-updated` or `doctor-visit-updated` that judged the answer; a friendly match's events reach it as a `match-answers` intent, which the match screen (`match/controller.ts`) dispatches for each step the server sends, and which counts each step of a match once (a step at or below the last one counted for that match, the view's `step`, adds nothing, nor does a batch that is not one). So a wrong answer, a refused intent, backing out or leaving adds nothing, the count never goes down, a hand-over of many animals counts once, and the other player's answers never count for this one. The count is in `snapshot()`, the save and `welcome`, goes along on `travel`, and starts at 0 in a new game; it stops at `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, the most a save holds. Enforced by `solved.test.ts` (battles of every species pair that can meet, doctor visits with every kind of sum, and matches from both sides, answered in forms `checkAnswer` takes and refuses, the kid deciding right or wrong before answering) and `local-authority.test.ts` § puzzles solved (a long game: the count is exactly the right answers judged, one at a time, and the view agrees; saved mid-battle and picked up; a new game at 0; a match's steps for one side and the other; a step passed again or late, and malformed batches, adding nothing; a match step in the middle of a wild battle). Design-time: #94.

### Every answer judged goes in the puzzle record under its topic, and a topic's bonus makes a right answer worth the kid's average one

Every `answer-judged` event carries its puzzle's topic (`puzzleTopic`: a missing number in a times table is `mul`, a fence `perimeter`, a floor `area`, else the kind), and `recordAnswers` reads the record from those events as `countSolved` reads the count: one more tried for each, one more right for each right one, the latest 20 of each topic in wild battles kept as ones and zeros (a doctor's or a match's answer never goes there: a miss costs nothing there), in a match only the side asked about, and the very same record back when nothing was judged. `LocalAuthority` applies it beside the count and says `puzzles-changed` (the whole record) after `solved-changed`; it is in `snapshot()`, the save (`puzzles`, written once something was judged) and `welcome`. `topicBonus` is the formula of [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Puzzles": every topic 1 (none at all) for a kid with no answers, every answer wrong, or every topic answered alike; otherwise `p̄ / p` within ×0.7 and ×1.6, so `p · bonus = p̄` until a bound holds it, a topic answered right less often never gets a smaller bonus, and a topic met once moves the mastered ones by under a tenth. A record's `recent` never holds more right or wrong answers than its counts, and a topic this build lacks, named as an id, is kept as it was whatever it holds. A wild battle takes the bonus as it starts (`startBattle`'s `bonus`, kept in `BattleState` and its save), and a right answer hits for `attackDamage(…, bonus)` of its puzzle's topic: the attack's damage times the bonus, rounded once. Enforced by `record.test.ts` (the topic read off every kind's prompts at every difficulty, by hand; the window and the counts; a match's side; the doctor's and a match's misses never reaching the bonus; one wrong answer in a new topic; the formula written out again from the prose over 2,000 random records; the bounds and the order; every species × attack × level × bonus against `power × level × bonus` rounded; a battle started with a bonus landing the drawn topic's for 160 puzzles; a match with none) and `save.test.ts` § the puzzle record in a save. Design-time: the human's call, 2026-10-09.

### Difficulty is monotonic in tier and attack index, and strictly increasing in level

`puzzleDifficulty(tier, n, level)` never decreases when the tier or the attack index increases, and `(1, 1, 1)` maps to 1 while `(5, 4, 3)` maps to 10. Within an attack every level asks exactly one difficulty more than the level below it, and hard never passes 10, so a level that hits harder always asks at a higher difficulty. (The puzzles themselves can overlap between neighbouring difficulties; § "Operands climb with difficulty" promises bigger numbers only two difficulties apart.) This holds because an attack's easy level is capped before the levels are added ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Animals" has the formula); a clamp on the finished difficulty would fold the top levels together instead. Enforced by `puzzles.test.ts` § difficulty mapping: every attack in the catalog asks a harder puzzle wherever it hits harder, and any tier's first eight attacks climb one step per level. Incident: #32. The final clamp at 10 gave a bear's Maul and Crush difficulty 10 on medium and on hard, so medium asked the same sums for less damage.

## Battle

### Within a species, damage strictly increases with attack index and with level

With `solved` true and no topic bonus (as in a friendly match, or a topic at ×1), `attackDamage(spec, n, level, solved)` is strictly greater than `attackDamage(spec, n−1, level, solved)` and than `attackDamage(spec, n, level−1, solved)`; attack N at level 3 is the species maximum and attack 1 at level 1 its minimum. At any one bonus within its bounds the hit still grows strictly with the level; across topics it need not grow with the attack, since a wild battle's attack N may ask a topic the kid finds harder than attack N + 1's ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Puzzles"). Holds because `power` strictly increases along `attacks` and the level multipliers ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Animals") are increasing; rounding cannot break it as long as every power is at least 2 (a power of 1 rounds to 2 at both level 2 and level 3) and consecutive powers differ by ≥ 1. Enforced by `battle.test.ts` over every species in the catalog — a new species with a non-increasing power table fails the suite — and, for the level at every bonus, `record.test.ts`. Design-time.

### A wrong answer deals zero damage

`attackDamage(…, solved = false)` is 0. No partial credit, no consolation hit. In the reducer, an `answer-judged` with `correct: false` is followed by `missed`, never by a player `hit`, and the opponent's HP is unchanged — for any input, including an empty string; a correct one is always followed by a `hit` for exactly `attackDamage` (with the battle's bonus for the puzzle's topic). Enforced by `battle.test.ts` and `battle-reducer.test.ts` § answers over every species pair that can meet. Design-time.

### A battle is a pure function of `(seed, party, wild, leashQuality, bonus, intents)` and never mutates its input

`applyBattleIntent(state, intent, seed)` returns a new state and leaves the one it was given untouched; the Rng for the n-th accepted intent is `new Rng(hashInts(seed, n))`, so replaying the same intent log from the same seed yields identical states and events, and a rejected intent (wrong phase, bad attack index or level, a switch to nobody, not an object) returns the same state reference with a single `rejected` event. The seed is passed by the authority on every call and is **not** in `BattleState`: the state goes to the client, and a client that knew the seed could predict every leash roll and every wild attack. This is what lets a client and a server agree without trusting each other. Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts`: the catalog sweep deep-freezes every input state, the replay test plays every species pair that can meet from the same seed twice and compares, and a test asserts the state has no `seed` key. Design-time.

### A battle ends with probability 1, and HP stays a whole number in `[0, maxHp]` on both sides

Every round the wild animal either hits for its attack's `power`, which is ≥ 1 for every attack in the catalog, or misses, which it can only do against an animal of its own tier or fiercer and then with probability `WILD_MISS_CHANCE` = 0.44 < 1. So each round the party's total HP falls by at least 1 with probability ≥ 0.56 whatever the player answers, and a party of total HP `H` is knocked out within a finite number of rounds with probability 1 (expected at most `H / 0.56` rounds against a player who never hits). Every action on the player's turn — an answered attack, a leash that breaks free, a switch — either ends the battle or hands the wild animal a turn; the one free action, picking who replaces a knocked-out animal (`choose-animal`), happens at most once per knock-out, and nothing stands a tired animal up again mid-battle, so no sequence of switches can stall a battle. Damage is clamped at 0 and `startBattle` refuses an animal outside `0..maxHp`, or two animals sharing an id (a duplicate would give the party phantom HP and make writing HP back ambiguous). Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts` over every species pair that can meet, about 60 battles per species spread over the animals it meets (at least 3 seeds a pair; 25 before #89's animals, when the pairs were a fifth as many), with a 60%-accurate random player who also throws the leash, switches animals and occasionally runs, so every outcome is reached, by § switching's player who switches whenever anyone else is standing (always lost, the wild animal untouched), and by `battle.test.ts` (`power ≥ 1`). Design-time; restated when wild misses arrived (the round no longer always costs HP) and when switching did.

### A wild animal misses only an animal of its own tier or fiercer, and exactly when its roll says so

On its turn the wild animal draws its attack (`rng.int(1, N)`) and then a miss roll (`rng.next()`) from the intent's Rng, both every turn; it misses exactly when its tier is ≤ the tier of the party animal in front *on that turn* and the roll is below `WILD_MISS_CHANCE`, emitting `missed { attacker: 'opponent' }` and leaving the party's HP unchanged. Because the miss roll comes after the attack pick and nothing draws after it, every turn against a smaller animal draws and hits exactly as it would if misses did not exist, so a battle in which every animal that fights is smaller than the wild one plays exactly as if wild animals never missed; once a bigger party member steps in, by a switch or after a knock-out, its turns can miss. Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts` § the wild animal, which recomputes both draws for every species pair that can meet over 40 seeds and checks a fox that knocks out a squirrel and then misses the bear that steps in at the expected rate, by § switching, which recomputes the reply to a switch against the newcomer for every newcomer and wild animal that can meet (who leaves never changes the reply, so each pair has one animal leaving, taken in turn), by the catalog sweep, and by the seed-2024 golden replay (a fox against a squirrel and a rabbit). Incident: PR #8's balance table — with wild animals that never missed, a kid on the easiest puzzle who was always right beat an animal of their own tier 15% of the time.

### Only a `switch` changes the animal in front, and a switch changes no HP by itself

`active` changes only when a `switch` intent is accepted, and `canSwitchTo(state, i)` — the one rule the reducer and the client's party list both use — accepts it only while the player is choosing (`choose-action` or `choose-animal`) and only for a party member that is standing and not already in front. A switch on the player's turn is followed by the wild animal's reply against the newcomer, which is the only HP change in that step; a switch that replaces a knocked-out animal changes no HP and gets no reply. A tired animal never comes back in, and a switch mid-puzzle is refused, so switching can neither dodge a committed attack nor revive anyone. Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts`: the catalog sweep checks on every step that `active` moves only with a `switched` event from a `switch` intent and that no party member the wild animal did not hit changes HP; § switching recomputes the reply for every newcomer and wild animal that can meet and checks `canSwitchTo` against the reducer on every state of a switching player's battles; § rejected intents covers the animal in front, a tired one, missing and non-integer indices and a puzzle in progress. Design-time.

### Only an animal that can go where a battle is fought ever fights in it

A battle carries its realm (`BattleState.realm`: land, the water from the boat, or the air after a bird followed the glider down, whatever tile the kid landed on), set when it starts and never changed. `startBattle` sends out `leadIndex(party, realm)`, the first animal standing that can fight there (`canFightIn`), and refuses a battle with none, or against a wild animal that can't fight there; `canSwitchTo` refuses one that can't (`cannot-fight-here`); and the battle is lost once no animal that can fight there is standing, whoever else still stands in the boat. So out on the water a squirrel never fights, and never keeps a battle going that the swimmers have lost; up in the air only birds fight, and a robin that tires with a squirrel standing loses. Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts` § on the water (who starts, the refusals, the loss with animals standing in the boat, and random battles against every animal of the water in which every step's animal in front swims and nobody in the boat loses HP) and § up in the air (the same for every bird, the swan in all three realms), `save.test.ts` (a battle on the water picked up only out on the water, with a swimmer in front against a wild animal that swims; a battle in the air picked up wherever the kid stands, on land or in the boat, with a bird in front against a wild bird), and `fight.test.ts` (a battle seen from outside is read only with two animals that can both fight where it is fought). Design-time: the human's rule, "you can't fight on water unless you have an amphibious animal", and #91's air battle, where only animals that fly fight.

### What the battle screen previews is the hit that lands

The hit badges, each level's damage on the level picker, the lighter segment on the wild animal's HP bar and "That would tire it out!" come from the engine's `attackDamage`, `landHit` and `hitSpan`, called on the state on screen with the battle's bonus, and the reducer lands a right answer through the same `landHit`. Before the puzzle is drawn an attack whose topics hit differently shows its span, softest to hardest, and the bar and "tire it out" go by the softest, so they promise only what every puzzle it can ask lands; once the puzzle is drawn, the badge and the bar show exactly the damage and the HP left that a right answer to it takes, and "Correct!" that hit. Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts` ("landHit, as a screen previews a hit, is exactly the hit a right answer lands": every species × attack × level against a wild animal at full HP and at every HP round the hit); the client half, that the panel computes no damage of its own, by review. Design-time.

### A catch happens only on a leash throw whose seeded roll beats `catchProbability`

`leash-thrown.success` is exactly `rng.next() < catchProbability(hp / maxHp, catchRate, leashQuality)` with the battle's own Rng, a success ends the battle `caught` with the wild animal at its current (non-zero) HP, and a failure hands the turn to the wild animal. Enforced by `battle-reducer.test.ts` § the leash, which recomputes the roll independently for every species at four HP bands. Design-time.

### Catch probability is non-increasing in HP fraction and never a certainty

`catchProbability(hp, rate, leash)` is monotonically non-increasing in `hp`, follows [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Catching" (halving, cap and all), and stays under certainty whatever the leash quality. Enforced by `battle.test.ts` § catchProbability. Design-time.

## Match

### A match ends when a side has no animal standing, or a player leaves or times out

The sibling of § "A battle ends with probability 1": a match has no wild animal whose attacks always come, so its end rests on the players. A right answer hits an animal that is standing for at least 1 (every power is ≥ 1), nothing in a match raises HP, and a knock-out that leaves its side someone standing asks only for a free pick, so a match lasts at most as many right answers as both teams have HP, and ends with probability 1 as long as both kids keep playing and either is right some of the time. `leave` and `timeout` end it at once, from either side, in every phase. There is no draw and no turn limit: two kids who never answer right, or only switch, play on until one of them leaves, and a kid who stops sending anything (walks away mid-puzzle, say) holds the match until the other leaves or the authority reports them gone with `timeout`, which the server does for a page that dropped out and did not come back in 30 seconds, and, with its turn clock, for a turn sat on 3 minutes without a key (`presence/matches.ts`; `matches.test.ts` § dropping out, § the turn clock). Enforced by `match.test.ts`: random matches between kids right 20–100% of the time, who switch, leave and time out, all end, with no more hits than HP; a kid who switches whenever they can never stalls one; leaving and timing out work in every phase; two kids who only switch still play after 500 turns and stop the moment one leaves. Design-time.

### Only the side whose turn it is acts, and turns alternate

Every phase but the end names the side that acts, and an attack, an answer, a switch or a pick from the other side is refused (`not-your-turn`); only `leave` and `timeout` are taken from either side. An answer, right or wrong, and a switch pass the turn and add one to `turn`; an attack, a pick after a knock-out and the end do not, so the side that started plays exactly the odd turns. Enforced by `match.test.ts` § turns, and by the random matches, which check the side that acts against the turn's parity after every step. Design-time.

### No match view or event ever holds a puzzle's answer

The state keeps the open puzzle, answer and all, and never leaves the authority. A player is sent `matchView(state, side)`, built field by field (never by spreading the state) with the puzzle as a `ShownPuzzle`, and the events, in which `puzzle-shown` carries a `ShownPuzzle` and `answer-judged` only whether the answer was right; so both players get the same events. An answer on the wire would be one look in the browser's tools away from any kid; without it, a kid has to do the sum. The redaction does not make cheating impossible: every prompt is plain arithmetic, which a modified client could work out by itself, and a match that changes nothing gives it nothing to win. Enforced by `match.test.ts`: the random matches scan every view of both sides and every event for a key named `answer`, and check that every view of a puzzle in progress stays the same when its answer is swapped for another, so no field can carry it under another name. Design-time: the human's rule, the waiting player sees the puzzle without its answer.

### A match is a pure function of `(seed, parties, (side, intent) log)`, never touches the parties, and keeps its seed out of its state

`applyMatchIntent(state, side, intent, seed)` returns a new state and leaves the one it was given untouched. The coin is `new Rng(hashInts(seed, hashString('coin')))` and the n-th accepted intent draws from `new Rng(hashInts(seed, n))`, so the same log replays to the same states and events, which lets a server log a match and replay it. A refused intent returns the same state reference with a single `rejected` event. `matchTeam` and `startMatch` copy what they take, so nothing a match does can reach a player's party. The seed is the authority's and never in `MatchState`. Enforced by `match.test.ts`: the random matches deep-freeze every party and state and try a wrong intent at every step; the replay and golden tests; and the start, which holds no seed. Design-time.

### A match asks and hits exactly as a wild battle does

Both reducers play an attack through `battle/attack.ts`: the puzzle at `puzzleDifficulty` of the attacker's own tier, attack and level, of a kind the attack can ask; the answer judged only by `checkAnswer`; a hit of `attackDamage`. A match has no topic bonus: it hits as a wild battle with none. So a right answer in a match deals exactly the formula's damage and a wrong one nothing, for any input, and a change to how an attack asks or hits changes both. Enforced by `match.test.ts` § an attack, over every land species × attack × level against the formulas with twelve inputs, right and wrong, and by the battle reducer's tests, which pass unchanged through the same code (the seed-2024 golden battle included). Design-time.

### No frame the server sends a page ever holds a puzzle's answer

The server keeps the match's state, answer and all (`presence/matches.ts`), and sends each page only `matchView` for its side and the events of each step, through the one `send` that builds every match message; a refused intent goes back as a code alone. So what a kid's browser receives, and could read in its developer tools, never holds the sum's answer: the kid has to do the sum. Enforced by `match-socket.test.ts`, which plays a whole match over real sockets, answers read from the server's own state, and scans every raw frame both pages received for a key named `answer` and for a puzzle holding more than its kind, difficulty and prompt, and checks that each frame is exactly what the page's parser reads (nothing on the wire it would drop); and by `matches.test.ts`, whose fake pages check every message the same way. Negative control: sending the state's own phase in a view fails both. Design-time: the brief's rule, NO answer ever crosses the wire.

### A friendly match changes nothing in the player's game but the puzzles solved and the puzzle record

A match is the server's: the page sends only what the kid chose and shows what comes back (`match/controller.ts`), and no `MatchIntent` reaches the player's own authority. The one thing the page takes from a match into the game is the kid's own answers: each step's events go to it once as a `match-answers` intent, which counts the right ones with `countSolved` and records every one with `recordAnswers`, by the page's side, and never takes a step of a match twice. So after a match the save is exactly as before it, but for `solved`, which went up by exactly the kid's right answers the page was sent, and `puzzles`, which took exactly the kid's answers (a step whose message was lost on a dying socket is not sent again, and goes uncounted; never more): HP, knock-outs, the party, the tokens, where the player stands. Enforced by `match-controller.test.ts`, which plays a whole match against the real authority, through `dispatch`, and the real reducer standing in for the server, and compares the authority's snapshot before and after (so a step the page passed twice would show as a count short of the answers it passed). Design-time.

### Every invite and match ends, on every way out, and leaves no timer and no player behind

An invite ends on its answer, its 20 seconds, a move or a busy state or another world that breaks the challenge rule (checked again on every `where` and on the Yes), a Never mind, or a socket closing; a match on its end, a leave, 30 seconds away, the turn clock, or the server stopping, and a rematch nobody has played yet also when it is called off (§ "Back to exploring wins over a Rematch? it crosses"), which lets the kid who went back go at once, and the other once their page says it let go, comes back and is told, or runs out of time (two minutes at most); a match that ended goes once both said they went back to exploring (`done`), left it (the leaver at once), asked or were asked for another, or dropped out and did not come back within the 30 seconds (a page back in time is shown how it ended), or after two minutes. Each way clears the timers it set and forgets a player with no socket, invite or match (`presence/matches.ts`). A server that stops ends every match at once, with no winner, and the pages say the game is updating. Enforced by `matches.test.ts` § every way out (after a stop, and after every player closed their game and the waits ran out, no timer is left, no match and no player known) and the cases for each way an invite ends. Design-time.

### Only a match going on, or one a page has not put up yet, keeps a player busy

A match that ended is kept for its rematch (`lingerMs`), with its players in it, so a page on the result can still say Rematch?; that keeping must never make anyone busy. The server refuses an invite for being in a match only while the match goes on (`live` in `presence/matches.ts`), or while the player's page has not put it up (`unshown`: set whenever the page is sent the match, as it starts, a rematch too, and as the page comes back; cleared once the page shows it has it up, by a `where` that says `match` or a `play`, `here` or `rematch` naming it, or when the page lets the match go: a leave from a page that never had it up lets it go, while a leave that only came too late, from a page that had it up, is refused, and that page shows the result). Once it has, a player on the result is busy by what their page says (`where`'s `match`, the rule's `they-busy`), and one back to exploring is free. The one who leaves is let go of at once, after their page is sent how it ended (they skip the result, so their page never says `done`), and a player asking or asked for another lets go of a match they had finished, the other page told its Rematch? is off. Enforced by `matches.test.ts` § free again once a match is over: the leaver, on either turn, asked by a friend and asking one at once; a page whose `done` never came asked and asking; a page back from a reload busy until it shows the result, free once it has walked away from it or let it go; a hidden tab's player busy through the end of their match until their page shows it; a kid free after a rematch they played, whose page said `match` all along; a leave that came too late refused; a match going on still busy. ### Back to exploring from a result is final: that match never comes back to a page of the kid's

The kid's Back on a result goes with a `done`, which can miss the server: a socket that is away never sends it, and one that dies as it goes can lose it. The server then still has the kid on that result, and a socket of theirs back within its 30 seconds hears the match named in its `hi` and is sent it, as any page back in a match is (`joined`, `resume`). So the page keeps the match the kid went back from (`wentBack` in `match/controller.ts`), and so does the browser, under the player's `pid` (`animath.wentBack.<pid>`), so a reload or another tab of theirs keeps it too and no other player's page reads it: no page of theirs puts that match up again, whatever the server sends of it, and each says `done` again whenever a `hi` names the match, so the other page hears the rematch is off and the kid is free. Only the kid's own Back counts: a result a page let go of for another reason (another window took over, say) comes back when the server sends it, as to any page back in a match. Its rematch, which the other kid's Rematch? can start before the server hears the `done`, is the next entry's (#147). Enforced by `match-controller.test.ts`: Back pressed with the socket away and the socket back in time (`done` again at every `hi` that names the match, none once one does not), a view of the match already on its way as the kid pressed Back, a new page of the kid's (a reload, another tab) and another player's page in the same browser, which keeps its own result, a browser too full to keep the newest Back that still holds an older one (the page's own counts as much as the browser's: whichever the `hi` names), and a result that follows the kid back to the window another one took it from. ## Authority

### The authority's randomness is keyed by the world seed and its step count, and its seeds never leave it

`LocalAuthority` rolls one encounter after each completed step with `new Rng(hashInts(worldSeed, hashString('encounter'), steps))` (`worldSeed` here is the seed of the world the player is in, in the land they are in: `landSeed(land, world)`), rolls for a bird over each tile the glider enters (while a bird stands, until one follows) with `new Rng(hashInts(worldSeed, hashString('sky'), steps))`, its own salt, so a walk's stream is the same whether flights came between or not, casts a fishing line with `new Rng(hashInts(worldSeed, hashString('fishing'), steps))`, a cast counting as a step, gives a battle the seed `hashInts(worldSeed, hashString('battle'), steps)` of the step it started on, and gives the n-th doctor visit of the game the seed `hashInts(worldSeed, hashString('doctor'), steps, n)` (so leaving and talking again on the same tile asks new puzzles); nothing in it reads ambient randomness or the clock except `mintId`, whose `crypto` call makes an animal's id, which must be unique rather than replayable, and `randomHome`, whose `crypto` call picks a new game's home world (so strangers don't all start in one world), which the save then holds. So a walk replays — the same steps meet the same animals and the same puzzles, at the druid too — which is what lets a server running the same code agree with a client. The battle and visit seeds stay in the authority: events carry only `BattleState` and `DoctorState`, which have no seed. A save holds the step and visit counts, from which the seeds are derived again on load, never a seed itself. Enforced by `packages/client/test/local-authority.test.ts` ("a walk meets the same animals and puzzles at the same steps, each with a fresh id", "a visit replays from the same intents, and a second visit asks new puzzles", "a doctor visit never shows its seed", "two games flown the same way meet the same birds on the same tiles", and a flight let go early meets the bird the flight glided tile by tile does) and, for the states, the engine's `battle-reducer.test.ts` and `doctor.test.ts`. Design-time.

### A game picked up from a save plays on exactly as if the page had never reloaded

`snapshot()` holds everything the authority's next event depends on — the world and the land the player is in (its seed is `landSeed(land, world)`), the lands left behind and the lands unlocked, position, facing, the step and doctor-visit counts, the party, the puzzles solved, the animal book, a battle in progress, and the worlds left behind — and `start({ game })` restores all of it, so the same intents after a reload meet the same animals, puzzles, leash rolls, wild attacks and doctor puzzles as without one. A reload is never a reroll: not of an encounter (the step count is saved, so the next step is the next one), not of a puzzle (a saved battle comes back mid-puzzle with the same puzzle), not of a throw. A doctor visit is not saved: a reload closes it, keeping what it healed. A flight is not saved either: a reload in the air is a let-go, since the snapshot is the game `land` would leave (§ "A loaded save never strands the player"), so the restored game is the original after `land`, and plays on as it does; with a bird following the glider (or one that would notice it on the tiles on to the landing), that is down in the bird's battle in the air, the same bird with the same id and HP, on the battle's seed of the landing's step. Enforced by `local-authority.test.ts` § saved games, which cuts one scripted game at ten points (in explore, at the battle menu and mid-puzzle), sends each cut through a save document and JSON, and requires the restored game's next 60 intents to produce the same events as the original's, and checks that the next doctor visit after a restore asks the puzzle the original would have; by § names and worlds, where a game saved in another world picks up there and meets the same animals on the same steps; by § the glider, which cuts a game over the take-off tile and every tile of two flights, lets the original go there, and requires the restored game to equal it and play the rest of the flights and 80 more intents, battles among them, the same; by § birds in the air, which cuts three flights a bird followed (the bird noticing the glider early, midway and over its last tiles) at the take-off tile and round the tile the bird noticed the glider over, at least eight cuts in all, and requires the restored game to equal the original after `land`, its battle in the air and its bird included, and both to play 20 intents the same; and by the engine's `save.test.ts` § readBattle, which picks up every state of 25 real battles and checks the next step is identical. Design-time (the human asked that a reload never lose progress).

### Nothing runs behind the title, and only a starter starts a game

Until a game is under way — before the first one, and after `leave-game` — `LocalAuthority` takes no intent but `new-game` and emits nothing else: no step, no encounter roll, no doctor visit, no party edit. `new-game` is accepted only then, and only for a choice the engine's `chooseStarter` accepts (one of `STARTERS`, a nickname that is text) with, when it names the player, a name `checkName` takes (`not-a-name` otherwise). `leave-game` is accepted only while exploring, so neither it nor a new game after it is a way out of a battle or a doctor visit. The autosave writes and sends nothing while the title is up: not before a game starts (`begin` after Continue, or a new game's `welcome`), and nothing after `game-left` but the save it makes of the game being left. Continue starts from the save the page carries on from (`resumable()`), so a tab that walked on while the title was up never has its step count walked back. Enforced by the engine's `starters.test.ts` (every species of the catalog, and malformed choices), `local-authority.test.ts` § the title (walking the reed with no game under way meets nothing; the refusals, mid-puzzle and at the druid included; a game left and picked up plays on exactly as without leaving), and `autosave.test.ts` § the title. Nor is anyone present behind it: the presence socket opens at a game's `welcome` and closes at `game-left`, and a throwaway game never opens it (`presence-controller.test.ts`). Design-time.

### A player's name is always one `checkName` keeps

Whatever takes a player's name — the title, an authority, a loaded save — asks the engine's `checkName` (`names.ts`): 2 to 16 letters or digits in words joined by single spaces or hyphens, no rude word, tidied (trimmed, single spaces, NFC), and a name it keeps passes again unchanged. The title goes on only with a name it takes and says why not otherwise; `LocalAuthority` checks `new-game`'s name and `choose-name` again and keeps only the tidied name; `restoreGame` checks a stored one again, so a name that is no longer allowed (the rude list grew, a hand-edited save) loads as no name, and the kid is asked for a new one rather than the save being set aside. An account's save on the server stores a name as sent, like the rest of the save ([[DEFERRED]] "The server stores whatever save the client sends"), so whatever shows a name to other players, or takes it as an account's name, asks `checkName` on the server: the presence socket for a guest's `hello` (an account holder is shown by the account's name) and registration. Enforced by `names.test.ts` (real names that must pass, rude ones in every spelling that must fail, each reason, and random strings: a kept name is a fixed point within the rules), `save.test.ts` ("a stored name that is not one (…) loads as no name"), `local-authority.test.ts` (refused names start nothing and change nothing), `title-controller.test.ts` (each refusal keeps the box up with its reason), and on the server `presence-socket.test.ts` ("refuses a name the rules refuse, and closes") and `account.test.ts` ("says why a name is refused, with the rule the name broke"). Design-time: names are seen by other players.

## Saves

### An unreadable save never stops the game, and is never thrown away

`readSave` sorts every stored document into readable, `newer` (a later build wrote it: § below) or `invalid`, and start-up turns an invalid one into a playable game: a new game that leaves the save where it is until the kid has played (a starter picked on the title, a battle ended, the party changed or an edit to it accepted, the tokens or items changed, a tile cleared, a world travelled to, a name chosen; a step, a flight, a doctor visit alone or a refused edit is not: `Autosave.handle` sorts every event), then moves the old text to the first free slot of `animath.save.unreadable` (`.2`, `.3`, …) before the first write, and with no slot free never writes over it. Every other set-aside (`animath.save.replaced`, `animath.save.previous`) works the same way. On the server, an account's unreadable save is not replaced until the kid has played either, and the write that replaces it copies it to `account_save_backups` first. Enforced by `autosave.test.ts` (an unreadable save is untouched by walking, a doctor visit or a refused edit and set aside by a catch; a second one is kept beside the first; with every slot taken nothing is written over), `save.test.ts` § readSave and the upgrade seam, and `account.test.ts` ("a stored save this build cannot read is kept aside when a save replaces it"). Design-time; the second-save case from the adversarial review of #19, where the slot was skipped when taken and the save written over.

### A save a newer build wrote is never loaded, written over or set aside

`readSave` calls a document `newer` when its `version` is later than `SAVE_VERSION` or, of the right shape, it names an id, shaped as every catalog id is (`isContentId`), that this build cannot carry on with because none of its catalogs has it (`findUnknownContent`: a species in the party or in the animal book's `seen`, `caught` or `freed`, or in a saved battle its realm, an animal's species or the puzzle's kind; a land, the one the player is in or one left behind, or a species in a land left behind's party; a land unlocked this build lacks is kept and does nothing, as an item is), since species only grow (§ "A species that has shipped never leaves the catalog"); a shape no build writes is `invalid`, whatever else it names. An item it does not have is kept as it is and does nothing, so such a save reads. Only ids are seen: a battle a later build's other growth made (an attack added to a species, a realm a species newly goes to, a new phase) is dropped on load as if the kid had run away ([[DEFERRED]]). Such a save waits, untouched, for a build that can read it, in the browser and on the server. A page that finds one at start is behind it (`newer`) and starts nothing, not even the title; one that meets it later (another tab's save, or the server's copy at the first comparison or a save's `409`) is behind it from then on, so the kid never plays on in a fork of a game a newer build has moved on. Before any write to the key, a page checks that no newer save has landed there unseen, so neither a new game's first write nor setting an unreadable save aside moves one. The server never replaces one (`canReplace`: `409`, whatever the `seq`, nothing kept aside) and stores none it cannot read (`503`: it is the older one, and the client tries again later). A page whose save the server turns away as a conflict while holding a lower `seq` (a server older than the save it holds, which this page reads) tries again only as it does when the server is out of reach, backing off and then resting until the kid plays, rather than asking again and again. Enforced by `save.test.ts` (newer against invalid, ids of no catalog's shape invalid, an unknown item read; 300 real saves, battles and animal books among them, each read as newer with any one of its species, the book's three lists' included, realm or puzzle kind swapped for an unknown one; `canReplace` never over a newer save), `autosave.test.ts` (four kinds of newer save at start, from another tab, landing unseen just before a new game, an unreadable save's set-aside or a walk, and on the server at start and at a send: never written, moved or sent over; the turned-away save retried as if out of reach, and taken once the server can), `behind.test.ts`, `account.test.ts` (`409` whatever the `seq`, nothing kept aside; `503`, nothing stored, no account made), and `account-session.test.ts` (a newer build's copy of an account's game in this browser is neither written over nor set aside by logging in to the account or by making it again with the guest game, which then stays in the guest's key; either way of being newer, a later version or a species this build lacks). Incident: #89's planning — an older build called a save holding a species it did not know `invalid`, so with two builds live during a deploy, and accounts syncing one save across devices, a stale tab would have set a kid's newer game aside and started a new one in its place. Fixed before any new species shipped. The animal book's lists joined the check with the book: a build that has the book reads it back keeping only the kinds its catalog has (§ Animal book), so carrying on with a newer build's save would drop the newer kinds at its next write.

### A loaded save never strands the player

`restoreGame` gives a game a kid can play from any readable save: a position the player can't be on with what they own (`isPassable` with `gearOf` the saved items: the world generator changed under it, or water without the boat) becomes the spawn tile, an HP above the species' maximum is cut to it (`startBattle` would throw on it at the first encounter), an empty party in Nordland gets the starter (in a later land an empty party is a kid who has just flown in, and picks a starter of that land beside the druid they came down at, `needsStarter`; a land with no starters yet, not built, adds none, and never another land's: the kid walks it with nobody, meets nothing, and flies home from the druid they stand beside), a party with no animal that can fight on land (only sea animals: no save a kid's game writes holds one, though a `?party=` game, saved nowhere, can start with one) gets the starter behind them, and a battle saved with it is dropped, as it was not fought with the starter, so the grass is never out of reach, and a team that needs the druid (`needsDoctor`) comes back exactly as tired as it was, wherever it stands: a reload is never a heal. The live game left it a druid to get to (§ "A knock-out never strands the player"), and a restore that asked `doctorComes` again, where the player stands, healed a team that a glide had taken into a pocket no tent is walked to from, where the live game kept it tired: a reload, or Quit to title and Continue, was a free heal, and a game picked up played on differently from the game that saved it (the adversarial review of #116). Out on the water with the boat the player stays in it. No save is made in the air: `LocalAuthority.snapshot()` of a game in flight is the game as landing now would leave it (`landFlight`: on the landing tile, the steps flown to it, a tree or a rock there cleared, and with a bird following the glider its battle in the air under way), so a save written mid-flight, over water or a tree, holds ground the player can stand on, and neither `restoreGame`'s guard nor an older build that knows no flying ever sends them to the spawn tile (an older build reads a battle in the air as a newer build's save, and never loads it). A saved battle comes back only when `readBattle` accepts it against the restored party and the player is still where it was fought, in the realm it was fought in (a battle saved before battles had one was fought on land), or, for a battle in the air, which starts wherever the glider came down, on any tile they can stand on, on the ground or in the boat, with an animal in front and a wild one that can both fight there. Enforced by `save.test.ts` over 400 random saves of every shape, some with the boat (a tile they may stand on, every animal's HP as saved, cut to its maximum, someone standing unless the team needs the druid, an animal that can fight on land, and a party `startBattle` accepts where one of them stands), by "a team that needs the doctor comes back exactly as tired as it was, a tent in reach or none" (walled in, out at sea, a walker in the boat, only a crab standing ashore), by "gives a party of only sea animals the starter too" (red without the rule), by "out on the water with the boat, the player is still in it; without the boat, back on the spawn tile", and by the `readBattle` cases, a battle on the water among them, and a battle in the air picked up on land and in the boat, never out on the water without it; by `local-authority.test.ts` ("a reload is no heal", and a tired team that glides into such a pocket, then reloads); and by its § the glider ("a game saved in the air is on the ground where letting go would land it", over every tile of three flights: across the lake, over trees without the axe and with it). Design-time; the same promise as "A knock-out never strands the player". Without the boat check, a game that somehow lost its boat would put the kid on a water tile where every step is blocked; saving a flight as the tile under it (the first shape #91 sketched, an `airborne` field beside the position) would have put a kid over water or a tree in every older tab and on every older device, which sends such a save to the spawn tile. The rest on reload changed with the walk to the druid (2026-09-27): a free rest would have undone it at every reload.

### A new game never loses the game it replaces

New game on the title (a `welcome` with `newGame: true`) is a game of its own: a fresh lineage, no extras, numbered on past every save the page has seen and past whatever the key holds when it first writes. Its first write moves whatever the save key holds — the game the kid left, or another tab's later save of it — to the first free slot of `animath.save.previous` (`.2`, `.3`, … up to 200, since a kid trying the starters puts one away per try) before writing, and with no slot free leaves it where it is and does not save the new game in the browser. A newer build's save is never touched, one that landed there unseen just before the first write included (the page is behind it instead: § "A save a newer build wrote…"), and an unreadable one goes to `animath.save.unreadable` as usual. The page remembers the lineage the kid left (`putAway`). On the server (an account's) the new game's save numbers past the `seq` of the game the kid put away (jumping past it when the server was ahead, never adopting it), so that game lands in `account_save_backups` like any game a save replaces. A game the title never showed — the server's, when it was out of reach at start — is not put away: the bigger game wins as it always did, and the new one goes to `animath.save.replaced`. Enforced by `autosave.test.ts` § the title (the old save kept byte for byte, a second new game beside the first, thirty starters tried in turn, full slots never written over, the server ahead, the server out of reach at start, a newer build's save, a tab still playing the old game), with negative controls. Design-time: the human asked that New game never wipe a kid's animals by accident; the offline case, the slots and the walk-back (§ "Nothing runs behind the title, and only a starter starts a game") were found by the adversarial review of #29.

### Every save an older build wrote upgrades to one this build reads, and the upgrade loses nothing

`readSave` chains `SAVE_UPGRADES` from a document's version to `SAVE_VERSION`, and each upgrade is total and lossless: every document valid in its version comes out valid in the next, and the next can be turned back into it exactly. v1 → v2 puts the game in World 1, its home, and keeps everything else as it was: the position, facing and cleared tiles become World 1's, and the party, tokens, items, counts, battle, lineage, `seq` and extras are untouched; what v2 has no place for (a seed other than World 1's, a v1 extra under a name v2 took: `name`, `home`, `world`, `worlds`, and `solved`, `seen` and `caught`, which v2 took later) is kept under `v1`, as it was. v2 → v3 adds the kinds set free (`freed`: every kind in the book's `seen` or `caught` that no animal in the party is, in the book's order; none for a save without a book) and keeps everything else as it was, a v2 extra named `freed` (or `v2`) under `v2`. It reads every v2 document the same way, one a page still open on an older build writes after the update included (until that page meets a v3 save and stops), so such a page's sightings count as set free, and one of several of a kind set free there does not. v3 → v4 (lands, #191) puts the game in Nordland (`land`) and keeps everything else as it was, the position, cleared tiles, worlds left behind, party, tokens, items and battle as Nordland's; a v3 extra named `land`, `lands`, `unlocked` (or `v3`) goes under `v3`. A v3 save restores to the very game the v3 build restored, in Nordland. v4 → v5 (the puzzle record) gives the game no record and keeps everything else as it was; a v4 extra named `puzzles` (or `v4`) goes under `v4`. A v1 document that was unreadable stays unreadable. So an update never sets a kid's game aside as unreadable, and never changes where they stand or what they have. Enforced by `save.test.ts` § the v1 → v2 upgrade (500 random valid v1 documents, extras under v2's names and seeds of every size among them, each upgraded to a valid v2 document it can be rebuilt from; 200 games in the prototype world restored in World 1 with the same position, party, tokens, items, clearings and counts; unreadable ones stay so), § the v2 → v3 upgrade (400 random v2 documents, with books and with extras under v3's names, each upgraded to a valid v3 document it can be rebuilt from, its kinds set free re-derived by the rule), § the v3 → v4 upgrade (400 random v3 documents, extras under v4's names among them, each upgraded to a valid v4 document in Nordland it can be rebuilt from, and restoring to the same game as with no land named), § the v4 → v5 upgrade (every random document of the earlier sections goes through it too, and is rebuilt from; a v4 extra under `puzzles` is kept under `v4`) and by the kid's own save, run through the upgrade, the old and the new `restoreGame` (identical, field by field) and `LocalAuthority` before the upgrade shipped. Design-time: the first version bump, with one real kid's game 7,400 steps in.

### An older build's save is kept as it was before this build writes over it

Before a page writes its first save over a document an older build wrote (one it read through the upgrade, from the key or another tab's walk), it moves that text, exactly as it was, to the first free slot of `animath.save.upgraded` (`.2`, `.3`, … up to `.20`). A New game's first write is the exception: it already moves the old text, as it was, to `animath.save.previous` (§ "A new game never loses the game it replaces"), so nothing goes to `upgraded` as well. With nowhere to keep it (storage full, every slot taken) it writes over it all the same, unlike the other set-asides: the upgrade loses nothing (above), so the new save holds everything the old one did, and holding back would leave the game unsaved in the browser, reload after reload. The server does the same with an account's copy: the first save after an update copies the older build's document to `account_save_backups` before replacing it (`replacesAnotherGame`), and a save an older build still sends (a page open from before the update) is taken, upgraded. So an upgrade that got something wrong can be undone by hand. Incident: the adversarial review of #92 found the first version holding back the save when the copy would not fit. Enforced by `autosave.test.ts` (the kid's shape of save: kept byte for byte at the first write and only then; a second beside the first; with every slot taken or no room for a copy, the game still saves and a reload carries on from it) and `account.test.ts` (an older build's save is taken upgraded; the first save after an update keeps the old document, once). Design-time: the brief for numbered worlds ("Anything that rewrites a save must never lose one").

### A field this build does not know is written back as it was

`validateSave` keeps every top-level field `SaveV5` does not name; the autosave takes those extras (`saveExtras`) from the save it carries on from, and `saveDocument` writes them back unchanged in every save after; the server stores a document as sent. So a later build can add a top-level field to the save without a version bump: an older build still at play (a tab open from before an update, another device of an account) never drops it, though it does not change it either. Unlike an id none of its catalogs has (§ "A save a newer build wrote…"), a field it does not name never makes a save a newer build's. A field that an older build must not carry on with unread takes the bump instead (`SAVE_UPGRADES`): the older build then calls the save newer and leaves it alone. `freed` took it (version 3): an older build playing on would set animals free and leave them out of the list; so did `land` (version 4): an older build would play a game in The Arctic as one in Nordland; and `puzzles` (version 5): an older build would judge answers and leave them out of the record. Enforced by `save.test.ts` ("keeps fields it does not know, at the top level and on an animal"; "saveExtras returns only the fields SaveV5 does not name, and saveDocument writes them back") and `autosave.test.ts` ("a field a later build added comes back in every save as it was": through a walk, a catch, a count, the server's copy and a reload). Checked against `main` before #94's `solved` shipped without a bump: main's build played a save holding `solved: 312` through a battle, travel and every write, and kept it; with the extras dropped, the same check went red.

### A species that has shipped never leaves the catalog

A save naming a species the catalog does not have reads as a newer build's (§ "A save a newer build wrote…"), which no build would ever load again: deleting one from `catalog.ts` would lock every kid holding one out of their game. A species therefore leaves the catalog only with a version bump and a `SAVE_UPGRADES` step that turns it into one still there. Enforced by `save.test.ts` ("still reads a party of any species that has shipped"), which reads a save of every species id that has ever shipped and fails when the catalog grows an id the list lacks. Incident: PR #21's first HUMAN_TODO note said undoing the frog was a deletion from the catalog; the adversarial review showed what that would do to saves (then: every such save set aside for a new game).

### A page never writes over a save it has not seen

Before each write to `localStorage` a page reads the key again and writes only if it holds exactly the text this page last read or wrote. (The one exception is a new game's first write, which moves whatever the key holds aside first: § "A new game never loses the game it replaces".) If another tab saved in between, this page carries on from that save only when `sameProgress` says the other tab merely walked: the two differ only in whereabouts (position, facing, steps, visits, lineage, `seq`), a missing `tokens`, `items`, `solved`, `edits`, `worlds` or `lands` read as none, a missing `land` as Nordland, a missing book as the one the save proves (its lists compared as sets) and the lands unlocked as its kinds set free unlock them, so a save from before a field and one that writes it out hold the same progress, while a tile cleared, a world travelled to and a name are progress. It takes the other save's `seq` and lineage, and its step and visit counts when they are higher (`LocalAuthority.catchUp`) (a count never goes back, so a walk's encounters and doctor puzzles are never keyed twice). That holds with the druid's card open, whose visit keeps the number and seed it opened with, and in a battle, which is keyed on the higher count from then on, as a reload keys it from the save. Otherwise it is behind and never writes again, locally or to the server, until it reloads. A server answer that finds the key changed carries on from a walk the same way before it decides anything. A `storage` event is judged against the key as it is when the event arrives, not the value the event carried, and a key that has gone is never written back. Two pages writing in the same instant can both pass the check, because a page's view of `localStorage` is brought up to date only between tasks. The page whose save was written over, unseen, keeps it aside (`animath.save.replaced`, `keepOwnSave`) when it finds itself behind while its own last save is neither the save now in the key, nor played on from (the same lineage, a higher `seq`), nor kept aside anywhere, and when a `storage` event carries a save of its game numbered no higher than its own and not it, even when the key has moved on since (`overwrittenUnseen`), so it is never gone. Enforced by `autosave.test.ts` § two tabs and § two tabs, each with the real authority (a lagging view of the store stands in for the same instant), and `local-authority.test.ts`, each case with a negative control. Incidents: PR #19's Phase 2 — judging the event's value made a tab that had already carried on from another tab's walk decide it was behind, and reload in the middle of a battle. #58 — a tab with the druid's card open ignored the other tab's walk and wrote the step count back from 16 to 10.

### A page that is behind takes no play

A page that is behind the save runs no controller and lets no key reach the game, nor text a text box, until it has reloaded into the newest game. Why it is behind (`behind`) comes from the save that put it there, whichever page notices: `newer` when a newer build wrote it (checked first, and before every write, a new game's included), `window` when that save is still this page's game (the same lineage) played on elsewhere, `replaced` when another game has taken its place (a tab that took a bigger game from the server into the browser's save, and every other tab of the old game, as after a new game from the title), `gone` when the key has gone. Only the browser's own keys pass (with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd, and F1–F12), and `beforeinput` is cancelled, so nothing typed with AltGr or Option, pasted or dropped reaches a text box (only an input method's composition cannot be cancelled). A page that may have missed the news (back from the back/forward cache, resumed, or just focused) checks the save again. Behind another window, it reloads at once when it is the window in use, and shows the behind card when it is on screen while another window is in use. Behind for another reason, it reloads as soon as it is on screen (behind a newer build's save, the reload fetches the new version). It reloads itself at most 3 times a minute, and past that it shows the card; the kid's own reloads (Enter, Space, the card's button) always go and never count. Hidden, it waits, and it waits too while a login, a registration or a logout is on its way (a reload would cut it off: a logout the server never hears leaves the next kid on a shared computer in the account), whose own restart catches it up. So nothing a kid does in a page that can no longer save is taken and then thrown away. Enforced by `behind.test.ts` (no page state that is behind maps to `play`; a page reloads by itself only when visible and within the limit; the kid's reloads do not count), `autosave.test.ts` (the cause follows the lineage of the save that put the page behind; `recheck` finds what an event would have) and by the Phase 2 run of the fix for #26 on screen, where two pages play one save. Incident: #26 — after three reloads in a minute, a second window on screen stopped reloading and saving but went on taking play, so a battle won and an animal caught there vanished at the next reload.

### The server keeps the save with the higher `seq`, and never loses a game

An account's save lands only with a higher `seq` than the stored one, and never over a save a newer build wrote (`canReplace`); a save it replaces from another lineage, or one it cannot read, is copied to `account_save_backups` first (`replacesAnotherGame`), kept newest first within `ACCOUNT_BACKUP_BYTES` per account (always the newest, and at a kid's few kilobytes a game, hundreds of them). The decision runs in one transaction with the account's row locked (`writeAccountSave`), so of racing writes exactly one lands; its `409` carries the stored save. Enforced by `account.test.ts` § the account save (409s, set-asides, a New game's replacement kept aside, an older build's save and one from before `seq`, eight racing writes of one `seq` and six racing games, which both fail with the row lock removed; the budget: thirty small games all kept, megabyte games only the newest two) and `save.test.ts` § which save wins. Design-time; the racing and upgrade cases were the retired anonymous backup's, moved to the account's save when it went.

On the page, the server's copy of the same game with the same `seq` wins only when the two differ as this build reads them, each upgraded first (`sameSave` in `autosave.ts`): an older build's copy on the server and the same save upgraded in this browser are one game, so the page neither takes it nor reloads, and its next save sends the upgraded game past it. Enforced by `autosave.test.ts` ("an older build's save of the same seq here and on the server is one game…": three loads in a row, with the older text here and with the upgraded text a looping page left, never behind, nothing set aside, and the next save sent). Incident: 2026-10-08, after saves v3 and v4 shipped (#194, #196): every account's copy on the server was version 2, a tie with the browser's save compared the upgraded game here with the raw version-2 copy, called it a fork, took it (the same game), and reloaded into the same tie, for ever: a logged-in kid saw only "Your newest game is ready.", and its button reloaded into the same card.

The page settles the two copies the same way. Its first request is a `GET`, and the save with the higher `seq` carries on: this page's is sent; the server's (another device played more, or saves of this game that never reached this browser's storage) is adopted, the local one moved to the account's `replaced` key, and the page reloads into it. A tie between two games goes to the server's, and only between saves: at `seq` 0 neither has one, so nothing is adopted. A `409` settles the same way, and after one the server's copy is the game even when the page has saved on since, unless it is the page's own save, sent before and not heard back (`sameJson`). Out of reach, a `503` (a server older than the save sent) or a `429` (too many saves a minute): it retries at 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 s, then rests, with no timer, until the kid plays again. A `400` or `413` is a bug: one `console.error`, and nothing more is sent this visit. A `401` (`logged-out`) ends the pushes (`loggedOut`); the game saves on in the browser. Every one of these is decided from the API's own JSON answer; a page from anything in between (a tunnel's warning page, a proxy's error or login page) counts as out of reach, whatever its status. Enforced by `autosave.test.ts` § an account's save on the server (the bigger game wins and the tab reloads; a save from before `seq` never loops; quiet retries, then rest until a catch; a refused save reported once and not sent again; a `409` for a save that already landed) and `account-sync.test.ts` (two devices at one `seq` with different games; refused, then saved on past the server's number; its own save not heard back). Design-time.

### An account's save a newer build wrote is never written over; one no build could read is saved past

An account's save follows § "A save a newer build wrote…" wherever the page meets it: at login (`takeAccountGame` never takes one, and never writes over a newer build's copy in the browser), at load and after a `409`, the page is behind it (`newer`), and nothing is pushed over it. An account's game is played on several devices, so at load a page that holds the account's game in the browser asks the server before anything starts (for no longer than start-up waits, the session check included): an old tab never plays on in a fork of a game a newer build saved elsewhere. With the server out of reach it starts as before, and meets the newer save at the first comparison. Behind such a save, a player logged in may log out (the card's Log out, Escape) to play the guest game meanwhile: that writes the pointer and nothing else. A server save that is only invalid (a shape no build writes) is left until the kid has played, then saved past, the server keeping it in `account_save_backups` (`replacesAnotherGame`). Enforced by `account-sync.test.ts` (a newer build's save at load, with and without the account's game in the browser, and found while playing: nothing starts or is pushed, both copies unchanged; an invalid one saved past only once the kid has played), `account-api.test.ts` (start-up's one deadline) and `account-session.test.ts` ("never writes over a newer build's copy, nor takes a server save it cannot read"). Design-time: the orchestrator's rule for #89, and #97's classification.

### The guest game and an account's game never share a key, and no move between them writes over a save

The guest game lives under `KEYS`, each account's under `accountKeys(nameKey(name))`, and an `Autosave` writes only the family it was built with. Registering moves the guest game into the account (the account's keys first kept aside, the guest's text put away in `previous`), logging in writes the account's keys only when the server's save is further along (the other kept aside), and logging out removes only the pointer. Enforced by `account-session.test.ts` and `account-sync.test.ts` (an account's game leaves the guest keys byte for byte as they were). Design-time ([[DECISIONS]] § Accounts: "logging in never swaps one game for another behind a kid's back").

### An account's save goes to the server only for the account the page plays

The session cookie names whichever account this browser logged in to last, in any tab, so a cookie that is not this page's account (a logout the server never heard, a login in another tab) would send this game into someone else's. Two guards, one on each side. `accountSaveServer` asks `SessionCheck` first: the save routes hear nothing until `/api/account/me` has named this page's account (by `nameKey`), an answer that names another or nobody stops the pushes (`logged-out`), and no answer is asked again later. And every request to the save routes names its account (`x-animath-account`), which the server checks against the session as it answers ([[ARCHITECTURE]] § HTTP API, session auth), so a cookie that changes hands between the check and the request cannot carry the save either. The header carries the account's name, which the server keys itself (`nameKey`), so a browser whose Unicode tables differ from the server's still names the right account. A logout names its account the same way and never ends another account's session; a logout still waiting to be sent is held while a login or a registration is on its way (either ends the browser's old session), so a page starting meanwhile cannot end the new one, and is sent again if that page goes away before its answer (`LOGOUT_HOLD_MS`). Logging out leaves the `animath.account` pointer when another tab has pointed it at another account meanwhile. Enforced by `account-api.test.ts` ("sends nothing to the save routes while the session is not this account's", "names its account in each request…"), `account-controller.test.ts` (the waiting logout held aside), `account-session.test.ts` (the hold, and the pointer) and the server's `account.test.ts` § the account a request names. Design-time; the second guard from PR #96's adversarial review: the check stood for the whole page, and a login in another tab while a push was on its way sent the push with the new account's cookie.

## Server

### No migration after the anonymous backup shipped drops, alters or empties its tables

`players`, `saves` and `save_backups` hold the retired anonymous backup's games: in the human's local database, the newest save of each browser that played through the tunnel before the backup was retired, which the export that moves a game to production reads ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves). Every migration after `0002` only adds: no `ALTER`, `DROP`, `TRUNCATE`, `DELETE`, `UPDATE` or `INSERT` on those tables, and no dropping their index. Enforced by `migrations.test.ts`, which reads every later `.sql` file (with a check that the pattern catches each of those statements). Design-time: the accounts brief, written while the kid was playing, and kept by the cleanup that retired the backup; the one migration allowed to drop them waits for [[DEFERRED]]'s trigger, and retires this rule with them.

### Every migration file is in the journal, in order

The migrator applies a `.sql` file only when `drizzle/meta/_journal.json` lists it, and only when its `when` is later than the last one applied; anything else is skipped in silence, and the run still says it applied the migrations. So every file has an entry, the entries are in file order with `idx` counting up, and each `when` is later than the one before. Enforced by `migrations.test.ts`. Incident: lawcel, whose setup this project copies, has five early migrations missing from its journal, so a database migrated from scratch there gets a broken schema; [[DEVELOPMENT]] § Migrations has the recipe.

### Only login, register, a welcome link, `/me` (once a month) and a logout that names no account send the session cookie

A welcome link's POST logs in, as a login does, and sends the cookie; its GET never does. The save routes and the WebSocket upgrade read the session (`sessionUser`, and `sessionUserFromCookieHeader` for the upgrade's raw header) and never answer with a `Set-Cookie`, neither to slide a session nor to clear a dead one. A request still on its way with the old cookie when the browser logs in to another account answers after the login; had it sent a cookie, clearing the ended session or sliding the old token, the browser would have dropped the new one and the kid would be logged out mid-game. `/me` clears nothing either, and slides a session (sending its cookie again) only once it is `SLIDE_AFTER_DAYS` (30) into its year, because another tab of the same browser can log in while a page is starting. The game's logout names its account (`x-animath-account`) and sends no cookie either: it ends the session, which leaves a cookie that opens nothing, and a cookie it cleared could land after a login another tab made at the same moment. Only a logout that names no account clears the cookie. Enforced by `account.test.ts` ("a save route never sends the cookie…", "an expired session logs nobody in, and nothing clears its cookie but logout", "logout deletes the session…", § the account a request names, and the once-a-month slide), `accounts-ready.test.ts` for `/ready`, which a page asks before `/me`, and `welcome.test.ts` ("names its account, and nothing else, and sends no cookie"). Incident: the adversarial review of this feature (#85), before it shipped; the `/me` half was found re-reading its fix, and the named logout's in PR #96's review.

### The server stores no password, no session token and no welcome link's token, only their hashes

A password is stored as a salted scrypt hash with its parameters (`passwords.ts`), never as sent, and two accounts with one password have different hashes. A session's token lives in the browser's cookie, a welcome link's in the link; the server keeps their SHA-256 (`sessions.token_hash`, `welcome_tokens.token_hash`), so a copy of the database logs nobody in. No request line carries a welcome token either, so no log keeps one: the printed link has it after `#`, which a browser never sends, and the page sends it in a header to look the link up (`GET /api/account/welcome`, whose path takes no token) and in a body to use it. nginx's error log wrote a request line whole whenever the app did not answer (since #115 only when nginx runs out of memory), which is how a token in the page's address or the API's path would have landed there (PR #110's review). A link typed with `?welcome=` still works, and the access logs, nginx's and the app's (`hideWelcomeTokens`), show its token as `[hidden]`. Passwords are compared by hash in constant time, and a name with no account, or an account whose welcome link waits (`NO_PASSWORD`), costs a login the same hash (`verifyDecoy`), so the answer's timing does not tell a free name from a taken one. Enforced by `account.test.ts` ("stores a salted scrypt hash, never the password, and only a hash of the session token"), `welcome.test.ts` ("…a link stored only as a hash", "is asked with its token in a header…", § the request log), the client's `account-api.test.ts` and `account-controller.test.ts` (the header, then the body) and `passwords.test.ts`. Design-time ([[DECISIONS]] § Accounts).

### A welcome link logs in once, within its 14 days, and only while its account has no password

A link `admin import-save` printed opens an account made with no password. Its POST marks it used in the same transaction that sets the password and makes the session, on the condition that it is unused and unexpired, and sets the password only if the account still has none; so of uses racing each other exactly one logs in, an expired link logs nobody in, and a password the admin sets (`reset-password`) uses the link up. Its lookup names the account of a live link and nothing else: a spent one says only that it is spent. A password the rules refuse leaves the link as it was. Enforced by `welcome.test.ts` (§ the welcome link: once only, eight racing uses, expiry, the admin's password, a refused password). Design-time: the brief for moving the kid's game, whose adversarial review looked at the token's entropy, single use, expiry, timing, the limits and what the GET reveals.

### Exporting a kid's save never writes to the database it reads

`admin export-local-save` runs against the database a kid may be playing on at that moment. It reads through a session of its own (`openReadOnly`) whose first statement is `SET default_transaction_read_only = on`, checked with `SHOW` before anything else, and asks it only `SELECT`s. The file it writes is readable by its owner alone, never over another file, and never inside a repository. It never picks between two different games by when they were saved: an account found by the kid's name may be another kid's who took the name he had as a guest, so copies of two lineages write nothing until `--from` names one. Enforced by `welcome.test.ts` (§ export-local-save: an `INSERT` through the session fails as read-only, `findSaves` asks nothing but `SELECT`s, a folder inside the repository is refused, the file's mode is 0600, different games write nothing without `--from`). Design-time: the brief for moving the kid's game, written while he played; the lineage rule from PR #110's review, which found the default took the game saved last.

### A save a client sends is checked before anything walks it, so one no save could be is a 400, never a 500

Every way a save reaches the server (the guest game `/register` brings, the save `PUT`, the admin's `import-save`) runs the engine's `validateSaveWrite` on it before anything else touches it: a size measured with `JSON.stringify`, a copy, the insert. The check walks the document once and never deeper than `MAX_SAVE_DEPTH` (64: a game's save is 4 deep, `save.ts`), so it answers for any document, while the other walks run out of stack a few thousand levels down, well inside the 1 MiB body limit: V8's `JSON.stringify` on objects under the key "0" from about 2,500 levels, Postgres' jsonb before 20,000. What it refuses ([[ARCHITECTURE]] § HTTP API) is a `400`. The limit is part of the save's format: a build reads a document deeper than its own limit as broken (`invalid`, which the server replaces, keeping it aside), never as a later build's, so a build that saves deeper, or raises `MAX_SAVE_DEPTH`, bumps `version`, which every older build reads as newer before it walks anything (§ Saves: a newer build's save is never written over). `readBattle`, which takes any value, reads only what a save's battle can be for the same reason. Enforced by `save.test.ts` (§ how deep a save nests: at the limit and a level past it, 400,000 levels through every reader, a game's own save 4 deep), `account.test.ts` (a guest game and a `PUT` nested 100,000 deep, as lists and as objects under "0", each a `400`; one at the limit stored as sent) and `welcome.test.ts` (`import-save` refuses one and says why). Incident: #133, from the cold security review: the check itself went down one call a level with no bound and threw `RangeError` some 5,000 levels down, so a 12 KB body was a `500` on the public `/register` and on the save `PUT`; and `/register` measured the guest save with `JSON.stringify` before checking it, which throws on objects under "0" whatever the check does.

### The game offers an account only while the server says it can keep one

A server can run while accounts cannot work: its database lacks their tables (a development database `pnpm db:migrate` has not reached) or does not answer. There "Save my game" can only end in "We can't reach the game's home right now. Try again soon!", a promise broken to a kid. So `GET /api/account/ready` says whether the database answers and has every table in `ACCOUNT_TABLES`, and the save card (`openPrompt`), "Make an account" and "Log in" (`menuItems`), "I have an account → Log in" (`title.rows`), a spent welcome link's "Log in" (`AccountCard`, `welcomeChoiceKey`) and the web app's hint all wait for `account.ready`, which only the server's own `{ ready: true }` sets; no answer is a no. Until its first word (`account.readyHeard`) the title keeps the places of its row and its hint, empty (`title.slots`, `loginPending`), so they fade in there on a yes without moving the rows: a kept place is no row, and nothing presses it. A new way to offer an account follows it too. When the server stops saying yes, the rows go, each menu's cursor staying on its row (`keepingCursors`), a pause menu cursor whose row went landing on Keep playing and never on the row after it ("Log out" follows "Log in", and acts at once), and a save card that is up goes, unanswered, until it says yes again; an account card that is up stays with the kid's typing. "Log out" and an account's sync do not wait for it: a logged-in game saves in the browser and sends when it can, as offline. Enforced by `account-controller.test.ts` (the save card, a spent welcome link's login), `pause-controller.test.ts` and `title-controller.test.ts` (the rows), `account-api.test.ts` (`accountsReady`), `account-ready.test.ts` and the server's `accounts-ready.test.ts`. Incident: PR #96 shipped the save card, then hourly, to the human's development server, whose `mathgame` database then lacked the accounts' tables: the kid playing there was told after every hour of play to keep his animals safe, and "Save my game" said the game's home could not be reached.

### An error report carries nothing that tells whose page it was, and a stranger's report stays bounded

A page's report of an error ([[DECISIONS]] § Deployment) is exactly six fields: the message, the stack, the build, the mode, the browser (family, version and kind of device, never the user agent) and the window's size. It goes without the cookie (`credentials: 'omit'`), its text with every address cut to its path (no host, query or fragment, where a welcome link's token can be) and the kid's own words hidden: their names and nicknames and whatever is typed in a box, the password box included (`ReportContext.words`, which `main.ts` fills). The server reads no session for it, keeps exactly the report's fields (`client_errors` has no column for anything else, and a field it does not know is dropped), and counts the sender's address for its limits in memory, never storing or logging it; a report it cannot keep leaves a log line with a database code and none of its text. Anyone can send one, so the server bounds it before keeping it: 8 KiB a body, a message of 300 characters on one line, a stack of 20 lines and 2,000 characters, every control and format character gone (a terminal's escape sequences, a bidirectional override), 30 reports per address in 10 minutes, and the table 30 days and 5,000 rows at most, the oldest let go first. No limit is shared by every address: one a stranger with a few addresses could use up would refuse every kid's report, so a flood can only fill the table, never silence it. A tab sends each error once and three an hour at most, remembered across reloads by a mark of each, never its words (`SENT_KEY`), which keeps a class of breaking pages behind one address inside nginx's guard on POSTs, which logging in shares. The health watch puts none of a report's text into a notification or a Slack message, and tells of new errors once an hour at most. Enforced by the client's `error-reports.test.ts` (the six fields, the words hidden, addresses as paths, no cookie, three a tab an hour across a reload, marks kept and not words) and the server's `client-errors.test.ts` (the table's columns, nothing unknown kept, no address in a row or the log, the cleaning, every bound, the limit per address, a flood from 250 addresses leaving the next one's report kept, the retention, the log line of a report not kept). Design-time: the brief for the error reports, whose adversarial review found the shared limit a stranger could use up, the reports using up nginx's guard with logging in, and the reporter's chunk written in syntax older browsers cannot read.

## World

### Every world is playable from its spawn

`spawnPoint(seed)` (`world/spawn.ts`) is a grass tile from which a druid's tent is at most `SPAWN_DOCTOR_STEPS` (12) steps away on foot, without a tool or the boat (`nearestTent`), and from which at least `SPAWN_ROOM` (1,000) places can be reached on foot, a move at a time as a kid moves (onto the ice the whole slide, so only the places a slide stops on count): the nearest such tile to the origin. A new game starts there, and so does a first visit to a world, so a kid never starts inside water or a tree, walled in, or a long way from the druid who heals their animals. It searches the tents within 256 tiles of the origin and past that falls back to the nearest grass tile, which no world reaches: every one of the 9,999 was checked when the rule landed (all within 12 steps of a druid, the spawn at most 74 tiles from the origin). Enforced by `worlds.test.ts`, over 250 world numbers (grass, the druid, the room, tall grass within 12 tiles) and, tile by tile for a dozen, that no nearer tile would do; `world.test.ts` checks the tile is walkable for 25 seeds. Incident: design-time; before numbered worlds the spawn was the nearest grass tile, and the druid was 11 to 90 steps from it in the first 41 seeds tried. The Arctic's spawn is found by the same rule from its own origin (`ARCTIC_ORIGIN`, (5, 9)) with snow as the plain ground (`isPlainGround`), and is (5, 9) itself, snow by the tent at (5, 7) with the band's water at most 4 steps south: `arctic.test.ts` checks it over 51 world numbers, and the room on foot.

### Going to another player never puts the player where they could not stand, or walk on from

`LocalAuthority`'s `go-to` puts the player only on `arrivalSpot`: a tile they could stand on themselves in the world as they left it (ground on foot, and the water too with the boat), never the friend's own tile, from which the places they can get to, the way they get about and a move at a time (onto the ice the whole slide, `moveFrom`, as `nearestTent` reads it), lead at least `ESCAPE_REACH` (64) tiles from the friend across or down, so a kid who goes to a friend on an island, in a nook of trees or out at sea never lands in a pocket they cannot leave, however roomy it is inside; with no such tile within `ARRIVAL_RADIUS`, the player stays where they are (`go-to-refused`). A first rule asked only for 40 tiles reachable, and put a kid without a boat on a 51-tile island near World 1's spawn for good (the adversarial review of #93). And it is no step: the step count stays, so nothing is rolled and the walk after it meets what it would have met. Enforced by `arrival.test.ts` (against a brute force of the rule that moves by slides, in three Nordland worlds and two of The Arctic's, the facing, and six closed pockets of 40 to a few hundred tiles in five worlds) and `local-authority.test.ts` § going to another player (the spot, no step, the refusals, the same encounters after it). Design-time.

### Deep water is water with water all round it, two tiles out, and nothing else is

A tile is `deepwater`, in the `sea` biome, exactly when it is water and every tile within `DEEP_WATER_MARGIN` (2) of it, diagonals included, is water too; every other water tile is `water`, and no land tile is in the sea. So deep water is always at least three steps from land, and a narrow river has none: the shallows are a border a kid crosses before anything that lives out there. A chunk reads the elevation of its tiles and a two-tile margin once, and must give the tiles `tileAtWorld` gives; `travelKindAt`, which `nearestTent` reads to spare the check, is `tileAtWorld`'s kind with deep water read as water. Enforced by `world.test.ts` § deep water, which re-derives the rule from the tiles' kinds over three seeds, and "agrees with tileAtWorld on every tile, deep water at the chunk edges and negative coordinates included". Design-time.

### World 1 is the world every save from before numbered worlds stands in

Every save written before numbered worlds holds a position in one world, the seed of `'prototype'`, and the v1 → v2 upgrade puts every one of them in World 1, so World 1 is that world exactly: `worldSeed(1)` is 821322741, its tiles are the ones those saves stand among, and its spawn is (−2, 6). A change to what `generateChunk` gives for that seed, or to where its spawn is, can put a saved kid in the water or a wall ([[DEFERRED]] "A saved position assumes today's world generator"). Deep water turned some water tiles into deep water and changed nothing else: read back as water in the river biome, every tile within 64 of the spawn tile is what it was before (0 land tiles of 205,861 within 256 changed); the spawn rule of numbered worlds keeps World 1's nearest grass tile, whose druid is 7 steps away. Enforced by `world.test.ts` ("changed no land"), which pins the world within 64 tiles of spawn by a checksum of every tile, and the same checksum with deep water read back as water, and by `worlds.test.ts` (World 1's seed, its spawn (−2, 6) with the druid 7 steps away, the nearest grass tile of all). A change to generation or to the spawn rule fails them: check what the change does to saved games (a position on a tile no longer walkable, or walled in) before updating them. Incident: design-time, when deep water became the first change to the world since kids started saving games; World 1 since numbered worlds, when the kid's game was 7,400 steps in.

### Only a tree, a rock or an ice block is ever cleared, only by its tool, from beside it and facing it, or (a tree or a rock) by coming down on it from the glider

`clearTile` clears a tile only when it is the one next to the player that they face, it is a tree, a rock or an ice block in the world as they left it, and they own its tool (`clearingTool`, by the land the seed is: the axe in Nordland and the arctic axe in The Arctic for a tree, the pickaxe for a rock, the ice pick for an ice block); anything else is a refusal that changes nothing. An ice block leaves what it stood on (`under`): snow, water, or on the ice a fishing hole, never ice, so no slide ever stops where a block stood and every slide is still undone by the move back (§ "A slide on the ice…"). The glider's landing clears through `clearLanding`, the same rule for the tile the player comes down on: a tree or a rock not yet cleared, its tool owned, and nothing else (never an ice block, which no glide comes down on) (and a flight comes down on a tree or a rock only with its tool: § "A flight always ends on a tile…"). And whatever the overlay holds (a hand-edited save may name any tile), `clearedTile` turns only a tree or a rock into ground, and an ice block into what it stood on: an edit never touches water, a tent or tall grass, never closes a tile that was walkable, and never makes a tile start a battle. A tent a kid could clear would remove a druid; water would let them walk off the map before the boat exists. Enforced by `edits.test.ts` (every kind of tile edited round three seeds; walkability only ever gained; `clearTile` from every walkable tile near spawn facing every way checked against `clearableAhead` and the tools owned; each refusal; `clearLanding` on every tile near spawn, stumps and gravel among them, with every set of tools), each red with its check removed, by `arctic-shop.test.ts` (every ice block near spawn in three Arctic worlds broken from each side with the ice pick and refused with Nordland's tools; a spruce only with the arctic axe), and by `flight.test.ts` (every landing on a tree or a rock over hundreds of random flights clears it with its tool, and no other landing clears anything). Design-time: the human's son asked for an axe for trees and a pickaxe for rock; the landing's clear is the human's call on #91 (2026-09-27).

### A flight always ends on a tile the player can stand on, at most 20 tiles out, and a flight with nowhere to land never starts

`takeOff` (`world/flight.ts`) fixes a flight's reach at take-off: the last tile of the `GLIDE_TILES` (20) ahead the player could come down on with what they own (`isLandable`: ground always, water with the boat, a tree with the axe and a rock with the pickaxe, which the landing clears, a tent never), in the world as they left it; with none, there is no take-off (`nowhere-to-land`) and nothing changes. `glideOn` never goes past the reach, and `landFlight` comes down on the first landable tile at or after the one the glider is over (never the take-off tile), which is at the latest the reach. So no flight ends past 20 tiles, however late a kid lets go over water or trees (the reach is the last landable tile, not the 20th), none ends on a tile they could not walk or sail on from, and nobody is ever put back where they took off. The authority takes nothing but `glide` and `land` in the air, so neither the world nor what the player owns changes under a flight, and the reach stays landable. A flight is steps, one per tile flown, and never rolls a walk's encounter (the tall grass's, the deep water's), not on the tiles flown over nor on the landing tile; only a bird may notice the glider (§ "An encounter can only start on an encounter tile"). Enforced by `flight.test.ts` (the issue's table for every kind and set of tools; 1,500 random take-offs in three worlds, near water, trees, rocks and tents with some of the line already cleared, against a brute force of the rule, refusals included; every release point of 700 of them landing within the reach on a tile the player can stand on with their gear, cleared when it was a tree or a rock; take-offs either side of chunk borders and zero, every way; and World 1's lines: the lake north of the start, 19 and 20 water tiles, a 30-tile lake, a row of trees, a rock field, a tent, ground before a lake that runs past the reach), each red with the rule's check changed, and by `local-authority.test.ts` § the glider (the intents, the refusals, no battle over the deep water or on landing on the reed, the steps). Design-time: #91, the human's rule "you couldn't start a jump over water with over 20 tiles to land (unless you have a boat!)".

### Every spot of the tents' lattice in The Arctic is a tent a kid can walk to and away from

In every world of The Arctic (`arctic.ts`) the tile on every spot of the tents' lattice (x mod 23 = 5, y mod 19 = 7) is a tent, and no other tile is; every tile within `TENT_CLEARING` (2) of it is snow, and no water but the open-sea band's lies within `LEAD_FREE` (6). So from Nordland to The Arctic and back the kid comes down at the tent they left (§ "A flight comes down beside a druid…"), and no tent stands on an island: from beside every tent a kid on foot reaches at least 300 places to stand, the way they really move (`moveFrom`, a slide being one move), and every move is undone by the move back (§ "A slide on the ice always ends…"), so they get back too. Enforced by `arctic.test.ts` ("puts a tent on every spot…": 169 spots and two far ones in five worlds, the clearing, the leads, no tent off the lattice; "lets a kid walk (and slide) away from every tent…": 83 tents in three worlds, by a flood fill over `moveFrom`) and `lands.test.ts` (every walkable-sided tent of five Nordland worlds to The Arctic and back to the same tent). Design-time (#191 step 4).

### A slide on the ice always ends, is one move, and is undone by the move back

No tile of The Arctic's ice borders a tile a kid can't step onto: there it is a bank of snow (`isBanked` in `arctic.ts`). So every slide ends off the ice, and from every place a kid can stop, the move the other way after any move brings them back where they were: the places a kid reaches are joined both ways, and no slide leaves anyone in a pocket. `moveFrom` (`world/slide.ts`) carries a step onto the ice on over every tile of ice ahead until a tile that can't be stepped onto stops it on the ice in front (facing it) or a tile that isn't ice stops it on that tile, and never past `MAX_SLIDE` tiles; The Arctic never makes a straight run of ice longer than `ICE_RUN` (22): each row and each column of the ice has a stopper (a snowdrift or an ice block) once in every `ICE_STOP` (23) tiles at an offset of its own (`isStopper`), and no edit makes ice. The authority's `move` takes the whole slide at once (one `player-moved` with its `tiles`, every tile a step, the encounter rolled on the last tile only), so no save, no reload and no other intent ever lands in the middle of a slide. Elsewhere `moveFrom` is a single step, so Nordland's walking is as it was. Enforced by `arctic.test.ts` § sliding on the ice ("leads nowhere a kid cannot come back from": every move from the first 6,000 places reached from the spawn of five worlds, on foot and with the boat, undone by the move back; "never borders the ice…"; every walkable tile of four windows of lakes and sea ice, every way, with and without the boat, against the test's own slide; no run of ice longer than `ICE_RUN` in a row or a column, read past the windows' edges; the cap) and "have no Arctic tile and no slide" (Nordland), `arctic-shop.test.ts` ("leave a world that still leads nowhere…": the same check with every ice block and spruce within 70 tiles of three spawns broken), and `local-authority.test.ts` § sliding on the ice (one event, the steps, the save at the end, a slide into a fishing hole stopping facing it). Design-time (#191 step 4): a slide with no stopper would never end. Incident: the adversarial review of #199 found pockets in five Arctic worlds out of ten, places a kid slid into and could never leave (two snow tiles walled by trees joined by a row of ice, entered by a slide down onto the middle of that row, which stopped on the ice in front of an ice block); the banks made every move reversible.

### A ski move is the authority's, never saved halfway, and no skim leaves a kid where they can't get back

Every tile a kid on skis crosses is decided by the engine (`skiMove` for a held step, `coast` for letting go), each move whole in one intent, every tile a step, and only its last tile rolls an encounter, never a deep-snow tile flown over at top speed (the first `DEEP_CARRY` in a row). The speed is the authority's memory only, never in the save, and every intent but a held arrow, a coast and a party pick stands the kid still (as does a reload), so no save is ever taken mid-coast. A coast ends on a deep-snow tile it slows on and rolls there, so no deep-snow tile is crossed without its roll unless flown over at top speed (the review of #205 found a coast walking on over one). A tap is a walk (two steps held one way before any speed), so letting go after one never coasts. A skim without the boat lands only on ground from which the kid can take the run-up back (`runUpBack`) or walk round (`walksBack`); with the boat it ends in the boat or on ground. The ice slides as ever (`moveFrom`), skis or not. Enforced by `skis.test.ts` (engine: the speed ladder, the tap, the coast, turns and walls, the ice against `moveFrom`, deep snow 2 and 3, every skim along the leads of three worlds landing only where the kid gets back, the shore with and without the boat, 500+ random ski moves ending on a tile a kid can stand on in a straight line, rolling only on an encounter tile) and the client's `skis.test.ts` (the authority's speeds and coast, stood still by Enter and by a reload, nothing saved, the wire's `skis`). Design-time (#191 step 6).

### The poles never meet

Every tile of The Arctic north of the open-sea band's middle line (y < 16.5) is in a biome of the north pole, every tile south of it in one of the south (`BIOME_POLE`, `poleAt`), and rows 12 to 21 are the band's water for every x, so no kid crosses on foot and no Arctic animal's table is met on the wrong pole. ### Travelling keeps every world as the player left it

`travel` (`world/worlds.ts`) remembers the world left exactly as the player leaves it — where they stand, the way they face, the tiles they cleared there — and picks the world reached up exactly as they left it, or at its spawn, facing down, on a first visit. The worlds left behind hold each world once and never the one the player is in, the world left most recently first; past `MAX_WORLDS_KEPT` (100) the one left longest ago is forgotten, never home. Only a spot the player could no longer stand on (the boat gone from a hand-edited save) starts them at that world's spawn, its clearings kept. The party, tokens, items, name and step and visit counts go along untouched, and travel is taken only while exploring, so no battle or doctor visit is ever left behind in another world. Enforced by `worlds.test.ts` (a first visit, a round trip, a spot no longer standable, the cap with home kept, and 300 random journeys over eight worlds checking that each world keeps exactly the clearings made in it and the list keeps its rules) and `local-authority.test.ts` (in a battle or at the druid it does nothing; going back finds a chopped tree still gone; a game saved in another world picks up there and meets the same animals on the same steps). Design-time: numbered worlds.

### Nordland's worlds are the worlds there were before lands, and every land's every world has a seed of its own

`landSeed('nordland', n)` is exactly `worldSeed(n)`, so every save from before lands, and World 1 tile for tile, is where it was; each later land's worlds take the next 10,000 seeds after the land before it, so no two worlds of any lands share a seed and `landOfSeed` says back the land of each. A change that moved Nordland's seeds would put every saved kid in another world. The Arctic's generator is chosen by the seed (`landOfSeed` in `generateChunk`, `tileAtWorld`, `travelKindAt`), so every Nordland world is tile for tile what it was before The Arctic had a map. Enforced by `lands.test.ts` § the lands' seeds (every one of the 9,999 world numbers of every land, World 1's seed and spawn) beside `worlds.test.ts`' pin of World 1, and by `arctic.test.ts` § Nordland's worlds (49 chunks, a sample of `travelKindAt` and the spawn of five Nordland worlds, pinned by a checksum taken before #191 step 4; no Arctic tile and no slide in Nordland). Design-time (#191).

### A flight comes down beside a druid the kid can talk to, and a flight out and back comes home to the tent it left, but for a first arrival, which comes down at the spawn tent

`tentArrival` (`world/tent-map.ts`) puts the kid on walkable ground beside a tent of the land reached, facing it (`canTalkToDoctor` holds), so they can always fly on or back; the tent on the spot they flew from when the land has one there that a kid can stand beside on foot, else the land's nearest such tent in a straight line within `TENT_MAP_SPOTS` (with none, the world's spawn). So from Nordland to a land with a tent on every lattice spot (The Arctic of #191 step 4) and back, the kid stands beside the tent they left. The one exception: `fly` maps an arrival that asks for a starter (`needsStarter` of the land reached and the party kept there: empty, on a first visit or one left before a starter was picked) from the world's spawn (`spawnPoint`) instead of from the tent flown from, so it comes down at the spawn tent (in every Arctic world the one at (5, 7)) wherever it came from, and its way back is mapped from there ([[DECISIONS]] § Lands, the human's call); an arrival with any animal of the land kept, whether or not the kid ever stood in that world, is mapped from the tent flown from. Enforced by `lands.test.ts` § fly (from every walkable-sided tent of five Nordland worlds: never been, left before a starter, with and without the world, all at the spawn tent and home from there; an animal kept, with and without the world, at the same-spot tent and home to the tent left) and § the tent mapping (300 random lands against a brute force of the rule; every walkable-sided tent of five Nordland worlds through a stub land with every tent and back; Nordland's nearest tent from every spot of a 17×17 patch, never one further than another; a path cleared to a boxed-in tent) and `local-authority.test.ts` § lands. Design-time (#191).

### A flight never loses or mixes what a land holds

`fly` (`lands/fly.ts`) leaves the land the kid flies from as it stands, its party, money, items and worlds (the one flown from among them, as they left it), and gives back the land reached exactly as it was left, or nothing of it on a first visit; a land is never the one the kid is in and its own left behind, never left behind twice, and its worlds keep `MAX_WORLDS_KEPT` with home; every land's cleared tiles share the one `EDITS_BUDGET` (`fitStays`), the world reached kept whole. A trip to another world number keeps the kid in their land. Enforced by `lands.test.ts` § fly (40 random runs of flights, earnings and trips, each land's party, money and items checked against the test's own book; the budget across lands) and `local-authority.test.ts` § lands (a flight and a flight back, a save in The Arctic picked up and played on the same, a trip to another number there). Design-time (#191).

### A save is never in the plane, and the screens hear of a flight only once the plane has left

The fare's right answer (or a yes to the surprise trip) flies the kid at once in the authority (`travelled` to the land reached, its party, its belongings, and `starter-wanted` on a first arrival), and the autosave hears those events as they come, so the game saved from then on is the one beside the tent reached: a reload mid-flight comes back there, never in the air and never in the land left. The page's other screens (the world, the HUD, presence, the doctor's card, the title) hear none of them until the plane is off the screen in the land left (`PlaneController.intercept`), then all of them in order; no key does anything from the plane's landing to the kid's getting off (`plane.busy`), and presence says `plane` until the plane has gone (`plane.active`). Enforced by `plane-controller.test.ts` (the snapshot is in The Arctic the moment the fare is paid; not one event from the flight on reaches a screen before the plane has left, then all of them in order; a trip to another world is no flight; the parts' timing and the kid's ride into the plane and out) and `title-controller.test.ts` § a land's starters (they come up only once nothing else is on screen). Design-time (#191 step 7: "Reload mid-flight must be safe (a save is never \"in the plane\")").

### Everything that walks reads the world as the player left it

A tile the player cleared is ground everywhere a tile is judged walkable for them: the authority's `move`, `restoreGame` (a save standing on a stump stays there, not sent to the spawn tile), the way to a tent (`nearestTent` walks the paths they cut: behind `knockOut` and `doctorComes`, and the arrow to the druid, `DoctorWay`, fed the overlay on screen), the lead walking behind (`Follower.place`), the chunks on screen (`ChunkRing`, `editedChunk`) and the title's scenery. One site reading the seeded world instead would disagree with the others: a kid standing in a gap they chopped would be moved to the spawn on reload, or pointed past the gap to a tent they can't reach, or looked after by a druid who came for no reason, or the follower put inside a tree. Enforced by `save.test.ts` ("keep a player standing where they cleared"), `tents.test.ts` ("walks the paths a player chopped", against its own flood fill), `doctor.test.ts` and `local-authority.test.ts` (a battle lost in a spot chopped open: the path they cut is the way out, and without it the druid comes), `follower.test.ts` (put on a stump), `render-memory.test.ts` (the chunk comes back without the tree; the title's team gathers on a stump), each red with its site reading the seeded world. Design-time.

### A tent search answers the same whatever was searched before

`nearestTent` reads the seeded ground from a cache of whole chunks (`tents.ts`): each chunk's kinds exactly as `generateChunk` gives them, deep water read as water (`travelKindAt`'s), for one seed at a time, emptied when another seed is searched, the oldest chunks dropped past `CHUNKS_KEPT`; a tile the player cleared is read from their overlay before the cache. So a search is a pure function of `(seed, from, maxSteps, edits, gear)`, and the way to the druid, looked for again at every step of a tired kid's walk, reads each chunk once instead of every tile every step. A cache that kept a chunk across seeds, or a stale one, would point a kid at a tent in another world, or through a lake. Enforced by `tents.test.ts` ("reads each world as it is, whatever was searched before": three worlds taken in turn and places up to 400 tiles apart, each search against its own flood fill; "is deterministic and does not depend on what was asked before"), and by `world.test.ts`, which pins `generateChunk` to `tileAtWorld` tile for tile. Design-time: the arrow to the druid was the second caller on a per-step path ([[DEFERRED]]'s trigger).

### The cleared tiles in a save stay within their budget, and only far chunks grow back

The tiles cleared in every world a save holds share one `EDITS_BUDGET` (24,000 characters). A clear that takes the current world's overlay past it lets whole chunks grow back, farthest from the player first, until it is within it again; and the worlds left behind get what the current world leaves, home's first, then the most recently left, each cut back from the chunks farthest from where the player stood there (`fitWorlds`, after every clear and on restore). Travelling only moves a world's clearings between the current world and the worlds left behind, so it never takes the total past the budget, and `restoreGame` trims a save that holds more the same way (only a hand-edited save or another client can), so no save this build writes is over it; the budget holds the 25 chunks round the player at their fullest (`MAX_ENTRY_LENGTH` each), so no chunk on screen ever grows back and nothing appears under the kid. The text is canonical, so the same tiles are the same save (`sameProgress`), and a client applying `tile-cleared`'s `pos` and `regrown` holds the authority's overlay exactly. Without the budget a keen chopper's save would grow for good: past the 64 KiB a browser lets an account's save sent as the page closes carry, and on towards the server's body limit (`SAVE_MAX_BYTES`), where sending it stops. Enforced by `edits.test.ts` (5,000 tiles round home fit and load fast; the worst case trims only the farthest chunks and none within two of the player), `worlds.test.ts` and `save.test.ts` (three worlds' clearings far past the budget come back within it, home's kept whole, the latest left trimmed from far away; random journeys keep every world's clearings as they were), `save.test.ts` (a save half again over the budget comes back within it, the player standing where they cleared), `local-authority.test.ts` (the UI's view equals the authority's while far chunks grow back) and `account.test.ts` (the fullest overlay beside six named animals mid-battle stays under both limits). ## Encounters

### Every species in the catalog can be met somewhere

For a lead of any tier `L`, in every land (The Arctic's frozen lakes have no encounter tile: what lives under their ice is hooked through a fishing hole, whose table keeps only the promises [[DECISIONS]] § Lands gives it), each biome's encounter table, in the realm of its encounter tile (tall grass is land; deep water, the sea biome's, is water), lists exactly the species of that realm living there, whatever their tier — plus, inside `WILD_RADIUS`, at the river and in the mountains, every tier-`L` species of the realm that doesn't live there as a visitor when a resident is bigger than tier `L` — all with positive weight, and for every species at least one habitat grows its encounter tile (tall grass, or deep water out at sea) within 8 chunks of spawn. The bell never weighs a tier nothing, and the ground around a tile only re-weighs the species already there, each to a quarter to four times its biome share (§ "The ground changes which animal comes out"), so neither takes a species off a table. Every land species lives in a land biome and every animal of the water alone in an open sea (Nordland's `sea`, The Arctic's `arctic-ocean` and `southern-ocean`) and, in The Arctic, under the ice too, never on land, so every lead can meet every species of its realm (the sea animals from the boat, with a lead that swims). Positive is not likely, nor always reachable: near home two or more tiers above the lead weigh under 1 in 5,000 together (§ "Fierce animals are rare near spawn"), and four above (a bear, a moose, a European bison, a humpback whale or an orca for a starter) `9^−16`, under the `2^−32` steps a roll's number takes (`Rng.next`), so no roll there picks one; from the wild radius every species of the realm sits ten thousand steps or more above nothing, for every lead, on any ground. A species whose only habitat never grows an encounter tile is unreachable — which the otter was, because the river biome was water and sand only, until the banks got reeds. Up in the air (#91) the sky over every biome has birds (`skyTableAt`: the birds whose skies it is, the sea eagle's reaching the sea, and visitors in every sky where a bigger bird flies), every bird is on a sky of every lead near home and far out, and far out every one can come out; so every bird can be met in the air too, the sea eagle over the deep water, and the buzzard, which lives in the meadow, in its tall grass as well. Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` ("lists every animal living there, whoever leads…", "every species is on a table of every lead…, and far out every one can come out", "near home a starter meets a wolf hardly ever and a bear or a whale never…", "grows tall grass or deep water in at least one habitat", "lists only animals that live in the realm the tile is in", "lists exactly the biome's species…", and § the sky: "lists only birds, over every biome; every bird flies over a sky of every lead…") over every lead and several seeds, the sea's included, and `habitat.test.ts` ("only the sea animals live only in the water…", "only the birds fly, each over the skies of where it lives…").

### A land's tables hold only its own animals, and in The Arctic only its pole's

Every table (`encounterTable`, and the tile's and the sky's built on it) lists only species of the land it is asked for (`EncounterSite.land`, the land the kid is in), each living, or as a visitor flying or walking, somewhere of the biome's own land and pole (`samePlace`): a biome of another land is quiet there, so The Arctic laid out on Nordland's ground before its map holds nothing, and a Nordland kid never meets an arctic animal. Every species is in exactly one land's `species`, and lists only biomes (and skies) of that land; in The Arctic only one pole's, but for the Arctic tern, on both as in nature (#192: no penguin in the north, no polar bear in the south). An animal of the water alone lives only in the open seas and under the ice, and what a fishing hole can hook is its ice's animals of the water alone, #192's table (`encounters.test.ts` § the fishing holes: under a frozen lake the Arctic char, under the Arctic's sea ice the polar cod, the ringed seal and the Greenland shark, under the Antarctic's the krill, the icefish, the Weddell seal and the toothfish; each swims in its pole's open sea too). Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` ("keeps each land to its own animals, and in The Arctic each pole…": every lead, biome, realm, land and a sweep of distances; the puffin never visits the south's sky) and `lands.test.ts` ("every species is of one land, and lives and flies only there…"). Design-time (#191, #192): the visitors near home were any species of the lead's tier, which would have sent a puffin to the Antarctic's sky and a squirrel to The Arctic's.

### Fierce animals are rare near spawn and never rarer further out, whoever leads

For every lead tier `L`: inside `SAFE_RADIUS` tiles of the spawn tile, in every biome, tier `L + 1` holds at most 1/10 of the encounter table and tiers `L + 2` and up together under 1 in 5,000, and wherever anything of tier `L` or above lives, tier `L` holds the majority; the share of tiers above `L`, and of tiers `L + 2` and up, never falls with distance, and tier `L`'s never rises; beyond `WILD_RADIUS` no visitor remains and a tier `k` above the lead weighs what a tier `k` below it does. It holds by construction on the biome's table (`encounterTable`): each tier's residents together weigh the bell of its distance `k` from the lead's tier, with its side above the lead pulled in near home and moving geometrically out to the side below it at the wild radius ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Wild encounters" has the formulas; `TIER_SIGMA` and `NEAR_ONE_UP` in `encounters.ts`), so the tiers above only grow; visitors come on top of the lead's tier, adding `VISITORS_WEIGHT` of its bells to it times `1 − danger` whatever their number (shared with the residents of its size where they are few beside them, § "No visitor is commoner…", which moves nothing between tiers), and only thin out. With σ = 1 the lead's tier keeps the majority near home even with every tier living beside it (53.6% for a wolf in the forest, the least), and a σ much above 1.1 would lose it. The mountains, where most residents are bigger than a small lead, keep their start through visitors of the lead's tier (`encounters.ts`), as a red deer's mountains did while none of its tier lived there, before the eagle-owl (#89); a new biome with bigger animals and none of some lead's tier keeps it only if it gets visitors too. The Arctic's land biomes keep it without visitors: wherever an animal lives there, every size between it and the smallest lives there too, which #192's second wave made true by placing the reindeer on the fells and the Arctic tern and the gyrfalcon on the sea ice, as in nature; before that, the fell's wolves and gyrfalcons left a reindeer in front none of its size near home (the raven 71% of the table, the two tier-4 animals 13%, one tier up past its 1 in 10), and the sea ice's glaucous gull and polar bear did the same to a tern or a gyrfalcon. Its ice has no encounter tile in the water, and its fishing holes hook only the animals of the water (`holeTable`), never the polar bear or a penguin that walks and swims there. A new species or a retuned constant that breaks any of these fails the suite.

The skies keep the same promises (#91): a sky's table (`encounterTable` in the air realm) is built by the same bell, and visitors come in every sky, not only at the river and in the mountains. Without them the river's, the mountains' and the sea's skies, where every bird is bigger than a robin, would send a robin near home a heron or an eagle every time; with them a robin there meets robins, and one tier up is at most 1 in 10 wherever it flies. Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` § the sky: every lead's sky tables against the test's own copy of the rule at every distance, the near-home bounds and the majority, and bigger birds never rarer with distance on any of a set of grounds.

The table on a tile (`encounterTableAt`) keeps the near-spawn part on every tile, in every world, on any ground: a tier's share there is its biome share times its animals' mean ground factor raised to `danger`, which is 0 inside `SAFE_RADIUS`, so the ground only re-divides each tier's share among its animals. Both distance rules also hold on any fixed ground, and by construction: there the logarithm of a tier's weight on the tile grows with `danger` by its bell's growth plus the logarithm of its animals' mean factor, which is at least `k²·(ln(1/NEAR_ONE_UP) − 1/2σ²)` = 1.70·k² for a tier `k` above the lead, and at most `ln HABITAT_BOOST` = 1.39 for any other tier: the bell below the lead never moves, and the lead's own tier, whose three extra bells thin out by `1 − danger` (the visitors', and what its residents share of them), grows at most `ln 4 − 3/4` = 0.64 (its residents' and visitors' ground both at 4, at the safe radius), whatever share of the three bells the residents take (#136). So `ln(1/NEAR_ONE_UP) − 1/2σ² ≥ ln HABITAT_BOOST` is load-bearing, and a test pins it. The lead's own tier may gain there as a kid walks out: where animals of different tiers favour different ground, the ground's pull as `danger` rises takes share from the animals below it, never from the bigger ones. That is up to 15.6 points for a tier-5 lead in an open glade of the forest, where the bison, of the open grass, is its size (#89), 57.0% of the table near home to 72.6% from the wild radius, and 8.3 for a red deer in front on meadow grass among trees, 57.4% to 65.7%, where nothing bigger lives to come on either; the world never gets gentler, which is what the promise is for. Only the far endpoint is the biome's: from `WILD_RADIUS` out the ground sorts the tiers by where their animals live, as the human asked ([[DECISIONS]] § Gameplay).

Enforced by `encounters.test.ts`: § encounterTable checks every lead's table against its own copy of the rule at every distance, the tiers' shares against their bells whatever their numbers of kinds, the near-home bounds and the majority, both distance rules and the own tier's on the biome's table, the symmetry far out, and the inequality; § encounterTableAt checks both distance rules on every ground in a set; § the start checks every tier's share against the biome's inside the safe radius, for every lead, biome and 729 grounds. Incident: PR #6's tables kept the promise only in biomes with a tier-1 resident, so near spawn the river was all otters and the mountains 83% wolves — and with the prototype seed the tile next to spawn is a river reed, and in 1 world in 7 the nearest tall grass to spawn is on a mountain. PR #64's first cut multiplied every weight by the ground at every distance, which let wolves and bears pass 5% by rocks near home for a lead of tier 2 or 3, in the prototype world too; the adversarial review found it, and the tiers near spawn were given back their biome shares. #89: with the stoats and adders by the rocks and the roe deer and badgers by the trees, the rule's old clause, the own tier's share never rising on any ground, failed in 279 steps of the sweep (a tier-2 or tier-3 lead in the meadow), each time with the tier below losing and nothing bigger ever falling; the human approved the clause about the bigger animals in its place. The bell's first cut had the visitors share out their tier's bell instead of coming on top of it: as they thinned out near the wild radius, the residents they left behind (the lizard on a rocky tile, the frogs by the water) made the starter's tier commoner there and the bigger animals rarer, by 6.5 points in the mountains; the sweep on a set of grounds found it. Its second cut weighed each visitor as a resident, so a new small animal living at the river made the river's bigger animals commoner there near home (1 in 37 to 1 in 30); the adversarial review found it, and the visitors became one block. A block of one bell then made the river's own frogs, rats and toads half the starter's small animals by the reeds, and its win there right 7 times in 10 fell to 37.5%, under `balance.test.ts`'s band; three bells put it back where it was.

### No visitor is commoner than an animal of its size living there

On the biome's table (`encounterTable`), at every distance, for every lead and biome, a visitor weighs no more than any resident of the lead's tier there. `visitorShares` (`encounters.ts`) gives the visitors `VISITORS_WEIGHT` bells to share evenly, unless each one's share would then be more than the tier's `1 + VISITORS_WEIGHT` bells shared evenly by residents and visitors, and then it is that; the residents take what the visitors leave, and both parts beyond the residents' own bell thin out by `1 − danger`, so the tier's weight, and every tier's share, is exactly what the bell and the three bells make it. The two ways meet where three visitors come for each resident (the starter's reeds, nine for three), so a new kind never makes the weights jump. On a tile the ground can still favour a visitor over a resident, up to four times, as it favours any animal of the ground it likes. The skies keep it too (#91), their tables being `encounterTable`'s in the air: a bird visiting a sky weighs no more than one of its size flying there (with a tier-2 bird in front, the heron and the buzzard over the forest weigh what its tawny owl does). Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` ("no visitor weighs more than an animal of its size living there…", every lead, biome and distance of the sweep; the wolf's hills and the starter's reeds pinned; "for every lead is the table [[PRODUCT]] §4 writes out…" against its own copy of the rule; and § the sky, "no visitor weighs more than a bird of its size flying there…", the four skies where it bites pinned). Incident: #136, with #89's second wave: the visitors' three bells went to however many kinds visited, and the sea eagle, the one tier-4 animal that visits the mountains, weighed all three beside the four tier-4 animals living there, twelve times each of them: 62% of a wolf's battles in the hills near home, 56% in World 1's with the ground. Capping each visitor at a resident's share alone would have fixed it and brought back #128's second-cut failure: the tier's weight would have fallen with the cap, making bigger animals commoner wherever it bit (the eagle-owl from 12% to 29% of a wolf's hills), and a new resident of the starter's size at the river would have made its reeds harder.

### Neither the lead nor the ground changes how often something comes out

`rollEncounter(rng, site, leadTier, caught)`'s first draw is the encounter chance, and no table, ground or book feeds into it, where `leadTier` is the tier of the lead where the step lands, the party's first animal that isn't tired and can fight there (`rollEncounterFor` picks it: § "Nothing challenges a team…" below). Every biome has animals of the realm of its encounter tile, so on every tall grass and deep water a step starts a battle for a tier-5 lead exactly when it would for a tier-1 lead, on any ground; only where nothing of the tile's realm lives (tall grass in the sea, which the world never grows, and deep snow on a frozen lake, which The Arctic's map never lays: its drifts are plain snow) is the roll `null`, without a draw. A stronger lead therefore meets exactly as many animals as the starter, on the same steps, only bigger ones on the whole. Up in the air, likewise, `rollSkyEncounter` draws its one in twenty first: a bird notices the glider over the same tiles whoever leads in the air, on any ground, over any kind of tile (`encounters.test.ts` § the sky). Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` ("neither the lead nor the ground changes whether a step starts a battle", seed for seed, over every lead, biome and a set of grounds; "stays quiet without a draw only where nothing of the tile realm lives…") and by `packages/client/test/local-authority.test.ts` § the lead decides who comes out, which walks the reed by spawn with a squirrel, a fox (in front, and behind a tired squirrel) and a bear, on the same steps. Design-time: the human's rule, 2026-09-25. Its other half, that nothing two or more tiers below the lead came out, went with the bell (2026-09-27), and with it the grass a big lead found quiet.

### The ground changes which animal comes out, never whether one does or which ones could

`encounterTableAt` lists exactly the species of the biome's table, in its order. Each species' factor is `HABITAT_BOOST ^ s` (`habitat.ts`), where `s`, from 0 to 1, is how fully its favourite terrain counts within `HABITAT_RADIUS`. Within a tier, the tier's share goes to its animals in proportion to biome weight × factor; a tier's share is its biome share times its animals' mean factor raised to `danger`, normalised. So every animal keeps between a quarter and four times its biome share, none appears and none vanishes. When one tile around turns into a species' favourite terrain (open ground from water, trees or rocks included), that species' share never falls, whoever leads, wherever, at any distance: every species favouring that terrain gains by the same factor `g` and every other species by no more, so every tier's weighed share grows by some `x_u ≤ g`, and the species' share changes by `g · x_t^(danger − 1)` over a weighted mean of the `x_u^danger`, which is at least 1 (`x_t` is its own tier's). A malformed ground (a missing count, `NaN`, a negative) throws on an encounter tile, because a `NaN` weight would fall through the pick's rounding fallback to the last, fiercest animal every time (PR #6's failure with a `NaN` distance). `surroundings(seed, pos)` is a pure function of the world, like a chunk. Enforced by `habitat.test.ts` (the count against a brute-force one on both sides of both axes, the factor against the rule's literals, malformed grounds) and `encounters.test.ts` § encounterTableAt (the same species and the quarter-to-four bound over every lead, biome, distance sample and 729 grounds; every lead's table re-derived from [[PRODUCT]] §4's literals on every ground, the starter's on all 729 and the others' on 125; every move of one tile between grounds; the rate on any ground). Design-time: the human's request, 2026-09-26.

### An encounter can only start on an encounter tile, or on a tile the glider enters, and only by a bird

`rollEncounter` returns `null` for every tile kind that `isEncounterTile` rejects (everything but tall grass, The Arctic's deep snow and deep water: the shallows, the ice, a fishing hole too), without drawing from the rng, so a walk's random stream depends only on the steps taken onto encounter tiles. And only a step walked or sailed rolls it: a flight with the glider rolls no walk's encounter, over tall grass and deep water or down on it (§ "A flight always ends on a tile…"). Up in the air only a bird can notice the kid (#91): the authority rolls `rollSkyEncounter` over each tile the glider enters (the landing tile included, and the tiles a let-go glides on to), only while a bird stands in the team (the lead in the air), from the sky's own salt, and stops once one bird follows; its battle is in the air, and starts as the kid lands. Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` § rollEncounter and § the sky (only birds on a sky's table), and by `local-authority.test.ts` § the glider (160 tiles of deep water flown with an otter in front, and a landing on the reed at 81 step counts where a step onto it meets animals: no battle) and § birds in the air (a team with no bird standing, over 480 flights: no bird, no battle; with a robin, a bird on 45–70% of 17-tile flights, never two, its battle in the air right after the landing). Design-time; #91 for the birds.

### Nothing challenges a team with nobody standing who can fight where the step lands

`rollEncounterFor(rng, site, party, caught)` is the one roll a step makes (`LocalAuthority.move`): it takes the lead in the realm of the tile (`encounterRealm`, then `leadIndex`: on the tall grass the first animal standing that walks, out on the deep water the first that swims), and is exactly `rollEncounter` for that lead's tier; with no lead it is `null` and draws nothing. So a team that needs the druid (`needsDoctor`: after a lost battle, every walker tired) walks to one in peace, and a boat with no swimmer standing sails in peace, and neither shifts the rolls of the steps after. Enforced by `encounters.test.ts` § rollEncounterFor (1,500 random teams, tired and standing, of every species, on every biome's encounter tile: the lead's roll and its draws exactly, or `null` and no draw; a tired team on every tile kind; a crab standing is nobody in the grass and someone at sea) and `local-authority.test.ts` (400 steps on the reed after a loss meet nothing, and after the druid the reed meets animals again; 300 steps on the deep water after a loss at sea meet nothing; a tired team's go-to, trip and reload). ### The book picks which animal of a size comes out, never how big

Every roll (`rollEncounter`, `rollSkyEncounter`, `castLine`) picks from its table through `favourUncaught(table, caught)`, with `caught` the animal book's species caught (`LocalAuthority`'s book): within each tier, a kind not in `caught` weighs `UNCAUGHT_BOOST` (2) times its table weight, and the tier keeps exactly its share of the table. So the book never changes whether a step starts a battle, nor the share of any size, and every promise about sizes (the near-spawn caps, the bell, the ground's) holds whatever the kid has caught; the same species are listed in the same order, and a tier all caught or none of it, an empty book included, is unchanged. ## Druid

### A knock-out never strands the player

`knockOut` never moves the player and heals nobody, and it leaves a team that needs the druid (`needsDoctor`: nobody standing who can fight where the player is, nor on land) only where a tent is within `TENT_SEARCH_STEPS` of them the way they get about: on foot, or with the boat (`gear`) over the water too, in the world as they left it. Where none is, a druid comes (`doctorComes`) and the whole party is at full HP, unless the kid owns the paraglider (`glider`), which flies them out as it flew them in. The same rule (`careFor`) runs wherever the live game puts a player without walking or flying there: a go-to and a trip to another world (`LocalAuthority.careForTeam`). A walk never leaves the ground a tent is reached over, and a glide can always be flown back the way it came, so a kid is never left with a team that can't battle and no way to a druid; and never healed for free: a spot no tent can be walked to from is reached by gliding in (a go-to lands only where the ground opens 64 tiles wide, `arrivalSpot`'s `ESCAPE_REACH`; the second cold review of #116 sampled worlds 1 to 8 over ±2,500 tiles and found every spot with no tent a walk away to be a walled pocket no go-to reaches), so a druid there for a kid with the glider made a trip out and back, as often as they liked, a full heal (the adversarial review of #116, after glides into such a pocket stopped healing on reload); a loaded save does not ask again (§ "A loaded save never strands the player"). That holds because `nearestTent` is a search over the moves the player can make from where they are (`isPassable` ground, and onto the ice the whole slide `moveFrom` makes, each tile a step), not a straight-line nearest: the tent nearest as the crow flies can be on an island or boxed in by trees, and a kid sent there could never arrive; and a way that stopped on the ice could cross a lake a kid only slides past the far end of. Its stand is one a kid can stop on, the fewest tiles away by the moves a kid makes (`arctic-shop.test.ts` § the way to a tent on the ice, against a slow search of its own over `moveFrom`, red with the slide taken out of the search). Out on the water, a battle is lost with animals that can't swim still standing, so `knockOut` is told the realm and asks only that every animal that could fight there is tired; a team with a walker standing can still battle on land, needs no druid, and none comes. Up in the air a battle is lost once every bird is tired, and the kid is on the tile the glider came down on: `knockOut` looks at the team there, on the ground (or on the water, in the boat), never in the air, and the way to a tent goes from there, walked or sailed, never flown. Every lost battle is looked at where the kid stands (`tileRealm` of their tile), so a fish hooked through a fishing hole and fought in the water is lost on the bank, where a walker still standing walks on and no druid comes. The arrow to a tent (`DoctorWay`) points only at a tent `nearestTent` found, so it never leads where the kid can't get. Enforced by `tents.test.ts`, which checks `nearestTent` against its own flood fill over the tent lattice from random starts of every kind and sign, on foot and, from the water and beside it, with the boat, and never lets a boxed-in tent be chosen; by `doctor.test.ts` § knockOut (the party as a real lost battle left it, near spawn in three worlds; a walled-in tile, where a druid comes; out on the water with the boat; up in the air, looked at on the tile the glider came down on; the path the kid cut; the refusals) and § needsDoctor and doctorComes (the rule re-derived over 3,000 random teams in every realm; never to a kid with the glider; `careFor` the knock-out rule's own); and by `local-authority.test.ts` (the walled-in spot with and without its path, the losses at sea, a trip back to a spot no tent is walked to from, where a druid comes to a kid without the glider and never to one with it, however often they go out and back) and `doctor-way.test.ts` (the arrow's tent is `nearestTent`'s at every step of a 400-step walk, after a go-to and a trip). Design-time; the rule changed from a free heal beside the nearest tent to the walk on 2026-09-27, the human's rule.

### A wrong answer at the druid changes nothing, and healing stops at full

In a doctor visit, an answer `checkAnswer` rejects leaves every animal's HP as it was and puts a different puzzle in place of the one missed (a missed fare too: no flight leaves without the right answer to its fare, and a paid one costs nothing but the puzzle, `doctor.test.ts` § flying from the druid); a right one sets exactly the hurt animals of the picked one's species to their `maxHp`, and no other; no step lowers an HP or raises one above `maxHp`. Enforced by `doctor.test.ts` over every species at three HP levels × 25 seeds × every shape of wrong answer (beside a hurt animal of another species, which must stay hurt), "one right answer heals every hurt animal of the patient's species, and no other", and by a check on every step of every visit that each HP change comes with a `healed` event. Design-time; healing by species since the druid's shop (a team of many of a kind).

### Nothing leaves the team, and no token or tool changes hands, without a right answer to its sum

At the druid, animals leave the party only on a right answer (`checkAnswer`) to a hand-over's sum, exactly the animals the hand-over named, made better (at `maxHp`) as they go, and never so many that nobody standing stays who can fight on land, where every tent stands (`keep-one`, `keepsATeam`, which the druid's card asks too: a team of only tired animals meets nothing in the grass, and would walk away from the druid needing one; a team of only sea animals could battle nothing on the way back, and `doctor.test.ts` goes red with a standing crab counted); the tokens rise only then, by exactly the hand-over's reward (`homeTokens`). An item joins `items` only on a right answer to a purchase's sum, once (`already-owned`), and the tokens fall only then, by exactly its price, never below 0 (`not-enough-tokens` before the sum is even asked). A wrong answer asks the same sum and changes nothing; `back`, another pick or `leave` puts the sum away and changes nothing. The authority writes what a right answer did back at once (`party-changed`, `belongings-changed`), and the save carries it, so a reload never undoes it and never completes a sum that was not answered. Enforced by `doctor.test.ts`, whose `apply` checks every accepted step of every visit against these rules (the random kid of the replay sweep hands animals over, buys, backs out and misses, and meets every refusal), by its hand-over and buying tests over every item and every shape of wrong answer, and by `local-authority.test.ts` ("animals gone home and tokens given, and an item bought, are written back at once"). The negative controls go red: a purchase on a wrong answer, a balance one token off, and "keep one" counted without asking who is standing. ### An item is on sale only once what it does is built

Design-time.

### Everything in the druid's card stays in it, and nothing covers its buttons

At every size a kid plays on ([[UI_SPEC]] § Frame: the phones held sideways, with an iPhone's notch and without; the tablets, with an iPad's home indicator and without; the laptop; and 768×1024 held upright with a keyboard), in both languages, with a finger and with a keyboard, in every state and beat of the card: every piece stays inside its card and the safe area, and what a row of the list shows between the list's sides; Back, the number pad's keys, the tabs, Set free, Bye and the confirm's choices are whole, and nothing lies over them; the right-hand side's pieces keep apart; and the doctor's line keeps off the badge and the tokens. That holds with the widest names (twelve W's, three of them going home at once), the priciest tool (the paraglider), four- and five-digit balances and rewards, the longest heal prompt, and a heal of each of the Arctic's picture kinds and its balance (#192: the biggest picture each asks at a tier-2 animal's healing difficulty), which has the whole card. ### A doctor visit is a pure function of `(seed, party, intents)` and keeps the seed out of its state

Like a battle: `applyDoctorIntent(state, intent, seed)` never mutates its input, draws the n-th accepted intent's puzzles from `new Rng(hashInts(seed, n))`, and returns the same state reference with one `rejected` event for an intent that does not fit. The seed is not in `DoctorState`, which goes to the client; a client that knew it could see the next puzzle before asking for it. Enforced by `doctor.test.ts` (frozen input on every step, the replay sweep, and a check of the state's keys). Design-time.

## Animal book

### The animal book only grows, and every kind in the party is in it, caught

A species goes into the book (`animals/book.ts`) at the event that shows it — seen as a wild battle starts (`recordBattle` with the battle's first state), caught at the leash throw that lands (`recordBattle` with that step's events), set free at the right answer to a hand-over's sum that sends one of its kind home (`recordWentHome` with that doctor step's events: one of several is enough, and a kind still on the team is set free all the same) — and nothing else puts one in (a friendly match changes nothing but the puzzles solved: § Match), and nothing takes one out: running away, a lost battle, setting an animal free at the druid, travelling, a reload. Every kind caught or set free is seen too. Every kind in the party is caught: a new game's starter (`newGame`), whatever party a game is started with (`LocalAuthority.run`, `recordParty`: a `?party=` game's too), and a save's party (`restoreGame`, which gives a save from before the book the book its party and a saved battle's wild animal prove). Enforced by `book.test.ts` (sightings, catches and kinds set free in any order only grow it; over battles with every species, the wild animal seen from the battle's first state and caught exactly at the step whose throw landed; over random visits through the doctor's reducer, a kind set free exactly at the step that took one of it out of the party), `save.test.ts` § the animal book in a save (a kid's save from before the book back-filled, eleven animals of eight kinds among them; a saved battle's animal met; kept as saved, set free or not; mended when short; round trip), `local-authority.test.ts` § the animal book (a walk's animals met once each, the catch said right after the throw, a kind set free still caught, and set free, through travel and a save; a wrong answer sets nothing free; one fox of three set free, and a second one says nothing new) and `match-controller.test.ts` ("leaves the animal book alone": a match against kinds the kid never met changes nothing). Design-time: the human asked for animals caught "whether they still have them or not".

### The animal book never names a species the catalog lacks

`seeSpecies`, `catchSpecies` and `freeSpecies` ignore an id no species of the catalog has, so no saved battle or hand-edited save can put one in the book. A kid's own save naming one would read as a newer build's (§ Saves) and stop their game until a newer build came. Enforced by `book.test.ts` ("never writes an id the catalog does not have"). Design-time.

## Party

### The party changes only while exploring

`applyPartyIntent(party, intent, activity)` refuses every intent — choosing a lead, moving, naming — unless `activity` is `explore`, returning the same party with one `rejected { reason: 'not-exploring' }`; up in the air with the glider the activity is `flight`, and the screen sends none (a number key, a card, a drag do nothing there). A battle, and a doctor visit, each work on their own copy of the party and write it back when they end: an edit made in the meantime would be undone, and a lead chosen mid-battle would name a different animal than the one fighting. The authority says what the player is doing; the engine applies the rule. Enforced by `party.test.ts` ("changes nothing outside explore") and `local-authority.test.ts` (mid-battle edits are refused, and the battle writes back the party it began with). Design-time.

### The party is always in species bundles, and a catch always joins it

Every animal of a species stands with the others of its kind, so the HUD's cards top to bottom are the battle order. `applyPartyIntent` returns a party in bundles from every move it accepts (`select-lead`, `lead-species`, `reorder` only within a bundle, `move-species`; a party that is not in bundles is bundled first), and `rename` changes no one's place. A caught animal joins through `joinParty`: at the end of its kind's bundle, or as a new bundle at the end, however many animals there are. A party from outside is put in bundles on the way in, keeping who leads (`bundled`: when gathering each kind behind its first animal would put a standing animal in front of the lead, as a squirrel caught after a fox that leads behind a tired squirrel, the lead's bundle goes first): `restoreGame` bundles a loaded party (an old save's, whose kinds could be mixed) and a saved battle's party the same way, with the same animal in front, so the battle plays on; the authority bundles `?party=`. Enforced by `party.test.ts` (`bundled` over 400 parties in any order, the same lead before and after, every intent over random parties of up to 150 animals keeping the bundles, `joinParty`'s place over every test party, 300 catches in a row), `save.test.ts` (random saves restore in bundles with the same lead; a save from before bundles, mid-battle, plays on with the same hits) and `local-authority.test.ts` (catches past the old six join their bundle). Design-time: the human asked for one card per species and no cap on the party; the six-animal cap had let a seventh catch go home. The adversarial review of #66 found the first cut of `bundled` changing who led an old save whose first animal was tired.

### The lead is `leadIndex`, wherever it is shown or used

The animal whose card the HUD and the pause menu mark "goes first" (and that an open card tags itself), the one `startBattle` sends out and the one whose tier a step's roll is for (`rollEncounterFor`, and up in the air the authority passes to `rollSkyEncounter`) are all `leadIndex(party, realm)` for where the player is: the first animal in party order with HP above 0 that can fight there (`canFightIn`: out on the water, one that swims; up in the air, a bird), which on land in a party kept in bundles is the first one standing on the first card that has one. The client reads the realm from the player's tile (`game.realm`, the engine's `tileRealm`), and up on the glider it is the air, from `took-off` to `landed`, as the authority's is; the one following the trainer in the air, the chaser's battle's first animal and the "goes first" tag are all the lead in the air. (Mid-battle the player decides who is in front, by a switch or by picking who steps in after a knock-out, and the party's order doesn't change: § "Only a `switch` changes the animal in front".) Nothing computes the lead another way, and there is no stored "selected" animal to drift from it: choosing a lead (`select-lead`) moves that animal's bundle to the front and it to the front of its bundle, and is refused for a tired one and for one that can't fight where the player stands (`cannot-fight-here`); choosing a card (`lead-species`) does the same for its first animal standing, and is refused when all of them are tired or the species can't fight there. Enforced by `party.test.ts` (after every accepted `select-lead`, and after every move, `startBattle` sends out `leadIndex`; out on the water, over every party, only a swimmer goes first and it is the one that fights there; `leadIndex` re-derived for both realms) and `local-authority.test.ts` ("a chosen lead is the animal that steps into the next battle", "choosing a lead in explore changes who comes out", "out on the water only an animal that swims goes first", "the lead in the air is the first bird standing, whoever leads on the ground"), and `explore-controller.test.ts` (up in the air "goes first" is the first bird standing, over the water never the swimmer, and with no bird nobody). Design-time; anything new that depends on the lead reads it here.

### A stored nickname is a fixed point of `normalizeNickname`

Every nickname in the authority's party came out of `normalizeNickname`, and cleaning it again changes nothing: 1 to 12 code points of letters (each with at most four marks that belong on it: on a Latin, Greek or Cyrillic letter one of the listed accents; on another letter a mark of its own script, for the listed scripts, or a named generic mark such as an Arabic vowel), digits, `-`, `'`, `.` and single inner spaces, at least one letter or digit, NFKC, no mark on anything but a letter, and nothing default-ignorable (the Hangul fillers are Unicode "letters" that draw as nothing, so a name of them would look blank). Every way a nickname enters the party goes through it — a `rename`, a new game's starter, and the party the authority starts with (`?party=`, or a loaded save, its battle included) — so every screen shows a name as stored and they all agree. One cleaning pass is not enough — about 1 random string in 400 changes on a second pass, because dropping a character can put two Hangul jamo side by side for NFKC to join, and NFKC can turn a letter into an apostrophe look-alike — so the cleaner repeats until a pass changes nothing. Enforced by `party.test.ts` (6,000 strings drawn from hostile Unicode ranges, every rename in random runs of edits, checks that a plainly typed name, in Latin letters or in alphabets that put marks on letters, comes back as typed, and a named list of decorating marks that must all go), `local-authority.test.ts` ("a party it starts with is cleaned like a rename", "cleans a saved nickname the way a rename would, in the party and in a saved battle") and `starters.test.ts` ("cleans the nickname the way a rename does, and drops one with nothing usable left"). Design-time; a one-pass cleaner fails the fuzz. The adversarial reviews of the party-pause PR found, in turn: every mark dropped (misspelling Hindi and Hebrew names); a cap of two that misspelled Burmese "ကျော်" and pointed Hebrew; and a block list of decorations that missed the underline, the overline and a dozen more. The rule is now an allow rule (a mark stays only on a letter it belongs on), and the named lists pin all three.

## Input

### A press acts on one screen, and a new choice never takes a pick from a mash

Each key press goes to exactly one screen, the one up when it goes down, chosen by `main.ts` before any screen acts on it; explore's `Keyboard` hears no key press by itself and starts off, so the key that closes the title, a result card or the druid's card is never also a step or a word with the druid. A tap on an overlay button presses only when its pointer went down on the button and comes up on it (or hardly moved) while the same screen is up, each pointer followed on its own (`input/taps.ts`); the number pad and the D-pad press as the finger lands instead, and the browser's click from a pointer presses nothing. Every screen that puts up a choice a kid can't take back takes a pick only after a quiet moment since the choice came up and since the last Enter, Space or number key, counted or not (`input/pick-guard.ts`; which screens, and how long: [[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode, "A quiet moment"), so a mash at any pace never picks. Enforced by `battle-controller.test.ts`, `doctor-controller.test.ts`, `title-controller.test.ts` and `keyboard.test.ts`, which mash at 2, 4 and 8 presses a second, unevenly and late every fourth (`test/mash.ts`), through the menu, the knock-out list, a won result card, the druid's list at the tent and after each heal, the confirm, the starters, the name box and Talk; by `match-controller.test.ts` ("takes Yes only after a quiet moment, never from a mash") and `account-controller.test.ts` (the account cards' Enter and taps wait their quiet moment); by `keyboard.test.ts` (no key-press listener of its own, nothing taken before explore has the screen); and by `pointer-input.test.ts` (a finger lifted over a button it did not go down on, or on another screen, presses nothing; two fingers tap on their own). Each goes red with the code it replaced. Incidents: #27, a fixed guard after the menu came back, which a long mash outlasted; #37, a mash measured by the gap between two presses (0.3 s), which one slightly slow press in an uneven four-a-second mash broke in 4 turns out of 4, skipping "You won!" too; #38, the explore keyboard queued a talk before `main.ts` routed the Enter to the title's Continue; #39 and #40, the browser's click, which landed on the battle row under a thumb lifted from the D-pad, and never came for a second finger's tap on Talk or Menu.

### A tap presses what its finger touched, or nothing

A pointer key says which list it comes from as well as which row, so two lists a pointer can reach at once never share a key: the pause menu's team and settings are `row:<i>` and the rows beside them (the picked animal's options, or a card's options and animals) `option:<i>` (`input/press.ts`), and the explore HUD's party column names a card by its species and an animal by its id, never by place, and a list under a modal is `inert` (the title's menu under the confirm, whose rows are `row:<i>` too; the shade stops a finger, `inert` a screen reader's click). A button whose key changed while a finger rested on it (a team row another finger moved up) presses nothing when the finger lifts (`input/taps.ts`), which is also why a card carried in a drag is given another key until it is dropped: the drag is never also a tap. The same holds between two presses: the second half of a double click or tap that lands, within `DOUBLE_TAP_MS`, where the first half's button has just moved away from (a re-sort put another there, or the screen changed), on a button with another key, presses nothing. One animal's options, and one card's screen, are a screen apart from another's (`main.ts`), since a tap on the team swaps them in place. What the finger touched is the whole button, its pictures as much as its words: WebKit (Safari on an iPad) gives a finger's lift to the part of the button it landed on, not to the button that holds the pointer, and a picture there is an SVG element, which is no `HTMLElement`, so `watchTaps` asks the button about any node the lift came to, never only an HTML one. Enforced by `pause-controller.test.ts` (every row on the left, beside every animal's options in teams of one to six, and beside every card's screen and its animals' options in teams with cards of several, does what it does on the list and never an option; beside the name box, nothing) and `pointer-input.test.ts` (neither key reads as the other; a row that moved under a resting finger presses nothing; a double click's second half on the row that slid into the first one's place presses nothing, while one on a row that stayed presses it again; a lift on the star inside a level button presses that level, and one on another button's star nothing), each red with the code it replaced; the title's `inert` and the options' screen are checked on screen only (the covered menu out of the accessibility tree; two fingers over CDP). Incidents: #45, the options read every `row:<i>` as their own, so with three animals and the fox's options open, the first team row did Go first for the fox, Language opened New name and Sound did Back; #72, a double click on Pip, second on an open card, put Pip in front, and its second click landed on Rusty, who had slid into Pip's row, so Rusty went first (a card of one animal further down, double-clicked, went to the top and then sent the card that slid down into its place there too); #175, on an iPad a tap on a level's star, Run's icon, a shop tool's picture or the animal book's icon pressed nothing, since the lift's target was passed on only when it was an `HTMLElement`, and every check had run in Chrome, which gives the lift to the button.

## Presence

### A socket is in one world at most, and two players see each other or neither

A socket is in the room of the land and world its last `where` named (one room per land of each world since #191: Nordland 42 and Arktis 42 are two), or in none, never two: another world, or another land of it, moves it, after telling everyone who saw it that it has gone (and it, that they have). Within a room the hub keeps who sees whom both ways at once: two players within `VIEW_RADIUS` see each other, two further apart than `VIEW_KEEP` do not, and every browser draws exactly the players it was told about and no one who left. Enforced by `presence-hub.test.ts` ("keeps lands apart within a world": the same tile in Nordland 42 and Arktis 42, unseen and unfound, then a flight that brings the two together), whose property test walks, moves between worlds and lands, replaces and drops random players and checks after every step that sight is both ways, stays in one world, follows the distances, and that each fake browser's `peer`/`gone` picture equals the hub's own record. Incident: the first version told the others a player had left for another world, but not that player, whose screen kept the old world's players.

### One identity is present once

A second socket of the same guest id or the same account takes the first one's place: the first is told `bye: replaced` and closed, and the others see one player, never two, under the same public id. The page told so stops (`elsewhere`) until it is played in again, so two windows never take the place back and forth. Enforced by `presence-hub.test.ts` and `presence-socket.test.ts` (by guest id and by account) and `presence-connection.test.ts`. Design-time.

### A deploy moves every player to the next server without drawing anyone twice

A server told to stop (SIGTERM, `restart()`) sends every socket `bye: restart` and closes it with `byeCloseCode('restart')` before any close is heard, so no page is told that another left (`gone`), and it takes no new socket. A page that hears either comes straight back (`RESTART_RETRY_MS`), not as a failure, and holds the players on its screen and in its list while it does; public ids are a keyed hash of who each player is with a secret every copy of the server shares (`idSecret`, from `DATABASE_URL`), so the next copy names each player as the last did and the page draws them on where they stood: nobody twice, no poof, no note. With a public id salted afresh per server, as first built, every deploy would have drawn each player twice for three seconds, the old figure fading beside the new. Enforced by `presence-socket.test.ts` (the restart, the same id on two copies sharing the secret), `presence-connection.test.ts` (straight back, by the bye or by the code alone) and `presence-controller.test.ts` (the hop: one figure, no note, no poof). Design-time.

### Nothing off the socket is used before the engine has read it, and nothing a client made up is passed on

Every message, both ways, goes through `parseClientMessage` / `parseServerMessage` (`net/protocol.ts`) before it is used: null for anything that is not exactly a message (every number whole and in its bounds, every text in its length, text only, within `MAX_MESSAGE_BYTES` from a browser and `MAX_SERVER_MESSAGE_BYTES` from the server), and a new object of the known fields, so a field a client added never reaches another client. The server builds every `peer` from what it parsed, and leaves the furthest off a roster too long for a browser to read (`fitRoster`). A browser once read the server with the 4 KiB bound of what it may send, and dropped every roster of a world of about 40 players with long names: the list, Go to and the notes froze. Enforced by `net.test.ts` (every message through JSON and back; any field swapped for junk or missing refused, but a hi's `boot`, which a server from before it leaves out; extra fields dropped), `presence-socket.test.ts` (junk, binary, too big, a flood), `presence-hub.test.ts`, whose fake peers check that everything the hub sends reads back as the same message through the wire's bound, fifty of the longest names among it, and `presence-connection.test.ts` (a roster over 4 KiB read whole). Design-time.

### Presence never stands in the way of play

Nothing in the game waits on the socket, and nothing the socket meets makes it throw: no WebSocket at all, a server that is down, junk from the server, a send on a closed socket. Without a server the game plays exactly as it does alone; a dropped socket is tried again on its own (`BACKOFF_MS`), and the page says only, in the pause menu's list, that it is looking for the others. Enforced by `presence-connection.test.ts` ("never throws") and `presence-controller.test.ts`. Design-time.

### A battle seen from outside carries no word and no answer

What the players near a battle are sent of it (`net/fight.ts`) is numbers and species ids: a puzzle travels as its `PuzzleFace`, whole numbers within `MAX_FACE_NUMBER` in the places its kind puts them, and the watching page writes it out with the engine's `facePrompt`; an animal goes by its kind, never by a nickname, since a page could fill a nickname with any phrase that passes the name rules, a new one every step, to every kid near it; an event says only whether an answer was right. So a page that lies about its own battle can show the others a made-up fight, but never put a word on anyone's screen (there is no chat), and a kid who watches never sees an answer. Enforced by `fight.test.ts` (whole wild battles and matches: every view and step read back unchanged, no key named `answer`, `input`, `prompt`, `id` or `seed`, and no text but species, sides and kinds, though the battles and matches it plays have nicknamed animals; a nickname off the wire, anywhere, is not read), `face.test.ts` (the formatter and the reader, inverse over every kind and difficulty; text the formatter did not write reads as no face), `net.test.ts` (anything inside a `battle` or a `fight` swapped for junk is refused), `presence-socket.test.ts` (a report with words or nicknames in it reaches the other page without them) and `matches.test.ts` (a match's `fight` frames hold no answer and no prompt). Incident: the first version sent nicknames as the name rules keep them, and the cold review showed a changed page could turn them into a chat, a phrase of twelve letters in every animal of every report.

### A battle is seen only by the players who see one of its players, never by its own, and nothing of it outlasts its end

The hub keeps a battle's view on its players and sends it only to the players who see them (`battle`, `match`), a match once to each who sees either of its two, never to the two; one who comes near is sent it as it stands, right after the player's own `peer`. It takes a page's report only while that page's last `where` says it is in a battle, and forgets the view when the battle ends, when the page says it does anything else (exploring, up in the air, at the druid) or leaves the world. Enforced by `presence-hub.test.ts`, whose fake browsers fail on a `fight` of a player they do not draw or of their own, whose property test sends random reports from pages doing anything, and whose cases cover late arrivals, ends, busy changes, another world and a match with one page away; and by `matches.test.ts` (a third player sees each step of a match, its two players none). Design-time.

### No mark at the edge of the screen is under a piece of the explore HUD, and no way's names cover another's

A friend's arrow, and the way to the druid's tent, ride one track round every piece of the explore HUD, and stand where the line from the player to what they point at first leaves it (`spotOnTrack`, `presence/edges.ts`), each with its own reach: never within it of a piece, so never under the party cards, the top right corner, the coordinates, the message line or a touch control. The arrows were once placed on a plain rectangle inside the screen's edges, drawn under the HUD, and hid under the party cards, the tokens, the D-pad and Menu (#121); two friends far off one way got the very same spot, and one name showed (#140). Friends whose arrows would stand together share one (`gatherWays`), and each way's names keep clear of the pieces, the other arrows and each other (`placeNames`). A piece of the explore HUD counts only if it is marked `data-keep-clear` (`keep-clear.ts`): a new one that is not can hide an arrow. Enforced by `edges.test.ts` (every half degree on the HUD as measured on a tablet and two phones, random screens and pieces, the names at every other degree and in pairs of ways), `presence-controller.test.ts` (two friends on one bearing, one arrow and both names) and `doctor-way.test.ts` (the marker 44 px clear of a tablet's HUD, on its line).

## Rendering

### What leaves the screen for good is disposed, so memory stays flat however far the player walks

Taking an object out of a scene frees nothing: three.js keeps a drawn geometry's buffers and vertex arrays, and an `InstancedMesh`'s instance buffers, until that geometry or mesh is `dispose()`d, for as long as the renderer lives. So a chunk that leaves the 5×5 ring is disposed (`ChunkRing` → `disposeChunkGroup`: its ground's and its props' instance buffers, and the one geometry of its own, its tent's campfire glow), and so is a battle figure when it is replaced or the battle is left (`BattleScene.setFigure`, `end`). A shape many meshes draw is built once and never disposed: the ground box and `PROP_GEOMETRY` in `tiles.ts`, and the druid's shapes at every tent (`DOCTOR_GEOMETRIES` in `doctor.ts`), listed in `SHARED_GEOMETRIES` (which `disposeChunkGroup` skips) and drawn by every chunk and the battle backdrops. A figure's shape (a species, a trainer's look, a boat's trim, a glider's colour) is merged once for every figure of its kind and freed with the last of them: each figure that goes lets go of it once (`merge.ts`, `release`, which `disposeFigure`, `disposeBoat` and `disposeGlider` call), so a kind no longer drawn holds nothing. Materials are module constants (every merged figure draws with one of two, `merge.ts`), except where a thing fades alone: another player's trainer, boat and glider copy theirs, freed when they go, and a poof borrows one of the few kept for poofs, never freed, since three.js frees a shader program with the last material that drew with it and the next poof would compile it again, a stall each time (#152; `poof.test.ts`); a battle's knock-out dust does the same with the dusts kept for it (`SPARE_DUST`, #159; `battle-scene.test.ts`). A chunk therefore owns the instance buffers of its ground and of its props' meshes, and where it has a tent the glow its campfire paints, built for the ground round it, and nothing else on the GPU, and a new kind of prop is a mesh of a new shared shape, never a geometry per tile. Enforced by `render-memory.test.ts`, which counts live geometries and instanced meshes through their `dispose` events without WebGL: a walk round a square five chunks out never holds anything off screen and ends holding what it started with, a jump or a new world frees the old ring, chunks draw only shared shapes but for one glow a tent, ten battles in a row leave no figure behind, and the lead walking behind and the title's scenery free every figure they stop showing; by `others.test.ts`, where twenty other players come, walk, jump and go and leave no geometry but the poof's shared puff, nor any of the materials each trainer fades with; and by `battle-controller.test.ts` ("frees both figures once the result card is left"). The battles seen from outside lend their animals from a pool (`FigurePool`: at most two a kind and `KEPT` in all, the rest freed as they come back, and all of them on a new world), and their rope, loop and stars are shared shapes; `fights.test.ts` has thirty battles come and go and leave no figure's geometry behind. Each of those tests starts with no figure shapes (`forgetShapes`), as a page does, so a figure another test built and left shares nothing with its own. Incident: #24 — nothing was ever disposed, and every decoration built its own geometry, so 616 steps and 25 battles took live GL buffers from 2,247 to 44,544 and the JS heap from 22 to 45 MB.

### A figure drawn in one go draws exactly what its parts drew

`merge.ts` draws each figure as one mesh (#152): its parts merged, their colours on its vertices, the parts that move on their own on bones (the animals, the trainers) or on groups of their own (the boat, the glider). What a kid sees does not change: every species, every trainer's look, the boat and the glider draw the triangles their parts drew, each where its part put it, wound the same way round (a part seen from inside turned over, one seen from both sides there both ways) and in its part's colour, in every pose the animations give them: breathing, walking, a tool swung, the glider's lines held, wings spread and beating, lying down, the boat swung under and rocking, the glider opening, and wherever a scene places them. The facets take their light from where they are (flat shading), never from their normals, so the same triangles are the same pixels. A part merging would draw differently (not flat-shaded Lambert, see-through, textured, hidden) is refused as it is merged. A merged figure measures as its parts did as built (`Box3.setFromObject`, how it lies down), so every scene sizes and places it as before. Enforced by `merge.test.ts`, which compares both triangle for triangle without WebGL, and holds that a skinned figure's bounding sphere keeps every pose in view. The `?zoo` line-up drawn by both builds at one frozen instant differs only where two runs of one build do (the butterflies, the druid's turn, the resting z's).

### The world's lights never change in number

three.js writes the number of each kind of light into every lit material's shader and its program's cache key, so a light added or taken away compiles every lit program on screen again, on the main thread, in the frame it is drawn. The world's scene holds two lights for the page's life, the fill and the sun (`renderer.ts`); nothing a chunk, a figure or an effect adds is a light, and a campfire's warm light is a painted glow (`campfire.ts`, [[DECISIONS]] § Client). The programs a tent draws with, and those of what the scene holds hidden until it is needed (the flier's shadow, the landing ring), are compiled as the page starts (`GameRenderer.warmUp`). Enforced by `render-memory.test.ts` ("hold no light"): a walk through twenty columns of chunks, past their tents, finds no light in any chunk. Incident: #151 — every tent had a point light, which came and went with its chunk; each number of tents not seen yet compiled about five programs at once, and the step froze for 0.26–0.75 s on a CPU four times slower, again and again in the first minutes of play; and each light cost every lit pixel about 1.7 ms of GPU a frame (#150).

### What the screen shows of the world is always built, and the ring's edge comes a piece a frame

`ChunkRing.update` builds at once whatever the 3×3 chunks round the player lack (`SHOWN_RADIUS`: they hold the whole view), so the player never walks, glides or jumps onto ground that isn't drawn. The ring's edge, which the screen never shows, is built by `work`, which the renderer runs once a frame: one piece (a chunk, or a step of its tent's glow), then more only while 4 ms last and never a chunk after another piece; a chunk that leaves the ring before its turn is never built. Enforced by `render-memory.test.ts` ("build what the screen shows at once"): on a step into a new column of chunks, the five at the edge come one a `work`; after a jump, the 3×3 is there at once and the old edge's work is dropped. Incident: #150 — a step across a chunk's border built five chunks in one frame, 81–97 ms on a CPU four times slower, and painting the campfires' glow with them would have added more.

## Serving

### Every file the build writes is one Safari 15.0 reads and runs

The game's scripts parse in Safari 15.0 and call nothing it lacks unless they ask for it first, and its stylesheets hold nothing Safari 15.0 drops that a screen stands on ([[DECISIONS]] § The page). The build is written for it (`BUILD_TARGET`, esbuild lowering what it can), and fails over what is left (`oldBrowsers` in `packages/client/browsers.ts`): syntax newer than ES2022, a class's static block, a regular expression's lookbehind, in a literal or in a string a `RegExp` is built from (esbuild turns an unreadable literal into a `new RegExp` that throws when it runs), a built-in newer than 15.0 by a name no other shares (`Object.hasOwn`, `.at(i)`, `structuredClone`, `.findLast()`, `AbortSignal.timeout`, …) anywhere but in the branch of a test that asks for it (`typeof`, `in`); `:has()`, an at-rule it skips whole, a selector list it drops for one pseudo-class, a `subgrid` or a dynamic viewport unit with no value before it, and a `color-mix()` outside `@supports` (`colorMixFallbacks` gives each one its colour). The error reports' chunk parses as ES2017, and the page's inline script as ES5. A browser that still cannot read the game gets the too-old card, never an empty page, and the error reports hear it, as a `SyntaxError` or, where a Safari before 15 tells only the script's element, as "a script did not run". Enforced by the build itself, which CI runs before the tests and the image's build runs again, and by `browsers.test.ts`, which pins what the check finds and the colours it works out. Incident: #169, a lookbehind in the battle's narration, and three.js's static blocks, kept every Safari before 16.4 (iPadOS 15.0 to 16.3) from reading the game at all.

### A browser keeps only the files a build named after their content, and asks for the page on every visit

The server marks a file `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` only under `/immutable/`, where Vite writes every file it builds under a name that carries its content hash (`build.assetsDir`), so a changed file always has a new name. Everything else, `index.html` included, is `no-cache`: `index.html` names the build's files, so a deploy reaches every kid at their next load, and a file `public/` copies over (the models and textures planned under `/assets/`) keeps its name when it changes. Nothing the server does not find gets the header: a 404 marked `immutable` would be kept for a year. Enforced by `app.test.ts` § the built client (`cacheControl` in `app.ts`, and the header set only on a file `serveStatic` found). Design-time.

### A path the API does not answer is a 404, never the game's page

Every path under `/api` no route answers, and `/ws`, is `404 { error: 'not found' }`; a file missing under `/immutable/` or `/assets/` is a 404 too. Only other paths get `index.html`. During a deploy the old app and the new one answer side by side for a few seconds, and nginx sends a request for a file of the client (under `/immutable/` or `/assets/`) that one of them answers with a 404 to the other one (`nginx/app.conf`): a page of `index.html` with a 200 there would reach the browser as a script, and the game would not start. Enforced by `app.test.ts` § a path nothing answers, § the built client. The nginx half was checked on the local stack with two builds overlapping: with the retry, 80 requests for either build's script and 40 for a public file only one build had all answered 200; without it, 38 of 80 and 22 of 40 were 404s. Incident: the pre-deploy review (2026-09-27) found the server answering unknown `/api/*` paths and `/ws` with `index.html` and a 200.

### A deploy's swap keeps no request waiting on an address that has gone

A container that leaves Docker's network takes its address with it, and an address no container holds answers nothing, not even a refusal, while each nginx worker keeps its last answer for `app` for 5 s (`nginx/http.conf`). So nginx gives the app 250 ms to take a connection before it tries the other address (`proxy_connect_timeout`, `nginx/app.conf`), and `scripts/deploy.sh` reloads nginx the moment the canary has stopped, so that no worker keeps its address. No request, and no presence socket coming back, waits on a gone address longer than those 250 ms. Checked on the local stack with a request every 50 ms and four presence sockets across a swap: over seven swaps, 7,831 requests all answered 200, none slower than 0.3 s, and every socket was back within 0.3 s; with the fix reverted, 27 of 1,629 requests took over a second (the slowest 22.5 s), and a socket took 5.1 s. On production, through #127's own deploy (2026-09-28), nginx answered all 332 health checks sent across it, the slowest in 37 ms; one was retried, from the old app that refused it to the canary, in 6 ms. Incident: #114, requests that hung for 5 and 10 s on production right after `Removing the canary...`, on two deploys on 2026-09-27.

### Applying nginx's config refuses no connection unless nginx's service changed

A changed template is rendered again in the running nginx, as its entrypoint does at a start, and nginx reloads: its new workers take new connections at once, and the old ones keep what they hold, WebSockets included (`scripts/lib/nginx-apply.sh`). Only a changed service definition recreates nginx (a template that does not render as it was checked fails the deploy, with nginx untouched), and a recreate refuses every connection until nginx is back: nginx's graceful stop waits for its WebSockets until Docker kills it 10 s on. Checked on the local stack with a request every 50 ms and four presence sockets open: a changed template applied in place twice, and none of 739 requests failed and no socket closed; with the fix reverted, the recreate failed 208 of 620 requests over 10.4 s and cut all four sockets (close code 1006). A config nginx refuses as it applies it (`nginx -s reload` exits 0 all the same) fails the deploy, with nginx serving as it was: the reload counts once nginx runs new workers. Checked by a template that asks for a port a second nginx holds: the apply failed and said why; with the check reverted it said "reload" and succeeded. Found while planning #126's template change (2026-09-28), which the recreate would have applied in the middle of a deploy; the script's comment had put a recreate at ~2 s.
