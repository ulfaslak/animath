# Product: Animath

## 1. Vision

A cheerful low-poly adventure for kids in which every attack is a math puzzle. You explore a procedurally generated world, meet wild animals in the tall grass, battle them Game Boy Pokémon style with the animals you have already caught, and catch them with a leash when they are weak. Stronger animals ask harder math and hit harder. There is no grinding for levels: an animal is exactly as strong as the puzzles you can solve with it.

Built for the human's kid and their friends. Single player first, then a shared world where they play together and against each other.

The game is called **Animath**. The repo and package names (`mathgame`, `@mathgame/*`) predate the name and stay as they are.

## 2. Players

- **Kids aged roughly 6–12**. Reading level: short words, big text. The puzzle ladder spans from single-digit addition to three-digit multiplication and square roots.
- **Devices**: a laptop or desktop browser with a keyboard, or a tablet held sideways, played with fingers alone (touch controls, [[UI_SPEC]] § Pointer and touch). A mouse works every menu too. Phones are not a target.
- **No login needed.** Open the link and you are in. The game is saved in the browser as you play, so a reload, or coming back another day, picks up where you left off; while the game is shared from the human's machine, the server also keeps a backup under an anonymous id the browser remembers. An account is optional, never required: it keeps the game on the server and brings it to another tablet or computer (§4 "Saving").

## 3. Core loop

Explore → step into tall grass → wild encounter → battle by solving puzzles → catch it (leash) or beat it → heal knocked-out animals at a doctor's tent, and help the animals you don't keep home to the wild for tokens → buy tools with the tokens → venture into fiercer biomes with a stronger party.

## 4. Game rules

This section is the spec the engine implements. Formulas here are the source of truth; the code in `packages/engine` must match them, and a change to either updates the other in the same PR.

### Modes

Two modes only, borrowed from the Game Boy games: **Explore** and **Battle**. Fixed camera angle, no zoom, no rotation. Everything else about the presentation is our own (see [[DESIGN]], [[UI_SPEC]]).

### World

- Grid-based. The player moves one tile at a time in four directions.
- Procedurally generated from a seed, in 16×16 chunks, infinite in every direction. The same seed always yields the same world, so a shared world needs no map download.
- **Numbered worlds.** Every world has a number from 1 to 9999, and the number is all it takes to make it: the same number is the same world on every machine, and neighbouring numbers are unrelated worlds. World 1 is the world every game was played in before worlds had numbers, tile for tile, with the start at (−2, 6). A new game starts in a world of its own, its **home**: a number picked at random from 2 to 9999, so strangers don't all start in one world. A game saved before numbered worlds is in World 1, and World 1 is its home.
- **Where a world begins** (its spawn, where a new game or a first visit starts): a grass tile a doctor's tent is at most **12 steps** from on foot, without a tool or the boat, with room to roam (at least 1,000 tiles in reach on foot): the nearest such tile to the middle of the world. In World 1 that is (−2, 6) on the engine's grid, the doctor 7 steps away. The coordinates a kid sees count from the spawn, so every world's reads x 0 · y 0 ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode).
- **Going to another world** (while exploring, never in a battle or at the doctor): the world left is kept as the kid left it — where they stood, the way they faced, the trees and rocks they cleared there — and the world reached is picked up the same way, or at its spawn, facing down, on a first visit. The animals, tokens, tools and name go along; so does the step count, so the animals ahead keep coming in order. A game remembers the last 100 worlds it left, and its home always; a world forgotten starts at its spawn again. A kid travels from the pause menu's Worlds screen: to any world by its number, or home.
- Biomes: **meadow** (easy animals), **forest** (mid), **river** banks (easy–mid, water animals), **mountain** (hard), and the **sea**: the deep water out in the middle of the lakes, reached only by boat, where the sea animals live.
- Tiles: grass; **tall grass** (where encounters happen); sand; water, shallow along every shore and **deep** further out; rock and trees (blocked); **tent** (a doctor; blocked too, you talk to the doctor from the tile beside it). Water is blocked on foot and open to a player with the boat.
- **The boat** (from the doctor's shop, §4 "Tokens and the doctor's shop") takes the player out on the water, shallow and deep: with it a step onto any water tile sails there, and a step from the water onto walkable ground lands. Rock, trees and tents stop a boat as they stop a walk.
- **Deep water** is water with water all round it, two tiles out: the 5×5 square around the tile is all water. So the shallows along every shore are at least two tiles wide, a river narrower than five tiles has no deep water, and deep water is always at least three steps from any land. It is the sea biome. In World 1 it lies four steps from the start, straight up into the lake just north of it.
- Doctors sit by a small tent with a campfire, in the woods or near water. Not rare, not everywhere.
- **Clearing the way.** With the **axe**, a kid chops down a tree; with the **pickaxe**, breaks a rock: stand next to it, face it, and interact, as with a tent. The tile is ground from then on (a stump on the forest floor, gravel on the mountain), walkable and never tall grass, so it starts no battle; it stays cleared, saved with the game. Trees grow only in the forest and rocks only in the mountains, the snow-capped peaks included. Tall grass, sand, water and tents are never cleared, whatever tools the kid has. Without the tool, facing a tree or a rock says the doctor sells one. Clearing changes where a kid can walk, never who comes out of the grass (§4 "Wild encounters" reads the world as it was made).
- **The paraglider** (from the doctor's shop): hold Space (on a tablet, the Fly button) and the kid jumps into the air and glides straight ahead, the way they face, over anything (trees, rocks, tents, water), a tile about every 0.15 s; there is no steering. A tap of Space still talks, chops or breaks; Enter never flies. **Where they can come down** depends only on what they own: ground always (grass, tall grass, sand, a stump or gravel they made); water, shallow or deep, only with the boat (they land in it); a tree only with the axe and a rock only with the pickaxe, and landing on it clears it, exactly as a chop from beside it does; a tent never. **The reach** is fixed at take-off: the last tile of the 20 ahead they could come down on. With none, they don't take off ("Too far! There's nowhere to land that way.") — a lake more than 19 tiles across still needs the boat. **Letting go** (Space up, the finger off Fly, Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, the window left) lands them on the first tile at or after the one they are over that they can come down on, never the one they took off from; **holding on** lands them at the reach. So a flight always ends where they can stand, at most 20 tiles out, and nobody is ever put back. A ring on the ground shows where letting go would land them (over water without the boat, on the far shore). Each tile flown is a step; nothing up there notices a kid, so a flight never starts a battle, not on the tile they land on either. Taking off from the boat works: the boat swings onto their back. They can fly as often as they like. From the start of World 1, a flight straight up crosses the lake north of it and lands on the sand of its far shore, 14 tiles up, wherever they let go over it.
- **Room for thousands.** A game keeps every tile its kid cleared, in every world together, up to about 24 KB of them in the save: thousands of tiles as kids clear them (paths and clearings), fewer only if they are spread one to a chunk all over the world. Past that, the cleared tiles farthest from the kid grow back, a chunk at a time: first in the worlds the kid has left (the home world last of those), then far away in the world they are in, never one of the 25 chunks round the kid, so the save stays small.

### Animals

- A **species** has a tier (1–5), max HP, a catch rate, 1–4 attacks (typically 2–4), the biomes it lives in, the ground it favours there (§4 "Wild encounters") and the realms it can go in: on land, in the water, or both. The frog, the otter, the common toad and the beaver are **amphibious**, going in both; the six **sea animals** go in the water only; the others go on land only. Encounters happen in the tall grass and reeds on land, and on the deep water out at sea (§4 "Wild encounters"): an animal that lives only in the water never comes out of the grass, and one that lives on land never out of the sea.
- **An animal fights only where it can go.** On land, every land and amphibious animal can fight. Out on the water, in the boat, only the ones that swim: the amphibious animals and the sea animals. On land a sea animal can't fight: it "lives in the sea", as a squirrel can't swim. The human's rule: "you can't fight on water unless you have an amphibious animal". Where each species lives is its biomes: the frog and the otter swim, but they live by the river, and are met in its reeds.
- **Animals have no levels.** A species' strength is entirely its attack table and HP. Tier is a label for "how fierce", used to scale puzzles, to decide who comes out of the grass for which lead, and whether a wild animal can miss; catch odds come from each species' own catch rate.
- Attacks are ordered weakest to strongest, n = 1..N. Each attack can be used at level 1, 2 or 3.
- **Damage** grows with both: `damage(n, level) = power_n × [1, 1.6, 2.4][level]`, rounded. Attack N at level 3 is the species' hardest hit; attack 1 at level 1 its softest. Within a species `power` strictly increases with n.
- **Puzzle difficulty** grows the same way. An attack's easy level asks `base[tier] + 0.75·(n−1)`, rounded, with `base = [1, 2, 4, 5, 7]` for tiers 1–5, but never more than 8; each level up asks exactly one more: `difficulty(tier, n, level) = min(round(base[tier] + 0.75·(n−1)), 8) + (level−1)`. So a squirrel asks difficulty 1–4 and a bear 7–10, and every level of an attack asks one difficulty more than the one below it, all the way to 10: medium and hard never ask at the same difficulty. Neighbouring difficulties can still share a puzzle now and then ("Puzzles" below promises bigger numbers two difficulties apart), so a harder level asks harder sums on average, not every time. The cap of 8 only reaches the bear's two strongest attacks, Maul and Crush, which ask 8, 9 and 10 (they asked 9, 10 and 10 before it, #32): their easy and medium each got a step easier for the same damage. Four attacks in the bear's four difficulties must share ladders. Roar, Maul and Crush all ask 8, 9 and 10, so at a given difficulty only the kind of sum and the damage set them apart, and Crush hits hardest.
- Tier ladder: squirrel, rabbit, frog, shrew, wood mouse, brown rat, hedgehog, mole, common lizard, common toad, robin, stag beetle (1) · fox, otter, roe deer, badger, pine marten, stoat, adder, grey heron, tawny owl, raccoon, beaver (2) · red deer (3) · wolf (4) · bear (5). Don't face a bear with a squirrel. At sea: crab, starfish (1) · turtle (2) · dolphin (3) · octopus (4) · whale (5); don't face a whale with a crab.
- **The animals of the Nordic countryside** (#89; the first wave, the small ones, is in): every animal lives wild in temperate Northern Europe or on both sides of the North Atlantic (the raccoon came from North America and lives wild in Europe now too), is named by its everyday name, the species a kid can learn, and is sized and placed as in nature. Within a tier the numbers stay inside the band the prototype animals set (tier 1: 18–26 HP, a catch rate of 0.8–0.9, 2–3 attacks of power 3–8; tier 2: 30–36 HP, 0.6–0.7, 2–3 attacks of power 5–12), so the balance promises below hold; each animal's character is in the shape of its numbers: the hedgehog is a tank with a weak first attack, the shrew a glass cannon. Tier-1 animals ask sums and number patterns, and times tables only on a second or third attack, as the frog does; nothing asks sharing (division) or square roots below tier 3, so the tawny owl's Talon Grab asks times tables and missing numbers ([[DECISIONS]] § Gameplay).

### Wild encounters

- Each step that lands on **tall grass**, or out at sea on **deep water**, has a **1-in-10** chance of starting a wild battle: one encounter per ten such steps on average, whichever animal leads the party and wherever it is, since animals live in every tall grass and every deep water: no grass is ever quiet. No other tile ever starts one: not the shallows along a shore, so crossing a river in the boat is always peaceful.
- **The lead decides who comes out, on a bell.** The lead (§4 "Party") is the first animal in the party that isn't tired and can fight there, the one that steps into the battle first, and the player can choose it while exploring. The human asked for "almost a bell shape dist around the tier animal you wield, but everything is possible really". With `L` the lead's tier and `k = t − L` how many tiers a tier `t` is above it (negative below it), the animals of tier `t` living in the biome together weigh **`e^(−k²/2)`** next to the lead's own tier, a bell with σ = 1 tier: the lead's own size **1**, one size away **0.61**, two **0.14**, three **0.011**, four **0.0003** (above the lead, near home, it is pulled in: the distance rule below). So the lead's own size is the likeliest wherever it lives, the sizes beside it next, and every size living there can come out, but for the few the distance rule pulls in near home. A tier weighs the same however many kinds of animal it has; they split its weight evenly (and then the ground has its say, below). A tier the biome doesn't hold never comes out there, and the tiers it does hold share its place: a bear in the meadow, where the biggest animal is the red deer, meets red deer 92% of the time, a tier-2 animal 7.6% and a small one 0.2%; a red deer at the river meets its four tier-2 animals 82% of the time and its three small ones 18%.
- The animal comes from the biome's **encounter table**: every species whose habitats include the biome, plus the visitors below, each weighed again by the ground around the tall grass (below). It appears at full HP.
- **Distance rule: near home the bell's side above the lead is pulled in.** Fierce animals are rare near the start and grow commoner far away. With `d` the straight-line distance in tiles from the spawn tile, `danger = clamp((d − 32) / 96, 0, 1)`. A tier below the lead weighs `e^(−k²/2)` at any distance. A tier `k` above it weighs **`9^(−k²)`** inside the **safe radius** (32 tiles): one tier up **1/9**, two up 1 in 6,561, three up about 1 in 400 million, and four up never: a roll's number moves in steps of 2^−32, and `9^−16` falls between two of them, so a starter meets no bear or whale within 32 tiles (from about 80 tiles out it can); from the **wild radius** (128 tiles) out it weighs `e^(−k²/2)`, as much as a tier as far below, so far from home the bell is symmetric; in between it weighs `9^(−k²·(1−danger)) · e^(−k²·danger/2)`, so every tier above the lead grows commoner with every step out, the further above the faster. Near home, where the lead's size and the one above it live, **one battle in 10 is the bigger animal**, and hardly ever anything bigger still.
- **Visitors near home.** Animals come down to the water and up the hills: at the river and in the mountains, wherever an animal bigger than the lead lives there, every species of the lead's tier that doesn't live there also comes out, as a visitor. The visitors together weigh as much as the lead's own tier, times `1 − danger`, however many kinds they are, and split it evenly: as much again as the lead's size living there inside the safe radius, thinning out to nothing at the wild radius. They come on top of the bell, so near home they make the lead's size twice as common there, and as they thin out they take their weight with them. The meadow and the forest get no visitors. With the starter in front, the nine small animals that don't live at the river (squirrels, rabbits, shrews, wood mice, hedgehogs, moles, lizards, robins and stag beetles) come down to it, and the eleven that don't live in the mountains go up the hills; with a tier-2 animal in front, the nine tier-2 animals that don't live in the mountains come up the hills (the river has nothing bigger than tier 2, so no visitors there); with a red deer, red deer do, the only tier-3 animal there. Far out, for the starter, the river is its seven residents (frogs, brown rats, toads, otters, herons, raccoons and beavers) and the mountains its five (wolves, bears, lizards, stoats and adders). The visitors are what keeps the river's tier-2 animals rarer by the water near home: without them they would be one encounter in ten there, where with them they are one in 19, and the mountains' stoats and adders likewise.
- **Never quiet.** Every biome has animals, so no lead finds its grass quiet; where nothing of its own size lives, the sizes nearest its own come out, on the usual 1 grass step in 10: a wolf at the river meets its tier-2 animals 92% of the time and its small ones 8%, a bear 97% and 3%. A kid meets smaller animals more often with a smaller lead, chosen while exploring (§4 "Party").
- **At sea.** Out on the deep water the table is the sea's: the six sea animals, weighed from the lead's tier by the rules above, with no visitors (the frog, the otter, the toad and the beaver swim, but live by the river, and the toad in the forest too). The lead there is the first animal standing that swims, the frog, the otter, the toad, the beaver or a sea animal; with none, nothing comes out. Every sea animal favours the water and the deep water is all water, so the ground never moves them. Near home, with a frog in front: 45% each crabs and starfish, 10% turtles, a dolphin 1 time in 7,000, a whale never; with an otter: 58% turtles, 18% each crabs and starfish, 6.5% dolphins, an octopus 1 time in 11,000; with a whale: 57% whales, 35% octopuses, 7.7% dolphins, 0.6% turtles, a crab or a starfish 1 time in 5,000. Far out the sea is the symmetric bell around the lead: with a frog, 57% crabs and starfish, 35% turtles, 7.7% dolphins, 0.6% octopuses and a whale 1 time in 5,000. In the prototype world the deep water is four steps from the start, straight up, and the first sea animal comes out on the 10th step there on average (half the time by the 7th, 9 times in 10 by the 22nd).
- **The ground around the tall grass.** Animals live near the ground they like, because the human asked for it ([[DECISIONS]] § Gameplay). Every species favours one kind of ground: **water**, **trees**, **rocks** (the mountains' boulders and peaks) or **open** grass, with none of those three near. Around a tall-grass tile the game counts the water, tree and rock tiles among the 28 tiles within 3 of it, as the crow flies (the tile itself left out; grass, tall grass, reeds, sand and tents are none of the three), in the world as it was made: a tree a kid chopped down still counts as a tree here (§4 "World", "Clearing the way"). Water, trees or rocks count fully from 7 of those tiles; open ground counts fully with none of the three near and not at all once they make 7 together. An animal's factor is `4^s`, where `s`, from 0 to 1, is how fully its favourite ground counts: with plenty of its ground around it comes out 4 times as often as an animal of its size with none of its ground, twice as often with half as much. **Near home the ground only chooses among animals of the same size**: each tier keeps the share the biome's table gives it, and its animals divide that share by their biome weight times their factor. So the start is exactly as gentle as the bell makes it, on every tile. **Further out it moves the sizes too**: a tier's share is its biome share times its animals' average factor (weighted by their biome weights) raised to `danger`, normalised, so from the wild radius on every animal's weight is simply its biome weight times its factor. The ground never adds an animal to a table or takes one away, never decides whether a step starts a battle, and more of an animal's ground nearby never lowers its share; each animal keeps a quarter to four times its share of the biome's table.

Where every animal that could come out favours the same ground, it changes nothing: far out the river's reeds are 21% each frogs, brown rats and toads and 9.4% each otters, herons, raccoons and beavers, all of the water, and the mountains 62% lizards, 19% each stoats and adders and 0.7% wolves, all of the rocks, on any ground.
- Near spawn, on the biome's table (before the ground). **Starter (tier 1) in front**: meadow 10% each of its nine small animals, 2% each of its five tier-2 ones (fox, roe deer, badger, stoat, adder), a red deer 1 time in 7,000; forest 12.9% each of its seven small ones (squirrel, shrew, wood mouse, hedgehog, toad, robin, stag beetle), 1.7% each of its six tier-2 ones (fox, roe deer, badger, pine marten, tawny owl, raccoon), a red deer 1 time in 7,000, a wolf practically never and a bear never; river 15.8% each frog, brown rat and toad and 5.3% each of the nine small visitors, 1.3% each of its four tier-2 ones (otter, heron, raccoon, beaver); mountains 47% lizards and 4.3% each of the eleven small visitors, 2.6% each stoat and adder, a wolf practically never and a bear never. **A tier-2 animal**: meadow 11.6% each of its five tier-2 animals, 6.5% red deer, 3.9% each small one; forest 9.7% each of its six tier-2 animals, 6.5% red deer, 5% each small one, a wolf 1 time in 11,000; river 15.6% each otter, heron, raccoon and beaver, 12.6% each frog, brown rat and toad; mountains 19% each stoat and adder and 4.3% each of the nine tier-2 visitors, 23% lizard, a wolf 1 time in 17,000. **Bear**: forest 57% bear, 35% wolf, 7.7% red deer, 0.1% each tier-2 animal; mountains 62% bear, 37.5% wolf, 0.3% each stoat and adder. Far out the bell is symmetric: for the starter a far meadow is 57% small animals, 35% tier-2 ones and 7.8% red deer, a far forest 57%, 35%, 7.7%, 0.6% wolves and a bear 1 time in 5,000, a far river 62% small and 38% tier-2; for a red deer a far forest is 40% red deer, 24% each wolves and tier-2 animals, 5.5% each bears and small ones.
- Promises, for every lead: its own tier is the likeliest wherever it lives, on the biome's table at any distance; inside the safe radius, on every tile of every biome, one tier up is at most 1 in 10 and two or more tiers up under 1 in 5,000 together, and wherever anything its size or bigger lives its own tier is the majority, whatever the ground; on any ground, the share of animals bigger than it never falls as you walk away from spawn, nor the share of those two or more tiers bigger. (Its own tier's share may rise on some ground on the way, where animals of different sizes favour different ground: then what shrinks is the animals smaller than it.) From the wild radius out, a tier as far above the lead as another is below weighs the same on the biome's table, and the ground then sorts them by where they live. In World 1 the spawn tile is one step from a river reed with the lake all round it: with the starter in front an encounter there is a frog, a brown rat or a toad three times in four (25% each), one of the nine small visitors about 1 time in 5 (2.1% each), and one of the river's four tier-2 animals 1 time in 19 (1 in 76 each); with a tier-2 animal in front it is one of the four tier-2 animals 62% of the time (15.6% each), and one of the three small ones the other 38% (12.6% each).
- River banks are sand with **reeds**: ordinary tall-grass tiles, where encounters go, drawn as reed beds so they read as the river's, on about 3 bank tiles in 10. That is where the river's animals live: frogs, brown rats, toads, otters, herons, raccoons and beavers. Most reeds are by the water: in the prototype world 56% are next to it and 87% have water within 3 tiles; a few lie on dry sand further off.

### Battle

- Turn-based, one action per turn, like the Game Boy games. The player's animal is seen from behind, the opponent from the front.
- The player always acts first. A round is one player action followed by the wild animal's reply, unless the action ended the battle.
- Player's turn: pick an attack and a level → a puzzle appears → answer. **Correct** → full damage for that attack and level. **Wrong** → the attack misses (0 damage) and the turn is over. Once an attack is picked there is no backing out: giving up on the puzzle is the same as answering it wrong. No timer in v1; an open question is whether a gentle timer or a speed bonus should exist.
- The puzzle's difficulty comes from the **player's** animal — `difficulty(tier, n, level)` with that species' tier — and its kind is one the chosen attack can ask. A bear in your party asks bear-hard questions.
- Opponent's turn (wild animal): picks one of its attacks uniformly at random and hits for that attack's level-1 damage (its `power`). Wild animals don't solve puzzles. Facing an animal of its **own tier or a fiercer one**, a wild animal is careful and **misses 44% of the time** (11 attacks in 25); facing a **smaller** animal it never misses. It sizes up whoever is in front, turn by turn: a fox never misses your squirrel, but can miss the bear that steps in after it.
- So a kid who always picks the easiest puzzle (the weakest attack at level 1) and gets it right beats an animal of their own tier about 80% of the time, averaged over the catalog's same-tier pairs; right 7 times in 10, about 46%. The starter squirrel against the tier-1 animals the ground brings out near home (in World 1 shrews and hedgehogs 12% each, squirrels, wood mice and robins 10%, rabbits 9%, and the rest): about 72% and 40%; in the meadow alone, where the rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs and moles are 18% each, 68% and 36%; by the river, 72% and 39%. Against a rabbit alone it is 61% and 28%, against a brown rat the same, against a mole, the hardest small animal for it, 55% and 27%, and against a frog 76% and 39%. A frog in front wins about as often as a rabbit: 96% against a squirrel, 72% against a rabbit, 79% against another frog, and near home against the tier-1 animals 79% and 46% (the rabbit 81% and 49%). Against everything that comes out near home in World 1, the tier-2 animals included, the frog wins 70% and 41%, the squirrel 64% and 36%. One tier up on the easiest puzzle is still a loss (every time, in all 158 pairs one tier apart that can meet), and a battle in which every animal you send out is smaller than the wild one plays exactly as if wild animals never missed.
- Other actions on the player's turn: throw a leash (wild battles), flee (wild battles; always succeeds in v1 and costs nothing), or switch animals.
- **Switching**: instead of acting, send in another party member. The switch is the turn: the wild animal replies at once, against the newcomer, and sizes it up like any animal in front, so bringing in a bigger animal makes a miss possible. You can't switch to the animal already in front, to a tired one or to one that can't fight where the battle is (below), and not in the middle of a puzzle: once an attack is picked, the turn is committed. Every switch you choose hands the wild animal a turn, so switching back and forth only wears your party down and can never stall a battle.
- When the player's animal is knocked out and another party member that can fight there is still standing, the player picks who steps in: any animal that isn't tired and can fight there. That switch is free (the wild animal has just had its turn), and then the player chooses an action. HP lost in a battle stays lost afterwards.
- A battle ends **won** when the wild animal reaches 0 HP, **lost** when every animal in the party that can fight there is knocked out, **caught** on a successful leash throw, or **fled**.
- **On the water**, from the boat, only the animals that swim fight (§4 "Animals"): the first one standing steps in, only another one standing can be switched in (the others "can't swim" and sit it out in the boat), and the battle is **lost** once every one that swims is tired, whoever still stands in the boat. On land the sea animals sit a battle out the same way ("lives in the sea"). The sea animals are met out on the deep water (§4 "Wild encounters", "At sea"), and a battle there is drawn on the water, both animals low in it, the wild one swimming home or staying in the water at the end.

### Friendly matches

Two players can battle each other, just for fun. A match changes nothing: every animal's HP, the knock-outs, the party and the tokens are exactly as they were before it, whoever wins. Only the kid's count of puzzles solved goes up, by their own right answers, as it does anywhere (§4 "Puzzles").

- **Challenging.** Standing within two steps of another player in the same world (diagonals count: any tile of the 5 × 5 square round you), both of you on land and exploring, you can challenge them. They have 20 seconds to say yes or no, and can walk on meanwhile; walking out of reach, sailing off, meeting a wild animal, going to the doctor or opening the menu is a no that nobody has to say. You wait for the answer (Never mind takes the ask back). After a no, or no answer, you wait 30 seconds before asking the same player again; after taking an ask back (or leaving the game, or walking off, while asking), 10. A player is asked by one challenger at a time: a second is told the player is busy with someone else. Two players who ask each other at the same moment both said yes. You can't challenge, or be challenged, while in a match, asking, or being asked, and a player with no animal that can fight on land can't challenge (the button says why).
- **Teams.** Each player brings the first three animals in their party order that can fight on land, tired ones too, all at full HP for the match; a party with fewer brings what it has. A match is fought on land, so the sea animals sit it out.
- **Turns.** A coin flip decides who goes first, and both screens say who ("Bo starts!", "You start!"), and then the players take turns. On your turn you pick an attack and its level (easy, medium or hard) and solve its puzzle, or you switch to another animal that is standing, which uses the turn. The other player sees your puzzle while you think, but never its answer, nor what you type, and reads that you are thinking. The other player's animals go by their name ("Bo's Rabbit"), and a nickname with a rude word in it is never shown to the other player: that animal goes by its kind's name.
- **Attacks** are asked and land exactly as in a wild battle (§4 "Animals", "Battle"): the puzzle's difficulty comes from your own animal's tier, the attack and the level, so a kid with bigger animals solves harder sums, and a right answer hits for the attack's damage at that level. A wrong answer misses. Nobody misses on their own: the careful miss belongs to wild animals. There is no leash and no running away.
- **Knock-outs.** When an animal is knocked out, its player picks which one steps in, for free, and then takes their turn. A player with no animal left standing loses.
- **Leaving.** Either player can leave the match at any time, their turn or not, and the other one wins; the one who left goes straight back to exploring. A player whose game drops out (the page closed, the connection lost) has 30 seconds to come back, and the match waits for them, saying so to the other; back in time, the match goes on where it was (a reloaded page picks it up after Continue), or, if it ended meanwhile, they see how; not back in time, they lose the same way ("Bo had to go"). There is no draw and no turn limit: two players who never answer right, or only ever switch, play on until one of them leaves.
- **Sitting on a turn.** A slow puzzle-solver is never hurried: every key on your turn (typing, choosing) counts as being there. After 90 seconds on your turn without a key, the match asks "Still there?" ("I'm here!", or any key, and the clock starts again); after 3 minutes without one, the match ends and the other player wins ("Bo wandered off").
- **After the match.** Both see who won, kindly: "You won! Great match, Bo!" or "Ada won this time! Good match!". "Rematch?" starts a new match between the same two once both have said yes (a new coin, the same teams if nothing changed), and "Back to exploring" ends it for you and tells the other. A rematch waits two minutes at most.
- **A new version of the game.** When the game is updated in the middle of a match, the match ends for both with no winner: "The game is updating. Let's play again in a moment!". A moment later both are back, and "Play again" asks the same friend again (one tap each: the second "Play again" is a yes).
- **How it plays out**, over 1,000 simulated 3-against-3 matches per line, each kid picking a random attack at a random level: two teams of tier-1 animals whose kids are both right 7 times in 10 play about 20 puzzles, 10 each (16 when both are right 9 times in 10, 28 at 5 in 10; one match in ten takes more than 26), and two teams of bears about 27. Always picking the easiest attack about doubles that (44 puzzles for tier 1, 72 for bears); always the hardest, 14. The kid who goes first wins about 55% of even matches, 57–60% when both are right 9 times in 10. Skill counts: right 9 times in 10 against 7 in 10, with the same animals, wins about 4 matches in 5. Size counts more: at the same accuracy a team one tier smaller wins at most 1 match in 12, and a tier-1 team against a tier-2 one almost never (1%); only much better sums close a one-tier gap (right 9 times in 10 against 5 in 10: tier 1 against tier 2 wins 32%, tier 2 against tier 3 60%). Those lines are for fixed teams (squirrel, rabbit and frog; fox, otter and fox; three of a kind above). Teams of three drawn at random from a tier's land animals play the same way (#89): two tier-1 teams play about 20 puzzles and the kid who goes first wins 60%, right 9 times in 10 against 7 wins 79%, a tier-1 team against a tier-2 one wins 2% at the same accuracy and 40% when right 9 times in 10 against 5, and two tier-2 teams play about 21 puzzles.

### Catching

- Throw a leash when the wild animal is weak. `P(success) = catchRate × leashQuality × 2^(−hp/0.2)` where `hp` is the fraction of HP remaining, capped at 95%.
- Every 20% of HP halves the odds. A squirrel (rate 0.9) at 10% HP ≈ 64%; a bear (rate 0.2) at 10% ≈ 14%, at 50% ≈ 3.5%.
- `leashQuality` is 1 for the starter leash; better leashes come from the future shop.
- A failed throw costs the turn. A caught animal keeps the HP it had when the leash landed, and always joins the party (§4 "Party"): behind the others of its kind, or as a new kind at the end.

### Knock-out and healing

- An animal at 0 HP is **knocked out** and can't battle until healed.
- **Talking to a doctor**: stand on a tile next to a tent, face it and interact. Walking into a tent turns you to face it. Standing beside it without facing it is not enough.
- The doctor helps any animal below full HP, knocked out or only hurt.
- Doctors heal a whole species in exchange for a solved puzzle: pick an animal, solve its puzzle, and it and every other hurt animal of its species in the team are back to full HP. Difficulty scales with the tier (`base[tier] + 1`: every small animal 2, the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog among them, every tier-2 animal 3, the fox and the otter among them, deer 5, wolf 6, bear 8), so healing bears is harder than healing squirrels, and it is the same for one bear as for three. The puzzle's kind is one the animal's own attacks ask, so a kid meets the kind of sum they already know from battle.
- **A wrong answer at the doctor costs nothing.** HP stays where it was and a different puzzle takes its place, as many times as it takes. The player can pick another animal, go back to the list or leave at any time.
- If every animal in the party that could fight where the battle was is knocked out (out on the water, every one that swims), the player is taken to the nearest doctor's tent and stands beside it, facing it, and the doctor heals the whole party for free. No other penalty (assumption: this is a kids' game).
- **Nearest means the way the player gets about**: the fewest steps over walkable ground, never through water, rock, trees or another tent; with the boat, over the water too. A tent on an island (without a boat) or boxed in by trees is never where the player wakes up, so they always land somewhere they could have got to. The tile they stand on beside the tent is always ground, never water. Ties go to the tent further up, then further left, then the side in front of the door (below the tent), then left, right and behind. If no tent is within 200 steps, a doctor comes to the player instead: they stay where they are (out on the water, in the boat), and the party is healed all the same.

### Tokens and the doctor's shop

- **Why wild animals attack.** They are a little bit sick, which makes them grumpy. An animal the kid brings to the doctor is made better and goes home to the wild, and the doctor thanks the kid with **tokens**.
- **Tokens** are a whole number, never below 0, saved with the game. A new game starts with none.
- **Helping animals home.** At the doctor the kid picks any animals of the team, tired ones too (the doctor makes them better first), and always keeps at least one that isn't tired and can fight on land, where every tent stands, so the team can still battle when it walks on (a crab alone could not). A whole kind can be picked at once; when every one of it going would leave no such animal, the first of it that is one stays. Each animal of tier `k` brings `k·(k + 1)` tokens: **2, 6, 12, 20, 30** for tiers 1 to 5. A bigger animal is harder to catch and needs a bigger lead to meet often (§4 "Wild encounters"), so it brings more, and catching the smallest animals over and over is the slow way to the shop.
- **The shop** sells tools, one of each, for good: the **axe** (8 tokens; chops trees), the **pickaxe** (13; breaks rocks), the **boat** (21; sails on water) and the **paraglider** (34; glides over anything, §4 "World"). A tool is on sale only once what it does is built, and all four are; were nothing on sale, the shop would say new things are coming soon. With too few tokens, or a tool already owned, the tool can't be bought, and the card says why. A boat or a paraglider bought grows onto the trainer's back; closing the card after buying the paraglider says how to fly it ("Hold Space to fly!", on a tablet "Hold Fly to fly!").
- **Every token that changes hands is a sum.** Helping animals home asks "You have 12 tokens. You get 8 more. How many will you have?" (`12 + 8 = ?`), once per hand-over however many animals go; buying asks "You have 23 tokens. The axe costs 8. How many will you have left?" (`23 − 8 = ?`). The numbers are the real ones, the engine judges the answer, and only the right answer completes it: the animals go home and the tokens are counted in, or the tool is the kid's and its price comes off. A wrong answer says "Not quite! Try again." and asks the same sum again, at no cost. Backing out, picking something else or leaving is always free, and nothing happens that was not answered.
- **A gentle confirm** comes before a hand-over's sum: "Say bye bye to Fox and Rabbit?", with "No, not now" lit first.
- **What the numbers are for** (a model, not a promise; the numbers are for tuning after play): a kid battles near home with the starter in front, about one battle a minute and a half, catches something in about 2 battles of 5, and hands most catches over. By the engine's own battles, encounter tables and catch odds, with a 7-year-old's pace for each puzzle and beat, that is **0.6 tokens a minute** if every catch goes home and 0.46 if 3 in 4 do, so the axe is in reach after **13–17 minutes**, the pickaxe after **21–28** and the boat after **34–46**: the three prices keep the 3 : 5 : 8 of the 15, 25 and 40 minutes aimed at. The paraglider, the next step of the same ladder (34), comes after **57–74**: the dearest tool, which the human asked for ("a relatively large number of tokens"). A kid who farms squirrels and rabbits at the reed by the start, throwing the leash at every one, gets about 1.2–1.4 tokens a minute: the axe in 6–7 minutes, the boat in about 15–17 and the paraglider in 24–28, twice as fast, not ten times. A fox in front, played the same ordinary way, earns about 1.4 a minute (the paraglider in about 24 minutes), as much as that farm (near home a fox meets a small animal about 1 battle in 3), and farmed the same way about 3.2; a bear far out about 4.8. So a stronger team brings the later tools sooner, and farming the smallest animals is never the fastest way.
- **Losing is not a way to tokens**: nothing about a lost battle gives or takes any.

### Puzzles

- Kinds in v1: addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, missing operand ("7 + ? = 12"), next number in a sequence, square root. Every answer is a whole number.
- Difficulty is a 1–10 scalar. Each kind declares the range it supports; each attack declares the kinds it can ask; the engine picks a kind that fits.
- **Every operand comes from a band with a floor and a ceiling**, so a harder attack never asks an easier question: a difficulty-3 attack cannot ask "4 + 1". Both ends of every band climb with difficulty (neither ever falls). For every kind but sequences a puzzle always shows a bigger number than any puzzle of the same kind two difficulties down; a sequence is never one that two difficulties down could ask.
  - Addition, subtraction and the missing addend draw both numbers from one band per difficulty: 1–5 · 6–10 · 11–20 · 21–50 · 51–100 · 101–200 · 201–500 · 501–1000 · 1001–5000 · 5001–10000. Subtraction is the same fact family read backwards (`(x + y) − x`), so nothing is ever "− 0" or "− itself".
  - Multiplication, division and the missing factor are a **big** factor times a **small** one (the times table). Big: 2–5 · 6–9 · 6–9 · 11–20 · 21–50 · 21–50 · 51–100 · 51–100 · 101–500 for difficulties 2–10; small: 2–5 · 2–5 · 6–9 · 6–9 · 6–9 · 11–20 · 11–20 · 21–50 · 21–50. So difficulty 2 is the small tables, 4 is the hard corner of the table (6–9 by 6–9), 5 is teens by a digit, 7 is two-digit by two-digit. Ten is never a factor: "10 × 7" is a freebie. Division is the family backwards (`(big × small) ÷ small`), from difficulty 3; the missing factor hides the big one. From difficulty 4, half of the missing-number puzzles are a missing factor ("4 × ? = 20") instead of a missing addend.
  - Square roots ask for a root in 2–5 · 4–8 · 6–10 · 9–12 · 11–15 · 13–20 · 16–30 · 21–50 for difficulties 3–10.
  - Counting sequences step by 1–2 at difficulty 1, 2–5 at 2, from `d` up to `2d + 2` at 3–9, and 10–25 at 10; only difficulty 1 counts by ones. They start from 0–5 at difficulty 1, 6–10 at 2, and so on in fives up to 46–50 at 10, so two difficulties never ask the same counting sequence.
  - The other sequence patterns join as difficulty climbs — doubling at 3 (tripling too from 6), squares at 5, triangle numbers and add-the-last-two at 7 — and each starts further along the higher it goes: a pattern's first term at difficulty `d` is always bigger than any first term it had at `d − 2`. So a textbook opening ("1, 2, 4, 8", "1, 4, 9, 16", "1, 3, 6, 10") is asked only within a difficulty of the one that introduces it, and at difficulty 10 doubling or tripling starts from 15–25, squares from 8²–12², triangle numbers from the 6th–10th and add-the-last-two from a first term of 6–10.
  - Every sequence has one right answer. It fits exactly one pattern, and it never ends in three evenly spaced numbers unless it counts all the way: "2, 3, 5, 8, ?" is never asked (adding the last two says 13, "the gaps grow by one" says 12), nor is "4, 4, 8, 12, ?" (20, but a kid counting on from the end says 16).
- Input is a number. Answers are judged by the engine, never by the UI.
- **Puzzles solved.** The kid keeps a running count of the puzzles they have solved. Every right answer adds one, wherever it was asked: an attack in a battle, a heal at the doctor, a token sum (one per hand-over, however many animals go, and one per purchase), an attack in a friendly match (the kid's own answers, never the other player's). A wrong answer adds nothing, and the count never goes down. Easy or hard, a puzzle counts one. It is saved with the game and goes along to every world, like the tokens; a new game starts at 0. Nothing is won with it: no milestones, rewards or badges.
- The catalog is designed to grow: the kinds to come are in §6, not v1.

### Party

- A party holds every animal the player catches: there is no limit.
- **The party is in bundles, one per kind.** Every animal of a species stands with the others of its kind, and the player chooses the order of the bundles and of the animals within each. The bundles top to bottom are the battle order. A caught animal joins the end of its kind's bundle; the first of a new kind starts a bundle at the end.
- **The lead is the first animal in party order that is not tired and can fight where the player stands**: on land, the first one standing in the first bundle that has one; out on the water, the first one standing that swims. It is the one that steps into the next battle there and the one wild animals size up before they come out (§4 "Wild encounters"). With none standing that swims, out on the water there is no lead: nothing challenges the player there, and the lead on land rides in the boat. Likewise on land with only sea animals standing (after a battle at sea that a sea animal ended standing, won, run from or caught, while the land's animals were tired): nothing comes out of the grass and nobody follows, until a doctor makes one of them fit. Once a battle has started, the player decides who is in front: a switch, or picking who steps in after a knock-out (§4 "Battle"); neither changes the party's order. There is no separate "selected" animal: choosing a lead moves its bundle to the front and it to the front of its bundle.
- While exploring — not in a battle, not at the doctor — the player can choose any animal that is not tired and can fight where they stand as the lead (out on the water, one that swims: "Squirrel can't swim!" otherwise; on land, not a sea animal: "Crab lives in the sea!"); choose a bundle to go first (its first animal standing leads; not when all of them are tired, nor out on the water when they can't swim, nor on land when they live in the sea); move a bundle up or down, a bundle of tired animals too (the first animal standing behind it leads); move an animal up or down within its bundle (a tired one too: a tired animal at the front is simply skipped); and name any animal.
- **Nicknames**: at most 12 characters, from letters (any alphabet), digits, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and dots. A letter keeps up to four accent marks that belong on it: the usual accents on a Latin, Greek or Cyrillic letter, or its own script's marks for the scripts the engine lists (the Indian scripts, Arabic, Hebrew, Thai, Burmese, Khmer and a dozen more); a script not listed keeps its letters and loses its marks. An accent that no single letter carries counts as a character. Everything else a kid types — emoji, symbols, marks that decorate a letter (an underline, a strike-through, a circle around it) — is left out, runs of spaces become one, and spaces at either end are trimmed. A name with no letter or digit left is no name, and the animal goes by its species' name again.

### Starting out

- The game opens on a **title screen**: Continue (when there is a saved game), New game, and the Language and Sound settings. A throwaway game (below) skips it.
- **Your name first.** New game asks the player's name before anything else ("What's your name?"): 2 to 16 letters or numbers, in words with single spaces or hyphens between them, in any alphabet, æ, ø and å included, and nothing rude (a short list of English and Danish words, never a real name that merely holds one: Scunthorpe, Hassan and Pikachu are fine). A name that won't do stays in the box, with why, kindly: "Type your name first.", "A name needs at least 2 letters.", "That's a long one! 16 letters at most.", "Only letters and numbers, please.", "Let's pick another name." It is the name other players will see, and an account's username (§6). A game saved before players had names asks once, on Continue, before it goes on.
- **New game** lets the player pick their first animal from the **starters**: the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog, tier-1 animals that fight on land, where a game starts. The other small animals are caught, not chosen: a first minute choosing among twelve small figures would be a worse first minute, and the shrew, at 18 HP, a harder start (#89). The picked animal can be given a name, or none; the name is cleaned like any nickname (§4 "Party"). The game starts in its home world (§4 "World"), at its spawn, facing down, with that one animal at full HP, nothing walked and no doctor visited.
- Every starter is the same size, so every starter meets the same animals on the same steps.
- A throwaway game (`?new`, `?zoo`, `?shop`, or a `?party=` or `?tokens=` without a typo) skips the title, is saved nowhere, and starts in World 1, with no name, with a squirrel, or the `?party=` party, and no tokens, or the `?tokens=` tokens.
- **New game while a game is saved** asks first, and the answer starts on "No"; then the name box holds the saved game's name, to keep or change. Starting over puts the saved game away: it is kept in the browser and on the server, never deleted, but the game has no way to go back to it; that is a job for the human ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database).

### Saving

- **Always saved, never a save button.** The game is saved in this browser after every change (a step or a bump, a battle turn, a catch, a heal, animals helped home, tokens given or spent, a tree chopped or a rock broken, a change to the team), and when the page is hidden or closed. A reload, or coming back another day on the same browser and the same address, carries on exactly where you were: the same place, facing the same way, the same animals with the same HP.
- **A reload is not an escape.** A battle in progress is saved too: a reload picks it up with the same wild animal, the same HP, and the same puzzle if one was up. The step count is saved, so the animals ahead on a walk stay the ones they were; reloading never rerolls an encounter, a battle's puzzle or a throw. A doctor visit is not saved: a reload ends it, what it healed stays healed, and a hand-over or a purchase whose sum was answered stays done; one waiting for its sum never happened.
- **A backup on the server** (a guest's game, while the game is shared from the human's machine; on the public server a guest's game lives in the browser, and an account keeps it on the server). Each player's save is also copied to the server, in the background, when the server can be reached. A browser that still knows the player but has lost its save gets the game back from there. The game never waits on the server, except for a moment at start in that case (2.5 s at most), and plays and saves the same without it. A browser that won't store anything says so ("This browser can't keep your game, so it starts new every time.").
- **Two windows.** Both show the same game. A window that falls behind a catch, a battle or anything else made in the other one takes no more play. When you come to it, it loads the newer game and says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!". While it is on screen but you play in the other one, a card says "You kept playing in another window." When the other window starts a new game, this one goes back to the title as soon as you look at it, with Continue on the new game. Nothing is ever rolled back, and nothing played in a window that is behind is thrown away, because none is taken. Walking around in both is fine: the window you play in carries on from where you are.
- **A save that won't load** leaves the title with New game only (unless the server's backup of the game can be read: then Continue picks that up), and the new game starts with a message ("Your saved game didn't load, so here is a new one."). The old save is kept: it stays where it was until you have picked a starter, then it is set aside, never deleted.
- **A save made by a newer version of the game** (while an update is on its way, a window still open on the old version can meet one: one with the new version's animals) is never touched by the old version, in the browser or on the server. The old window plays no further and reloads, which brings the new version and the game with it; if it may not reload by itself just then, a card says "A new version of the game is ready." with a button, "Tap to update!" ("Update!" and Enter on a keyboard).
- **After an update** that changes how games are saved, a saved game carries on exactly where it was (a game from before numbered worlds is in World 1), and the old save is kept too, as it was: in the browser (`animath.save.upgraded`) and on the server.
- **Starting fresh** is New game on the title (§4 "Starting out"): the saved game is put away, not deleted. Nothing in the game deletes a save; clearing the site's data in the browser does. `?new` in the address (like `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop`) plays a throwaway game that is saved nowhere, leaving the saved one alone.
- **An account, if you want one.** After every hour of play in a guest's game (with the game on screen), the next time you are exploring a card offers to keep your animals safe with a secret password: "Save my game" or "Not now", which it asks again after the next hour. The pause menu offers "Make an account" and "Log in" to a guest, and "Log out" once you are logged in; the title has "I have an account → Log in". Your name is your username, and nothing else is asked: no email. A name someone has already is picked again, and your character takes the new one. The password has at least 4 letters or numbers, and "Show" shows it as you type.
- **Making an account takes your game along**: from then on it is saved in the browser first, then on the server, and it is the one you get when you log in on another tablet or computer ("Welcome back, Nini!"). The game you play as a guest and your account's game never mix: logging in leaves the guest game where it is, and logging out goes back to the title, with the guest game if there is one ("You're logged out. Your game waits safely in your account."). The account's game waits in the browser for the next login too.
- **Played on somewhere else?** When another device got further in your account's game, this one takes the newer game and says so ("You played on somewhere else. Here's your newest game!"); the game it had is kept aside, never deleted. A game saved by a newer version of Animath is never written over: the game asks to update ("A new version of the game is ready.").
- **An account needs no server to play.** With the server out of reach an account's game plays and saves in the browser, and goes to the server again when it can, without a word. A new account is offered only while the server can keep one: when it cannot (it is out of reach, or its database is not ready for accounts), there is no hourly card and no row to make an account or log in, and they come back once it can. When the server says you are logged out (a new password from a grown-up, or a year away), the game says so once and saves only in the browser until you log in again. A forgotten password is set again by a grown-up: there is no email to send one.
- **Bringing a game to a new home.** A game played before the game had its own address comes along in an account a grown-up makes for it, with a welcome link. Opening the link says "Welcome back, Aslak! Your animals are waiting. Pick a secret password." ("Show" shows it as you type), and "Let's play!" carries on exactly where the game was: the same world and place, the same animals, tokens and tools. From then on it is an ordinary account: log in with the name and that password anywhere. A game this browser played as a guest stays where it is. The link works once, for 14 days: opened again, it says "This link has been used already" and offers to log in, or to play as a guest; too old, it asks for a new one from a grown-up.
- **Quit to title** (the pause menu's Start screen row) saves the game as it stands and goes back to the title, where Continue picks it up exactly there.
- **In the air**: a game is never saved up in the air. Saved mid-flight (the page hidden or closed, or the save after each tile flown), it is the game as letting go right then would leave it, so a reload lands the kid where letting go would have: on the ground, or in the boat, never over water or in a tree, never back at the spawn.
- **Out on the water**: a game saved in the boat picks up in the boat, where it was. A save whose player stands on water without the boat (one edited by hand: nothing in the game takes a boat away) never leaves them stuck out there: it starts again from the spawn tile.

### Playing together

- **Who you see.** The other players in your world, and only them: a player in another world is never seen or listed. Within 24 tiles of you (a little more than the screen shows) you see them walk, step by step, and they see you; you keep seeing each other until you are more than 28 tiles apart. Everyone in the world is on the pause menu's list, Who's here, nearest first, with about how many steps away they are (exactly, up to 20), which way, and what they are doing.
- **How they look.** Every player is a trainer like you, in a shirt and cap their name gives them, the same on every screen and never your own coral and blue, with their name over their head, the animal that goes first for them following them, and their boat on their back or under them out on the water. Up in the air with the paraglider they glide as you do, over the tiles they fly (never walking on the water or through trees), their glider open over them in their shirt's colour and cream, and nobody follows them until they are down. While they are busy a little bubble over their name says with what: a battle with a wild animal, the doctor, the menu, a friendly match (no bubble in the air: the glider says it; the list says "flying"). Two players on one tile stand a little apart on it, so nobody hides another.
- **Coming and going.** A player who comes into view fades in and one who leaves it fades out; one who turns up out of nowhere near you (they went to someone, or came into the world) comes in a little poof of dust. When someone comes into your world a note at the top of the screen says so ("Ada is here!"), and when someone leaves it ("Bo went home"): not for who was there when you came, not for someone back within ten seconds (a reload, a hiccup), and never more than one note at a time, so players who come together come in one note ("Ada and 3 more are here!"). Notes show over the world only, never over a battle, the doctor or the menu, and one that waited too long is dropped.
- **Finding each other.** Worlds are endless and friends can be hundreds of tiles apart. An arrow at the edge of the screen points to each of the four nearest players you can't see, with their name. The list's "Go to <name>" takes you next to them at once, in a poof, however far: to the nearest tile beside them you could stand on yourself and walk on from (never boxed in: never on an island or in a nook of trees you couldn't get off, however big, and on land when there is land as near as the water), facing them. When there is nowhere near them to stand (they are out at sea, or on an island, and you have no boat), you stay where you are and the message line says so. Going is not walking: no step is taken and nothing jumps out of the grass. Only while exploring.
- **Once.** You are in the world once, however many windows you have open: the one you play in is the one the others see, and one you left says "You're playing in another window."
- **What the others know of you.** Your name, your world, where you stand and which way you face, the animal following you, whether you have a boat, and whether you are busy. Not your team (a friendly match shows the other player the animals you bring to it, and nothing else of it), not your tokens, not where in the world you have been. There is no chat.
- **Seen by nobody**: a game that is not under way (the title), a practice game (`?new` and the other throwaway games), a window behind another, and a game without a name.
- **Without the server** the game plays exactly as it does alone, and the others come back by themselves when it does. When the server is updated, everyone moves to the new one in a blink nobody sees: the others stay where they are on screen, and nobody is said to have gone home. A game too old for the server reloads itself once, into the new version, while you are exploring, where you were.

## 5. Feature inventory

What is built and observable today. Keep current: add a bullet when a feature ships, remove it when one is deleted, move items up from §6 as they land.

### Title

- The title screen comes first ([[UI_SPEC]] § Title): "Animath" in big bouncing letters over the world where the game stands, the trainer and one of each kind in the team (or, for a new player, the starters) breathing beside it, the camera drifting slowly. Continue (with the name of the animal that goes first and how many there are), New game, and the Language and Sound settings.
- New game asks your name first, in a card over the world ("What's your name?"), and goes on only with a name that will do, saying kindly why not; then the starters. A game saved before names asks once, on Continue ("Your animals are waiting for you!").
- New game shows the starters side by side, big, and the lit one's card names every kind of sum its attacks ask; the kid picks one with the arrows and Enter, and names it or not. With a saved game, New game asks first ("Start a new game?"), and only a deliberate "Yes" starts over.
- Quit to title: the pause menu's Start screen row saves and goes back to the title; Continue carries on from the same spot.
- Every row, language and choice takes a click or a tap; on the starters a tap on an animal or its name lights it and "Pick the Frog!" picks it.
- "I have an account → Log in" for a guest, and "Playing as Nini" once logged in. Opened from the Home Screen as a web app with nothing saved, a hint says how to bring over a game played in Safari (#90).

### Touch and mouse

- Pointer everywhere ([[UI_SPEC]] § Pointer and touch): every row, button and card a key reaches also takes a click or a tap, as the same key press, behind the same guards. In a battle a tap only highlights a row (an attack, Leash, Switch, Run, an animal on the switch list) or sets an attack's level, and Go! does it, since a pick spends the turn; on the starters a tap only lights an animal; everywhere else a tap does the row at once. The result card goes on from a tap anywhere. A tap counts only on what the finger went down on, on the screen it went down on, so a thumb lifted as a battle starts presses nothing. A slow tap counts however long the finger rests, and a double click counts once when its first click sent what it pressed elsewhere.
- Touch controls on a tablet, or after any touch: a D-pad that walks as the arrow keys do (hold to keep walking), Talk and Menu buttons that work with the other thumb still on the D-pad, a Fly button over Talk once the paraglider is owned (held, it flies as Space held does), and a number pad beside every puzzle, so the tablet's own keyboard never covers a sum. Rows and buttons a finger tall; hints that say "tap"; the name boxes use the tablet's keyboard, with the box moved clear of it. A key pressed on a real keyboard switches back to the keys.
- The page behaves as a game on a tablet: no zooming, scrolling, pull-to-refresh or text selection under a finger, and a tablet (or a phone) held upright is asked to turn sideways. On a screen with a notch or a home indicator the world fills the screen and nothing to read or tap sits under them.

### Explore

- Procedural chunked world with four biomes on land that read at a glance ([[DESIGN]] § Palette): the meadow's bright grass and flowers, the forest's darker floor crowded with dark pines, young trees and bushes, river banks of sand with reed beds, the mountains' grey-green turf with pebbles, boulder fields and snow on the peaks. Water, tall grass (reeds at the river), trees, rocks and doctor tents, rendered low-poly with a fixed camera. Out in the lakes the deep water, the sea, is a deeper blue than the shallows, two tiles wide or more, along every shore.
- The boat ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode): bought at the doctor's shop, it grows onto the trainer's back, where they carry it like a shell, its brown bottom out and a coral rim round it. A step onto the water swings it off their back, over and under them as they hop in, and out on the water they sail from tile to tile in it, rocking gently, a coral pennant at the stern; a step back onto land swings it onto their back again. With reduced motion it snaps into place instead.
- The paraglider ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode; §4 "World"): bought at the doctor's shop, it grows onto the trainer's back, a little rolled-up canopy of coral and cream (above the boat when they carry it too). Held a quarter of a second, Space (Fly on a tablet) makes them crouch, jump and rise as the canopy opens over them, and they glide straight ahead, high over trees, rocks, tents and water, a shadow under them and a soft ring on the ground where letting go would land them (with the axe or the pickaxe over a tree or a rock the landing will clear). Let go, they glide on to the ring and come down, chopping or breaking what they land on with its tool, in the boat on the water; holding on brings them down at the farthest tile of the 20 ahead they can land on. With nowhere to land that way, a little hop and "Too far! There's nowhere to land that way." Their lead shrinks away as they take off and is back beside them when they land. A tap of Space still talks, and facing nothing says "Hold Space to fly!". A whoosh as they take off, a soft landing. Calmer with reduced motion (no bob or sway, the canopy snaps open and shut). No battle ever starts up there.
- The lead walks behind the trainer ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode): the animal that goes first follows one tile behind, bouncing along and turning as it walks, and never gets in the way. When who goes first changes it swaps with a little pop; while every animal is tired nobody follows, until the doctor makes one fit. Out on the water an animal that swims (a sea animal too) swims behind the boat, low in the water, and a game picked up in the boat finds it there, never on a beach beside it; with none, the lead hops into the boat and stands on the little deck at its bow, facing forward, big enough to know, until the trainer lands. A sea animal never follows on land.
- Out on the water only the animals that swim go first: the "goes first" tag moves to the first one standing (the otter, the frog, a sea animal), a number key or a card for one that can't swim says so ("Squirrel can't swim!"), and the pause menu greys its Go first and says why ("Squirrel can't swim, so it can only go first on land."). On land a sea animal is told the same way that it lives in the sea.
- Butterflies flutter over the land round the trainer (and behind the title): decoration, never an animal to meet.
- Other players in your world ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode, "Playing together"; §4 "Playing together"): trainers in their own shirts and caps with their names over their heads and their lead following, walking, sailing and gliding as you do; a bubble while they battle, see the doctor, take a break in the menu or play a match; fading in and out, and a poof when one turns up out of nowhere; a note at the top when someone comes into your world or goes home; arrows at the edge of the screen to the nearest out of sight.
- Grid movement with arrow keys / WASD (Caps Lock or not); blocked tiles (water without the boat, rock, trees, doctor tents) stop you; a tap always moves one tile. Browser shortcuts (Cmd+D, Ctrl+S) are left to the browser.
- Chopping and breaking (§4 "World"): facing a tree with the axe, "Press Enter to chop the tree" (the touch controls' Talk reads "Chop" and glows); Enter swings the axe over the trainer's shoulder, and the tree tips over away from the trainer and shrinks away with a few chips of wood flying, leaving a stump. With the pickaxe a rock shakes and cracks into pebbles, leaving gravel, snow and all on the peaks. The trainer walks through the gap at once. Without the tool, the first bump into a tree or a rock says the doctor sells one (once a game), and Enter there says it each time. A chop and a crack sound as the tool lands. Calmer with reduced motion.
- The player is a small trainer figure — a kid in a cap — that faces the way it walks, swings its arms and legs with every step, stands on top of hills and breathes while idle.
- A crude but recognisable low-poly figure for every species, built from primitives with one exaggerated tell each (the sea animals too, each tell in the top of the animal, over the water where it swims: a crab's claws, a starfish standing upright on one of its arms, a turtle's shell, a dolphin's fin, an octopus's eight arms curling up round its head, a whale's spout fountaining out; the small animals of the countryside too: a shrew's long snout, a wood mouse's huge ears, a rat's bare pink tail, a hedgehog's dome of spines, a mole's pink digging hands, a toad's warts and copper eyes, a robin's red breast, a stag beetle's antler jaws, a roe deer's white heart of a rump, a badger's striped face, a pine marten's yellow bib, a stoat's black tail tip, an adder's coil and zigzag, a heron's long legs and dagger beak, a tawny owl's round pale face, a raccoon's mask and ringed tail, a beaver's paddle tail and orange teeth). The birds' wings are their own parts, ready to beat. They fight in battles; the `?zoo` grid (see [[CHEATSHEET]]) shows them all at once.
- Party HUD: one card per kind in battle order ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode). A card of one animal shows its name (nickname if it has one) and an HP bar with numbers; a card of several is a stack, "Rabbit ×4" with how many are ready, tired soon (their HP bar red) and tired, and a slim bar of all their HP together, so a pile that is nearly out never reads "All ready". Pointing at a stack with the mouse, or tapping it, shows its animals beside it, each with its name and HP; clicking or tapping one makes it go first. A card whose animals are all tired is greyed with a "tired" tag. With two or more cards each shows its number key, and the lead's card is outlined and tagged "goes first". The column scrolls when it is long, and a card is dragged up or down to put the kinds in another order, to the very top or bottom too (on a tablet, after holding it still a moment; a card held at the column's edge keeps it scrolling).
- Tokens in the top right corner (a gold coin with a heart, "23 tokens"), and right under them the puzzles solved (a green tick, "312 puzzles": every right answer in a battle or at the doctor adds one, a wrong one nothing; §4 "Puzzles"). Under those the tools the kid owns, each with its picture and name, and under those the world the kid is in ("World 42"), the number to tell a friend.
- Coordinates in the bottom right corner ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode): where the kid stands, "x 40 · y 1000", counted from the world's spawn (x 0 · y 0 in every world), x growing to the right and y up the screen, negative numbers included; every step, sail and glide updates them. With the touch controls on they stand over Talk (or Fly).
- Choosing the lead from explore: the number keys 1–9 pick the card in that place, whose first animal standing goes first (§4 "Party"); a card of tired animals can't go first, and the message line says so.
- Pause menu (Escape in explore): the team's cards in battle order, where a card can go first or move up or down, and a card of several opens to list its animals, each of which can go first, move up or down within its card, or get a nickname typed in a name box; a greyed Go first says why (tired, going first already, or unable to go first where the trainer stands); then Worlds (the world the kid is in beside it), Who's here (how many others are in the world), the Language and Sound settings, "Keep playing" and "Start screen" (Quit to title). Go waits, greyed, with a line saying why, until the number is a world to go to. The trip is short and joyful: the world closes into a circle of sky round the trainer and the new one opens out of it with a whoosh and a chime, and the world's number pops up big ("World 42!") with "A new world to explore!", "Back where you left off." or "Home again!". Calmer with reduced motion.
- Message line: the latest message for a few seconds, the controls hint for the first few steps, and what Enter does in front of the player: "Press Enter to talk to the doctor" while facing a tent, "Press Enter to chop the tree" or "… break the rock" while facing one with its tool ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode).

It feels much better now.") and counts the tokens in. **Shop**: the tools on sale (the axe, the pickaxe, the boat and the paraglider) with their pictures, prices and what they do; buying asks the sum of the tokens left, and the right answer hands the tool over. While no tool is on sale, the shop says new things are coming soon. Escape or Back goes back from a puzzle, Bye leaves at any time. The doctor speaks English and Danish, like the rest of the game, and tells why wild animals are grumpy: they are a little bit sick.

### Encounters and battle

- Wild encounters: each step onto tall grass may start a battle, with the species picked by biome, the ground around the tall grass, distance from spawn and tier compared with the party's lead, on a bell around the lead's size (§4 "Wild encounters"). Every lead meets animals on the same steps, in every biome: mostly its own size, the sizes beside it now and then, and every size living there is possible; near home the bigger ones stay rare, and where nothing its size lives, the biggest there come out (a bear in the meadow meets red deer).
- Sea animals: six of them (crab, starfish, turtle, dolphin, octopus, whale; tiers 1 to 5) live out on the deep water, where each step in the boat may start a battle with one, by the same rules, as long as an animal that swims is standing to go first (§4 "Wild encounters", "At sea"). Caught, they join the team and fight only at sea; on land they "live in the sea", can't be chosen to go first there, and the doctor keeps an animal that walks with the kid when others go home.
- Animals live by their ground: frogs, otters, brown rats, toads, herons, raccoons and beavers by the water; squirrels, foxes, deer, wood mice, robins, stag beetles, roe deer, badgers, pine martens and tawny owls by the trees; wolves, bears, lizards, stoats and adders by the rocks; rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs and moles in open grass. With plenty of its ground around, an animal comes out four times as often as one of its size with none; near home the ground only picks among animals of the same size, so the start is as gentle as ever, and further out it sorts the sizes too. The first reed by the start, with the lake all round it, is mostly frogs, brown rats and toads.
- Frogs live in the river reeds, the first of them one step from the start: a third tier-1 animal, green and squat with big eyes, whose attacks ask number patterns and times tables. The small animals that live elsewhere still come down to the water near home, so the river's bigger animals stay rare there (1 in 19 together at that first reed).
- Eighteen small animals of the Nordic countryside (#89, the first wave): the shrew, wood mouse, brown rat, hedgehog, mole, common lizard, common toad, robin and stag beetle (tier 1), and the roe deer, badger, pine marten, stoat, adder, grey heron, tawny owl, raccoon and beaver (tier 2), each where it lives in nature (§4 "Animals"). Near home, with the starter in front, a zoo of small animals comes out of the grass (World 1, every tall grass within 32 tiles): shrews and hedgehogs 10% each, squirrels, wood mice and robins 9%, rabbits and moles 8%, brown rats, toads and stag beetles 7%, frogs 5%, lizards 4%, and the tier-2 animals 9% together. The toad and the beaver swim, and fight from the boat. The deer is now the red deer.
- Battle mode, Game Boy style ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode): the two animals face each other on a patch of the biome (out on the sea, the deep water, both animals swimming low in it, and the wild animal swims home or stays in the water at the end, not in the grass), with status boxes, a narration line, an action menu and the puzzle panel. The menu has two kinds of moves that look different: the attacks as chunky warm tiles, each with its own level (easy, medium or hard) and a hit badge with the damage at that level, and under them a row of round picture buttons for Leash (a ring and a word for how likely a catch is), Switch and Run. Until something is picked, the right card previews the highlighted move: an attack's kinds of sums as operator chips (+ − × ÷ √ ?), its three levels side by side with what each hits for, "That would tire it out!" when that hit would knock the wild animal out, and the sentence for readers; the wild animal's HP bar shows the part the hit would take off. "Correct!" shows the hit's badge, a hit lands with a comic burst beside the status box, bigger for a harder level, and a glow on a status box shows whose turn it is. Every attack is a puzzle answered by typing a number. The wild animal's reply and knock-outs are played out one line at a time. Mashing through them never picks: every choice that spends something or can't be taken back (the battle's menu, lists and result card, the doctor's list, the title's confirm, starters and name box, and Talk) waits for a quiet moment before a press counts, and Go! lights up when it would.
- Switching animals mid-battle (§4 "Battle"): Switch opens the party list, with tired animals greyed, the one in front marked, and those that can't fight where the battle is greyed and tagged ("can't swim" out on the water, "lives in the sea" on land); several of a kind stand under a heading with their name and how many ("Rabbit ×12"), a long list scrolls with its cursor, and left and right jump from one kind to the next; the switch takes the turn. After a knock-out the same list asks who goes next, and that pick is free. With a party of one the Switch row is greyed and says to catch a second animal.
- Catching with the leash; a caught animal always joins the party, with the HP it had, behind the others of its kind: there is no limit to the team.
- HP lost in a battle stays lost afterwards; a knocked-out animal stays tired and sits out battles until healed.
- Losing: when every animal that can fight there is tired (out on the water, every one that swims), the result card says "Good try!", and the player is back in the world beside the nearest tent they could get to (on foot, or by boat from the water), facing it, with the whole party healed and the doctor's line on the message line (§4 "Knock-out and healing").

### Friendly matches

- The Challenge button (§4 "Friendly matches"; [[UI_SPEC]] § Friendly matches): standing within two steps of another player, "Challenge Bo" shows above the message line (C, a click or a tap), greyed with why when it can't be pressed now (they are busy or out on the water, you are on the water, you have no animal that can fight on land, they said no a moment ago). Asking shows "Asking Bo…" with the time left and Never mind; being asked shows "Ada wants a friendly match!" with Yes and No and the time left, while you walk on. However an ask ends without a match, the message line says why ("Bo said no this time.", "Bo walked away.").
- The match is played on the battle's screen: the other player's animal as "Bo's Rabbit" with a paw, both names, the coin ("Bo starts!"), your attacks, levels and puzzles as in a wild battle, and Switch and Leave under them. On their turn their puzzle shows without its answer, with "Bo is thinking…", and Leave the match stays at hand. A switch, a knock-out and the pick of who goes next play out as in a battle.
- The end, warm for both, with Rematch? and Back to exploring; "Still there?" after a long wait on your turn; a note when the other drops out ("Bo dropped out. Waiting for them… 25"); and, when the game is updated mid-match, "The game is updating. Let's play again in a moment!" with Play again, lit once the friend is back.
- Your right answers in a match count toward your puzzles solved; nothing else of your game changes. Others see you "in a friendly match" (the star over your name).

### Saving

- The game saves itself in the browser after every change (the tokens and tools, the puzzles solved, and every tree chopped or rock broken, too), and when the page is hidden or closed; a reload or a later visit carries on exactly where you were, in the middle of a battle or a puzzle included (§4 "Saving"). "Welcome back!" on the message line when the first Continue after opening the page picks the saved game up.
- A backup on the server, sent in the background when it can be reached; a browser that lost its save but still knows the player gets its game back from it.
- Two windows of the game never undo each other: the one that falls behind takes no play, says so on a card while you play in the other, and loads the newer game ("Here's your newest game!") when you come to it.
- A save that cannot be read starts a new game with a message and is kept, set aside once the new game has been played. A newer version's save is never touched: a window on the old version plays no further and reloads for the new one ("A new version of the game is ready."). `?new`, `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop` play a game that is saved nowhere.
- A game saved by an older version carries on after an update exactly where it was (one from before numbered worlds in World 1, its home), and the old save is kept as it was, in the browser and on the server.
- Optional accounts (§4 "Saving"): the hourly "Keep your animals safe!" card, making an account with the game on screen, logging in and out from the pause menu and the title, the account's game on any tablet or computer, the guest's game kept apart, a newer game from another device taken with a kind word, and a game saved by a newer version never written over ("A new version of the game is ready."). A welcome link opens a game a grown-up brought over from another computer: "Welcome back, Aslak!", a password to pick, and the game where it was.

### Sound and feel

- Sound: short, soft sounds made while the game runs, no sound files ([[DESIGN]] § Sound) — a jingle when a wild animal jumps out, blips on the menus, a chime for a right answer and a soft bonk for a miss, a thump for a hit, a puff when an animal gets tired, the leash's whoosh, tick-tock and its fanfare or boing, a sparkle at the doctor, coins clinking when tokens change hands, a ding for a new lead and a fanfare for a win, a whoosh as the paraglider takes off and a soft thud and two notes as it lands. Every sound goes with something on screen.
- Sound setting: on by default, a Sound row in the pause menu and on the title, and M on any screen except while typing; remembered on this device.
- Little flourishes ([[UI_SPEC]] § Sound and juice): an iris that closes on the player and opens on the wild animal, a tired animal lying down to rest in a ring of dust with z's rising over it, sparkles along a healed animal's HP bar, the trainer's walk and the lead's bouncing behind it. A system set to reduce motion gets calmer versions.
- Big moments feel big: a caught animal cheers with a spin and stars while confetti bursts round it and two poppers rain confetti over the scene, and its result card shows its name in big letters over a burst of rays; a win gets a hop and a few stars, and stars round "You won!".

### Language

- The whole game in Danish and English, the animals' and attacks' names included. It starts in the language the browser prefers (Danish or English, else English); Language in the pause menu and on the title switches every word on screen at once and is remembered on this device (`?lang=da` / `?lang=en` in the address picks one for a visit).

### The page

- A name, and a fox for an icon in the browser's tab and on a Home Screen. A shared link shows a preview: the starters under "Animath", and a line in English and Danish. Search engines are kept away: a shared link is how the game is found.
- A browser that cannot draw the world, or runs no JavaScript, gets a kind card saying so instead of an empty page ("This browser can't draw Animath's world. Ask a grown-up for help!").

### Engine

- Puzzle catalog: 7 kinds across difficulty 1–10, seeded and deterministic.
- The count of puzzles solved (§4 "Puzzles"): `countSolved` adds one for every answer a battle, a doctor visit or a friendly match judged right (in a match, only the kid's own side's), read from the events where each answer is judged.
- Difficulty mapping (tier, attack, level) and healing difficulty.
- Damage and catch-probability formulas.
- Species catalog: 32 species, tiers 1–5: 26 on land (12 at tier 1, 11 at tier 2, then the red deer, the wolf and the bear; the frog, the otter, the common toad and the beaver swim too) and 6 in the sea (crab and starfish at tier 1, then turtle, dolphin, octopus, whale).
- Wild encounter tables and the per-step roll: a bell over tiers around the lead's (σ = 1 tier, pulled in above the lead near home), each tier's weight split among the animals of it living there, with visitors of the lead's tier near home at the river and in the mountains where bigger animals live, on top of the bell, weighed by the ground within 3 tiles of the tall grass (`surroundings`, `encounterTableAt`): each species favours water, trees, rocks or open grass, and near spawn the ground only chooses within each tier (§4 "Wild encounters"). Species list the realms they live in (land, water; the frog, the otter, the toad and the beaver both; the sea animals water only), and deep water is the water realm's encounter tile, where only the sea animals come out.
- Battle reducer: `startBattle` and `applyBattleIntent` play a whole wild battle by the rules in §4 — attacks, puzzles, answers, the wild animal's reply (which can miss an animal its own size or bigger), leash, flee, switching, knock-outs and the player's pick of who steps in — as intents in, events out.
- Friendly-match reducer (§4 "Friendly matches"), played on the server: `matchTeam` picks the team a party brings (a rude nickname dropped), `startMatch` flips the coin, `applyMatchIntent` plays one side's attack, answer, switch, pick after a knock-out, leaving or timing out, and `matchView` is what each player is sent, with no answer in it. Attacks go through the same code as a wild battle's. `challengeRefusal` is who may challenge whom, for the page's button and the server alike.
- Party rules (§4 "Party"): the party in species bundles (`bundles`, `bundled`); `applyPartyIntent` chooses the lead (an animal, or a kind's first animal standing), moves an animal within its kind or a whole kind, or names an animal, only while exploring, and keeps the bundles; `joinParty` puts a caught animal behind its kind, with no limit; `normalizeNickname` cleans a typed name; `leadIndex` is the lead that battles and encounters use.
- The starter rule (§4 "Starting out"): `STARTERS` (the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog, by id) and `chooseStarter`, which refuses anything else and cleans the starter's name; the authority's `new-game` intent asks it.
- World generation (§4 "World"): `generateChunk` builds any 16×16 chunk from the seed (biomes, tall grass and reeds, trees, rocks, water shallow and deep, tents on a sparse lattice), `spawnPoint` finds the start, and `nearestTent` the nearest tent on foot, by the paths the kid cleared too, or with the boat over the water too.
- Numbered worlds (§4 "World"): world numbers 1–9999 and their seeds (`worldSeed`; World 1 is the world played so far), a spawn in every world a doctor is at most 12 steps from and never boxed in (`spawnPoint`), and `travel`, which keeps each world as the kid left it, the worlds left behind within their count and their share of the clearings' budget. A new game's home is a random world from 2 to 9999; travel is in the protocol (`travel`, `travelled`), with no screen for it yet.
- Names (§4 "Starting out"): `checkName`, the one rule for a player's name (2–16 letters or numbers, no rude words, English and Danish), and `nameKey`, the same name whatever its case.
- World edits (§4 "World"): `WorldEdits`, the tiles a kid cleared, over the seeded world (`editedTileAt`, `editedChunk`); `clearTile` checks and does a clear (the tool owned, the tile a tree or a rock, beside the kid and faced), and keeps the save's share within its budget.
- The save document (§4 "Saving"): one shape, `SaveV2` (with the player's name, home, world and the worlds left behind), that the client and the server check and read the same way (`validateSave`, `readSave` with the upgrade from `SaveV1`, `restoreGame`, and `canReplace` for which save wins), with the tokens, the tools owned, the puzzles solved and the tiles cleared.
- Doctor rules (§4 "Knock-out and healing", "Tokens and the doctor's shop"): whether the player faces a tent, a doctor visit (`startDoctorVisit` and `applyDoctorIntent`, intents in, events out) that heals a hurt species per solved puzzle, helps animals home for tokens (`tokensForTier`), keeping one that walks on land (`keepsATeam`), and sells tools, each behind the sum of the tokens after it (`tokenPuzzle`), and the knock-out rule (`takeToDoctor`), which finds the nearest tent the kid can get to (on foot, or by boat from the water) and heals the whole party.
- The item catalog: `ITEMS` (the axe, the pickaxe, the boat and the paraglider, with prices and whether each is on sale: all four are) and `hasItem`, the check a tool's effect asks; `gearOf` and `isPassable`, where the boat lets the player go.
- Flight rules (§4 "World"): `takeOff` (the reach, the last tile of the 20 ahead the kid can come down on with what they own, or no take-off), `glideOn`, `landingDistance` (where letting go lands, which the ring shows) and `landFlight` (down there, clearing a tree or a rock with its tool: `clearLanding`); the authority's `take-off`, `glide` and `land`, each tile flown a step and never an encounter.
- Where each species can go and fight (§4 "Animals"): `realms` and `canFightIn`; the lead, a battle, a knock-out and the lead chosen in explore all ask it for where the player stands.
- Playing together (§4 "Playing together"): the presence messages and their checks (`net/protocol.ts`), shared by the browser and the server; who is near whom (`net/nearby.ts`); where going to another player lands (`arrivalSpot`).

### Server

- Health endpoint reporting database reachability and the build that is running.
- Online at its own address, https://animath.xyz (www.animath.xyz leads there): every change deploys itself, with no gap while it does, and the page carries the build it came from. While the game does not answer (a first start, or the server down) the address shows "Back in a moment! The game is getting ready." in the kid's language, and reloads itself into the game. The kids who played before it went live still play over the tunnel until their games move over (§6).
- Anonymous identity (development only: off on the public server): a player is created with one request and gets an id plus a secret, for the guest's backup.
- Optional accounts: register (with the guest's game), log in and out, and the account's save, which lands only with a higher save number (an older one gets the server's copy back). A password is kept only as a slow, salted hash; logging in and registering are rate-limited; a grown-up resets a forgotten password with the admin command.
- Moving a kid's game from another server: the admin command reads the kid's newest save from the database it was played on, without writing there, and another makes an account for it here, with no password, and a one-time welcome link (14 days) through which the kid picks one and is logged in.
- One backup save per player, stored and returned as a versioned document (the world number and home, the player's name, position, facing, the step and doctor-visit counts, the game's id and the save's number, a party of any size, a battle in progress). A page still open from before an update backs up as before (its save is upgraded on arrival). A backup lands only with a higher save number than the one stored; a different game or an unreadable save it replaces is kept aside.
- Presence: a WebSocket at `/api/ws` through which players in one world see each other, list who is there and go to each other; every message checked, too big, too many and too odd ones refused, dead connections dropped, pages from other sites refused, one presence per player.
- Friendly matches on the same socket: the invite, the match played in the engine's reducer with a seed only the server holds, each page sent its own view with no answer in it (a test reads the real frames), the turn clock, 30 seconds to come back, the rematch, and every match ended kindly when the server stops for a new version.
