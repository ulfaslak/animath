# Product: Animath

## 1. Vision

A cheerful low-poly adventure for kids in which every attack is a math puzzle. You explore a procedurally generated world, meet wild animals in the tall grass, battle them Game Boy Pokémon style with the animals you have already caught, and catch them with a leash when they are weak. Stronger animals ask harder math and hit harder. There is no grinding for levels: an animal is exactly as strong as the puzzles you can solve with it.

Built for the human's kid and their friends, who play it together: in a shared world they see each other explore, watch each other's battles and challenge each other to friendly matches (§4 "Playing together", "Friendly matches").

The game is called **Animath**. The GitHub repo is `ulfaslak/animath`; the package names (`mathgame`, `@mathgame/*`), the clone's folder and the image's name predate the name and stay as they are.

## 2. Players

- **Kids aged roughly 6–12**. Reading level: short words, big text. The puzzle ladder spans from single-digit addition to three-digit multiplication and square roots.
- **Devices**: a laptop or desktop browser with a keyboard, or a tablet held sideways, played with fingers alone (touch controls, [[UI_SPEC]] § Pointer and touch). A mouse works every menu too. Phones are not a target, though a phone held sideways plays, and the explore screen, the battle's screen (wild battles and friendly matches alike), the witch doctor's card and the animal book are laid out for one.
- **No login needed.** Open the link and you are in. The game is saved in the browser as you play, so a reload, or coming back another day, picks up where you left off. An account is optional, never required: it keeps the game on the server and brings it to another tablet or computer (§4 "Saving").

## 3. Core loop

Explore → step into tall grass → wild encounter → battle by solving puzzles → catch it (leash) or beat it → heal knocked-out animals at a witch doctor's tent, and set the animals you don't keep free, back to the wild, for tokens → buy tools with the tokens → venture into fiercer biomes with a stronger party.

## 4. Game rules

This section is the spec the engine implements. Formulas here are the source of truth; the code in `packages/engine` must match them, and a change to either updates the other in the same PR.

### Modes

Two modes only, borrowed from the Game Boy games: **Explore** and **Battle**. Fixed camera angle, no zoom, no rotation. Everything else about the presentation is our own (see [[DESIGN]], [[UI_SPEC]]).

### World

- Grid-based. The player moves one tile at a time in four directions.
- Procedurally generated from a seed, in 16×16 chunks, infinite in every direction. The same seed always yields the same world, so a shared world needs no map download.
- **Numbered worlds.** Every world has a number from 1 to 9999, and the number is all it takes to make it: the same number is the same world on every machine, and neighbouring numbers are unrelated worlds. World 1 is the world every game was played in before worlds had numbers, tile for tile, with the start at (−2, 6). A new game starts in a world of its own, its **home**: a number picked at random from 2 to 9999, so strangers don't all start in one world. A game saved before numbered worlds is in World 1, and World 1 is its home.
- **Where a world begins** (its spawn, where a new game or a first visit starts): a grass tile a witch doctor's tent is at most **12 steps** from on foot, without a tool or the boat, with room to roam (at least 1,000 tiles in reach on foot): the nearest such tile to the middle of the world. In World 1 that is (−2, 6) on the engine's grid, the witch doctor 7 steps away. The coordinates a kid sees count from the spawn, so every world's reads x 0 · y 0 ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode).
- **Going to another world** (while exploring, never in a battle or at the witch doctor's): the world left is kept as the kid left it — where they stood, the way they faced, the trees and rocks they cleared there — and the world reached is picked up the same way, or at its spawn, facing down, on a first visit. The kid stays in the land they are in (§4 "Lands"): the animals, tokens and tools of that land go along, and so do the name and the step count, so the animals ahead keep coming in order. A game remembers 100 of the worlds it has left: its home always, and the others it left most recently; a world forgotten starts at its spawn again. A kid travels from the pause menu's Worlds screen: to any world by its number, or home.
- Biomes: **meadow** (easy animals), **forest** (mid), **river** banks (easy–mid, water animals), **mountain** (hard), and the **sea**: the deep water out in the middle of the lakes, reached only by boat, where the sea animals live.
- Tiles: grass; **tall grass** (where encounters happen); sand; water, shallow along every shore and **deep** further out; rock and trees (blocked); **tent** (a witch doctor's; blocked too, you talk to him from the tile beside it). Water is blocked on foot and open to a player with the boat.
- **The boat** (from the witch doctor's shop, §4 "Tokens and the witch doctor's shop") takes the player out on the water, shallow and deep: with it a step onto any water tile sails there, and a step from the water onto walkable ground lands. Rock, trees and tents stop a boat as they stop a walk.
- **Deep water** is water with water all round it, two tiles out: the 5×5 square around the tile is all water. So the shallows along every shore are at least two tiles wide, a river narrower than five tiles has no deep water, and deep water is always at least three steps from any land. It is the sea biome. In World 1 it lies four steps from the start, straight up into the lake just north of it.
- A witch doctor stands at every small tent, his pot bubbling on a campfire beside him, in the woods or near water. Not rare, not everywhere. The game calls him "the witch doctor" ("heksedoktoren" in Danish), never "the doctor" alone, and so do these docs.
- **Clearing the way.** With the **axe**, a kid chops down a tree; with the **pickaxe**, breaks a rock: stand next to it, face it, and interact, as with a tent. The tile is ground from then on (a stump on the forest floor, gravel on the mountain), walkable and never tall grass, so it starts no battle; it stays cleared, saved with the game. Trees grow only in the forest and rocks only in the mountains, the snow-capped peaks included. Tall grass, sand, water and tents are never cleared, whatever tools the kid has. Without the tool, facing a tree or a rock says the witch doctor sells one. Clearing changes where a kid can walk, never who comes out of the grass (§4 "Wild encounters" reads the world as it was made).
- **The paraglider** (from the witch doctor's shop): hold Space (on a tablet, the Fly button) and the kid jumps into the air and glides straight ahead, the way they face, over anything (trees, rocks, tents, water), a tile about every 0.15 s; there is no steering. A tap of Space still talks, chops or breaks; Enter never flies. **Where they can come down** depends only on what they own: ground always (grass, tall grass, sand, a stump or gravel they made); water, shallow or deep, only with the boat (they land in it); a tree only with the axe and a rock only with the pickaxe, and landing on it clears it, exactly as a chop from beside it does; a tent never. **The reach** is fixed at take-off: the last tile of the 20 ahead they could come down on. With none, they don't take off ("Too far! There's nowhere to land that way.") — a lake more than 19 tiles across still needs the boat. **Letting go** (Space up, the finger off Fly, Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, the window left) lands them on the first tile at or after the one they are over that they can come down on, never the one they took off from; **holding on** lands them at the reach. So a flight always ends where they can stand, at most 20 tiles out, and nobody is ever put back. A ring on the ground shows where letting go would land them (over water without the boat, on the far shore). Each tile flown is a step; nothing on the ground notices a kid up in the air, so a flight never starts a battle in the grass or at sea, not on the tile they land on either. Only a bird can: with a bird in the team, a wild bird may notice the glider and follow it down, and its battle is fought in the air as they land (§4 "Wild encounters", "In the sky"; §4 "Battle"). Taking off from the boat works: the boat swings onto their back. They can fly as often as they like.
- **Riding** (the harness, from the witch doctor's shop): with it, a lead big enough to carry a kid carries them on its back wherever they go on land. Six are: the red deer, the wild boar, the wolf, the bear, the moose and the European bison, the big land animals of tier 3 and up; the lynx and the wolverine, at about 20 kg, are not, and nothing smaller is. Riding is only a look: the kid goes where, as fast and as far as on foot, and meets exactly what they would on foot. A smaller lead walks behind as ever. Out on the water the boat carries the kid (a big lead that can't swim rides in it, as before), and up in the air nobody carries them: off the animal's back as they sail or take off, back on as they step onto land or come down.
- **Room for thousands.** A game keeps every tile its kid cleared, in every world and every land together, up to about 24 KB of them in the save: thousands of tiles as kids clear them (paths and clearings), fewer only if they are spread one to a chunk all over the world. Past that, the cleared tiles farthest from the kid grow back, a chunk at a time: first in the worlds the kid has left (the home world last of those), then far away in the world they are in, never one of the 25 chunks round the kid, so the save stays small.

### Animals

- A **species** has a tier (1–5), max HP, a catch rate, 1–4 attacks (typically 2–4), the biomes it lives in, the ground it favours there (§4 "Wild encounters") and the realms it can go in: on land, in the water, up in the **air**, or more than one. The frog, the otter, the common toad, the beaver and the mute swan are **amphibious**, going on land and in the water; the fourteen **sea animals** go in the water only. The **birds** fly: the robin, the grey heron, the tawny owl, the buzzard, the mute swan (which also swims), the eagle-owl, the golden eagle and the sea eagle go on land and in the air, and **only birds fly** (a stag beetle does in nature, but not in the game: a rule a kid can hold in their head). The others go on land only. Encounters happen in the tall grass and reeds on land, on the deep water out at sea, and up in the air over any tile the glider enters (§4 "Wild encounters"): an animal that lives only in the water never comes out of the grass, one that lives on land never out of the sea, and only a bird ever notices the glider.
- **An animal fights only where it can go.** On land, every land and amphibious animal can fight, the birds too. Out on the water, in the boat, only the ones that swim: the amphibious animals and the sea animals. Up in the air, after a bird followed the glider down, only the birds: the others "can't fly" and sit it out on the ground, as the animals that can't swim sit out a sea battle in the boat. On land a sea animal can't fight: it "lives in the sea", as a squirrel can't swim. The human's rule: "you can't fight on water unless you have an amphibious animal", and the air is a place as the water is. Where each species lives is its biomes: the frog and the otter swim, but they live by the river, and are met in its reeds; a bird is caught in the grass like any land animal, and met in the air over the biomes whose sky it flies (§4 "Wild encounters", "In the sky").
- **Animals have no levels.** A species' strength is entirely its attack table and HP. Tier is a label for "how fierce", used to scale puzzles, to decide who comes out of the grass for which lead, whether a wild animal can miss, and how many tokens an animal brings when it is set free; catch odds come from each species' own catch rate.
- Attacks are ordered weakest to strongest, n = 1..N. Each attack can be used at level 1, 2 or 3.
- **Damage** grows with both: `damage(n, level) = power_n × [1, 1.6, 2.4][level]`, rounded. Attack N at level 3 is the species' hardest hit; attack 1 at level 1 its softest. Within a species `power` strictly increases with n.
- **Puzzle difficulty** grows the same way. An attack's easy level asks `base[tier] + 0.75·(n−1)`, rounded, with `base = [1, 2, 4, 5, 7]` for tiers 1–5, but never more than 8; each level up asks exactly one more: `difficulty(tier, n, level) = min(round(base[tier] + 0.75·(n−1)), 8) + (level−1)`. So a squirrel asks difficulty 1–4 and a bear 7–10, and every level of an attack asks one difficulty more than the one below it, all the way to 10: medium and hard never ask at the same difficulty. Neighbouring difficulties can still share a puzzle now and then ("Puzzles" below promises bigger numbers two difficulties apart), so a harder level asks harder sums on average, not every time. The cap of 8 only reaches the third and fourth attacks of tier-5 animals: the bear's Maul and Crush, which ask 8, 9 and 10 (they asked 9, 10 and 10 before it, #32): their easy and medium each got a step easier for the same damage; the moose's Antler Shove, the European bison's Head Butt and Stampede, and at sea the humpback whale's Spout and Big Wave and the orca's Tail Slap and Wave Wash ask the same. Four attacks in the bear's four difficulties must share ladders. Roar, Maul and Crush all ask 8, 9 and 10, so at a given difficulty only the kind of sum and the damage set them apart, and Crush hits hardest; so do the bison's, the humpback whale's and the orca's last three attacks, and the moose's last two.
- Tier ladder: squirrel, rabbit, frog, shrew, wood mouse, brown rat, hedgehog, mole, common lizard, common toad, robin, stag beetle (1) · fox, otter, roe deer, badger, pine marten, stoat, adder, grey heron, tawny owl, raccoon, beaver, buzzard (2) · red deer, wild boar, mute swan, eagle-owl (3) · wolf, lynx, wolverine, golden eagle, sea eagle (4) · bear, moose, European bison (5). Don't face a bear with a squirrel. At sea: crab, starfish, moon jellyfish, plaice (1) · turtle, lion's mane, lobster (2) · dolphin, harbour seal, porpoise (3) · octopus, grey seal (4) · humpback whale, orca (5); don't face a whale with a crab.
- **The animals of the Nordic countryside** (#89, in three waves: the small ones, the big ones and the sea's): every animal lives wild in temperate Northern Europe or on both sides of the North Atlantic (the raccoon came from North America and lives wild in Europe now too), is named by its everyday name, the species a kid can learn, and is sized and placed as in nature. Within a tier the numbers stay inside the band the prototype animals set (tier 1: 18–26 HP, a catch rate of 0.8–0.9, 2–3 attacks of power 3–8; tier 2: 30–36 HP, 0.6–0.7, 2–3 attacks of power 5–12; tier 3: 46–56 HP, 0.45–0.5, 2 attacks of power 8–13; tier 4: 66–72 HP, 0.3–0.35, 3 attacks of power 10–19; tier 5: 105–110 HP, 0.2, 3–4 attacks of power 12–27, which the bear and the humpback whale, at 100 HP and 28, stay outside of as they were), so the balance promises below hold; each animal's character is in the shape of its numbers: the hedgehog is a tank with a weak first attack, the shrew a glass cannon, the wolverine the toughest of its tier and the hardest to catch, and the moose has the most HP of all but three attacks, where the bison has four. Tier-1 animals ask sums and number patterns, and times tables only on a second or third attack, as the frog does; nothing asks sharing (division) or square roots below tier 3, so the tawny owl's Talon Grab asks times tables and missing numbers ([[DECISIONS]] § Gameplay). The big ones ask times tables first, then patterns, and sharing and square roots on their strongest attacks, as the red deer, the wolf and the bear do.

### Wild encounters

- Each step that lands on **tall grass**, or out at sea on **deep water**, has a **1-in-10** chance of starting a wild battle: one encounter per ten such steps on average, whichever animal leads the party and wherever it is, since animals live in every tall grass and every deep water: no grass is ever quiet. No other tile ever starts one: not the shallows along a shore, so crossing a river in the boat is always peaceful.
- **The lead decides who comes out, on a bell.** The lead (§4 "Party") is the first animal in the party that isn't tired and can fight there, the one that steps into the battle first, and the player can choose it while exploring. The human asked for "almost a bell shape dist around the tier animal you wield, but everything is possible really". With `L` the lead's tier and `k = t − L` how many tiers a tier `t` is above it (negative below it), the animals of tier `t` living in the biome together weigh **`e^(−k²/2)`** next to the lead's own tier, a bell with σ = 1 tier: the lead's own size **1**, one size away **0.61**, two **0.14**, three **0.011**, four **0.0003** (above the lead, near home, it is pulled in: the distance rule below). So the lead's own size is the likeliest wherever it lives, the sizes beside it next, and every size living there can come out, but for the few the distance rule pulls in near home. A tier weighs the same however many kinds of animal it has; they split its weight evenly (and then the ground has its say, below). A tier the biome doesn't hold never comes out there, and the tiers it does hold share its place: a bear in the meadow, where the biggest animal is the red deer, meets red deer 92% of the time, a tier-2 animal 7.6% and a small one 0.2%. Since #89's big animals the meadow is the only land biome without every size (it has none of tier 4 or 5): the forest, the river and the mountains hold all five.
- The animal comes from the biome's **encounter table**: every species whose habitats include the biome, plus the visitors below, each weighed again by the ground around the tall grass (below). It appears at full HP.
- **Distance rule: near home the bell's side above the lead is pulled in.** Fierce animals are rare near the start and grow commoner far away. With `d` the straight-line distance in tiles from the spawn tile, `danger = clamp((d − 32) / 96, 0, 1)`. A tier below the lead weighs `e^(−k²/2)` at any distance. A tier `k` above it weighs **`9^(−k²)`** inside the **safe radius** (32 tiles): one tier up **1/9**, two up 1 in 6,561, three up about 1 in 400 million, and four up never: a roll's number moves in steps of 2^−32, and `9^−16` falls between two of them, so a starter meets no bear, moose, bison or whale within 32 tiles (from about 80 tiles out it can); from the **wild radius** (128 tiles) out it weighs `e^(−k²/2)`, as much as a tier as far below, so far from home the bell is symmetric; in between it weighs `9^(−k²·(1−danger)) · e^(−k²·danger/2)`, so every tier above the lead grows commoner with every step out, the further above the faster. Near home, where the lead's size and the one above it live, **one battle in 10 is the bigger animal**, and hardly ever anything bigger still.
- **Visitors near home.** Animals come down to the water and up the hills: at the river and in the mountains, wherever an animal bigger than the lead lives there, every species of the lead's tier that doesn't live there also comes out, as a visitor. The visitors add **three times** the lead's own tier to it there, times `1 − danger`, however many kinds they are: three times the lead's size living there inside the safe radius, thinning out to nothing at the wild radius. They come on top of the bell, so near home they make the lead's size four times as common there, and as they thin out they take their weight with them. ### Battle

- Turn-based, one action per turn, like the Game Boy games (the screen: [[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode).
- The player always acts first. A round is one player action followed by the wild animal's reply, unless the action ended the battle.
- Player's turn: pick an attack and a level → a puzzle appears → answer. **Correct** → full damage for that attack and level. **Wrong** → the attack misses (0 damage) and the turn is over. Once an attack is picked there is no backing out: giving up on the puzzle is the same as answering it wrong. There is no timer; whether a gentle timer or a speed bonus should exist is an open question for the human.
- The puzzle's difficulty comes from the **player's** animal — `difficulty(tier, n, level)` with that species' tier — and its kind is one the chosen attack can ask. A bear in your party asks bear-hard questions.
- Opponent's turn (wild animal): picks one of its attacks uniformly at random and hits for that attack's level-1 damage (its `power`). Wild animals don't solve puzzles. Facing an animal of its **own tier or a fiercer one**, a wild animal is careful and **misses 44% of the time** (11 attacks in 25); facing a **smaller** animal it never misses. It sizes up whoever is in front, turn by turn: a fox never misses your squirrel, but can miss the bear that steps in after it.
- So a kid who always picks the easiest puzzle (the weakest attack at level 1) and gets it right beats an animal of their own tier about 80% of the time, averaged over the catalog's same-tier pairs; right 7 times in 10, about 46%. The starter squirrel against the tier-1 animals the ground brings out near home (in World 1 shrews and hedgehogs 11% each, squirrels, wood mice and robins 10%, rabbits 9%, and the rest): about 72% and 40%; in the meadow alone, where the rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs and moles are 18% each, 68% and 36%; by the river, 72% and 39%. Against a rabbit alone it is 61% and 27%, against a brown rat the same, against a mole, the hardest small animal for it, 58% and 25%, and against a frog 76% and 38%. A frog in front wins about as often as a rabbit: 97% against a squirrel, 70% against a rabbit, 79% against another frog, and near home against the tier-1 animals 79% and 46% (the rabbit 81% and 49%). Against everything that comes out near home in World 1, the tier-2 animals included, the frog wins 72% and 42%, the squirrel 66% and 37%. One tier up on the easiest puzzle is still a loss (every time, in all 283 pairs one tier apart that can meet), and a battle in which every animal you send out is smaller than the wild one plays exactly as if wild animals never missed.
- Other actions on the player's turn: throw a leash (wild battles), flee (wild battles; always succeeds and costs nothing), or switch animals.
- **Switching**: instead of acting, send in another party member. The switch is the turn: the wild animal replies at once, against the newcomer, and sizes it up like any animal in front, so bringing in a bigger animal makes a miss possible. You can't switch to the animal already in front, to a tired one or to one that can't fight where the battle is (below), and not in the middle of a puzzle: once an attack is picked, the turn is committed. Every switch you choose hands the wild animal a turn, so switching back and forth only wears your party down and can never stall a battle.
- When the player's animal is knocked out and another party member that can fight there is still standing, the player picks who steps in: any animal that isn't tired and can fight there. That switch is free (the wild animal has just had its turn), and then the player chooses an action. HP lost in a battle stays lost afterwards.
- A battle ends **won** when the wild animal reaches 0 HP, **lost** when every animal in the party that can fight there is knocked out, **caught** on a successful leash throw, or **fled**.
- **On the water**, from the boat, only the animals that swim fight (§4 "Animals"): the first one standing steps in, only another one standing can be switched in (the others "can't swim" and sit it out in the boat), and the battle is **lost** once every one that swims is tired, whoever still stands in the boat. On land the sea animals sit a battle out the same way ("lives in the sea"). The sea animals are met out on the deep water (§4 "Wild encounters", "At sea"), and a battle there is fought on the water ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode draws it), the wild one swimming home or staying in the water at the end.
- **Up in the air** (#91), after a bird followed the glider down: the landing comes first, the bird swoops in to hover in front of the kid, then the battle's circle closes, and the battle is fought **in the air**, whatever the tile they came down on (tall grass, sand, or the boat on a lake). Only the birds fight (§4 "Animals"): the first one standing steps in, only another bird standing can be switched in (the others "can't fly": "Squirrel can't fly. It only fights on the ground."), and the battle is **lost** once every bird is tired, whoever still stands on the ground. It opens with "A wild Buzzard swoops down!", is fought in the sky ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode draws it), and ends with the wild bird flying home ("The wild Buzzard flies home to rest.") or, run from, staying up in the sky. After it the kid stands where they came down, in the boat on the water; lost, they stay there too, nobody healed (§4 "Knock-out and healing"): a walker still standing leads on the grass as ever, and with nobody who walks standing the team needs the witch doctor there, and the way to the nearest tent is walked or sailed, never flown. With every bird tired nothing up in the air notices them either, until a witch doctor has helped. A reload is no escape: a game saved while a bird follows the glider comes back down, in its battle.

### Friendly matches

Two players can battle each other, just for fun. A match changes nothing: every animal's HP, the knock-outs, the party and the tokens are exactly as they were before it, whoever wins. Only the kid's count of puzzles solved goes up, by their own right answers, as it does anywhere (§4 "Puzzles").

- **Challenging.** Standing within two steps of another player in the same world (diagonals count: any tile of the 5 × 5 square round you), both of you on land and exploring, you can challenge them. They have 20 seconds to say yes or no, and can walk on meanwhile; walking out of reach, sailing off, meeting a wild animal, going to the witch doctor or opening the menu is a no that nobody has to say. You wait for the answer (Never mind takes the ask back). After a no, or no answer, you wait 30 seconds before asking the same player again; after taking an ask back (or leaving the game, or walking off, while asking), 10. A player is asked by one challenger at a time: a second is told the player is busy with someone else. Two players who ask each other at the same moment both said yes. You can't challenge, or be challenged, while in a match, asking, or being asked, and a player with no animal that can fight on land can't challenge (the button says why).
- **Teams.** Each player brings the first three animals in their party order that can fight on land, tired ones too, all at full HP for the match; a party with fewer brings what it has. A match is fought on land, so the sea animals sit it out.
- **Turns.** A coin flip decides who goes first, and both screens say who ("Bo starts!", "You start!"), and then the players take turns. On your turn you pick an attack and its level (easy, medium or hard) and solve its puzzle, or you switch to another animal that is standing, which uses the turn. The other player sees your puzzle while you think, but never its answer, nor what you type, and reads that you are thinking. The other player's animals go by their name ("Bo's Rabbit"), and a nickname with a rude word in it is never shown to the other player: that animal goes by its kind's name.
- **Attacks** are asked and land exactly as in a wild battle (§4 "Animals", "Battle"): the puzzle's difficulty comes from your own animal's tier, the attack and the level, so a kid with bigger animals solves harder sums, and a right answer hits for the attack's damage at that level. A wrong answer misses. Nobody misses on their own: the careful miss belongs to wild animals. There is no leash and no running away.
- **Knock-outs.** When an animal is knocked out, its player picks which one steps in, for free, and then takes their turn. A player with no animal left standing loses.
- **Leaving.** Either player can leave the match at any time, their turn or not, and the other one wins; the one who left goes straight back to exploring, free at once: a friend can challenge them, and they can challenge a friend, straight away. Leaving is never one slip of a key: Escape asks "Leave the match?" first, with Stay the answer ready to press. A player whose game drops out (the page closed, the connection lost) has 30 seconds to come back, and the match waits for them, saying so to the other; back in time, the match goes on where it was (a reloaded page picks it up after Continue), or, if it ended meanwhile, they see how; not back in time, they lose the same way ("Bo had to go."). There is no draw and no turn limit: two players who never answer right, or only ever switch, play on until one of them leaves.
- **Sitting on a turn.** A slow puzzle-solver is never hurried: every key on your turn (typing, choosing) counts as being there. After 90 seconds on your turn without a key, the match asks "Still there?" ("I'm here!", or any key, and the clock starts again); after 3 minutes without one, the match ends and the other player wins ("Bo wandered off.").
- **After the match.** Both see who won, kindly: "You won! Great match, Bo!" or "Ada won this time! Good match! Want to play again?". "Rematch?" starts a new match between the same two once both have said yes (a new coin, the same teams if nothing changed), and "Back to exploring" ends it for you and tells the other. The result you went back from never comes back: pressed while the connection is gone, Back to exploring still holds when the connection returns, and the other hears it once you are back (or when your 30 seconds run out). Back to exploring also wins over a Rematch? it crosses: when the other's Rematch? reaches the game just before your Back, no rematch starts for you, and the other is back on their result, reading "Ada went back to exploring.", as after any Back. A rematch waits two minutes at most, and never keeps anyone busy: a kid back to exploring can be challenged, and challenge, at once.
- **A new version of the game.** When the game is updated in the middle of a match, the match ends for both with no winner: "The game is updating. Let's play again in a moment!". A moment later both are back, and "Play again" asks the same friend again (one tap each: the second "Play again" is a yes). When the game restarts without warning (it crashed), both see "The game restarted. Want to play again?" and the same "Play again": neither left, so neither is told the match ended while they were away.
- **How it plays out**, over 1,000 simulated 3-against-3 matches per line, each kid picking a random attack at a random level: two teams of tier-1 animals whose kids are both right 7 times in 10 play about 20 puzzles, 10 each (16 when both are right 9 times in 10, 28 at 5 in 10; one match in ten takes more than 26), and two teams of bears about 27. Always picking the easiest attack about doubles that (44 puzzles for tier 1, 72 for bears); always the hardest, 14. The kid who goes first wins about 55% of even matches, 57–60% when both are right 9 times in 10. Skill counts: right 9 times in 10 against 7 in 10, with the same animals, wins about 4 matches in 5. Size counts more: at the same accuracy a team one tier smaller wins at most 1 match in 12, and a tier-1 team against a tier-2 one almost never (1%); only much better sums close a one-tier gap (right 9 times in 10 against 5 in 10: tier 1 against tier 2 wins 32%, tier 2 against tier 3 60%). Those lines are for fixed teams (squirrel, rabbit and frog; fox, otter and fox; three of a kind above). Teams of three drawn at random from a tier's land animals play the same way (#89): two tier-1 teams play about 20 puzzles and the kid who goes first wins 60%, right 9 times in 10 against 7 wins 79%, a tier-1 team against a tier-2 one wins 2% at the same accuracy and 40% when right 9 times in 10 against 5, and two tier-2 teams play about 21 puzzles. The big ones too: two tier-3 teams play about 26 puzzles, two tier-4 teams 26 and two tier-5 teams 30; the kid who goes first wins 54–55% of them, right 9 times in 10 against 7 wins 80–83%, and a team one tier smaller wins 6% (tier 2 against tier 3), 4% (3 against 4) and 2.5% (4 against 5) at the same accuracy, and about half when right 9 times in 10 against 5.

### Catching

- Throw a leash when the wild animal is weak. `P(success) = catchRate × leashQuality × 2^(−hp/0.2)` where `hp` is the fraction of HP remaining, capped at 95%.
- Every 20% of HP halves the odds. A squirrel (rate 0.9) at 10% HP ≈ 64%; a bear (rate 0.2) at 10% ≈ 14%, at 50% ≈ 3.5%.
- `leashQuality` is 1 for the starter leash, the only leash there is: the witch doctor's shop sells no leash yet (§6).
- A failed throw costs the turn. A caught animal keeps the HP it had when the leash landed, and always joins the party (§4 "Party"): behind the others of its kind, or as a new kind at the end.

### Knock-out and healing

- An animal at 0 HP is **knocked out** and can't battle until healed.
- **Talking to the witch doctor**: stand on a tile next to a tent, face it and interact. Walking into a tent turns you to face it. Standing beside it without facing it is not enough.
- The witch doctor helps any animal below full HP, knocked out or only hurt.
- The witch doctor heals a whole species in exchange for a solved puzzle: pick an animal, solve its puzzle, and it and every other hurt animal of its species in the team are back to full HP. Difficulty scales with the tier (`base[tier] + 1`: every small animal 2, the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog among them, every tier-2 animal 3, the fox and the otter among them, every tier-3 animal 5, the red deer among them, every tier-4 animal 6, the wolf among them, every tier-5 animal 8, the bear among them), so healing bears is harder than healing squirrels, and it is the same for one bear as for three. The puzzle's kind is one the animal's own attacks ask, so a kid meets the kind of sum they already know from battle.
- **A wrong answer at the witch doctor's costs nothing.** HP stays where it was and a different puzzle takes its place, as many times as it takes. The player can pick another animal, go back to the list or leave at any time.
- **Losing means walking to a witch doctor**, because the human asked for it ([[DECISIONS]] § Gameplay). When every animal in the party that could fight where the battle was is knocked out (out on the water, every one that swims; up in the air, every bird), the battle is lost, and the player is back in the world where they stood, facing the way they faced: nobody is healed and nobody is moved. They walk (or sail) to a witch doctor's tent and heal there, as always, a puzzle a kind. Nothing else is lost: no tokens, no animals, only the walk.
- **A team that needs the witch doctor meets nothing.** Nothing comes out of the tall grass or the deep water while nobody standing can fight there (§4 "Party": there is no lead). A team **needs the witch doctor** when nobody standing can fight where the player is, nor on land: on land, when every animal that walks is tired (only sea animals standing, or nobody); out on the water, when every animal is tired. Until a witch doctor has made one of them fit, the line under the message says so ("Your animals are tired. Walk to the witch doctor's tent!", out on the water "… Sail to the witch doctor's tent!", or, walled in with the paraglider, "… Fly out to find the witch doctor's tent!"), and an arrow at the edge of the screen points to the nearest tent until it is in plain sight ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode). A walker standing in the boat after a battle at sea is lost can still battle on land: sailing on is sailing in peace, and needs no witch doctor, as it always did.
- **The nearest tent is the nearest the way the player gets about**: the fewest steps over walkable ground, never through water, rock, trees or another tent; with the boat, over the water too. A tent on an island (without a boat) or boxed in by trees is never the one pointed to, so the arrow always leads somewhere the kid can get to. Ties go to the tent further up, then further left. It is looked for again at every step on the ground or the water, from wherever the player now is (a go-to, another world and a reload too), up to 400 steps away, and it is the same however the kid got there; up on the glider it keeps the tent it had where the kid took off, until they are down.
- **A witch doctor comes where no tent is near, to a kid who can't fly out.** When a team that needs the witch doctor has no tent within 200 steps the way the player walks or sails (walled in, or simply no tent that close) as a battle is lost there, or as a go-to or a trip to another world puts the kid there, and the kid has no paraglider, a witch doctor comes instead: the player stays where they are (out on the water, in the boat), and the whole party is looked after all the same ("A witch doctor came by and looked after your animals. Everyone feels better!"). A kid with the paraglider flies out, as they flew in: a spot no tent can be walked to from is reached by gliding in, and a witch doctor there would make a trip out and back, or a battle lost there on purpose, a free full heal.
- **A reload is no heal**: a game picked up with a team that needs the witch doctor is exactly as tired as it was, where it was, wherever that is. A glide is no heal either: a kid who glides somewhere no tent can be walked to from keeps the team tired there, and glides back out, and a trip out and back from there heals nobody. A trip to another world or to a friend takes the team along as it is; a first visit to a world starts at its spawn, a witch doctor's tent at most 12 steps away (§4 "World").
- **Friendly matches are unaffected**: each side brings full-health copies of its animals, tired ones too, and a match changes nothing (§4 "Friendly matches").

### Tokens and the witch doctor's shop

- **Why wild animals attack.** They are a little bit sick, which makes them grumpy. An animal the kid sets free at the witch doctor's is made better and goes home to the wild, and the witch doctor thanks the kid with **tokens**.
- **Tokens** are a whole number, never below 0, saved with the game. A new game starts with none.
- **Setting animals free.** At the witch doctor's the kid picks any animals of the team, tired ones too (the witch doctor makes them better first), and always keeps at least one that isn't tired and can fight on land, where every tent stands, so the team can still battle when it walks on (a crab alone could not). A whole kind can be picked at once; when every one of it going would leave no such animal, the first of it that is one stays. Each animal of tier `k` brings `k·(k + 1)` tokens: **2, 6, 12, 20, 30** for tiers 1 to 5. A bigger animal is harder to catch and needs a bigger lead to meet often (§4 "Wild encounters"), so it brings more, and catching the smallest animals over and over is the slow way to the shop.
- **The shop** sells tools, one of each, for good: the **axe** (8 tokens; chops trees), the **pickaxe** (13; breaks rocks), the **boat** (21; sails on water), the **paraglider** (34; glides over anything, §4 "World") and the **harness** (34, what the paraglider costs, the human's call; rides a big lead, §4 "World"). A tool is on sale only once what it does is built, and all five are; were nothing on sale, the shop would say new things are coming soon. With too few tokens, or a tool already owned, the tool can't be bought, and the card says why. A boat or a paraglider bought grows onto the trainer's back; closing the card after buying the paraglider says how to fly it ("Hold Space to fly!", on a tablet "Hold Fly to fly!"), and after buying the harness how to ride ("Put a big animal first and hop on!").
- **Every token that changes hands is a sum.** Setting animals free asks "You have 12 tokens. You get 8 more. How many will you have?" (`12 + 8 = ?`), once per hand-over however many animals go; buying asks "You have 23 tokens. The axe costs 8. How many will you have left?" (`23 − 8 = ?`). The numbers are the real ones, the engine judges the answer, and only the right answer completes it: the animals go home and the tokens are counted in, or the tool is the kid's and its price comes off. A wrong answer says "Not quite! Try again." and asks the same sum again, at no cost. Backing out, picking something else or leaving is always free, and nothing happens that was not answered.
- **A gentle confirm** comes before a hand-over's sum: "Say bye bye to Fox and Rabbit?", with "No, not now" lit first.
- **What the numbers are for** (a model, not a promise; the numbers are for tuning after play): a kid battles near home with the starter in front, about one battle a minute and a half, catches something in about 2 battles of 5, and hands most catches over. By the engine's own battles, encounter tables and catch odds, with a 7-year-old's pace for each puzzle and beat, that is **0.6 tokens a minute** if every catch goes home and 0.46 if 3 in 4 do, so the axe is in reach after **13–17 minutes**, the pickaxe after **21–28** and the boat after **34–46**: the three prices keep the 3 : 5 : 8 of the 15, 25 and 40 minutes aimed at. The paraglider, the next step of the same ladder (34), comes after **57–74**: the dearest tool, which the human asked for ("a relatively large number of tokens"), and the harness costs the same (the human: "price it like the paraglider"). A kid who farms squirrels and rabbits at the reed by the start, throwing the leash at every one, gets about 1.2–1.4 tokens a minute: the axe in 6–7 minutes, the boat in about 15–17 and the paraglider in 24–28, twice as fast, not ten times. A fox in front, played the same ordinary way, earns about 1.5 a minute (the paraglider in about 22 minutes), a little more than that farm (near home a fox meets a small animal about 1 battle in 3), and farmed the same way about 3.3; a bear far out about 4.7. So a stronger team brings the later tools sooner, and farming the smallest animals is never the fastest way.
- **Losing is not a way to tokens**: nothing about a lost battle gives or takes any.

### Puzzles

- Kinds today: addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, missing operand ("7 + ? = 12"), next number in a sequence, square root, and the Arctic's seven (below). Every answer is a whole number (below 0 on a thermometer), or a clock's time of day.
- Difficulty is a 1–10 scalar. Each kind declares the range it supports; each attack declares the kinds it can ask; the engine picks a kind that fits.
- **Every operand comes from a band with a floor and a ceiling** (in the sums' kinds; the Arctic's climb their own ladders, below), so a harder attack never asks an easier question: a difficulty-3 attack cannot ask "4 + 1". Both ends of every band climb with difficulty (neither ever falls). For every kind but sequences a puzzle always shows a bigger number than any puzzle of the same kind two difficulties down; a sequence is never one that two difficulties down could ask.
  - Addition, subtraction and the missing addend draw both numbers from one band per difficulty: 1–5 · 6–10 · 11–20 · 21–50 · 51–100 · 101–200 · 201–500 · 501–1000 · 1001–5000 · 5001–10000. Subtraction is the same fact family read backwards (`(x + y) − x`), so nothing is ever "− 0" or "− itself".
  - Multiplication, division and the missing factor are a **big** factor times a **small** one (the times table). Big: 2–5 · 6–9 · 6–9 · 11–20 · 21–50 · 21–50 · 51–100 · 51–100 · 101–500 for difficulties 2–10; small: 2–5 · 2–5 · 6–9 · 6–9 · 6–9 · 11–20 · 11–20 · 21–50 · 21–50. So difficulty 2 is the small tables, 4 is the hard corner of the table (6–9 by 6–9), 5 is teens by a digit, 7 is two-digit by two-digit. Ten is never a factor: "10 × 7" is a freebie. Division is the family backwards (`(big × small) ÷ small`), from difficulty 3; the missing factor hides the big one. From difficulty 4, half of the missing-number puzzles are a missing factor ("4 × ? = 20") instead of a missing addend.
  - Square roots ask for a root in 2–5 · 4–8 · 6–10 · 9–12 · 11–15 · 13–20 · 16–30 · 21–50 for difficulties 3–10.
  - Counting sequences step by 1–2 at difficulty 1, 2–5 at 2, from `d` up to `2d + 2` at 3–9, and 10–25 at 10; only difficulty 1 counts by ones. They start from 0–5 at difficulty 1, 6–10 at 2, and so on in fives up to 46–50 at 10, so two difficulties never ask the same counting sequence.
  - The other sequence patterns join as difficulty climbs — doubling at 3 (tripling too from 6), squares at 5, triangle numbers and add-the-last-two at 7 — and each starts further along the higher it goes: a pattern's first term at difficulty `d` is always bigger than any first term it had at `d − 2`. So a textbook opening ("1, 2, 4, 8", "1, 4, 9, 16", "1, 3, 6, 10") is asked only within a difficulty of the one that introduces it, and at difficulty 10 doubling or tripling starts from 15–25, squares from 8²–12², triangle numbers from the 6th–10th and add-the-last-two from a first term of 6–10.
  - Every sequence has one right answer. It fits exactly one pattern, and it never ends in three evenly spaced numbers unless it counts all the way: "2, 3, 5, 8, ?" is never asked (adding the last two says 13, "the gaps grow by one" says 12), nor is "4, 4, 8, 12, ?" (20, but a kid counting on from the end says 16).
- **The Arctic's kinds** (#191, from the Danish Fælles Mål for grades 2–5): thermometers, kroner, fractions of an amount, floors and fences (area and perimeter), bar charts, the balance and the clock. Nothing asks them yet: the Arctic's animals will. Each climbs its own ladder of skills, 1 to 10, and within a skill no band ever narrows (`puzzle-kinds.test.ts`). All but the balance come with a picture (§5).
  - **Thermometer**: "It is 3°. It gets 5° colder. What does the thermometer show now?" (−2), or how many degrees warmer (colder) it got from one temperature to another. Difficulty 1 stays at 0° and above (changes of 2–5, within 10°); from 2 every puzzle crosses 0, one temperature below it and one above, neither on it. Within 10° by 2–6 at 2, 15° by 5–12 at 3; 4 asks only the degrees between two temperatures (5–15 apart); then both kinds, within 20° (changes 8–20), 25° (10–25), 30° (12–30), 35° (15–35), 40° (18–40) and 40° (25–60) at 5–10. The answer can be below 0: the kid types a minus.
  - **Kroner**: Danish money, coins of 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 kroner and notes of 50, 100 and 200, never øre. 1–4 count what is in the picture: 2–4 coins of 1–10 kr making 5–20 kr, 3–5 coins of up to 20 kr making 15–50, 4–6 pieces up to a 50 note making 40–100, 4–7 pieces up to a 200 note making 100–500. 5–7 pay for one thing and ask the change: with one coin or note (20, 50 or 100; the thing 11–95 kr), with one or two notes (100–300 kr; 35–290), with two to four pieces (150–500 kr; 60–480). 8–10 pay for two things: one or two notes of 100 or 200 (15–190 kr each), two to four notes (40–280), three to five pieces of 20 kr and up (75–450). The things always cost less than the money, and by less than its smallest coin or note, so every piece in the picture is needed, as when a grown-up pays.
  - **Fraction of an amount**: "What is ⅗ of 35?", the answer always whole. One piece first: ½ of 4–10 at 1, ½ or ¼ (one piece of 3–6) at 2, thirds to fifths (3–8 a piece) at 3, thirds to tenths (4–10) at 4. Then several pieces, in lowest terms (never ²⁄₄): of thirds to fifths (2–6 a piece) at 5, to eighths (3–9) at 6, quarters to tenths (4–10) at 7, fifths to twelfths (6–12) at 8, sixths to twelfths (8–15) at 9, eighths to twelfths (11–25) at 10. A whole is cut into 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12 pieces, never sevenths, ninths or elevenths.
  - **Floors and fences**: area is always the **floor** (how many floor tiles) and perimeter always the **fence** (how many fence pieces go all the way around), in words and in the picture, so a kid never mixes them up. The floor of a rectangle drawn in squares, 2–4 a side, at 1; the fence joins at 2 (2–5) and both go on at 3 (3–7); 4 writes the sides' lengths on instead (4–9); 5 is an L, a rectangle with a corner cut away (a quarter of each side or more, never a sliver), in squares (4–9); from 6 nothing is drawn in squares, and half the puzzles are a side missing ("The floor has 24 tiles. How many tiles long is the side with the ?"), the sides 5–12 at 6 and 7 (L-shapes from 7), 8–15, 10–20 and 12–25 at 8–10. The fence of an L is as long as its rectangle's.
  - **Bar chart**: "How many fish did each one catch?" Three to five bars, told apart by a coloured shape under each (a circle, a triangle, a square, a star, a heart; each its own colour) and the same shape in the question, never by a word. Read one bar (1); how many more one caught than another (2 on); two together (3 on); all of them (7 and 10). The lines across count in ones up to 6, 9 and 10 at 1–3, in twos up to 20 at 4 and 5, fives up to 50 at 6 and 7, tens up to 100 at 8–10; from 5 (in twos) and 9 (in tens) a bar may end halfway between two lines. Two bars compared are never as tall.
  - **Balance**: "8 + □ = 5 + 6", words-free: both sides weigh the same, and the box is the number that makes them. `a + □ = b + c` with numbers 1–9, 5–20 at 1–2; `a − □ = b − c` joins at 3 (10–30), 20–60 at 4; at 5 numbers 50–100 and `a × □ = b × c` (2–5 times 2–9); `a × □ + b = c` at 6 (2–5 times 2–9, plus 1–20); `a × □ − b = c` joins at 7 (2–9 times 2–9, 5–30); 3–9 times 6–15 (10–50) at 8; 6–12 times 11–20 (20–80) at 9, `a × □ = b × c` back; 11–20 times 11–25 (30–100) at 10. The box is never the number beside it on the other side.
  - **Clock**: an analogue clock face. Reading it: whole hours at 1, half past at 2, quarter past and to at 3 (the new mark at least half the time at 2 and 3), five minutes at 4, any minute at 5. Then time passing ("What time will it be in 1 hour and 20 minutes?"): a quarter, half or three quarters of an hour at 6, 20–55 minutes at 7, always across the hour (from 4:45, 25 minutes later), 1 hour 5 minutes to 2 hours 55 at 8 (each in fives), and from 9 back in time too ("What time was it 2 hours and 10 minutes ago?"), 1 hour 5 to 3 hours 55 in fives at 9, any minute to 4 hours 59 at 10. The answer is a time, typed as hours, a colon and two digits of minutes ("3:15"; "3.15" and "315" also count). A clock face cannot say morning or afternoon, so both are right: 3:15 and 15:15, 12:20 and 0:20.
- Input is a number, or a time on a clock. Answers are judged by the engine, never by the UI.
- **Puzzles solved.** The kid keeps a running count of the puzzles they have solved. Every right answer adds one, wherever it was asked: an attack in a battle, a heal at the witch doctor's, a token sum (one per hand-over, however many animals go, and one per purchase), an attack in a friendly match (the kid's own answers, never the other player's). A wrong answer adds nothing, and the count never goes down. Easy or hard, a puzzle counts one. It is saved with the game and goes along to every world, like the tokens; a new game starts at 0. Nothing is won with it: no milestones, rewards or badges.
- The catalog is designed to grow: the kinds to come are in §6.

### Lands

The human set lands' scope in #191 (2026-10-08), the spec every step of The Arctic builds from.

- **A land is a content pack laid over every world number**: its own map and tiles, animals, shop, currency, puzzles and the trainer's look. Today's land is **Nordland**; the second is **The Arctic** (Danish **Arktis**); more will follow. Every world number has every land: friends in world 42 can both fly to Arktis 42, and Arktis 42 is a world of its own, nothing like Nordland 42 but its number. A game starts in Nordland, and every game saved before lands is in Nordland.
- **What belongs to a land, and what to the kid.** Each land keeps its own party, its own money (Nordland's tokens; The Arctic's **ice dollars**, "iskroner"), its own items, and its own worlds as the kid left them: where they stood, which way they faced, what they cleared. The kid's name, the animal book, the puzzles solved, the step and visit counts and the home world are the kid's in every land. Animals and things stay in the land a kid leaves, and are waiting there when they come back.
- **Unlocking.** A land unlocks once the kid has **set free at least one of every species of the land before it** at a witch doctor's (§4 "The animal book"): The Arctic, once every one of Nordland's 50 is set free. An unlocked land stays unlocked, even if the land before it gains a species later. Nordland is always open.
- **Flying.** At any witch doctor's, a kid flies to any unlocked land. Every trip, both ways, costs one puzzle, the **fare**: one of the destination's own kinds of puzzle (Nordland's: an add or a take-away sum in the teens; The Arctic's: its seven new kinds, at their easy end), a taste of what waits there. Wrong, and another is asked; nothing else is spent. A flight keeps the world number, and is not a step: nothing is met on the way.
- **Where a flight comes down.** Every land keeps the same lattice of tent spots (x mod 23 = 5, y mod 19 = 7 on the engine's grid). A flight from a tent comes down at the tent on the same spot in the land reached, when it has one there that a kid can stand beside on foot, else at that land's nearest tent (in a straight line, then further up, then further left), beside it and facing it, in front of its door when that side is ground. The Arctic will have a tent on every spot (#191 step 4), so Nordland → The Arctic → Nordland comes home to the tent it left.
- **First arrival** in a land, with no animal of that land yet: the kid picks one of its three starters, as on the title, before going anywhere; meanwhile the witch doctor they came down at still talks, and can fly them back.
- **Lands and world numbers are two ways of going.** In The Arctic, going to another world number goes to another world of The Arctic, with The Arctic's animals and things; Nordland waits as it was left.
- **Playing together**: players see each other only in the same land of the same world (§4 "Playing together").
- **What is built** (#191 step 3): all of the above in the rules. The Arctic is registered but closed: it has no map, animals or shop of its own until steps 4 to 6, so no kid can fly there, and the witch doctor shows no travel option until step 7 (the plane, the travel option, the starter pick on arrival). A grown-up can try a flight in a throwaway game (`?lands`, [[CHEATSHEET]] § Hidden behaviour), where The Arctic is Nordland's kind of ground with nothing on it.

### Party

- A party holds every animal the player catches: there is no limit.
- **The party is in bundles, one per kind.** Every animal of a species stands with the others of its kind, and the player chooses the order of the bundles and of the animals within each. The bundles top to bottom are the battle order. A caught animal joins the end of its kind's bundle; the first of a new kind starts a bundle at the end.
- **The lead is the first animal in party order that is not tired and can fight where the player stands**: on land, the first one standing in the first bundle that has one; out on the water, the first one standing that swims; up in the air with the glider, the first bird standing (the **lead in the air**: the one a bird that notices the glider sizes up, the one that fights it, and the one that flies behind the kid; with none, nothing up there notices them and nobody follows them up). It is the one that steps into the next battle there and the one wild animals size up before they come out (§4 "Wild encounters"). With none standing that swims, out on the water there is no lead: nothing challenges the player there, and the lead on land rides in the boat. Likewise on land with nobody standing but sea animals, or nobody at all (after a lost battle, or a battle at sea that a sea animal ended standing, won, run from or caught, while the land's animals were tired): nothing comes out of the grass and nobody follows, the team needs the witch doctor (§4 "Knock-out and healing"), until a witch doctor makes one of them fit. Once a battle has started, the player decides who is in front: a switch, or picking who steps in after a knock-out (§4 "Battle"); neither changes the party's order. There is no separate "selected" animal: choosing a lead moves its bundle to the front and it to the front of its bundle.
- While exploring — not in a battle, not at the witch doctor's — the player can choose any animal that is not tired and can fight where they stand as the lead (out on the water, one that swims: "Squirrel can't swim!" otherwise; on land, not a sea animal: "Crab lives in the sea!"); choose a bundle to go first (its first animal standing leads; not when all of them are tired, nor out on the water when they can't swim, nor on land when they live in the sea); move a bundle up or down, a bundle of tired animals too (the first animal standing behind it leads); move an animal up or down within its bundle (a tired one too: a tired animal at the front is simply skipped); and name any animal.
- **Nicknames**: at most 12 characters, from letters (any alphabet), digits, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and dots. A letter keeps up to four accent marks that belong on it: the usual accents on a Latin, Greek or Cyrillic letter, or its own script's marks for the scripts the engine lists (the Indian scripts, Arabic, Hebrew, Thai, Burmese, Khmer and a dozen more); a script not listed keeps its letters and loses its marks. An accent that no single letter carries counts as a character. Everything else a kid types — emoji, symbols, marks that decorate a letter (an underline, a strike-through, a circle around it) — is left out, runs of spaces become one, and spaces at either end are trimmed. A name with no letter or digit left is no name, and the animal goes by its species' name again.

### The animal book

The human's words: "let's also introduce a thing where kids can see which animals exist (but if unseen thats a question mark) and which they have caught (whether they still have them or not)."

- **Every kind there is, in one book.** The book has a card for every species in the game, by size (tier, small to big) and within a size in the catalog's order: the starters first, the orca last. A species added to the game is in the book at once.
- **Three kinds of card.** A kind never met is a "?" and nothing else: no name, no picture, nothing that gives it away. A kind **seen**, met but not caught, shows its figure, a little faded, and its name. A kind **caught** shows its figure on a green disc (a blue one for a sea animal), its name and a green tick. Caught is for good: an animal set free at the witch doctor's stays caught.
- **Set free, the third stamp.** A kind the kid has set free at the witch doctor's (#191) gets a rose heart on its card's other corner, for good, whether one of its kind is still on the team or not. Setting one animal of a kind free is enough. This count is the way to the next land: setting free one of every Nordland animal unlocks The Arctic (§4 "Lands"); nothing on screen says so yet (#191 step 7).
- **What counts.** An animal is seen the moment a wild battle against it starts, however the battle ends (run from, won, lost). It is caught when a leash throw catches it. A starter counts as caught, and so does every animal in the party. A friendly match adds nothing: it changes nothing but the puzzles solved (§4 "Friendly matches"). Watching someone else's battle never counts either: the book is the kid's own battles.
- **The count**: "9 caught · 14 seen · 50 in all": kinds caught, kinds seen (the caught ones among them), and every kind there is. Beside it, after a rose heart, the kinds set free: "3 / 50 set free".
- **It is the player's**, as the party is: the same in every world, saved with the game, and a new game starts a new book with the starter alone. A game saved before the book gets one back from what its save shows: every kind in its party caught, and the animal of a battle in progress seen. Kinds set free, or met and run from, before then left no trace, and go in the book when they are met again. A game saved before the book kept the kinds set free counts, the human's rule to be generous, every kind in its book that is not on its team as set free: caught and helped home, or only met and run from.

### Starting out

- The game opens on a **title screen**: Continue (when there is a saved game), New game, I have an account (§4 "Saving"), and the Language and Sound settings. A throwaway game (below) skips it.
- **Your name first.** New game asks the player's name before anything else ("What's your name?"): 2 to 16 letters or numbers, in words with single spaces or hyphens between them, in any alphabet, æ, ø and å included, and nothing rude (a short list of English and Danish words, never a real name that merely holds one: Scunthorpe, Hassan and Pikachu are fine). A name that won't do stays in the box, with why, kindly: "Type your name first.", "A name needs at least 2 letters.", "That's a long one! 16 letters at most.", "Only letters and numbers, please.", "Let's pick another name." It is the name other players will see, and an account's username (§4 "Saving"). A game saved before players had names asks once, on Continue, before it goes on.
- **New game** lets the player pick their first animal from the **starters**: the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog, tier-1 animals that fight on land, where a game starts. The other small animals are caught, not chosen: a first minute choosing among twelve small figures would be a worse first minute, and the shrew, at 18 HP, a harder start (#89). The picked animal can be given a name, or none; the name is cleaned like any nickname (§4 "Party"). The game starts in its home world (§4 "World"), at its spawn, facing down, with that one animal at full HP, nothing walked and no witch doctor visited.
- Every starter is the same size, so every starter meets the same animals on the same steps.
- A throwaway game (a throwaway switch in the URL, without a typo: [[ARCHITECTURE]], the `flags.ts` row, lists them) skips the title, is saved nowhere, and starts in World 1, with no name, with a squirrel, or the `?party=` party, no tokens, or the `?tokens=` tokens, and no tools, or the `?items=` tools.
- **New game while a game is saved** asks first, and the answer starts on "No, go back"; then the name box holds the saved game's name, to keep or change. Starting over puts the saved game away: the browser keeps it, never deleted, and for an account the server keeps a copy too, among the account's latest set-aside games (§4 "Saving"). The game has no way to go back to it: that is a job for the human ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database, § Accounts).

### Saving

- **Always saved, never a save button.** The game is saved in this browser after every change (a step or a bump, a battle turn, a catch, a heal, animals set free, tokens given or spent, a tree chopped or a rock broken, a change to the team), and when the page is hidden or closed. A reload, or coming back another day on the same browser and the same address, carries on exactly where you were: the same place, facing the same way, the same animals with the same HP.
- **A reload is not an escape.** A battle in progress is saved too: a reload picks it up with the same wild animal, the same HP, and the same puzzle if one was up. The step count is saved, so the animals ahead on a walk stay the ones they were; reloading never rerolls an encounter, a battle's puzzle or a throw. A visit to the witch doctor is not saved: a reload ends it, what it healed stays healed, and a hand-over or a purchase whose sum was answered stays done; one waiting for its sum never happened.
- **A guest's game is this browser's.** Nothing else keeps it: another browser, another device, or this one with its site data cleared starts a new game. An account keeps its game on the server too (below). A browser that won't store anything says so ("This browser can't keep your game, so it starts new every time.").
- **Two windows.** Both show the same game. A window that falls behind a catch, a battle or anything else made in the other one takes no more play. When you come to it, it loads the newer game and says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!". While it is on screen but you play in the other one, a card says "You kept playing in another window." When the other window starts a new game, this one goes back to the title as soon as you look at it, with Continue on the new game. Nothing is ever rolled back, and nothing played in a window that is behind is thrown away, because none is taken. Walking around in both is fine: the window you play in carries on from where you are.
- **A save that won't load** leaves the title with New game only (unless it is an account's and the server's copy of the game can be read: then Continue picks that up), and the new game starts with a message ("Your saved game didn't load, so here is a new one."). The old save is kept: it stays where it was until you have picked a starter, then it is set aside, never deleted.
- **A save made by a newer version of the game** (while an update is on its way, a window still open on the old version can meet one: one with the new version's animals) is never touched by the old version, in the browser or on the server. The old window plays no further and reloads, which brings the new version and the game with it; if it may not reload by itself just then, a card says "A new version of the game is ready." with a button, "Tap to update!" ("Update!" and Enter on a keyboard).
- **After an update** that changes how games are saved, a saved game carries on exactly where it was (a game from before numbered worlds is in World 1), and the old save is kept too, as it was, in the browser while it has room for it (with none, the game is saved over it all the same, since the upgrade loses nothing) and, for an account, on the server ([[ARCHITECTURE]] § Saving).
- **Starting fresh** is New game on the title (§4 "Starting out"): the saved game is put away, not deleted. Nothing in the game deletes a save in the browser; clearing the site's data does. On the server an account keeps the games it set aside (a game put away, or replaced by another, or one that could not be read), newest first: hundreds of them, and past 2 MB of them the oldest go. `?new` in the address (like `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop`) plays a throwaway game that is saved nowhere, leaving the saved one alone.
- **An account, if you want one.** After every 1,000 steps in a guest's game, the next time you are exploring a card offers to keep your animals safe with a secret password: "Save my game" or "Not now", which it asks again after the next 1,000 steps. The pause menu offers "Make an account" and "Log in" to a guest, and "Log out" once you are logged in; the title has "I have an account → Log in". Your name is your username, and nothing else is asked: no email. A name someone has already is picked again, and your character takes the new one. The password has at least 4 letters or numbers, and "Show" shows it as you type.
- **Making an account takes your game along**: from then on it is saved in the browser first, then on the server, and it is the one you get when you log in on another tablet or computer ("Welcome back, Nini!"). The game you play as a guest and your account's game never mix: logging in leaves the guest game where it is, and logging out goes back to the title, which says the game is safe ("Your animals are safe!", "You're logged out. Your game waits in your account.") and offers to log in to it again first, by the account's name, with the guest game under it if there is one. The account's game waits in the browser for the next login too.
- **Played on somewhere else?** When another device got further in your account's game, this one takes the newer game and says so ("You played on somewhere else. Here's your newest game!"); the game it had is kept aside, never deleted. A game saved by a newer version of Animath is never written over: the game asks to update ("A new version of the game is ready.").
- **An account needs no server to play.** The game waits for the server only a moment as it starts (2.5 s at most). With the server out of reach an account's game plays and saves in the browser, and goes to the server again when it can, without a word. A new account is offered only while the server can keep one: when it cannot (it is out of reach, or its database is not ready for accounts), there is no save card and no row to make an account or log in, and they come back once it can. When the server says you are logged out (a new password from a grown-up, or a year away), the game says so once and saves only in the browser until you log in again. A forgotten password is set again by a grown-up: there is no email to send one.
- **Bringing a game to a new home.** A game played before the game had its own address comes along in an account a grown-up makes for it, with a welcome link. Opening the link says "Welcome back, Aslak! Your animals are waiting. Pick a secret password." ("Show" shows it as you type), and "Let's play!" carries on exactly where the game was: the same world and place, the same animals, tokens and tools. From then on it is an ordinary account: log in with the name and that password anywhere. A game this browser played as a guest stays where it is. The link works once, for 14 days: opened again, it says "This link has been used already" and offers to log in, or to play as a guest; too old, it asks for a new one from a grown-up.
- **Quit to title** (the pause menu's Start screen row) saves the game as it stands and goes back to the title, where Continue picks it up exactly there.
- **In the air**: a game is never saved up in the air. Saved mid-flight (the page hidden or closed, or the save after each tile flown), it is the game as letting go right then would leave it, so a reload lands the kid where letting go would have: on the ground, or in the boat, never over water or in a tree, never back at the spawn. With a bird following the glider (or one that would notice it on the tiles on to the landing), that game is down with the bird's battle under way, the same bird with the same HP: a reload is no escape.
- **Out on the water**: a game saved in the boat picks up in the boat, where it was. A save whose player stands on water without the boat (one edited by hand: nothing in the game takes a boat away) never leaves them stuck out there: it starts again from the spawn tile.

### Playing together

- **Who you see.** The other players in your world and your land, and only them: a player in another world, or in another land of yours (Arktis 42 when you are in Nordland 42), is never seen or listed. Within 24 tiles of you (a little more than the screen shows) you see them walk, step by step, and they see you; you keep seeing each other until you are more than 28 tiles apart. The 50 nearest others in the world are on the pause menu's list, Who's here, nearest first, with about how many steps away they are (exactly, up to 20), which way, and what they are doing.
- **How they look.** Every player is a trainer like you, in a shirt and cap their name gives them, the same on every screen and never your own coral and blue, with their name over their head, the animal that goes first for them following them, and their boat and paraglider as they use them, sat on their lead's back with the harness; nobody follows them up in the air. While they are busy a sign over their name says with what: a battle with a wild animal, the witch doctor, the menu, a friendly match (none in the air, where the glider says it; the list says "flying"). How they are drawn and move: [[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode, "Playing together".
- **Their battles.** A player near you in a battle is seen in it, beside them in the world: their animal and the one it fights (swimming at sea, flying in a battle in the air), each by its kind ("Rabbit") with its HP, every hit, miss, leash, switch and tired animal as it happens, and how it ends. Over the one thinking, a bubble shows the puzzle they are working out ("7 × 8 = ?"), never its answer, and then whether they got it right. A friendly match is seen the same way, the bubble over whoever's turn it is. Someone who comes near in the middle of a battle sees it at once, puzzle and all. Your own battle screen is just as it is: only the others see your battle from outside. At most six battles are drawn at once, the nearest. How they are drawn: [[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode, "Battles seen from outside".
- **Coming and going.** A player who comes into view fades in and one who leaves it fades out; one who turns up out of nowhere near you (they went to someone, or came into the world) comes in a little poof of dust. When someone comes into your world a note at the top of the screen says so ("Ada is here!"), and when someone leaves it ("Bo went home"): not for who was there when you came, not for someone back within ten seconds (a reload, a hiccup), and never more than one note at a time, so players who come together come in one note ("Ada and 3 more are here!"). Notes show over the world only, never over a battle, the witch doctor or the menu, and one that waited too long is dropped.
- **Finding each other.** Worlds are endless and friends can be hundreds of tiles apart. Arrows at the edge of the screen point the way to the players you can't see, the four nearest ways: friends who are one way share an arrow with all their names beside it (four, or three and "+2 more"; on a phone two), and no arrow or name is ever hidden under a button or a corner's panel. The list's "Go to <name>" takes you next to them at once, in a poof, however far: to the nearest tile beside them you could stand on yourself and walk on from (never boxed in: never on an island or in a nook of trees you couldn't get off, however big, and on land when there is land as near as the water), facing them. When there is nowhere near them to stand (they are out at sea, or on an island, and you have no boat), you stay where you are and the message line says so. Going is not walking: no step is taken and nothing jumps out of the grass. Only while exploring.
- **Once.** You are in the world once, however many windows you have open: the one you play in is the one the others see, and one you left says "You're playing in another window."
- **What the others know of you.** Your name, your world, where you stand and which way you face, the animal following you, whether you have a boat and a harness, and whether you are busy (gliding included); and while you are in a battle, the players near you see how it goes: your animal in front and the one it fights, by their kinds (never the names you gave them), their HP, the puzzle you are working out and whether you got it right, never what you typed. Not your team (a friendly match shows the other player the animals you bring to it, and nothing else of it), not your tokens, not where in the world you have been. There is no chat.
- **Seen by nobody**: a game that is not under way (the title), a practice game (`?new` and the other throwaway games), a window behind another, and a game without a name.
- **Without the server** the game plays exactly as it does alone, and the others come back by themselves when it does. When the server is updated, everyone moves to the new one in a blink nobody sees: the others stay where they are on screen, and nobody is said to have gone home. A game too old for the server reloads itself once, into the new version, while you are exploring, where you were.

### Touch and mouse

- Pointer everywhere ([[UI_SPEC]] § Pointer and touch): every row, button and card a key reaches also takes a click or a tap, as the same key press, behind the same guards. In a battle a tap only highlights a row (an attack, Leash, Switch, Run, an animal on the switch list) or sets an attack's level, and Go! does it, since a pick spends the turn; on the starters a tap only lights an animal; everywhere else a tap does the row at once. The result card goes on from a tap anywhere. A tap counts only on what the finger went down on, on the screen it went down on, so a thumb lifted as a battle starts presses nothing. A slow tap counts however long the finger rests, and a double click counts once when its first click sent what it pressed elsewhere.

Rows and buttons a finger tall; hints that say "tap"; the name boxes use the tablet's keyboard, with the box moved clear of it. A key pressed on a real keyboard switches back to the keys.

Every button is still a finger tall ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode, "On a phone held sideways").
- At the witch doctor on a phone held sideways, a puzzle and its number pad take the whole card, so the sum, the answer, Back and every key are in view, a heal, setting animals free and the shop alike; the list comes back as a right answer's reward plays on it ([[UI_SPEC]] § Doctor, "On a phone held sideways").

- Pause menu (Escape in explore): the team's cards in battle order, where a card can go first or move up or down, and a card of several opens to list its animals, each of which can go first, move up or down within its card, or get a nickname typed in a name box; a greyed Go first says why (tired, going first already, or unable to go first where the trainer stands); then Worlds (the world the kid is in beside it), Who's here (how many others are in the world), the Language and Sound settings, Make an account and Log in for a guest (while the server can keep an account) or Log out for an account, "Keep playing" and "Start screen" (Quit to title); and at the top, beside "Paused", the Animal book (with how many kinds are caught, "9/50"). Walking waits while it is open. Who's here lists the 50 nearest others in your world, how far and which way, and what they are doing; "Go to <name>" takes you next to them in a poof.
- The animal book (the pause menu's Animal book, [[UI_SPEC]] § Pause menu; §4 "The animal book"): a card for every kind of animal in the game, in a grid that scrolls — a "?" for one never met, its figure (faded) and name for one met, its figure on a green disc (blue at sea) with a green tick for one caught, for good, set free or not, and a rose heart on the other corner for a kind set free at the witch doctor's — "9 caught · 14 seen · 50 in all" and "3 / 50 set free" beside the title, and under the grid what the lit card says: "Who could this be? Go exploring and find out!", "You met a Brown rat. Catch one with your leash!", "Fox is on your team!", "Fox is on your team, and it counts as set free!", "You caught a Rabbit and set it free. It stays in your book!", "You met a Wolf, and it counts as set free!". Each figure is the game's own, drawn once as the book opens and popping in. Enter or a tap on a lit card that was met makes its animal hop. Arrows, a mouse and a finger work it, in both languages, on a tablet and on a phone held sideways.
- Worlds (the pause menu's Worlds screen, [[UI_SPEC]] § Pause menu): says which world the kid is in and which is their home, types a world's number (1 to 9999) on a big number pad or the keyboard and goes there, or goes home. Go waits, greyed, with a line saying why, until the number is a world to go to. The trip is short and joyful: the world closes into a circle of sky round the trainer and the new one opens out of it with a whoosh and a chime, and the world's number pops up big ("World 42!") with "A new world to explore!", "Back where you left off." or "Home again!". Calmer with reduced motion.
- Message line: the latest message for a few seconds, the controls hint for the first few steps, and what Enter does in front of the player: "Press Enter to talk to the witch doctor" while facing a tent, "Press Enter to chop the tree" or "… break the rock" while facing one with its tool ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode).
- The witch doctor at every tent ([[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode): in a purple robe and a tall floppy hat with a gold band and a feather, his staff in his hand and his pot bubbling on a campfire beside him. Never still: he breathes, sways, looks about and taps his staff, and when the kid comes near he hops and waves, turned their way. Gentler with reduced motion.
- The witch doctor's tents: facing a tent, Enter opens the witch doctor's card ([[UI_SPEC]] § Doctor), with three tabs, the kid's tokens beside his line, and a list that scrolls, grouped by species, for a team of any size. **Heal**: pick a hurt or tired animal, solve its puzzle and it and every other hurt animal of its kind are back to full HP; a wrong answer just brings another puzzle; the list stays live during a puzzle. **Set free**: pick animals to set free, to go home to the wild, one at a time or a whole kind at once from its own row ("Fox ×40"), each showing its HP bar ("tired" at 0 HP), never the last one that isn't tired (its check box shows it before a pick); the running total of the tokens they bring shows before the sum; "Set them free" asks "Say bye bye to …?" with "No, not now" first, then the sum of the tokens the kid will have; the right answer waves them goodbye ("Bye bye, Fox! It feels much better now.") and counts the tokens in. **Shop**: the tools on sale (the axe, the pickaxe, the boat, the paraglider and the harness) with their pictures, prices and what they do; buying asks the sum of the tokens left, and the right answer hands the tool over. While no tool is on sale, the shop says new things are coming soon. Escape or Back goes back from a puzzle, and Bye leaves from the list or a puzzle. The witch doctor speaks English and Danish, like the rest of the game, and tells why wild animals are grumpy (they are a little bit sick) and what setting them free does: he makes them all better and they go home to the wild.

### Encounters and battle

- Wild encounters: each step onto tall grass may start a battle, with the species picked by biome, the ground around the tall grass, distance from spawn and tier compared with the party's lead, on a bell around the lead's size (§4 "Wild encounters"). Every lead meets animals on the same steps, in every biome: mostly its own size, the sizes beside it now and then, and every size living there is possible; near home the bigger ones stay rare, and where nothing its size lives, the biggest there come out (a bear in the meadow meets red deer).
- Sea animals: fourteen of them (crab, starfish, moon jellyfish and plaice; turtle, lion's mane and lobster; dolphin, harbour seal and porpoise; octopus and grey seal; the humpback whale and the orca: tiers 1 to 5) live out on the deep water, where each step in the boat may start a battle with one, by the same rules, as long as an animal that swims is standing to go first (§4 "Wild encounters", "At sea"). Caught, they join the team and fight only at sea; on land they "live in the sea", can't be chosen to go first there, and the witch doctor keeps an animal that walks with the kid when others are set free.
- Battles in the air (#91; §4 "Battle"; [[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode): after a bird followed the glider down, a battle fought in the sky, the two birds flying over the clouds, their wings beating, only birds stepping in ("can't fly" on the others); a tired bird sinks onto a cloud to rest; the wild bird flies home, or stays up in the sky. Seen from outside, the two birds fly beside the fighter.
- Animals live by their ground: frogs, otters, brown rats, toads, herons, raccoons, beavers, mute swans, sea eagles and moose by the water; squirrels, foxes, red deer, wood mice, robins, stag beetles, roe deer, badgers, pine martens, tawny owls, buzzards, wild boars and lynxes by the trees; wolves, bears, lizards, stoats, adders, eagle-owls, wolverines and golden eagles by the rocks; rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs, moles and bison in open grass. With plenty of its ground around, an animal comes out four times as often as one of its size with none; near home the ground only picks among animals of the same size, so the start is as gentle as ever, and further out it sorts the sizes too. The first reed by World 1's start, with the lake all round it, is mostly frogs, brown rats and toads.
- Frogs live in the river reeds, in World 1 the first of them one step from the start: a tier-1 animal and one of the three starters, green and squat with big eyes, whose attacks ask number patterns and times tables. The small animals that live elsewhere still come down to the water near home, so the river's bigger animals stay rare there (1 in 37 together at that first reed).
- Eighteen small animals of the Nordic countryside (#89, the first wave): the shrew, wood mouse, brown rat, hedgehog, mole, common lizard, common toad, robin and stag beetle (tier 1), and the roe deer, badger, pine marten, stoat, adder, grey heron, tawny owl, raccoon and beaver (tier 2), each where it lives in nature (§4 "Animals"). Near home, with the starter in front, a zoo of small animals comes out of the grass (World 1, every tall grass within 32 tiles): shrews and hedgehogs 10.5% each, squirrels, wood mice and robins 9.6%, rabbits and moles 8.3%, stag beetles 7.2%, brown rats 5.8%, toads 5.3%, lizards 3.8%, frogs 3.3%, and the tier-2 animals 8.2% together. The toad and the beaver swim, and fight from the boat. The deer is now the red deer.
- Nine big animals of the Nordic countryside (#89, the second wave): the wild boar, mute swan and eagle-owl (tier 3), the lynx, wolverine, golden eagle and sea eagle (tier 4), and the moose and European bison (tier 5), each where it lives in nature (§4 "Animals"): the wild boar and the bison in the forest, the moose there and by the river, the mute swan and the sea eagle by the river, the wolverine and the golden eagle in the mountains, and the eagle-owl and the lynx in the forest and the mountains. The river, the forest and the mountains now hold every size of animal, so a big animal in front meets mostly its own size there: a wolf or a bear meets the moose and the sea eagle at the river. Near home the starter hardly ever meets one. The swan swims, and fights from the boat.
- Eight sea animals of the Nordic countryside (#89, the third wave): the moon jellyfish and the plaice (tier 1), the lion's mane and the lobster (tier 2), the harbour seal and the porpoise (tier 3), the grey seal (tier 4) and the orca (tier 5), out on the deep water with the six that were there, each the twin in numbers of a land animal of its size. From the boat near home with a frog in front, it is mostly the four small ones (§4 "Wild encounters"). The whale is now the humpback whale.
- Battle mode, Game Boy style ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode): the two animals face each other on a patch of the biome (out on the sea, the deep water, both animals swimming low in it, and the wild animal swims home or stays in the water at the end, not in the grass), with status boxes, a narration line, an action menu and the puzzle panel. The menu has two kinds of moves that look different: the attacks as chunky warm tiles, each with its own level (easy, medium or hard) and a hit badge with the damage at that level, and under them a row of round picture buttons for Leash (a ring and a word for how likely a catch is), Switch and Run. Until something is picked, the right card previews the highlighted move: an attack's kinds of sums as operator chips (+ − × ÷ √ ?, and 2 4 6 for number patterns), its three levels side by side with what each hits for, "That would tire it out!" when that hit would knock the wild animal out, and the sentence for readers; the wild animal's HP bar shows the part the hit would take off. "Correct!" shows the hit's badge, a hit lands with a comic burst beside the status box, bigger for a harder level, and a glow on a status box shows whose turn it is. Every attack is a puzzle answered by typing a number. The wild animal's reply and knock-outs are played out one line at a time. Mashing through them never picks: every choice that spends something or can't be taken back waits for a quiet moment before a press counts ([[UI_SPEC]] § Battle mode, "A quiet moment" lists them), and Go! lights up when it would.
- Switching animals mid-battle (§4 "Battle"): Switch opens the party list, with tired animals greyed, the one in front marked, and those that can't fight where the battle is greyed and tagged ("can't swim" out on the water, "lives in the sea" on land, "can't fly" in the air); several of a kind stand under a heading with their name and how many ("Rabbit ×12"), a long list scrolls with its cursor, and left and right jump from one kind to the next; the switch takes the turn. After a knock-out the same list asks who goes next, and that pick is free. With a party of one the Switch row is greyed and says to catch a second animal.
- Catching with the leash; a caught animal always joins the party, with the HP it had, behind the others of its kind: there is no limit to the team.
- HP lost in a battle stays lost afterwards; a knocked-out animal stays tired and sits out battles until healed.
- Losing: when every animal that can fight there is tired (out on the water, every one that swims; up in the air, every bird), the result card says "Good try!" and "Your animals need a rest. The witch doctor will make them feel all better!", and the player is back in the world where they stood, nobody healed. While no animal can fight there nothing jumps out of the grass, nobody walks behind the trainer, the line under the message says "Your animals are tired. Walk to the witch doctor's tent!", and an arrow at the edge of the screen (a little tent in a cream disc, its coral tip pointing the way) leads to the nearest tent the kid can get to until it is in plain sight; a witch doctor who heals one of them ends it all. With no tent within 200 steps, and no paraglider to fly out on, a witch doctor comes instead and looks after everyone there (§4 "Knock-out and healing").

### Friendly matches

- The Challenge button (§4 "Friendly matches"; [[UI_SPEC]] § Friendly matches): standing within two steps of another player, "Challenge Bo" shows above the message line (C, a click or a tap), greyed with why when it can't be pressed now (they are busy or out on the water, you are on the water, you have no animal that can fight on land, they said no a moment ago). Asking shows "Asking Bo…" with the time left and Never mind; being asked shows "Ada wants a friendly match!" with Yes and No and the time left, while you walk on. However an ask ends without a match, the message line says why ("Bo said no this time.", "Bo walked away.").
- The match is played on the battle's screen: the other player's animal as "Bo's Rabbit" with a paw, both names, the coin ("Bo starts!"), your attacks, levels and puzzles as in a wild battle, and Switch and Leave under them. On their turn their puzzle shows without its answer, with "Bo is thinking…", and Leave the match stays at hand. A switch, a knock-out and the pick of who goes next play out as in a battle. Escape, on either turn, asks "Leave the match?" with Stay lit and Leave beside it.
- The end, warm for both, with Rematch? and Back to exploring; "Still there?" after a long wait on your turn; a note when the other drops out ("Bo dropped out. Waiting for them… 25"); and, when the game is updated mid-match, "The game is updating. Let's play again in a moment!" with Play again, lit once the friend is back ("The game restarted. Want to play again?" when it restarted without warning). Whoever leaves, or goes back to exploring from the end, can challenge and be challenged again at once.
- Your right answers in a match count toward your puzzles solved; nothing else of your game changes. Others see you "in a friendly match" (the star over your name), and the players near you see the match itself: your two animals facing each other in front of you, the thought bubble over whoever's turn it is, every hit and the end.

### Saving

- The game saves itself in the browser after every change (the tokens and tools, the puzzles solved, and every tree chopped or rock broken, too), and when the page is hidden or closed; a reload or a later visit carries on exactly where you were, in the middle of a battle or a puzzle included (§4 "Saving"). "Welcome back!" on the message line when the first Continue after opening the page picks the saved game up.
- A guest's game is kept by the browser alone; an account's by the server too (below).
- Two windows of the game never undo each other: the one that falls behind takes no play, says so on a card while you play in the other, and loads the newer game ("Here's your newest game!") when you come to it.
- A save that cannot be read starts a new game with a message and is kept, set aside once the new game has been played. A newer version's save is never touched: a window on the old version plays no further and reloads for the new one ("A new version of the game is ready."). A throwaway switch in the URL (§4 "Starting out") plays a game that is saved nowhere.
- A game saved by an older version carries on after an update exactly where it was (one from before numbered worlds in World 1, its home), and the old save is kept as it was, in the browser and, for an account, on the server.
- Optional accounts (§4 "Saving"): the "Keep your animals safe!" card every 1,000 steps, making an account with the game on screen, logging in and out from the pause menu and the title, the account's game on any tablet or computer, the guest's game kept apart, a newer game from another device taken with a kind word, and a game saved by a newer version never written over ("A new version of the game is ready."). A welcome link opens a game a grown-up brought over from another computer: "Welcome back, Aslak!", a password to pick, and the game where it was.

### Sound and feel

- Sound: short, soft sounds made while the game runs, no sound files ([[DESIGN]] § Sound) — a jingle when a wild animal jumps out, blips on the menus, a chime for a right answer and a soft bonk for a miss, a thump for a hit, a puff when an animal gets tired, the leash's whoosh, tick-tock and its fanfare or boing, a sparkle at the witch doctor, coins clinking when tokens change hands, a ding for a new lead and a fanfare for a win, a chop and a crack as the axe and the pickaxe land, a whoosh and a chime for a trip to another world, a whoosh as the paraglider takes off and a soft thud and two notes as it lands, and a squawk when a wild bird notices the glider. Every sound goes with something on screen.
- Sound setting: on by default, a Sound row in the pause menu and on the title, and M on any screen except while typing; remembered on this device.
- Little flourishes ([[UI_SPEC]] § Sound and juice): an iris that closes on the player and opens on the wild animal, a tired animal lying down to rest in a ring of dust with z's rising over it, sparkles along a healed animal's HP bar, the trainer's walk and the lead's bouncing behind it. A system set to reduce motion gets calmer versions.
- Big moments feel big: a caught animal cheers with a spin and stars while confetti bursts round it and two poppers rain confetti over the scene, and its result card shows its name in big letters over a burst of rays; a win gets a hop and a few stars, and stars round "You won!".

### Language

- The whole game in Danish and English, the animals' and attacks' names included. It starts in the language the browser prefers (Danish or English, else English); Language in the pause menu and on the title switches every word on screen at once and is remembered on this device (`?lang=da` / `?lang=en` in the address picks one for a visit).

### The page

- A name, and a fox for an icon in the browser's tab and on a Home Screen. A shared link shows a preview: the starters under "Animath", and a line in English and Danish. Search engines are kept away: a shared link is how the game is found.
- A browser that cannot draw the world, or runs no JavaScript, gets a kind card saying so instead of an empty page ("This browser can't draw Animath's world. Ask a grown-up for help!").
- The game plays on an iPad or iPhone from iPadOS and iOS 15, and in every other browser as new. An older one gets a kind card in English and Danish instead of an empty page: "This iPad is a bit too old for Animath. Ask a grown-up to update it, or try another device."
- On an old address of the game (the tunnel's, where it was shared before it had its own), a card comes first, every time the page opens: "Animath has moved!", and a big button, "Play at animath.xyz", that goes there. A small "Keep playing here" under it starts the game at the old address after all, for a grown-up trying something out.

### Engine

- Puzzle catalog (§4 "Puzzles"): 14 kinds across difficulty 1–10: adding, taking away, times tables, sharing, missing numbers, number patterns and square roots, and the Arctic's seven (thermometers, kroner, fractions of an amount, floors and fences, bar charts, the balance and the clock), which nothing asks yet.
- Difficulty mapping (tier, attack, level) and healing difficulty.
- Damage and catch-probability formulas.
- Species catalog: 50 species, tiers 1–5: 36 on land (12 at tier 1, 12 at tier 2, 4 at tier 3, 5 at tier 4 and 3 at tier 5; the frog, the otter, the common toad, the beaver and the mute swan swim too, and the eight birds fly: the robin, the grey heron, the tawny owl, the buzzard, the mute swan, the eagle-owl, the golden eagle and the sea eagle) and 14 in the sea (4 at tier 1, 3 at tier 2, 3 at tier 3, 2 at tier 4 and 2 at tier 5).

### Server

- Online at its own address, https://animath.xyz (www.animath.xyz leads there); an update never takes it offline. While the game does not answer (a first start, or the server down) the address shows "Back in a moment! The game is getting ready." in the kid's language, and reloads itself into the game. The kids who played before it went live are sent there from the tunnel's address (§5 "The page"), and a grown-up moves their games over (below).
- Optional accounts (§4 "Saving"): register (with the guest's game), log in and out, one account per name whatever its case ("Nini" and "NINI" are one), and the account's game on the server: an older copy never replaces a newer one (the page that sent it gets the newer one back), and a game it replaces that is a different one, unreadable or an older version's is kept aside. Too many tries ask for a little break ("Lots of tries! Take a little break and try again in a minute."), and a grown-up resets a forgotten password ("Forgot your password? Ask a grown-up to help.").
- Moving a kid's game from another server: a grown-up reads the kid's newest save from the database it was played on (the tunnel's, which kept guests' games until they moved), without writing there, makes an account for it here, with no password, and sends a one-time welcome link (14 days) through which the kid picks one and is logged in.
- Playing together through the server (§4 "Playing together"): players in one land of one world see each other, list who is there and go to each other, each of them there once however many windows they have open.
- Friendly matches judged on the server (§4 "Friendly matches"): the invite, the match, each page sent its own view with no answer in it, the turn clock, 30 seconds to come back, the rematch, and every match ended kindly when the game updates.
