# Decisions

Flat lookup of rulings, organized by domain. A ruling's reason is at most the human's words that asked for it or the incident that settled it, never an argument.

## Language & tooling

TypeScript everywhere, strict, ES2022, `verbatimModuleSyntax`.
pnpm workspace with three packages: `@mathgame/engine`, `@mathgame/client`, `@mathgame/server`. Root scripts fan out with `pnpm -r`.
Node 26, pnpm 12. Dependency build scripts need approval in `pnpm-workspace.yaml` (`allowBuilds`).
Prettier with tabs, single quotes, no trailing commas, print width 100. Markdown is not formatted.
vitest for tests, `tsc --noEmit` / `svelte-check` for type checks.
No React, ever. No SvelteKit either: the client is a plain Vite app.

## Engine

The engine is a **pure TypeScript package with zero dependencies** and no access to the DOM, Three.js, Node or the network; what enforces it is [[INVARIANTS]] § Engine purity.
All randomness comes from a seeded `Rng`, or a seed, passed in by the caller: no `Math.random` and no `Date.now` in the engine ([[INVARIANTS]] § Engine purity).
The world is a pure function of `(seed, chunkX, chunkY)`, its randomness keyed by coordinates, never by call order ([[INVARIANTS]] § Engine purity).
What a player changes in the world is an overlay the authority keeps and saves (`WorldEdits`: the tiles they cleared), laid over the seeded world, never a change to the generator.
A species lists the realms it lives in (`land`, `water`, `air`), and an encounter happens in the realm of its tile (up on the glider, in the air): only species living there come out. An amphibious species lives on land and in the water. An animal of the water is a species in the catalog living in the water realm, not a catalog of its own; a bird is a land species that also lives in the air, with the skies it flies over (`skies`, its habitats unless it names others), not a catalog of sky animals.
Instance ids (`AnimalInstance.id`) are minted by the authority, never by the engine. An engine function that creates an animal returns it without an id (`WildAnimal`).
Grid world, 16×16 chunks, four-direction movement, screen-space `y` grows downward.
Puzzle answers are whole numbers only. Prompts are plain strings until a kind needs more.
Difficulty is an integer 1..10. Every puzzle generator declares the range it supports.
Gameplay formulas (damage, catch probability, difficulty mapping) live in the engine and nowhere else; [[PRODUCT]] §4 states them in prose and the two must agree.
Game state changes only through **intents** validated by an **authority** that emits **events** (`protocol.ts`). The client UI and renderer are consumers of events, never mutators of state.

## Gameplay

The rules are [[PRODUCT]] §4; these are the choices behind them.
A battle lets the player switch animals, because the human asked for it ("need ability to change combat animal during fight"). A switch costs the turn.
Who comes out of the tall grass is sized to the lead's tier, because the human asked for it ("index challenger animals based on the tier of the selected animal"), on a bell around it, because the human asked for that too ("the probabilities should just be smoothly adjusted so it's almost a bell shape dist around the tier animal you wield, but everything is possible really"): the lead's own size is the likeliest, and every size living there, smaller or bigger, is on the table, so no grass is ever quiet. The bell is over tiers, not species: a tier's share goes by its distance from the lead's, never by how many kinds of animal it has, so adding animals changes which ones come out, not how big they are. The visitors near home count as three more bells of the lead's size, however many kinds come: three keeps the river's reeds, where a new starter meets its first animals in World 1, as they were before the bell. No visitor is commoner than an animal of its size living there, so a guest is never the main animal of a biome it doesn't live in: residents and visitors share the four bells, which keeps a tier's weight free of how many kinds it has, where capping each visitor alone would not (the orchestrator's call on #136; the human may set it otherwise); the ground round a tile weighs visitors too. Near home the bell's side above the lead is pulled in, so a new starter's first battles are nearly all its own size and the big animals keep to the wilds; from the wild radius it is symmetric. The numbers, how the visitors share their bells, and the tests: [[INVARIANTS]] § Encounters.
A kid may lead with a small animal, which keeps the encounters small, and switch a big one in on the first turn: the human chose to keep that, since a strong animal is no use to a kid who can't solve its puzzles, so the game adjusts itself.
The frog stays, a tier-1 starter living by the river: an agent added it unasked (PR #21), and the human chose to keep it.
Who comes out of the tall grass also depends on the ground around it, because the human asked for it ("idk if animals are located near their natural habitats but that would make sense. amphibious near water, wood animals near trees, animals that like clifffs and terrain near rocks, etc."). Every species favours one kind of ground: water, trees, rocks or open grass. The ground only changes which animal of the table comes out, never whether one does or which could, and near spawn only among animals of the same size, so the start stays as gentle as the biome's table makes it ([[INVARIANTS]] § Encounters).
The player chooses the lead while exploring, because the human asked for it ("the ability to change the *selected* animal during explore mode").
A party has no cap: every animal caught joins it, because the human asked for it ("let's not put a cap on how many animals you can carry. indeed, let the user carry as many as they want").
The party is shown as one card per species, the animals of a kind stacked on it, and the player orders the cards by dragging them, because the human asked for it ("let's [stack] cards for same animals in the UI. user can mouseover cards to reveal the animal and HP underneath. and simply scroll cards on overflow. user can drag animal card (bundles) to change the vertical alignment").
The party is kept in species bundles, so the cards top to bottom are the battle order: a caught animal joins the end of its kind's bundle, and choosing a lead moves its bundle to the front.
Tokens are earned in a kid's own game, at the witch doctor, for animals set free, because the human asked for it: "the doctor could take some of these animals off you for "tokens". the whole narrative is that the animals you catch (that attack you in the wild) are actally a little bit sick and the ones the doctor takes of you gets healing and released into the wild".
The witch doctor sells things for tokens, because the human asked for it: "then we introduce that the witch doctor can actually sell things. my son requested a wood axe for chopping down trees, a pickaxe for chopping down rock and a boat for allowing him to go on water (where on deep water he could encounter wild sea animals)". The seller is the same witch doctor, not a new character.
The doctor is a witch doctor, in his name and in his looks, because the human asked for it: "i feel like the witch doctor needs to look like a witch doctor. he's not really animated. in danish it's "heksedoktor". he little wears a funny hat. rename from "dyrlæge". and "help home" should be "set free" i think. "slip fri" in danish." Every line calls him the witch doctor (heksedoktoren), and the animals he takes are set free ("Set free", "Slip fri"). The code still calls him the doctor for short (the `doctor` mode, `DoctorState`, the `doctor` copy keys), and so do the docs where they name the code: the rename is the player's words, not the code's names.
An item goes on sale only once what it does is built, so a kid never pays for a tool that does nothing ([[INVARIANTS]] § Witch doctor).
The axe chops down trees and the pickaxe breaks rocks, because the human asked for it: "my son requested a wood axe for chopping down trees, a pickaxe for chopping down rock". Enter (the touch controls' Talk) facing the tree or the rock does it, as facing a tent talks to the witch doctor; the tile stays plain ground (only thousands of clears later do the farthest chunks grow back). Nothing else can be cleared ([[INVARIANTS]] § World).
The tiles a player clears are theirs, kept in their own game and save, world by world: to a friend in the same world, the trees they chopped still stand, because every single-player rule, walking included, stays in the browser (§ Multiplayer). Sharing them waits for its trigger ([[DEFERRED]] "Cleared tiles are each player's own").
Who comes out of the grass never reads what a player cleared: encounter tables, and the ground round a tall-grass tile that habitat weighs, are the seeded world's. A path chopped through a forest is for walking; it does not change which animals live there, so the odds [[PRODUCT]] §4 states hold for every kid, and no kid can farm a species by clearing its trees.
Every token that changes hands is a sum the kid works out, because the human asked for it: "obviously for buying stuff (token transaction) there's an easy math puzzle the player has to solve, forcing them to calc how many tokensthey have left after spending thetokens". The sum is the tokens the kid will have, `tokens − price` for a purchase and `tokens + reward` for a hand-over, one sum however many animals are set free, and nothing changes hands before its right answer ([[INVARIANTS]] § Witch doctor).
One healing puzzle heals every hurt animal of the picked animal's species, at that species' healing difficulty: with a team of many of a kind, a puzzle per animal would be a chore.
Losing a wild battle heals nobody and moves nobody: the kid walks to a witch doctor, and while no animal of theirs can fight where they are, no wild animal challenges them, because the human asked for it ("if you don't have any health on any animals the behavior is that you exit battle mode and then have to walk back to a doctor. you won't get challenged by animals at all"). The one exception keeps a kid from being stranded: with no tent in reach and no paraglider to fly out on, a witch doctor comes to them, never to a kid with the glider, who flies out, since one in a spot only gliding reaches would be a free heal ([[INVARIANTS]] § Witch doctor, § Encounters). The screen points the way, a line under the message and an arrow to the nearest tent, and nobody follows the trainer meanwhile: the follower shows who can fight there, and a tired animal can't.
Friendly matches are unaffected by a tired team: each side brings full-health copies of its animals, tired ones too, so a kid whose animals are tired can still play a friend, and a match heals nobody (§ Multiplayer).
The kid keeps a running count of puzzles solved, one for every right answer wherever it is asked (an attack, a heal, a token sum), because the human asked for it: "A running count of puzzles solved gives them a little collector's pride in the puzzles themselves: "I've solved 312!"". It is only a count: no milestones, rewards, badges or leaderboards ride on it. A friendly match's right answers count too, the kid's own only: they are the kid's own effort, and the count changes nothing about a match (the orchestrator's call, 2026-09-27).
The kid keeps an animal book of every kind of animal there is, because the human asked for it ("let's also introduce a thing where kids can see which animals exist (but if unseen thats a question mark) and which they have caught (whether they still have them or not)"). A kind is seen only in the kid's own wild battles, as the battle starts: never in a friendly match, which changes nothing but the puzzles solved (§ Multiplayer; whether a match's animals should count is the human's call, [[HUMAN_TODO]]), and never by watching another player's battle from outside, which is the other kid's battle, known only from their page. Caught is for good, and the book, like the party, goes with the player to every world.
The boat takes the player out on the water, and only animals that swim fight there, because the human asked for it: "a boat for allowing him to go on water (where on deep water he could encounter wild sea animals). you can't fight on water unless you have an amphibious animal (otter, frog, etc). in water you obv only catch water animals. if you have the boat, it appears as a "small upside down boat" on your back and you simply walk into the water and a boat animates (rotates, translates and scales) to sit under you, smooth like that." Every species goes on land, in the water, or both (`realms`; the birds up in the air too, below), and fights only where it can go: on land every land and amphibious animal, out on the water the amphibious ones and the sea animals, up in the air the birds. The lead is the first animal standing that can fight where the player is; with none, nothing challenges the player there, so a kid can always sail in peace.
Deep water is its own place, the sea biome, out past a border of shallows along every shore ([[INVARIANTS]] § World says which water is deep). The sea animals live there and nowhere else; the frog and the otter, who swim, still live by the river, so "in water you obv only catch water animals" means the sea's own: out at sea only sea animals come out.
Deep water is the sea's tall grass: each step onto it rolls the same 1-in-10 as a step onto tall grass, from a table built by the same rules (tiers from the lead, the distance from spawn), with no visitors. The shallows start nothing, so a river is always crossed in peace.
The sea animals are one or more of each tier from 1 to 5 (fourteen since #89's third wave, which the human approved with it), each the twin in numbers of a named land animal of its tier (HP, catch rate, attack powers), so the land's balance carries over unchanged and a sea battle is as fair as a land one of its size. Their puzzles are their own mix, and the small ones never ask a times table.
The animals are those of temperate Northern Europe and its lakes and sea (#89, which the human wrote as the spec): every one lives wild there or on both sides of the North Atlantic, named by its everyday name, the species a kid can learn, and placed where it lives in nature. Nothing arctic, tropical or from the savannah, which belong to the worlds that come later. They arrived in three waves, in order: the small land animals, then the big ones, then the sea's.
Within a tier a new animal's numbers stay inside the band #89 sets for that tier round the prototype animals' numbers (tier 1: 18–26 HP, catch 0.8–0.9, powers 3–8; tier 2: 30–36 HP, 0.6–0.7, 5–12; tier 3: 46–56 HP, 0.45–0.5, 8–13; tier 4: 66–72 HP, 0.3–0.35, 10–19), so the balance promises hold without retuning; for tier 5 the band is 105–110 HP, catch 0.2 and powers 12–27, which the bear and the whale, at 100 HP and 28, stay outside of as they are (the moose, the European bison and the orca are inside it).
Division and square roots are asked from tier 3 up, never below. #89 wrote division for the tawny owl's Talon Grab at tier 2; it asks times tables and missing numbers instead, by the general rule. The human may flip this for the owl.
A sea animal lives only in the water (realm water): it is no starter, it never goes first on land, and at the witch doctor, whose tent is on land, a kid always keeps one animal standing that can fight on land when others are set free. Out at sea every animal in a battle swims, as it does behind the boat (how it looks: [[DESIGN]] § Aesthetic direction).
Without a swimmer standing, the lead on land rides in the boat, and out on the water a swimmer follows the boat: the follower shows, in the world, who can fight there. Up in the air the lead in the air, a bird, flies behind the glider; with no bird, nobody follows a kid into the air.
The witch doctor sells a paraglider that flies the kid up to 20 tiles straight ahead, over anything, because the human asked for it ("when player has a paraglider, they can hit and hold space to jump up to 20 tiles…", and "you couldn't start a jump over water with over 20 tiles to land (unless you have a boat!)"). It costs 34 tokens, the next of the tools' Fibonacci prices. Space held a quarter of a second takes off and a tap of Space still talks, chops or breaks, so Space keeps its old job; Enter never flies; on a tablet a Fly button holds Space. The glide goes the way the kid faces, with no steering, so where they can come down never changes under them in the air. The kid comes down only where what they own lets them stand: in the boat on water, and on a tree or a rock with its tool, which clears it (the human's call on #91), never on a tent; how far a flight reaches, and where letting go lands, is [[INVARIANTS]] § World. Up in the air nothing on the ground notices the kid, not on landing either; only a bird can (below). The trip to the witch doctor after a lost battle never uses the glider: it goes on foot or by boat.
Up in the air a kid with a bird in the team can annoy a wild bird, which follows the glider down, and the battle is fought in the air as they land, because the human asked for it (#91, which the human approved as the spec: "an air battle, so only animals that fly can fight in it. This is the same idea as the boat, where only swimmers fight at sea"). Only birds fly: a stag beetle does in nature, but the game keeps a rule a kid can hold in their head. Birds are land animals that also fly: caught in the grass, they fight on land and in the air, and are met in the air over the biomes where they live, and the sea eagle over the sea too. Every tile the glider enters rolls one in twenty for a bird, only while a bird stands in the team, and at most one bird follows a flight: the sky is the birds' place, so it is denser than the grass's one in ten grass steps, and a team without a bird always flies in peace. The sky's tables are the grass's rules in the air, with visitors in every sky, so near home a small bird in front mostly meets small birds. The battle is fought in the air whatever the tile the kid lands on, tall grass or the boat, and only the birds fight it.
The witch doctor sells a harness with which a kid rides on their lead's back, because the human asked for it ("a "harness" you can buy at the doctor which lets you mount bigger animals (ones you could imagine actually would be able to carry a kid). when you have it you automatically ride ON the animals that support it when they are selected. price it like the paraglider."). It costs 34 tokens, what the paraglider costs, and there is nothing to press: the lead carries the kid whenever it can. Which animals can is a fact of the species in the engine's catalog (`carries`): the six big land animals a kid could sit on in nature (red deer, wild boar, wolf, bear, moose, European bison), an agent's call the human may change. Riding is a look only, on land only: it changes no speed, no reach and no encounter, so every rule of where a kid goes and what they meet stays the one on foot, and the boat and the glider keep carrying the kid on the water and in the air.

## Client

Three.js (WebGL) for rendering. Flat-shaded low-poly meshes, one directional light with shadows and a hemisphere fill, the world's only lights. A campfire's warm light is painted, never a light in the scene: a glow laid over the ground, the tent and what stands near, worked out from the small light over the fire it stands for (`campfire.ts`), because a light is dear on the GPU and a new number of lights stalls the page (#150, #151; [[INVARIANTS]] § Rendering, "The world's lights never change in number", has the costs). The props and figures cast the sun's shadows; the ground's boxes don't (with the sun behind the camera, a step's shadow falls out of sight).
The world is drawn at up to 2 device pixels per CSS pixel on a phone-sized screen and 1.5 on anything bigger, as a budget of 1.8 million pixels allows but never under 1.5, and never more than the screen has (`pixelRatioFor`): the GPU's time goes with the pixels, and at an older iPad's size and 2× a laptop GPU of its class took 40 ms a frame (#150).
Fixed orthographic camera: pitch 50°, yaw 35°, 14 tiles of viewport height. No zoom, no rotation, no perspective changes in explore mode. Battle mode uses its own fixed camera, and so does the title's starter stage.
Svelte 5 (runes) for the DOM overlay only — HUD, menus, the puzzle panel. Svelte reads game state from a `$state` view that is filled from authority events; components never dispatch anything: a button presses a key, and the controller of the screen that is up turns keys into intents to the authority ([[ARCHITECTURE]] § Pointer input).
Placeholder geometry (boxes, cones) is acceptable until real models arrive. Real models are glTF.
Ground tiles render as one `InstancedMesh` per chunk.
A figure (an animal, a trainer, the boat and the glider a trainer carries) draws as one mesh, not a mesh per part: its primitives merged, their colours on its vertices, one shape shared by every figure of its kind, and the parts its animations move are bones of that mesh (the boat's pennant and the glider's roll, wing and lines, which show and hide, are meshes of their own) (#152). Figures are not instanced across a crowd.
Font: Nunito (Google Fonts). Rounded, friendly.
Sound is synthesized while the game runs, with WebAudio, from cue data in `src/audio/`: no sound files, no audio library. Cues play from the screens (controllers, the HUD), where events become visuals; the authority and the engine know nothing of sound.
Mouse and touch are key presses: a click or a tap on the overlay sends the key it stands for through the keyboard's own path (`input/press.ts`), plus the pointer's own keys for a row, a level, a language, and the party column's cards, animals and dropped cards. There is no second path from a pointer to an action.
Touch controls (D-pad, Talk, Fly, Menu, number pad, finger-sized rows) show where the main pointer is coarse, then follow the latest input: a touch shows them, a real keyboard's key hides them. The same build serves laptops and tablets.
Explore shows the kid's coordinates in the bottom-right corner, because the human asked for it ("let's also have a coordinates in the bottom right corner."). They are a maths book's: `x` grows to the right and `y` grows **up** the screen, though the engine's grid grows `y` down it (the orchestrator's call), and they count from the world's **spawn**, not the grid's origin, so every world starts at 0, 0 (an agent's call, 2026-09-27). A spawn is one tile per world, the same for everyone in it, so two friends in one world read the same numbers on the same tile. The conversion is one function in one place (`coordinates.ts`: [[ARCHITECTURE]] § Coordinate system), and whatever else shows a kid's coordinates or takes them ("go to x, y", an admin tool) uses the same one, moved into the engine if the server needs it.
The dev servers keep off the ports of another project on this machine ([[ARCHITECTURE]] § Ports and processes has them).

## The page

The game is a web page, not a standalone web app: no `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`, no manifest with `display: standalone`, because iOS gives a web app on the Home Screen storage of its own, apart from Safari's, so a guest's animals, kept in Safari's `localStorage`, would seem to vanish when the kid opened the game from its icon. An icon on the Home Screen is welcome (`apple-touch-icon.png`). iOS and iPadOS 26 open every page added to the Home Screen as a web app unless "Open as Web App" is switched off as it is added, and no page can change that; only an account carries a game across (§ Accounts).
Search engines are kept out, because it is a kids' game that shows players' names, with no moderation, shared by link: `robots.txt` turns every crawler away except the ones messengers send to draw a shared link's preview, and the page says `noindex`. One switch lets them in: `SEARCH_ENGINES_WELCOME` in `packages/client/vite.config.ts`, which writes both.
The world draws to the screen's edges, under a notch or a home indicator (`viewport-fit=cover`), and the overlay keeps inside the safe area ([[UI_SPEC]] § Frame), rather than the page being boxed in away from them.
The game plays in Safari 15 and up (iPadOS and iOS 15), and in every other browser as new, because many family iPads stay on iPadOS 15 or 16 (an iPad Air 2 or an iPad mini 4 can go no further than 15.8) and the human's son plays on a tablet: before #169 a Safari before 16.4 read none of the game, and the page stayed empty. The build is written for Safari 15 and fails over anything in it Safari 15.0 cannot read or run ([[INVARIANTS]] § Serving), rather than a bundle of polyfills: the game's own code keeps to what Safari 15.0 has, and what a dependency uses beyond it is lowered by the build or asked for first. A browser older than that gets a card, in English and Danish at once, instead of an empty page: "This iPad is a bit too old for Animath. Ask a grown-up to update it, or try another device." ("This iPhone" on an iPhone, "This browser" elsewhere). A browser new enough whose world cannot be drawn (no WebGL 2) keeps the card that says so, since updating would not help it.

## Copy and languages

All player-facing copy lives in per-language YAML files, `packages/client/src/copy/<code>.yaml`, because the human asked for it ("all copy lives in yaml files (not in code)"). Code refers to copy by key (`t('puzzle.keys')`) and holds no player-facing words, in TypeScript or in a Svelte template.
The game speaks English (`en`) and Danish (`da`), because the human asked for it ("my kid speaks danish. supported languages for now should be danish and english"). English is the fallback, so every key exists in English. The player switches language on the title or in the pause menu; the choice is remembered on the device.
Adding a language is adding a file and one registry line.
YAML is parsed at build time. The browser gets plain objects and ships no YAML parser.
The one exception is what a page says before any of the game's scripts run, since no copy file can be read there: the description and link preview a messenger reads, the card a browser without JavaScript shows, and the card a browser too old for the game's scripts shows, are in `index.html`, English then Danish; the page nginx shows while the game does not answer (`nginx/502.html`) carries `app.updating` in every language, word for word as the copy files write it (`updating-page.test.ts`).
The engine is language-free: species and attacks have ids, not names; events and refusals are codes; a line an authority sends is a copy key with numbers and species by id, never a string. The client picks the words.
Words are picked when they are shown, from the language on screen. State and events carry keys and params, never finished sentences, so changing the language re-words everything at once, without a reload.
A species' names, articles included, are written out per language as forms (`name`, `a`, `the`, `wild`, `aWild`, `theWild`, `bare`, and a bird's `aGrumpy`), never built from rules: Danish articles and adjectives follow the noun's gender.

## Server

Node with Hono (`@hono/node-server`). Serves the built client in production; Vite proxies `/api` to it in development, WebSocket upgrades included (the presence socket at `/api/ws`).
In production the server is one esbuild bundle, the engine inside, run with plain `node`; `tsx` runs it in development only.
WebSockets via `ws`, for presence and friendly matches. The server runs the same engine as the browser; which rules it decides, and which stay in the browser, is § Multiplayer.
Postgres via Drizzle ORM (`node-postgres` driver). Local Postgres in Docker, on a host port of its own ([[ARCHITECTURE]] § Ports and processes).
Migrations are hand-written SQL in `packages/server/drizzle/`, idempotent (`IF NOT EXISTS`), with a matching `_journal.json` entry. `drizzle-kit generate` is not used.
The retired anonymous backup's tables (`players`, `saves`, `save_backups`: § Saves) stay, rows and all, and nothing builds on them (§ Accounts).
Env from a repo-root `.env` loaded with `process.loadEnvFile`; no dotenv package.

## Saves

The save is local first, because the human asked for it: "need to persist state in localstorage so reloads are safe and users can come back to their game later and continue playing". The whole game lives in the browser's `localStorage` and is rewritten after every change (a step, a battle turn, a catch), so the game is persistent with no server at all.
A guest's game lives in the browser alone. An account's game has a copy on the server too (§ Accounts), sent in the background; the game waits for the server only at start, and only briefly. The anonymous per-browser backup, which kept guests' games in development while the game was shared through a tunnel, was retired once the human's kid's save had moved to production (2026-09-27), and its code went. Its tables and their rows stay, and no migration drops or alters them ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server): the human's local database keeps the tunnel's games in them, which the export that moves a game to production reads, and production's schema history stays simple. Dropping them waits for its trigger ([[DEFERRED]]).
A reload never loses progress, a battle in progress included ([[INVARIANTS]] § Authority).
A game is never saved in the air: the save of a game in flight is the game as letting go now would leave it, landed by the same rule (with a bird following the glider, its battle in the air under way, since a reload is no escape), so a reload in the air is a let-go and the save format holds no new field: a battle's realm can now be `air`, which an older build reads as a newer build's save. A save that held the tile under the glider would put a kid over water or in a tree for every page on an older build (a tab left open through an update, another device on the account), which sends such a save to the spawn tile.
One document shape for both copies, `SaveV2`, defined and checked in the engine. A new field is optional and needs no version bump; `version` goes up only when an old document becomes unreadable, with an upgrade that reads it: numbered worlds took version 2, whose upgrade puts every v1 save in World 1. The text of a save an older build wrote is kept as it was before this build first writes over it, in the browser (`animath.save.upgraded`) and, for an account, on the server (`account_save_backups`), so an upgrade that went wrong loses nothing.
A page never writes over a save it has not seen, and a page that falls behind another tab's save takes no more play until it has reloaded into the newest game (the rules: [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves).
Between the browser's save and an account's copy on the server the higher `seq` wins, both ways, and no game is lost on the way: the server keeps aside a game it replaces with another, or cannot read ([[INVARIANTS]] § Saves), and the browser keeps its own game, when the server's wins, under the account's `replaced` key.
A save the game cannot read is set aside, never deleted, in the browser and on the server ([[INVARIANTS]] § Saves).
A save a newer version of the game wrote is newer, not unreadable: an older build never loads it, writes over it or sets it aside, in the browser or on the server, and reloads for the new version instead. An item it does not have is kept and does nothing, so the save plays on; a species it does not have makes the save newer, so species only grow (what makes a save newer, and what enforces it: [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves).
There is no way in the game to delete a save. New game on the title puts the saved game away, never deletes it ([[INVARIANTS]] § Saves says where), and the game has no way back to it, only the human does. A throwaway switch in the URL (the list: [[ARCHITECTURE]], the `flags.ts` row) plays a throwaway game that reads and writes nothing and skips the title. Clearing the site's data is the only ordinary way to lose one.
A new game starts with a starter the player picks, and only a tier-1 species is a starter (the engine's rule, asked by the authority's `new-game` intent): the squirrel, the rabbit and the frog, named by id, because the human approved it with #89 (twelve small figures to choose from would make a worse first minute); the other small animals are caught. Continue hands the authority the save the client holds (`start({ game })`).
Per-device preferences (the language, sound on or off) have their own `localStorage` keys and are never part of the save.

## Multiplayer

The browser runs every single-player rule, for guests and account holders alike: walking, encounters, wild battles, catching, the witch doctor, the shop and the tools (the boat, the glider and the harness among them) stay in `LocalAuthority`, and the browser's `localStorage` save stays the working copy, so the game plays with no server at all and plays on when the connection drops. The server is the authority only for what two players share: presence (who is in which world, where, and doing what) and friendly matches (a match's state, its puzzles, and judging the answers). It takes the positions and the teams clients report on trust, because nothing a player gains can reach another player and a match changes nothing, so cheating gains nothing. A rule moves to the server when a gain can flow between players: trading, match rewards, shared world edits ([[DEFERRED]] "The server stores whatever save the client sends").
A world is a number from 1 to 9999, each number its own seed, because the human asked for it ("if we could let the user pick seeds to transition between instances of the world that would be cool. because i want multiplayer now! [...] so if my kid wants to play with his friend they pick the same seed and they can find each other"). World 1 is exactly today's world (the seed `hashString('prototype')`), so every game saved so far is in it, unchanged, and has it as its home. A new game starts in a random world from 2 to 9999, which is its home, so strangers don't all pile into one. Where a player stands, which way they face and the tiles they cleared are kept per world; the party, the tokens, the items and the name go with the player. So do the step and doctor-visit counts, which key every encounter and puzzle with the world's seed: one count that only rises never meets the same step twice in any world, and it stays one number another tab can catch up with. A battle in progress is always in the world the player is in, because travel waits until the player is exploring.
Every player has a name, guests included: others see it above their character, and it is their username if they make an account, because the human asked for it ("users need a name for their character (that is also their username when/if they log in), and when they see other characters they see their name"). Whether a name is allowed is engine code, so the browser and the server judge it by the same rules.
Players in the same world see each other through presence, over one WebSocket at `/api/ws` on the game's own origin, so an account holder's session cookie goes along and the one proxy path that serves the API serves it too; a guest sends a random guest id kept in `localStorage`. A client reports its tile, facing, lead, boat, harness and what it is busy with (a glide included) when they change; the server relays that to the players near it in the same world, and sends everyone in the world, less often, who is there and roughly where. The first message carries a protocol version, and a client too old for the server reloads itself while exploring, which is safe because its save is local. The messages and their checks are engine code, shared by both sides. There is no chat, because the human said so ("there's no chat in game").
A player near someone in a battle sees it beside them in the world, because the human asked for it ("how will it look to *others* when someone is in battle mode? [...] it would make sense that you could see the two animals which are fighting and maybe even a small visual on the problem being solved as a thought bubble over the other kids head [...] and damage counters too! [...] also leash attempts visualized. this logic could be reused for when friendly matchs are occuring seen from the outside"). A wild battle is the fighter's browser's (as every single-player rule is), so that page reports its own battle; a friendly match's is built by the server from its own match state, never from what a page says. What crosses between pages is numbers and ids, never free text: animals go by their kind, never a nickname, and no answer is ever in it, so a page can put no word on anyone's screen and there is still no chat (who is sent a battle, and how: [[INVARIANTS]] § Presence).
Two players in the same world can fight a friendly match, and it changes nothing but each kid's count of puzzles solved (§ Gameplay; [[INVARIANTS]] § Match), and both go back to exploring where they stood, because the human asked for it ("user interaction is they can fight each other. there is no transaction from the outcome of a match, it's just for fun (for now)"). The match runs on the server, in the engine's match reducer with a seed only the server holds, and each player gets a view of it without the answers. Each side brings a team from its own party at full health (which animals: [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Friendly matches").
A friendly match is asked for face to face: a player close to another, both exploring on land, asks, and the one asked says yes or no in a short time while walking on (walking off is a no; how close and how long: [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Friendly matches"), and a player is asked by one challenger at a time. The rule for who may ask whom is engine code (`challengeRefusal`), so the page's button and the server judge it alike; the server alone knows who is asking or in a match. Two players who ask each other at once both said yes.
A match has a turn clock of keys, never of answers: a kid who touches the keys on their turn is never hurried, one who has not for a while is asked if they are still there, and after longer the match is theirs to lose. A page that drops out has a while to come back. The times are in [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Friendly matches".
A match under way when its server stops (a deploy, or a crash that says nothing) ends there, for both, with no winner, and the pages offer to play again once both are back, one tap each; the server does not keep matches running while it drains, nor hand them to the next copy.

## Accounts

Accounts are optional: a guest plays on `localStorage` alone for as long as they like, and only a player who makes an account has their game kept in the server's database, because the human asked for it ("for simple onboarding users start playing immediately when they come on the site. state is managed in local storage, but after each played hour they are prompted to create a username and password so they can log in. it's only once they are logged in we start persisting their state in a postgres db on our server. but that process should be seamless, and if they play in their browser forever without logging, that's probably fine too."). The prompt comes after a count of the game's steps ([[PRODUCT]] §4 "Saving") rather than every hour of play, because the human later asked for it ("nudge kids to make account if they havent after each 1k steps"), in place of the hour. The username is the character's name, unique whatever its case; a kid whose name is taken picks another, and the character takes it too.
An account is a username and a password, nothing more: no email and no second factor, because the human asked for it ("no 2fa or email validation, just keep their username and hash their pw, there's no PII so easy does it"). The password is hashed with Node's built-in scrypt and a salt of its own, so hashing adds no dependency. A session is an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie, Secure in production, with a one-year sliding expiry; the database keeps its token hashed, and logging out deletes it. Logging in and registering are rate-limited per IP and per name. With no email, a forgotten password is reset, or an account deleted, by the human with an admin command.
`localStorage` keeps the guest game and the account game in separate slots, so logging in never swaps one game for another behind a kid's back: registering moves the guest game into the account, logging in leaves the guest game where it is, and logging out always goes to the title, with the guest game where it was. A logged-in game still saves to `localStorage` first and then to the server, with optimistic concurrency on `seq`; the server checks it with the engine's validator and keeps the 1 MiB limit. On load and on login the newer save wins, and a `409` means another device got ahead: the page loads the server's copy and says so kindly.
A game played before the public site moves there in an account the admin makes, opened by a welcome link, because the human asked for it ("my kid has been playing on the local server for a bit. i'd like it that he could log in here and you'd transfer the DB stuff to remote gracefully so he could continue playing there without losing his state."). The admin exports the save from the database it was played on and imports it on the new server, which makes the account with the save's name, the save, and no password, and prints a link that works once, for 14 days. The kid opens it, is welcomed back by name, picks the password, and plays on where he was; the admin never chooses or learns it. The browser on the old address cannot hand its save to the new one (another origin's `localStorage`), so the server's copy of the game is the one that moves.

## Development

All implementation work happens in a git worktree via `git gtr new`, never on `main`.
GitHub Issues (repo `ulfaslak/animath`) is the tracker. PRs merge with `--merge`.
Rendering changes are verified by reading a screenshot from `scripts/screenshot.mjs`; engine changes by vitest; balance changes by a property test over the whole catalog.

## Deployment

The game runs on a dedicated Hetzner VPS that mirrors lawcel's setup (Docker Compose behind nginx, Postgres in the same compose), except that there is no staging: a push to main without `[skip deploy]` gates on check, test and lint and goes straight to production. The human asked for it ("i'd prefer it if you copied my setup on ../lawcel exactly [...] well it's just a fun game, so nevermind a staging environment, push to main (unless skip tags) builds and goes on prod immediately"). The server is the game's own, to keep it apart from the business product on lawcel's, and the setup is lawcel's because it works. It has been live at https://animath.xyz since 2026-09-27. Before that the game was shared from this machine through a tunnel ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § The old tunnel), and a kid's tablet still opens it at that address, which the development server still answers. Old addresses point home: a page on any address that is neither the game's domain (or its `www.`) nor a developer's machine (`localhost`, a loopback, private or link-local address, a `.local` name) shows a card before the game, "Animath has moved!", whose big button, "Play at animath.xyz", opens the domain in the same tab, and plays on there only behind a small "Keep playing here" link, for a grown-up testing: a kid sees the move first, on every load. A card rather than a redirect, so nothing leaves the old address without the kid's tap, and a game still under the old address's `localStorage` stays reachable there. The domain is one value, deploy.env's `MATHGAME_DOMAIN`, which the development server reads as well as the image's build.
Certificates come from Let's Encrypt through nginx's own ACME module over HTTP-01, as on lawcel, because the human asked for it ("look at how we've done certs on lawcel, that's how it should be done here too!"): no certbot, no DNS API.
No log keeps a player's IP address (#115): the players are kids, and logs are read by whoever looks into an incident, agents included. nginx's access log has no client address. Its error log cannot be formatted and names the client in every line about a request or a connection, so it writes those only at `emerg`: nginx out of memory, which still names a client. Every level below holds lines a client can cause, with its address and its request line: `crit` a TLS handshake that fails in a way nginx does not know, `alert` a worker out of connections, which one client holding a thousand brings about. nginx's own lines (starting, reloading, the certificate module's failures) are at `notice` and name no one. The app's own log names no address. The access log tells what the dropped lines told, without the address: each request's status, and every app address nginx tried with what it answered and how long it took. The limits per address still count by address (nginx's in its memory, the app's from `X-Forwarded-For`); only the logs leave it out. How long the logs last: `/redeploy` § Logs and a look inside.
A page reports every error nothing on it caught to the game's own server, and to no third-party service (no Sentry, no analytics), so a bug in a browser the game was never tried in (Safari on an iPad, first) is known without a kid telling anyone. A report holds what finds the bug and nothing that tells whose page it was: no name, no account, no save, no cookie and no address, and the kid's own words hidden (its exact fields, what is hidden and its bounds: [[INVARIANTS]] § Server, "An error report carries nothing that tells whose page it was"). Only the admin reads the reports (`admin errors`), never a player, for 30 days at most.
Prod is watched from the developer's Mac, until the human says where its alerts go ([[HUMAN_TODO]]): every 10 minutes a launchd agent asks the game's health, reads its certificates' dates and the error reports new since its last look, and says what is wrong in a macOS notification, and in Slack once a webhook is set. It watches only while the Mac is awake and online ([[DEFERRED]] "Nothing watches prod while the Mac sleeps").
The checks gate the deploy inside the deploy workflow, since branch protection is not on this plan.
No third-party backend services (no Convex, Supabase, Firebase). Postgres and a Node process are the whole stack.
