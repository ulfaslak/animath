# Architecture

## Shape

A pnpm workspace with three packages and one rule about the arrows between them:

```
packages/engine   ← pure game rules. Imports nothing.
      ▲      ▲
      │      │
packages/client   packages/server
(Vite, Three.js,  (Hono, ws, Drizzle,
 Svelte overlay)   Postgres)
```

The engine knows the rules of the game and nothing about screens, sockets or databases. The client renders and collects input. The server keeps accounts and their saves, and the pages' error reports for the admin, and is the authority for what two players share: presence and friendly matches ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). Client and server may both import the engine; neither imports the other, and the engine imports neither. Anything that decides an outcome — damage, a catch, an answer's correctness, whether a tile is walkable — is engine code.

To see the current tree:

```bash
tree packages -I 'node_modules|dist' --dirsfirst
```

### The authority seam (how multiplayer slots in)

```
   UI / renderer            Authority                 engine
 ┌──────────────┐  intent  ┌──────────────┐  calls  ┌──────────┐
 │ keyboard,    │ ───────▶ │ validate,    │ ──────▶ │ rules,   │
 │ Svelte panel │          │ apply, emit  │ ◀────── │ formulas │
 │ Three scene  │ ◀─────── │              │         └──────────┘
 └──────────────┘  event   └──────────────┘
```

`Authority` (`protocol.ts`) is `dispatch(intent)` + `subscribe(listener)`. The client has exactly one authority instance; everything above it consumes events. `LocalAuthority` adds the save's hooks, `start`, `snapshot` and `catchUp`, for `main.ts` and the autosave ([[DECISIONS]] § Engine), and one read-only count, `stepsTaken`, for the save card's nudge.

- **Today**: `packages/client/src/authority/local.ts`: `LocalAuthority` runs the engine in-process. Its randomness (the encounter roll after each step, the sky's roll per tile flown, a battle's and a doctor visit's seed, a cast's catch: `Rng(hashInts(seed, FISHING_SALT, steps))`, a cast being a step) is keyed by the world seed and its count of completed steps (and visits), never `Math.random` ([[INVARIANTS]] § Authority). After a battle it emits `party-changed` (HP written back, a caught animal added; after a loss, as `knockOut` leaves it) and the closing `message`; after each heal or hand-over `party-changed`, and `belongings-changed` (whole) when tokens or items change. Every battle and doctor step goes through `countSolved` (a right answer → `solved-changed`) and `recordAnswers` (any judged answer → `puzzles-changed`). A wild battle starts with the record's `topicBonus`, as does a saved air battle. A battle's start and steps go through `recordBattle`; a grown book → `book-changed`. A match's server-judged events come in as `match-answers`, dispatched by `match/controller.ts` per server step: only this player's right answers count and their answers enter the record (not `recent`); nothing else changes, the book included. Each step counts once: `matchSteps` keeps each match's last counted `step` and ignores one at or below it, and a malformed batch is ignored (side not `a`/`b`, step not a whole number from 1, no match id, events not a hole-free list of at most `MAX_MATCH_EVENTS` objects with a `type`, more than one `answer-judged`, or one without a side and a boolean `correct`), so one step adds at most one puzzle solved. A `party` intent goes to `applyPartyIntent` with the activity (`battle`, `doctor`, `flight`, else `explore`) and realm, and is always answered with `party-edited` while a game is under way. An `interact` away from a tent goes to `castLine` at a fishing hole (`line-cast`, or `tool-needed` without the rod), else `clearTile` (`tile-cleared`, `tool-needed`, or `nothing-to-interact`); the authority keeps the `WorldEdits` overlay, walks and knocks out through it, and saves it. The starting party passes through `normalizeNickname`. It keeps the player's name (`choose-name` in any mode while a game is under way, through `checkName`), the home world, the current world, the worlds left behind, the current land, the lands left behind and the lands unlocked (`unlockLands`, after every hand-over). A doctor `flew` goes through `fly` once the visit ends: `travelled`, `party-changed`, `belongings-changed`, and with no animal of the land `starter-wanted`, after which only `pick-starter`, `interact` at the tent and `leave-game` are taken (and `party`, `choose-name`, `match-answers`, taken in any mode). `travel` (exploring only) goes through the engine's `travel`: `travelled` (seed, spawn, position, facing, overlay; party, tokens, items, solved, book, name and counts go along) or `travel-refused`; after a clear, `fitWorlds` trims the worlds left behind. The whole game fits in a `SavedGame`: `snapshot()` takes one any time, a battle included (its state, never its seed, which is derived again from the step count); `start({ game })` emits `welcome`, then `battle-started` for a saved battle. The client saves the game (§ Saving).
- **The title**: until a game is under way (at load and after `leave-game`) the authority takes one intent, `new-game { speciesId, nickname?, name? }`: `chooseStarter` and `checkName` (`not-a-name`) check it, the starter gets a minted id, the home world is random from 2 to 9999 (`randomHome`; the `homeWorld` option pins it; a throwaway game is in World 1); the answer is `welcome` with `newGame: true` (`false` for a game picked up) or `new-game-refused { reason }`. Mid-game it is refused (`game-in-progress`). `leave-game` (Quit to title) is taken only while exploring, answers `game-left` and stops the game where it stands (`snapshot()` still holds it). Continue is the host's call, `start({ game })` with the autosave's `resumable()`.
- **Words**: events carry what happened; the client words its own screens (battle narration, the druid, the line while the team needs the druid, who goes first after `party-edited`, the hint after `nothing-to-interact`). The authority's own lines (a battle's closing line, `doctor.came` after a go-to or trip) are `message` events carrying a `Line` from `LINES`: a copy key with species ids, never a sentence ([[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages).
- **Multiplayer**: every rule of a player's own game runs in the browser's `LocalAuthority`; the server is the authority only for what two players share ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer, which also says what moves next). Presence (`presence/`, § Presence): the page says where the player is and what they do, the server says who is near; a page in a wild battle reports it, and the server passes it only to players who see its fighter. Friendly matches (§ Friendly matches): the server plays them in the match reducer with a seed only it holds (`presence/matches.ts`); `match/controller.ts` sends the kid's choices and shows the views over the presence socket, not `Authority`, which hears only `match-answers`. Two intents carry the server's word: `go-to { near }` (`arrivalSpot` beside a friend) and `match-answers`.

Two things keep a rule cheap to move to the server: intents carry only what the player *chose* (a direction, an attack index, an answer string), never a computed outcome; and events describe what *happened* in enough detail to render without re-running the rules.

### Component reuse

One `PuzzlePanel` serves the battle and the druid's card (with one `NumberPad` on touch; on the druid's card also a token sum's story and Back), and one helper (`input/answer.ts`) types answers for both. One `BundleCard` draws a party card (one animal or a stack) and one `BundleAnimals` a card's animals, wherever the team is shown as cards (the HUD's column and open card, the pause menu's card); one `stackSummary` (`hp.ts`) words a stack on the HUD and the pause menu alike. The druid's list is not cards: its tabs share one grid of rows, so it draws its own rows with the same `HpBar`. One `HpBar` (`emptyTag`, `thin`, `thick`, `preview`, bands from `hpBand`). Every card (HUD, battle panel, druid, pause menu, title) has one look from the tokens `--panel-bg`, `--radius` and `--hud-shadow` in `styles.css`, not a shared component. New UI reuses these before inventing.

**The battle's pieces** take their data as props and assume nothing about a wild opponent, so any fight between two animals can be drawn with them: `StatusBox`, `AttackTile`, `MoveButton`, `LevelPicker`, `TopicChips`, `ActionPreview`; `menu.ts` takes the moves row as a list (`WILD_MOVES`). **A friendly match's screen is the battle's own** ([[UI_SPEC]] § Friendly matches): `MatchController` fills the same `battle` view with `battle.vs` and `battle.moves` (`MATCH_MOVES`, through the same `menuKey`, `actionAt`, `rowOf`), so `BattlePanel` draws both: status boxes from the view's teams, `acting` from the turn, tiles, picker, badges and preview (`hitSpan`; a match has no `battle.bonus`, so one number), the other's animal with its owner, the Leave door, a `waiting` screen with the other's `ShownPuzzle` (`PuzzlePanel`'s `watch`), `MatchResult`, `MatchNotes`. The chunky toy button is a look from tokens, not a component: `--press`, `--accent-edge`, `--edge` (a darker edge under the face that squashes flat when pressed). A list whose rows line up in columns (the switch list, the druid's list) is a grid of subgrid rows sized by its longest name; its edge columns are never a fixed width, because a subgrid's padding is a margin on its edge items and a fixed column can't make room for it (the caret slid into the gap against the name).

A component that shows one thing (an HP bar, a name) is keyed by that thing's id wherever another thing can take its place, so a transition never runs from one animal's value to another's (a newcomer's bar must not drain from the leaver's full bar).

### Copy

```
 src/copy/en.yaml ┐  yaml() at build time          ┌ language.current ($state)
 src/copy/da.yaml ┴─────────────▶ plain objects ───┤ t('key', params) ─▶ Svelte markup
                                                   └ language.onChange ─▶ code outside Svelte

 engine event ─▶ Line { key, params: numbers, animals, attacks, players } ─▶ words(line) at draw time
```

- **Where the words live.** Every player-facing line but what the page says before any script runs (`index.html`: [[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages) is in `src/copy/<code>.yaml`, nested by screen (`battle.*`, `doctor.*`, `pause.*`, `explore.*`, …), with every species under its engine id: seven forms (`ANIMAL_FORMS`: `name`, `a`, `the`, `wild`, `aWild`, `theWild` ("en vild ræv", "et vildt egern"), `bare`; a bird also has `aGrumpy`) and its attacks' names. Only the client reads them; both languages ship in the main bundle.
- **Loading.** `yaml()` parses a file when Vite builds, serves or tests it; the bundle holds data, no YAML parser. A syntax error or repeated key fails the build.
- **The language on screen.** `language.current` starts from `?lang=` (that visit only), else `localStorage['animath.language']`, else the first of `navigator.languages` the game speaks (`da-DK` counts as `da`), else English. The Language rows call `language.set()`: switch, remember (storage failures ignored), set `<html lang>`, call `onChange` listeners.
- **`t()` and lines.** `t()` reads `language.current` on every call, so markup re-renders on a change. What the screen says stays data until drawn (a `Line` for battle narration; a `Said`, `state/hud.svelte.ts`, for the message line, keeping an authority `message` as it came; a `DoctorLine`), worded in markup by `words(line)`, `saidWords`, `doctorWords`; a stored string would stay in its language. Code outside Svelte calls `t()` when it draws and redraws on `language.onChange`.
- **Names.** `names.ts` words animals and attacks (besides it only `doctor/lines.ts`'s `namesOf` and the HUD's following-bird line, `aGrumpy`, read species forms): a nickname is shown as stored for every form; a sentence picks its form (`{animal.theWild}`), capitalised at a sentence start; a nickname is never changed.
- **Gaps.** A key missing from Danish shows in English; missing from English, the key. In development each gap logs one `console.warn` (failing `scripts/screenshot.mjs`); production is silent. `copy-files.test.ts` fails on both.
- **Dev server.** An edited copy file swaps words in place: `language.svelte.ts` accepts `languages.ts`'s hot update, so the game and the store survive.
- **The engine emits no words** ([[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages): ids, events, refusal codes (`BattleRejection`, `DoctorRejection`, `PartyRejection`), `message` as a typed `Line` from `LINES`; prompts are maths notation (`7 × 8 = ?`). `no-words.test.ts` and `wordedStrings` keep it so.

### Pointer input

```
 tap on a button (taps.ts)  ─▶ press(key) ─▶ KeyboardEvent on window ─▶ main.ts ─▶ the screen that is up ─▶ its key handler
 number pad finger down     ─▶ press(key)                                          (explore: keyboard.keydown)
 D-pad finger down / up     ─▶ hold / release of an arrow key
```

- **One path.** A click or tap never reaches the authority, a view or a controller by its own way: buttons send key presses (`input/press.ts`), which `main.ts` routes to the screen that is up as a keyboard's (explore's via `keydown`). So every key guard (auto-repeat, busy, the quiet moment) holds for taps, and what a press means is said once, in the screen's key handler. Components stay readers of their views; a press is not a dispatch.
- **A tap** (`input/taps.ts`; what counts: [[UI_SPEC]] § Pointer and touch; keys it must never mix up: [[INVARIANTS]] § Input) is followed by `pointerId`, against the button's key (`data-press`) and the screen it began on (`main.ts` counts every change of what takes keys, one animal's options apart from another's); the key is pressed on lift if the button still names it. The browser's `click` is not used for a pointer: it comes only once every finger has lifted (Talk and Menu under a thumb on the D-pad would never press), and lands on whatever is under a finger lifted after the screen changed. A click without a pointer (`detail` 0, a screen reader) still presses.
- **The pointer's own keys** name what a keyboard can't: `row:<i>`, `option:<i>` (a second list beside the rows: the pause menu's options and a card's animals, the Worlds screen's buttons, the book's cards, Who's here's players, the druid's confirm, a match's result and "Leave the match?" buttons; why: [[INVARIANTS]] § Input), `level:<n>`, `language:<code>`, `tab:<id>`, `land:<id>`, `reveal`, and the party column's, by species never place (a drag can re-sort under a resting finger): `bundle:<species>`, `open:<species>`, `animal:<id>`, `move:<species>:<to>` (pressed by the HUD on the drop; the carried card has another key until then, so a drag is never a tap; a card only lifted keeps its key). Handlers read them with `tappedRow` and siblings. A row key on the battle's menus only moves the cursor (a pick spends the turn), and on the title's starters only lights one (a pick starts a game); elsewhere it moves the cursor and runs the handler's Enter.
- **Touch controls** are drawing only: `touch.on` (`:root.touch`) shows the D-pad, a puzzle's number pad, tap hints and finger-sized rows (the Worlds pad shows either way), never what a press does.
- The pretend key events are untrusted (`isTrusted` false), so `watchInput` ignores them and the page's own presses never switch touch controls off. They carry no `code`, so `Keyboard` files a D-pad arrow under its key name.

### Sound

```
 explore / battle / doctor / pause / ┐                          ┌ master (MASTER_VOLUME)
 title / match / account / travel    │                          │
 controllers, hud (a new lead)       ┴─ sfx.play(cue, opts) ────┤   → limiter → speakers
 cues.ts (data) ─ scheduleCue ─────────────── WebAudio nodes ───┘
```

- **Where a cue plays.** From the screens, where events already become visuals: a battle or doctor beat plays its cue, a cursor key or pick its blip, the HUD dings for a new lead. The authority and engine never hear of sound ([[DECISIONS]] § Client). The cue table: [[UI_SPEC]] § Sound and juice.
- **When sound can start.** `main.ts` calls `sfx.unlock()` in the capture phase of every `keydown`, `pointerdown`, `pointerup` and `touchend`, before any screen sees it, so the key that opens a menu can blip. `unlock` makes the `AudioContext` only on a press the browser counts (`navigator.userActivation.isActive`): Chrome does not count Escape or modifiers, and a context made on one logs a warning (failing `scripts/screenshot.mjs`).
- **The setting.** `sfx.on`, in `localStorage['animath.sound']` (`'on'` / `'off'`; failures ignored), on by default. M is `sfx.flip()`, which plays no cue (the chip shows it). Off disconnects the master gain and suspends the context; on makes a fresh master and resumes.
- **Without WebAudio**, or in screen tests (no context unlocked), `play` records the cue (`recent`, `onCue`) and schedules nothing; `test/sfx.test.ts` unlocks its own `Sfx` over a fake context.

### Coordinate system

Engine grid `(x, y)` maps to Three `(x, height, z)` with `z = y`; grid "down" is toward the camera, down and to the left on screen (the camera's yaw is 35°: [[DECISIONS]] § Client), the same everywhere because that yaw is fixed. Each tile is a 1 × 1 column centred on integer `x`, `z`, from `y = −0.5` up to its ground top, which is at `y = 0.5 + 0.25·height` for land and lower for water (`groundTop` in `tiles.ts`). Figures stand with their feet on that top face; the player hops 0.15 during a step. A figure faces `+z` (grid "down", toward the camera) at `rotation.y = 0`; `up` is `π`, `right` is `π/2`, `left` is `-π/2`.

A kid never sees the grid's numbers (only `?debug` shows them). The coordinates in the explore corner are `coordinates.ts`'s: `(x − spawn.x, spawn.y − y)`, from the world's `spawnPoint`, so grid "up" is `+y` there.

### Modes

Two modes, one on screen at a time (a friendly match is drawn on the battle's screen by `MatchController` from the server's messages, never from the authority: § Friendly matches). `battle-started` hands the screen to the battle controller: explore input off, and the battle scene and panel replace the world once the step into the grass has landed. The authority resolves each battle intent at once (`battle-updated`); the controller plays the events as beats and only then shows the new state, so the screen lags the authority on purpose. `battle-ended` puts the authority back in explore at once, but the screen stays on the result card until the player leaves it; only then does explore input return. The authority ignores walking during a battle and battle intents outside one; the engine refuses party intents during one (`not-exploring`), since the battle holds its own copy of the party and writes it back at the end.

The pause menu is not a mode: an overlay in explore (Escape) that switches explore input off while open and closes on `battle-started`, `doctor-visit-started`, `welcome`, `game-left` or `travelled`. The authority sees only its party intents and `leave-game`.

A trip to another world is not a mode: explore's own overlay (`travel/controller.ts`). Under its cover no screen takes a key (`keyScreen` gives none; M still flips the sound); `travel` goes only once shut, and it opens on `travelled`. The authority sees one `travel` intent; the transition is the client's alone.

The title is before the modes: no game is under way (`game.mode` is `loading` before the first, `title` after `game-left`); while `title.open` it takes every key (`keyScreen`) but M outside a text box, unless an account card is over it. Its world is the renderer's, aside and drifting (`lookAt`), with scenery figures that go on `welcome`; its starter stage is drawn instead of the world (`setStage`). It closes on `welcome`.

The doctor is not a mode: a card over explore, driven by `doctor-visit-started` / `-updated` / `-ended`, played back as beats. Each doctor event carries its visit's number (a `DoctorState`'s `step` starts at 0 every visit), and the card ignores other visits' events. Explore input is off while it is open; the authority ignores walking during a visit and doctor intents outside one. The authority opens a visit on `interact` when `canTalkToDoctor(seed, pos, facing)` holds, so it tracks the facing the client shows: `down` in a new game, then the `dir` of every `move` (`player-moved` or `player-blocked`), of `player-placed` and `landed`, and the `facing` of `travelled`; saved with the game and carried by `welcome`, so both sides agree after any `welcome`. The client computes the prompts with the same engine functions from the same events (`canTalkToDoctor`, `clearableAhead`, `clearTile`'s check, `holeAhead`). After a lost battle: `battle-ended`, `party-changed` (tired where fought; with no tent in reach and no glider, looked after by a druid who came, `doctor.came`) and the closing `message`; back in explore `DoctorWay` points the way. `player-placed` is a go-to: put beside another player, facing them, no tween. Looks and words: [[UI_SPEC]] § Explore mode, § Battle mode, § Doctor.

### Saving

Local first ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves). The rules the save keeps are [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves, and what a kid sees of them [[PRODUCT]] §4 "Saving". The pieces:

```
 LocalAuthority ──events──▶ Autosave.handle ──(one per intent)──▶ commit()
       ▲                        │                                   │
       │ start({ game })        │ snapshot()                        ├─▶ localStorage['animath.save']   the save
 Autosave.boot() ◀── localStorage (instant) · for an account,       │    (an account's: its own keys)
       │               one server GET (2.5 s at most)               └─▶ PUT /api/account/save          an account's
       └─▶ notice (a copy key for the HUD)                               (background, seq must rise)   copy
```

- **The document.** `SaveV5` (engine `save.ts`; its fields and the server's checks: § HTTP API; what each version added and each upgrade does: [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves, "Every save an older build wrote upgrades…"). A field is in every write from the build that brought it on, except `edits` (`WorldEdits`' canonical text), written only once something is cleared, and `worlds`, only once there is one. `steps`, `visits` and the party belong to the player across worlds; `pos`, `facing` and `edits` to the world they are in. `lineage`: a random id minted when a game starts, the same in all its saves; `seq` numbers a game's saves, one higher each time. `restoreGame` fills what an older document lacks: facing down, no steps, visits, tokens, items, puzzles solved, clearing, worlds left or name, and the book the save proves. Older versions are read through `SAVE_UPGRADES`.
- **When.** `Autosave.handle` sorts every event (playing, progress, or nothing: [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves, "An unreadable save never stops the game…") and writes once, in a microtask, after everything one intent caused. `flush()` writes at once on `pagehide` and when hidden. Saving starts with `begin()` (Continue) or a new game's `welcome`; `game-left` ends it ([[INVARIANTS]] § Authority, "Nothing runs behind the title…").
- **Start** (`boot()`; its plan the title shows, its game Continue's, `resumable()`): the local save as `readSave` sorts it (a game; unreadable, `held` until the kid plays; a newer build's, `frozen`, the page behind it; or none), and for an account's game with no readable save here one `GET` first, the session check included (`BOOT_WAIT_MS`). A guest's start never waits. Its notices: `save/notices.ts` (a readable local game says `save.welcomeBack` once Continue picks it up).
- **Two tabs.** Every tab keeps the exact text of the save it last read or wrote and reads the key again before each write; a `storage` event and `recheck()` run the same check. Catching up (`sameProgress`, `LocalAuthority.catchUp`), falling behind (`behind`) and keeping a save written over unseen (`keepOwnSave`): [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves, "A page never writes over a save it has not seen", "A page that is behind takes no play". While behind, `main.ts` asks `behindAction` each frame.
- **The server's copy**, an account's only (a guest's game has none since the anonymous backup was retired, [[DECISIONS]] § Saves): the same `Autosave` sends it through its `SaveServer` (`save/api.ts`; `accountSaveServer`). How the two copies settle: [[INVARIANTS]] § Saves, "The server keeps the save with the higher `seq`…".
- **Keys** (`save/storage.ts` `KEYS`): `animath.save`, `animath.save.unreadable`, `animath.save.replaced` (a page's own last save written over unseen; for an account's game, the browser's copy when a newer one came from the server), `animath.save.previous` (a game left for a new one, and the guest game's last save after `moveGuestGameIn`), `animath.save.upgraded` (an older build's save as it was). An account's keys have the same set-asides (`accountKeys`). A set-aside goes to the first free slot of its key (`.2`, `.3`, … up to `.20`, `MAX_SET_ASIDE`; `previous` up to `.200`, `MAX_PUT_AWAY`), never over one. `animath.player` (`{ id, secret }`) and `animath.player.previous`, the retired backup's identity, are never read or written and stay for the export ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Moving a kid's game to production). `animath.language` belongs to the copy module.
- **A throwaway switch** (listed in the `flags.ts` row): no store, no server, no title.
- **Accounts** ([[DECISIONS]] § Accounts). The browser plays the guest game or the logged-in account's (`account/session.ts`, the `animath.account` pointer), each under its own keys (`KEYS`, `accountKeys`): one `Autosave` per page, built at start with the game's keys and server. A guest's game has no server; an account's is `accountSaveServer`, behind a `SessionCheck` that asks `/api/account/me` once at start and lets nothing reach the save routes until the cookie is this account's ([[INVARIANTS]] § Saves, "An account's save goes to the server only for the account the page plays"; only `/ready` may go first, [[INVARIANTS]] § Server). Register, login and logout move what they must in storage (`session.ts`), then restart the page (`account/restart.ts`); another tab changing the pointer puts this page behind (`fallBehind`). A welcome link's page (`account/welcome.ts`) starts on the title with the welcome card ([[UI_SPEC]] § Accounts); its password logs in as a login does (`takeAccountGame`, the pointer, `restart('welcome')`). `pushNow` sends the newest save before a logout, waiting briefly. A guest game's next save card is kept beside the save (`SaveNudge`); `main.ts` opens it while exploring.

### Data model

- `players`, `saves`, `save_backups`: the retired anonymous backup's ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves), written by nothing and kept, rows and all ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server; dropping them is a [[DEFERRED]] item). `players`: `id uuid pk`, `secret_hash text` (SHA-256 of the browser's secret), `display_name text?`, `created_at`, `last_seen_at`; one row per browser that played as a guest on the development server. `saves`: `player_id uuid pk → players (cascade)`, `data jsonb` (a version 1 or 2 save document), `updated_at`; that browser's last save, read by `admin export-local-save`. `save_backups`: `id bigserial pk`, `player_id uuid → players (cascade)`, `data jsonb`, `reason text` (`replaced`, `unreadable`), `created_at`; for recovering a game by hand ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database). The human's local database holds the tunnel's games in them; production's are empty.
- `users`: `id uuid pk`, `name text` (as `checkName` gives it; also the character's name), `name_key text unique` (`nameKey(name)`, computed at registration or `admin import-save`: a change to `nameKey`'s rules needs a migration recomputing every row), `password_hash text` (`passwords.ts`), `created_at`. One row per account; nothing refers to the retired tables.
- `sessions`: `token_hash text pk` (SHA-256 of the cookie's token), `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (a year on; `/me` moves it a year out again once 30 days into that year, `SLIDE_AFTER_DAYS`). At most 20 per account: a new session clears the account's expired ones and keeps the 20 latest by `expires_at`, then `created_at`.
- `account_saves`: `user_id uuid pk → users (cascade)`, `data jsonb` (the `SaveV5` envelope below), `seq bigint` (the document's `seq`), `updated_at`. One save per account, written by the write guard (§ HTTP API).
- `account_save_backups`: `id bigserial pk`, `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `data jsonb`, `reason text` (`replaced`: a different game took its place, or this build's first save over an older build's save of the same game; `unreadable`), `created_at`. A save the server would otherwise lose, copied before a write replaces it, newest first within a per-account budget (the write guard). Nothing reads it; for recovering a game by hand ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Accounts).
- `welcome_tokens`: `token_hash text pk`, `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (14 days on), `used_at` (null until the link logs in). A link to an account `admin import-save` made with no password (`password_hash` is `NO_PASSWORD`, `'!'`); it works only while the account has none.
- `client_errors`: `id bigserial pk`, `created_at`, `message text`, `stack text`, `build text`, `mode text`, `browser text`, `screen text`, `test boolean` (default false). One row per report (`client-errors.ts`), nothing about the sender: no player, account or address. At most 30 days and 5,000 rows, the oldest dropped as a report comes in; indexed by `created_at` (`client_errors_created_at_idx`). Refers to no table; only `admin errors` reads it.

Every non-key reference has its own index (`save_backups_player_id_idx`, `sessions_user_id_idx`, `account_save_backups_user_id_idx`, `welcome_tokens_user_id_idx`); `name_key`'s uniqueness is `users_name_key_unique`.

Hot fields get promoted from `data` to columns when a query needs them: `account_saves.seq`, which `listAccounts` reads (the write guard reads the document). A leaderboard would be next; who is near whom is presence's, in memory.

### HTTP API

Errors are JSON `{ error: string }`, except the health check's. All routes are under `/api`. A route that reads a body answers `400 { error: 'body is not valid JSON' }` to non-JSON and a `400` to missing fields; every POST and PUT has a body limit, past which `413 { error: 'body is bigger than <n> bytes' }`: 4 KiB for login, logout and the welcome POST, 1 MiB + 4 KiB for register, 1 MiB (`SAVE_MAX_BYTES`) for the save PUT, 8 KiB (`REPORT_MAX_BYTES`) for an error report.

| Route | Auth | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/health` | none | `200 { ok: true, db: true, sha }`, or `503 { ok: true, db: false, sha }` when the database does not answer. `sha`: the running image's commit (`dev` outside one); each deploy checks prod reports the one it deployed. |
| any other path under `/api`, and `/ws` and below | none | `404 { error: 'not found' }`, never the game's page; the retired `/api/players` routes among them, everywhere. The client counts it as no answer (`offline`), unlike the save route's own `404` (`no save yet`). |
| `POST /api/account/register` | none | `201 { user: { name } }` and the session cookie. Body `{ name, password, save? }`: `save` is the guest game brought (`validateSaveWrite`, 1 MiB at most), stored as the account's. `400 { error: 'bad name', reason }` (`checkName`), `400 { error: 'bad password', reason: 'short' \| 'long' }` (`checkPassword`), `400 { error: 'bad save', detail }`, `409 { error: 'name taken' }` (by `nameKey`), `413 { error: 'save is bigger than 1048576 bytes' }`, `503` for a guest game a newer build wrote. Nothing is made unless all of it is. |
| `POST /api/account/login` | none | `200 { user: { name } }` and the cookie; `401 { error: 'wrong name or password' }` for a wrong password and an unknown name alike, in the same time. |
| `POST /api/account/logout` | none | `200 { ok: true }`: the session deleted and the cookie cleared, with or without one. With `x-animath-account` (as the game sends it) the session ends only if it is that account's, and no cookie is sent (one could land after another tab's simultaneous login and clear its cookie). `400` for a header that is not a name. |
| `GET /api/account/ready` | none | `200 { ready: boolean }`, `Cache-Control: no-store`: the database answers and has the accounts' tables (`AccountsReady`); `false` before `0003_accounts` or with no answer. The game offers an account only on `true` ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server). |
| `GET /api/account/me` | none | `200 { user: { name } }`, or `200 { user: null }`. |
| `GET /api/account/save` | session | `200` the save as stored (`SaveV5`, or an older version stored before an update), readable or not, or `404 { error: 'no save yet' }`. |
| `PUT /api/account/save` | session | `200 { ok: true }`; `409 { error, save }` with the stored save when its `seq` is the same or higher, or a newer build wrote it (`STORED_FROM_NEWER_BUILD`, whatever the `seq`); `400` (not JSON, or `{ error: 'bad save', detail }` from `validateSaveWrite`); `413` over `SAVE_MAX_BYTES`; `503 { error: SAVE_FROM_NEWER_BUILD }` for a document a newer build wrote (this server is older); `401 { error: 'not logged in' }` when the account was deleted meanwhile (`writeAccountSave`'s `gone`). |
| `GET /api/account/welcome` | none | The token in `x-animath-welcome` (`WELCOME_HEADER`), never the path: nginx's access log keeps every request line (its error log only when out of memory). `200 { name }` for a live link, nothing else; `410 { error: 'link used' }` (or its account has a password now), `410 { error: 'link expired' }`, `404 { error: 'no such link' }`, naming no account; `400` without the header. `Cache-Control: no-store`; never a cookie. |
| `POST /api/account/welcome` | none | Body `{ token, password }`: `200 { user: { name }, save }` and the cookie: password set, link used, this browser logged in (its old session ended), `save` as stored (or null). `400 { error: 'bad password', reason }` leaves the link as it was; `410`, `404` as the GET; only a live link costs a password hash. |
| `POST /api/client-errors` | none | An error report (`client-errors.ts`). Body `{ message, stack?, build, mode, browser, screen, test? }`: `message`, `stack` strings; `build` 7–40 lower-case hex digits or `dev`; `mode` lower-case letters and dashes (≤ 24); `browser` a letter then letters, digits, spaces and `._()-` (≤ 60); `screen` `<width>x<height>`; `test` a boolean (sent by hand). `204`, cleaned and cut to bounds; unknown fields dropped. `400 { error: 'bad report', detail }` naming the first wrong field; `415`, `403`, `413` as every POST; `429 { error: 'too many reports', retryAfter }` with `Retry-After` past `REPORT_LIMITS` (30 per address per 10 minutes; nothing shared); `503 { error: 'not kept' }` when the database refuses. No session read, no cookie, no address kept. Other methods: the API's `404`. |

**Session auth** is the `animath_session` cookie plus `x-animath-account`, the account the page plays (its name, URI-encoded, keyed by the server with `nameKey` on its own Unicode tables), checked against the session: the cookie names whichever account the browser logged in to last, in any tab. No live session → `401 { error: 'not logged in' }`; no header or not a name → `400`; another account's session → `401 { error: 'not logged in' }` too. None of these sends a cookie.

**`SaveV5`** (engine `src/save.ts`) is a versioned envelope, stored and returned as the engine reads it:

```ts
{
	version: 5,
	home: number,                  // the world the game began in, 1–9999
	world: number,                 // the world the player is in, 1–9999: its seed is landSeed(land, world)
	land?: string,                 // the land the player is in (written in every save; none is Nordland): a land this build lacks makes the save newer
	pos: { x: number, y: number },  // integer tile coordinates in `world`, any sign
	party: AnimalInstance[],       // any number, in species bundles, of { id: string, speciesId: string, nickname?: string, hp: number }
	name?: string,                 // 1–40 UTF-16 units; the player's name once chosen (checkName judges it on load)
	// Required in a write (`SaveWrite`):
	facing?: 'up' | 'down' | 'left' | 'right',
	steps?: number,                // whole; the authority's step count, in every world, which keys every encounter
	visits?: number,               // whole; doctor visits opened, which with steps key each visit
	lineage?: string,              // 1–64 characters; the game this save belongs to
	seq?: number,                  // whole ≥ 1; this save's number within its game
	// Optional in a write too.
	tokens?: number,               // whole; the tokens the druid gave, less what the shop took
	items?: string[],              // ids of 1–64 characters; the tools owned (an id this build doesn't know is kept, and does nothing)
	solved?: number,               // whole; the puzzles the player has solved
	puzzles?: { [topic: string]: { tried: number, right: number, recent: string } }, // the puzzle record, written once a puzzle was judged: whole counts, right ≤ tried, recent up to 20 of "0"/"1" (no more than tried, no more ones than right); a topic this build lacks is kept
	seen?: string[],               // the animal book: the species met, by id, in the order first met
	caught?: string[],             // the animal book: the species caught, by id, in the order first caught
	freed?: string[],              // the animal book: the species set free at the druid's, by id, in the order first set free
	edits?: string[],              // the tiles cleared in `world`: one "cx,cy:" entry per chunk and its tile indices in hex ("-1,0:0a91"); canonical
	worlds?: { world: number, pos: { x, y }, facing, edits?: string[] }[], // the worlds left behind, most recently left first, each once; all edits together within EDITS_BUDGET (24,000 characters)
	battle?: unknown,              // the battle in progress (a BattleState) in `world`, checked on load by readBattle
	// What above is the land's (pos, facing, edits, worlds, party, tokens, items, battle) is `land`'s.
	lands?: { land: string, party: AnimalInstance[], tokens?: number, items?: string[], worlds?: …[] }[], // the lands left behind, most recently left first, each once and never `land`; written once there is one
	unlocked?: string[]            // the lands unlocked, once one past Nordland is; an id this build lacks is kept
}
```

`version`, `home`, `world`, `pos` and `party` are validated strictly (`speciesId` shaped as a catalog id, `hp` whole ≥ 0, `id` unique in the party, worlds 1–9999; a species in the party or book, or a battle's realm, species or puzzle kind, that the catalogs lack makes it a newer build's, `readSave`'s `newer`, while an unknown item is kept and does nothing); `name`, `facing`, `steps`, `visits`, `lineage`, `seq`, `tokens`, `items`, `solved`, `puzzles` (`recordError`), `seen`, `caught`, `freed` (species-shaped ids), `edits` (`isEditsText`) and `worlds` (each once, number, position, facing, edits) when present; a write must carry the five named above. `battle` need only be storable: the client checks it on load (`readBattle`) and drops it if it cannot be picked up. Any extra field, top-level or per animal, is stored and returned as sent, so a newer client can add data without a server change; bump `version` when an old document becomes unreadable, or when an older build must not carry on with a save it cannot fully read ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves). A `PUT` of an older version is read through the upgrade (`validateSaveWrite`) and stored upgraded. "As sent" is JSON semantics: key order and `-0` are not kept, and a document with a NUL, a lone surrogate or a number overflowing to Infinity is a `400`, not a mangled row; so is one nested deeper than `MAX_SAVE_DEPTH` (64; a game's save is 6), refused before anything walks it ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server).

**The write guard** (`canReplace`, `replacesAnotherGame`, `writeAccountSave`). A `PUT` lands only when its `seq` is higher than the stored save's (`saveSeq`: its `seq`, or 0 when absent or unreadable), and never over a newer build's save (`isNewerSave`), whatever its `seq`; otherwise `409` with the stored save. Within a game that orders saves however the network delivers them, and the browser's compare-before-write keeps a game's saves in one line (§ Saving). Between two lineages the one saved more often wins. Before a write replaces a save of another lineage, one `readSave` cannot read, or an older build's (the first save after an update), the old document goes to `account_save_backups` as it was. Read, decision and write run in one transaction with the account's row locked, so of racing writes one lands and the rest see it. Set-asides are kept newest first within `ACCOUNT_BACKUP_BYTES` (2 MiB as Postgres stores them, always the newest): hundreds of few-kilobyte games, two of a megabyte, so no account can fill the disk.

**The retired anonymous backup** (`/api/players`, a guest's save under an id and secret the browser held) ran in development only, until the human's kid's game moved to production; its routes are gone, its tables stay (§ Data model, [[DECISIONS]] § Saves).

**Accounts.** A session is a random 32-byte token in the cookie `animath_session` (HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, `Path=/`, a year's `Max-Age`; Secure in production); the server stores only its SHA-256. Only login, register, the welcome POST, `/me` and a logout naming no account send the cookie, and only that logout clears it (the game's logout names its account and sends none: the ended session leaves a cookie that opens nothing). `/me` moves a session's end a year out once 30 days into its year (`SLIDE_AFTER_DAYS`), resending the cookie; the save routes and the WebSocket upgrade read the session and never answer with a cookie ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server). Every POST and PUT under `/api/account` must declare a JSON body (else `415 { error: 'send JSON' }`) and, with an `Origin`, come from this site (else `403 { error: 'wrong origin' }`): the `Origin`'s host and port must be the request's `Host` or a proxy's `X-Forwarded-Host` (so the dev proxy keeps the browser's `Host`, `vite.config.ts`); a `Host` without a port, as nginx's `$host` passes it, is compared by name. Rate limits in memory (`ACCOUNT_LIMITS`): per address every login try and every registration; per name failed logins from one address, failed logins from all addresses together (a higher bar), counted before the password is checked (so racing tries cannot slip under) and refunded when right, and registrations; per account save PUTs, checked before the body is read. A welcome link's GET and POST count together per address at the login's numbers (`welcomePerIp`). Past a limit, `429 { error: 'too many tries', retryAfter }` with `Retry-After`. The address is the socket's, or behind our proxy (a loopback or private peer) the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry; IPv6 counts by /64 (`rateKey`).

### Presence

Who is in which world, where, and what they are doing ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer): the server's to decide, in memory only. A restart forgets everyone, and every page says where it is again as its socket comes back.

```
 page (PresenceController)          /api/ws (presence/socket.ts)            PresenceHub
 hello {v, guest, name} ──────────▶ version? origin? account? name rules ─▶ join: one per identity
   ◀── hi {v, pid, name, match, boot}  /  refresh {v}  /  bye {reason}
 where {world, land, x, y, facing, lead, boat, harness?, busy} ──────────▶ into that land's room of the world;
   ◀── peer {pid, name, …} / gone {pid}   (the players near, both ways)       sight both ways
   ◀── roster {world, land, players}      (the 50 nearest others in the room, roughly, every 2 s if it changed)
 find {pid} ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────▶ found {pid, x, y} / lost {pid}
 battle {view, events}   (its own, while its where says battle) ─────────▶ kept on the player, passed on
   ◀── fight {pid, vs, view, events}       (a battle near you: someone's with a wild animal, or a match)
```

- **One socket per page**, at `/api/ws` on the game's own origin (Vite proxies it with `/api` in development), opened once a game is under way and closed on the title; never in a throwaway game.
- **Who.** An account holder by their session cookie, looked up once as the socket opens (`AccountOf`, `sessionUserFromCookieHeader`), shown by their username; anyone else by their hello's guest id, shown by its name once the name rules pass it. Nobody sees another's guest id: others know a socket by its public id, a keyed hash of the identity, the same on every server copy and across restarts, so a returning page is the same player to everyone. A second socket of the same identity replaces the first, which is told `bye: replaced` and closed (its page says the player went on in another window).
- **Rooms are lands of worlds.** A socket is in the room of the land and world its last `where` named (`placeKey`: `nordland:42`), or none; a roster names both, and a page reads only its own. Two players in a room see each other's every change within `VIEW_RADIUS`, and keep seeing each other until past `VIEW_KEEP`: a `peer` when they come near and on every change, a `gone` when they part, change world or leave. Everyone else in the room is on the roster, nearest first, with a rough bearing and steps.
- **Go to.** `find` answers exactly where a player in the asker's world stands; the page's authority then places the player beside them (`go-to`, `arrivalSpot`).
- **Battles seen from outside.** A page in a wild battle says so in its `where`, then reports each step (`battle`: the `FightView` after it and its `FightEvent`s, `net/fight.ts`), at most a few a second (`battleRatePerSecond`). The hub takes it only while the last `where` says `battle`, keeps the view on the player and sends it to all who see them (`fight`); one who comes near later gets the view as it stands, no events, right after the player's `peer`. A match's steps come from `matches.ts`, built from the server's own state, once to each who sees either player, never to the two. A battle's view goes when it ends, when its page does anything else, or when it leaves the world; nothing survives a restart (the page retells it to the next server). Only numbers and species cross, never a nickname: a puzzle travels as numbers and the watching page writes it with `facePrompt`, so no word or answer reaches another page.
- **Guards.** Every message goes through the engine's parsers; over 4 KiB closes the socket; 10 messages a second on average (20 at once), the rest dropped, and a socket that keeps flooding (over 40 dropped in ten seconds) is closed, as is one sending 10 non-messages, or no hello within 10 s. An `Origin` not the request's own host and port (or the host a proxy says) is refused; only on this machine's own name with no port (`localhost`, a loopback address: the local stack, whose nginx passes `$host` without the port) is the name enough (#164). At most 1,000 sockets, 40 from one address (the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry our proxy saw; IPv6 by /64; behind a proxy setting none, such as a bare tunnel, everyone is one address), 200 players in a land of a world; pings every 20 s, dropping a socket that missed the last; cuts off one that stopped reading (256 KiB queued, `maxBuffered`).
- **Versions.** A hello of another protocol version gets `refresh` and is closed; a page older than the server reloads once at a calm moment after saving; a newer one waits a minute and retries.
- **Deploys.** On SIGTERM every socket is told `bye: restart`, closed with its own code (`byeCloseCode`, 4005), and no new socket is taken. Such a page comes straight back (a quarter of a second, not a failure) to the copy taking over ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § What `deploy.sh` does); the others on its screen and list are held meanwhile (`HOLD_SECONDS`), and the new copy knows every player by the same public id, so they are drawn where they stood: no one twice, no poof, no note. While a deploy runs two copies, each has the players whose sockets it holds.

### Friendly matches

A match is the server's (`presence/matches.ts`), on the presence socket ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). The page asks, answers and plays by message; the server judges every rule it can with the engine and keeps the rest (who is asking, who is in a match) in memory, like presence.

```
 Ada's page                         server (Matches)                          Bo's page
 challenge {pid, team} ───────────▶ challengeRefusal on both last `where`s,
   ◀── asking {pid, ms}              one invite each, the waits ─────────────▶ invite {pid, name, ms}
                                    ◀──────────────────────────────────────── accept {pid, team} / decline {pid}
 withdraw ────────────────────────▶ or no match after all (a No, 20 s, a withdraw, out of reach, busy…):
   ◀── uninvite {pid, reason}        each side told why (`INVITE_ENDS`) ─────▶ uninvite {pid, reason}
                                    matchTeam ×2, startMatch(crypto seed)
   ◀── match {id, view(a), events}  ──────────────────────────────────────▶ match {id, view(b), events}
 play {id, intent} ───────────────▶ applyMatchIntent → both views + events ─▶ (a refusal: `rejected`, to Ada alone)
 here {id} (keys on her turn)       turn clock: nudge 90 s, timeout 3 min
   ◀── nudge {id} ("Still there?")
                                    a socket closed: away 30 s, then timeout; back in time: hi {match} + match
 rematch {id, team} ──────────────▶ both said yes: a new match               ◀── rematch-wish {side, yes}
 done {id} (Back to exploring) ───▶ the rematch is off for both
   (again after a hi that still names the match: it went while the socket was away)
   (after a rematch Bo's Rematch? started: called off ── match {calledOff} ──▶ his result back up)
 play {id, leave} ────────────────▶ the leaver is free at once, with no rematch
```

- **Who, where.** A player is known by identity (a guest id or an account), so a second window or a reloaded page is the same player in the same match. Positions and busy states come from presence (`hub.present`); a page's team goes through `matchTeam`.
- **No answer on the wire.** Every match message is built in one place from `matchView` and the step's events; a refusal is a code. `match-socket.test.ts` reads the pages' frames.
- **Stopping.** `restart()` (SIGTERM) stops the matches before telling sockets to come back: a match does not survive a server, and the pages say the game is updating ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). A server that stops without a word tells nobody: a page back to another run (`hi.boot`) than its match began on shows the same restarted card; back to the same run with no match, the match ended while it was away.
- **Busy.** Only a live match keeps its players busy for an invite, and a finished one a page has yet to put up (its `where` has not said `match` since it was sent: a hidden tab, a page just back). An ended match lingers for its rematch (`lingerMs`) only for pages still on its result; its leaver is let go at once, and a player asking or asked for another lets go of theirs (#139).

## Production

The game runs on its own Hetzner Cloud VPS (`mathgame-prod`, a cx23 in nbg1, Ubuntu 24.04, provisioned by `terraform/`), set up as lawcel's is. One Docker Compose project, `mathgame-prod` (`docker-compose.prod.yml`), runs in `~/mathgame`, a checkout of this repo that every deploy resets to the commit it deploys.

```
browser ──443──▶ nginx ──▶ app:3000 ──▶ postgres:5432
        ──80───▶ (ACME challenge, else → 443)    ▲
                                       backup ───┘ every 6 h → ~/mathgame/backups/
                                                 → every 6 h (launchd) → ~/mathgame-backups/ on the Mac
```

| Service    | Image                                            | Holds                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `app` | `ghcr.io/ulfaslak/mathgame:prod`, the `Dockerfile` | The server bundle (`dist/index.mjs`) and built client (`../client/dist`), plain `node` as the `node` user, tini as PID 1 (`init: true`). Env from `.env.production`. Not published: nginx reaches it as `app` on the compose network. |
| `postgres` | `postgres:18-alpine` | The database, in the volume `mathgame-prod_pgdata` at `/var/lib/postgresql`: postgres 18 keeps its cluster under it (`18/docker`); a mount at the old `…/data` path would leave the cluster in an anonymous volume. Not published. |
| `nginx` | `nginx:1.29.8` | The only published ports, 80 and 443. Let's Encrypt certificates by its own ACME module over HTTP-01, in the volume `mathgame-prod_acme_data`. |
| `backup` | `postgres:18-alpine` | `scripts/backup.sh`: a `pg_dump` at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC, read back whole before it counts, into `~/mathgame/backups/`, 30 days kept. |

**The domain** is one value, `MATHGAME_DOMAIN` in `deploy.env` (`animath.xyz`): nginx's server name and certificate, the deploy's health check, the offsite sync's host, and in the client the link preview's URLs and where an old address points (`VITE_GAME_DOMAIN`, `moved.ts`; passed to the image build). It has an A record only, `www` a CNAME to it: behind Docker's port proxy every IPv6 visitor would reach nginx from one address, collapsing per-address limits (nginx's, the accounts'). `www.<domain>` only redirects to the domain, never serves the game: a kid's game is saved under the page's address, so a second address would be a second, empty game.

**nginx** is the `nginx/` folder, mounted whole. At every start the image renders `nginx.conf.template` into `/etc/nginx/nginx.conf` with the domain: HTTP → HTTPS, the ACME issuer, the TLS server with HSTS, and a TLS server for `www.<domain>` with its own certificate answering every request with a 301 to the domain. `http.conf` and `app.conf` are shared with the local stack's `local/nginx.conf.template` (plain HTTP): Docker's DNS asked every 5 s with the app's address in a variable, so a replaced container is found without a reload; a short `proxy_connect_timeout` before nginx tries the app's other address, since a departed container's address answers nothing ([[INVARIANTS]] § Serving has its length); logs that name no client (an access log without addresses, giving the app addresses each request tried and their answers, a welcome token `[hidden]` by a `map` over the request line; an error log writing request and connection lines only at `emerg`, nginx's own (start, reload, certificates) at `notice`: [[DECISIONS]] § Deployment); gzip; a 2 MiB body limit, above the app's 1 MiB save; a flood guard on every POST per address, 60 at once then one a second, well above the account limits and the three error reports a tab sends an hour (keyed on the method, since nginx and the app spell paths differently; [[DEFERRED]] "Error reports share nginx's guard on POSTs with logging in"); security headers; `/api/` passing WebSocket upgrades with an hour's read timeout; `/immutable/` and `/assets/` asking the other app on a 404 (during a swap: [[DEVELOPMENT]] § What `deploy.sh` does); and `502.html`, shown in the kid's language while the app does not answer.

**WebSockets** belong under `/api/` (`/api/ws`): only there does nginx pass an upgrade and keep a quiet socket for an hour. `/ws` is a 404.

How a deploy, the backups and the health watch run: [[DEVELOPMENT]] § Deployment.

**Images.** CI pushes every build to GHCR as `:<commit sha>` and `:prod`; the server keeps the running image, `:prod` and three more for a quick rollback. It holds no registry credential: each deploy logs in with the workflow's token.
