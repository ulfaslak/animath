# Architecture

## Shape

A pnpm workspace with three packages and one rule about the arrows between them:

```
packages/engine   ← pure game rules. Imports nothing.
      ▲      ▲
      │      │
packages/client   packages/server
(Vite, Three.js,  (Hono, ws, Drizzle,
 Svelte overlay)   Postgres)
```

The engine knows the rules of the game and nothing about screens, sockets or databases. The client renders and collects input. The server persists and, later, arbitrates what two players share ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). Client and server may both import the engine; neither imports the other, and the engine imports neither. Anything that decides an outcome — damage, a catch, an answer's correctness, whether a tile is walkable — is engine code.

To see the current tree:

```bash
tree packages -I 'node_modules|dist' --dirsfirst
```

### The authority seam (how multiplayer slots in)

```
   UI / renderer            Authority                 engine
 ┌──────────────┐  intent  ┌──────────────┐  calls  ┌──────────┐
 │ keyboard,    │ ───────▶ │ validate,    │ ──────▶ │ rules,   │
 │ Svelte panel │          │ apply, emit  │ ◀────── │ formulas │
 │ Three scene  │ ◀─────── │              │         └──────────┘
 └──────────────┘  event   └──────────────┘
```

`Authority` (`protocol.ts`) is `dispatch(intent)` + `subscribe(listener)`. The client has exactly one authority instance and everything above it is a consumer of events.

- **Today**: `packages/client/src/authority/local.ts` — `LocalAuthority` runs the engine in-process. Its own randomness (the encounter roll after each step, a battle's seed, a doctor visit's seed) is keyed by the world seed and its count of completed steps (and of visits), never by `Math.random`; see [[INVARIANTS]] § Authority. After a battle it emits `party-changed` (the whole party, HP written back, a caught animal added), or after a lost one `taken-to-doctor` (see § Modes); after each heal or hand-over at the doctor, `party-changed` again, and `belongings-changed` (the tokens and items, whole) after tokens are given or a tool is bought. Every battle and doctor step goes through the engine's `countSolved`, and one that judged an answer right is followed at once by `solved-changed` (the whole count of puzzles solved). A friendly match's events, which the server judges, are to come in through `countMatchAnswers(events, side)`, the hook the match screen will call with each batch once: this player's own right answers count, and nothing else in the game changes. Nothing calls it until the match screen lands. A `party` intent goes to `applyPartyIntent` with what the player is doing (`battle` while one is in progress, `doctor` during a visit, else `explore`) and, while a game is under way, is always answered with `party-edited`: facts only. An `interact` that is not at a tent goes to the engine's `clearTile` with the tile in front: a tree or a rock the player has the tool for is cleared (`tile-cleared`), one they don't answers `tool-needed`, anything else `nothing-to-interact`; the authority keeps the overlay (`WorldEdits`), walks and knocks out through it, and saves it with the game. The party it starts with passes through `normalizeNickname`, as a rename does. It keeps the player's name (`choose-name`, in any mode while a game is under way, through `checkName`: `name-chosen` or `name-refused`), the home world and the world the player is in, and the worlds left behind; `travel`, only while exploring, goes through the engine's `travel` and answers `travelled` (a new seed, spawn, position, facing and overlay; the party, tokens, items, puzzles solved, name and counts go along) or `travel-refused`; after a clear, `fitWorlds` keeps the worlds left behind within what the budget leaves. The whole game fits in a `SavedGame`: `snapshot()` takes one at any moment, a battle in progress included (its state, never its seed, which is derived again from the step count), and `start({ game })` picks one up, emitting `welcome` and then, for a saved battle, `battle-started`. A doctor visit is not part of it: a reload closes the visit, keeping what it healed. The client saves the game (§ Saving).
- **The title**: until a game is under way (at load, and after `leave-game`) the authority takes one intent, `new-game { speciesId, nickname?, name? }`: the engine's `chooseStarter` checks the starter and `checkName` the name (`not-a-name`), the starter gets a minted id, the game gets a home world picked at random from 2 to 9999 (`homeWorld` in the options pins it; a throwaway game is in World 1), and the answer is `welcome` with `newGame: true` (a game picked up has `newGame: false`), or `new-game-refused { reason }`. Mid-game it is refused (`game-in-progress`). `leave-game` (Quit to title) is accepted only while exploring (in a battle or at the doctor it does nothing), answers `game-left` and stops the game where it stands: `snapshot()` still holds it. Continue is the host's call, `start({ game })` with the autosave's `resumable()`, because the save is the client's.
- **Words**: events carry what happened, and the client chooses the words for its own screens — the battle narration, everything the doctor says, the line after a lost battle, who goes first after a party edit (from `party-edited`), the hint for Enter away from a tent (from `nothing-to-interact`, which carries no words). The authority's own line, a battle's closing line, is a `message` event carrying a `Line` from the engine's `LINES`: a copy key and the wild animal by id, never a sentence ([[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages).
- **Multiplayer**: every game rule still runs in the browser's `LocalAuthority`, and the server decides no game outcome ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). What it decides is presence, who is where (§ `packages/server`, Presence): the page tells it where the player is and what they are doing, and it tells the page who else is near (`presence/`). The one intent fed by the server's word is `go-to { near }`: the presence server says where a friend stands, and the authority puts the player beside them (`arrivalSpot`). Which rules the server will decide next is [[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer.

Two things keep a rule cheap to move to the server: intents carry only what the player *chose* (a direction, an attack index, an answer string), never a computed outcome; and events describe what *happened* in enough detail to render without re-running the rules.

### Copy

```
 src/copy/en.yaml ┐  yaml() at build time          ┌ language.current ($state)
 src/copy/da.yaml ┴─────────────▶ plain objects ───┤ t('key', params) ─▶ Svelte markup
                                                   └ language.onChange ─▶ code outside Svelte

 engine event ─▶ Line { key, params: numbers, animals, attacks } ─▶ words(line) at draw time
```

- **Where the words live.** Every player-facing line is in `src/copy/<code>.yaml`, nested by screen (`battle.*`, `doctor.*`, `pause.*`, `explore.*`, …), and every species under its engine id: six forms (`name`, `a`, `the`, `wild`, `aWild`, `theWild` — "en vild ræv", "et vildt egern") and its attacks' names. Only the client reads the files. Both languages ship in the main bundle.
- **Loading.** `yaml()` in `vite.config.ts` parses a file when Vite builds, serves or tests it; the bundle holds its data and no YAML parser. A syntax error or a repeated key fails the build.
- **The language on screen.** `language.current` starts from `?lang=` (for that visit only), else the choice remembered on this device (`localStorage['animath.language']`), else the first of `navigator.languages` the game speaks (`da-DK` counts as `da`), else English. The pause menu's Language row calls `language.set()`, which switches, remembers (storage failures are ignored), sets `<html lang>` and calls the `onChange` listeners.
- **`t()` and lines.** `t()` reads `language.current` on every call, so Svelte markup that calls it re-renders the moment the language changes. What the screen says is kept as data until drawn — a `Line` (`lines.ts`: a key, and numbers, animals and attacks by id) for the battle narration and the message line, a `DoctorLine` for the doctor — and `words(line)` words it in the markup. A sentence stored as a string would stay in the language it was made in. Code outside Svelte (the Three.js layer, if it ever draws words) calls `t()` when it draws and redraws on `language.onChange`.
- **Names.** `names.ts` is the one place that turns an animal or an attack into words: a nickname is shown as stored and stands for every form; otherwise the species' forms come from the copy files. A sentence picks the form it needs (`{animal.theWild}`), and a form that starts a sentence gets a capital letter; a nickname is never changed.
- **Gaps.** A key missing from Danish shows in English; a key missing from English shows as the key. In development each gap logs one `console.warn`, which makes `scripts/screenshot.mjs` exit non-zero; production is silent. `copy-files.test.ts` fails on both.
- **Dev server.** An edited copy file swaps the words in place: `language.svelte.ts` accepts the hot update of `languages.ts`, so the game on screen and the `language` store survive it.
- **The engine emits no words** ([[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages). Species and attacks are ids; events say what happened; refusals are codes (`BattleRejection`, `DoctorRejection`, `PartyRejection`); a `message` is a `Line` from `LINES`, typed so no string can ride along. Puzzle prompts are maths notation (`7 × 8 = ?`) and need no words. `no-words.test.ts` and the sweeps' `wordedStrings` check keep it so.

### Pointer input

```
 tap on a button (taps.ts)  ─▶ press(key) ─▶ KeyboardEvent on window ─▶ main.ts ─▶ the screen that is up ─▶ its key handler
 number pad finger down     ─▶ press(key)                                          (explore: keyboard.keydown)
 D-pad finger down / up     ─▶ hold / release of an arrow key
```

- **One path.** A click or a tap never reaches the authority, a view or a controller by a way of its own: the overlay's buttons send key presses (`input/press.ts`), which `main.ts` routes to the screen that is up exactly as a keyboard's (explore's `Keyboard` included, through `keydown`). So every guard a screen keeps on its keys (auto-repeat, busy, the quiet moment before a new choice takes a pick) holds for taps, and what a press means is the screen's key handler's to say, in one place. Components stay readers of their views, as before; a press is not a dispatch.
- **A tap** (`input/taps.ts`) is a pointer that goes down on a button (`data-press` names its key) and comes up on the same button, or hardly moved, on the same screen (`main.ts` counts every change of what takes keys, one animal's options in the pause menu apart from another's), followed by its `pointerId` so each finger is on its own; the key is pressed as it lifts, if the button still names it, however long the pointer rested. A press that lands close to the last tap, soon after it, on a button with another key, while the tapped button has left the spot it was tapped on (a re-sort, a closed screen), presses nothing: the second half of a double click never lands on what slid into the first half's place. The browser's `click` is not used for a pointer: it comes only once every finger has lifted (Talk and Menu under a thumb on the D-pad would never press), and it lands on whatever is under a finger lifted after the screen changed (the battle row under a thumb held on the D-pad into a battle). A click without a pointer (`detail` 0, a screen reader) still presses. The number pad presses as the finger lands, and the D-pad holds and lets go by its own pointer.
- **The pointer's own keys** name what a pointer touched and a keyboard can't: `row:<i>`, `option:<i>` (the pause menu's options, and the doctor's confirm, each beside its rows; why a list of its own: [[INVARIANTS]] § Input), `level:<n>`, `language:<code>`, `tab:<id>` (the doctor's tabs), and the explore HUD's party column, which names a card by its species, never its place, since a drag can re-sort the column under a resting finger: `bundle:<species>` (a card of one animal: it goes first), `open:<species>` (a card of several: open or close it), `animal:<id>` (an animal on an open card: it goes first), `move:<species>:<to>` (a card dropped at place `to`; the HUD presses it itself on the drop, and gives the carried card another key from the moment it is carried until then, so the drag is never also a tap; a card only lifted, never moved, keeps its key and taps). A key handler reads them with `tappedRow` and its siblings beside the keys it knows. A row key on the battle's menus only moves the cursor (`menu.ts`), since a pick there spends the turn; elsewhere it moves the cursor and then runs the handler's own Enter.
- **Touch controls** are drawing only: `touch.on` decides whether the D-pad, a puzzle's number pad, the tap hints and the finger-sized rows show (the Worlds screen's pad shows either way: it is that screen's own way in) (`:root.touch` in the CSS), never what a press does.
- The pretend key events are untrusted (`isTrusted` false), and `watchInput` ignores them: the page's own presses never switch the touch controls off. They carry no `code`, so the `Keyboard` files a D-pad's held arrow under its key name.

### Sound

```
 battle / doctor / pause / title     ┐                          ┌ master (MASTER_VOLUME)
 controllers, hud (a new lead)       ┴─ sfx.play(cue, opts) ───┤   → limiter → speakers
 cues.ts (data) ─ scheduleCue ─────────────── WebAudio nodes ───┘
```

- **Where a cue plays.** From the screens, where events already become visuals: a beat of the battle or the doctor plays its cue as it runs, a key that moves a cursor or picks something plays its blip, the HUD dings when a party edit makes a new lead. The authority and the engine never hear of sound ([[DECISIONS]] § Client). The cue table is [[UI_SPEC]] § Sound and juice.
- **When sound can start.** `main.ts` calls `sfx.unlock()` in the capture phase of every `keydown`, `pointerdown`, `pointerup` and `touchend`, before any screen sees the key, so the key that opens a menu can already blip (a finger's tap presses as it lifts, the moment a browser counts a touch as the player's). `unlock` makes the `AudioContext` only on a press the browser counts as the player's (`navigator.userActivation.isActive`): Chrome does not count Escape or the modifier keys, and a context made on one of them logs a warning (which would fail `scripts/screenshot.mjs`).
- **The setting.** `sfx.on`, remembered in `localStorage['animath.sound']` (`'on'` / `'off'`; storage failures ignored), on by default. M is `main.ts` calling `sfx.flip()`, which plays no cue: the sound chip shows what happened. Off disconnects the master gain (whatever was playing ends unheard) and suspends the context; on makes a fresh master and resumes it.
- **Without WebAudio** or in tests, `play` records the cue (`recent`, `onCue`) and schedules nothing. Tests listen with `sfx.onCue`; nothing in them unlocks a context.

### Coordinate system

Engine grid `(x, y)` maps to Three `(x, height, z)` with `z = y`; grid "down" is screen-down because the camera's yaw is fixed. Each tile is a 1 × 1 column centred on integer `x`, `z`, from `y = −0.5` up to its ground top, which is at `y = 0.5 + 0.25·height` for land and lower for water (`groundTop` in `tiles.ts`). Figures stand with their feet on that top face; the player hops 0.15 during a step. A figure faces `+z` (grid "down", toward the camera) at `rotation.y = 0`; `up` is `π`, `right` is `π/2`, `left` is `-π/2`.

### Modes

Two modes, one on screen at a time. `battle-started` hands the screen to the battle controller: explore input is switched off, the world keeps drawing for a moment so the step into the grass lands while the iris closes on it, then the battle scene and panel replace it under the iris opening. The authority resolves each battle intent at once (`battle-updated`); the controller plays the events back as beats and only then shows the new state, so the screen lags the authority on purpose. `battle-ended` puts the authority back in explore at once, but the screen stays on the result card until the player leaves it; only then does explore input come back. Walking during a battle and battle intents outside one are ignored by the authority; party intents during one are refused by the engine (`not-exploring`), because the battle holds its own copy of the party and writes it back at the end.

The pause menu is not a mode either: an overlay in explore, opened by Escape, that switches explore input off while it is open and closes itself on `battle-started`, `doctor-visit-started`, `welcome`, `game-left` or `travelled`. The authority does not know it is open; it sees only the party intents the menu sends, and `leave-game` from its Start screen row.

A trip to another world is not a mode either: explore's own overlay (`travel/controller.ts`). The menu's Go hands the world to it; it closes a cover on the trainer, while which no screen takes a key (`keyScreen` gives none; M still flips the sound), sends `travel` only once the cover is shut, and opens it on `travelled`. The authority sees one `travel` intent and answers at once, as it does every intent; the transition round it is the client's alone.

The title is before the modes: no game is under way (`game.mode` is `loading` before the first one and `title` after `game-left`), and `title.open` takes every key. Its world is the renderer's, pointed aside and drifting (`lookAt`), with scenery figures that go on `welcome`; its starter stage is drawn instead of the world, as a battle scene is (`setStage`). It closes on `welcome`: Continue's `start({ game })` or a `new-game` the authority accepted.

The doctor is not a mode: a dialogue card over explore, driven by `doctor-visit-started` / `-updated` / `-ended` (the doctor controller plays each update back as beats, like the battle screen). Each doctor event carries its visit's number (a `DoctorState`'s `step` starts at 0 every visit), and the card ignores events of any other visit, as the battle screen ignores other battles'. The world keeps drawing under it; explore input is off while it is open, and the authority ignores walking during a visit and doctor intents outside one. The authority opens a visit on `interact` when `canTalkToDoctor(seed, pos, facing)` holds, so it tracks the facing the client shows: `down` in a new game, then the `dir` of every `move`, walked (`player-moved`) or blocked (`player-blocked`), and the `dir` of `taken-to-doctor`. The facing is saved with the game and `welcome` carries it, so a restored player looks the way they did and both sides agree after any `welcome`. The client computes the "Press Enter to talk to the doctor" prompt with the same engine function from the same events, and "Press Enter to chop the tree" with `clearableAhead`, the check `clearTile` makes. After a lost battle the authority emits `battle-ended` and then `taken-to-doctor`, which puts the player beside the tent without a tween, turns them to it and replaces the party; the battle screen's result card ("Good try!", with no closing line) waits on top meanwhile, and the message line words the doctor's line from `taken-to-doctor`. `player-placed` is a go-to: the player put down beside another player, turned to them, with a poof, no tween. See [[UI_SPEC]].

### Saving

Local first ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves). The pieces:

```
 LocalAuthority ──events──▶ Autosave.handle ──(one per intent)──▶ commit()
       ▲                        │                                   │
       │ start({ game })        │ snapshot()                        ├─▶ localStorage['animath.save']   the save

       └─▶ notice (a copy key for the HUD)                               (background, seq must rise)
```

- **The document.** `SaveV2` (engine `save.ts`), since numbered worlds: `version`, `home`, `world`, `pos`, `party` always; `facing`, `steps`, `visits` (doctor visits opened, which with `steps` key each visit's puzzles), `lineage`, `seq` on every write; `tokens` and `items`; `solved` (the puzzles solved, a whole number, in every write since the count came; a build from before it keeps it as an extra, [[INVARIANTS]] § "A field this build does not know is written back as it was"); `edits` (the tiles cleared with a tool in `world`, `WorldEdits`' canonical text within `EDITS_BUDGET`, written only once something is cleared; `restoreGame` trims one over it round the player, as a clear does); `worlds` (the worlds left behind, the one left most recently first, each `{ world, pos, facing, edits? }`, written only once there is one); `name` (the player's, once chosen); `battle` while one is in progress, always in `world`. `steps`, `visits` and the party belong to the player across worlds; `pos`, `facing` and `edits` to the world they are in. A doctor visit is not saved: a reload closes it, and what it healed, sent home or sold is already in the party, the tokens and the items. `lineage` is a random id minted when a game starts, the same in every save of that game; `seq` numbers the saves of a game, one higher each time. A v1 document (`version`, `seed`, `pos`, `party`, and the same later fields) is read through `SAVE_UPGRADES[1]`: World 1, its home, everything else as it was. Documents lack later fields and `restoreGame` fills them in (facing down, no steps, no visits, no tokens, no items, no puzzle solved, nothing cleared, no world left, no name), and stands the player where they were only if that tile is ground in the world as they left it; `sameProgress` reads a missing `tokens`, `items`, `solved`, `edits` and `worlds` as none, so a save from before the shop, the count, the tools or travel and one that writes none out hold the same progress, and a tile cleared, a world travelled to and a name are progress, never whereabouts.
- **When.** `Autosave.handle` marks the save dirty on every event that changes the game (a step or a bump, a battle start, turn or end, a party change, a doctor visit, a puzzle solved, a tile cleared, which is playing as a catch is) and writes once, in a microtask, after everything one intent caused. `flush()` writes at once on `pagehide` and when the page is hidden. Events emitted before `begin()` are the authority starting from the plan and write nothing. The title writes and sends nothing: saving starts with `begin()` (Continue) or with a new game's `welcome`, and `game-left` writes what is dirty, sends the backup and stops it.
- **A new game** (`welcome` with `newGame: true`, from the title) is a game of its own: a fresh lineage, no extras, its `seq` numbered on past every save the page has seen and past whatever the key holds when it first writes. That first write moves the key's text (the game the kid left, or another tab's later save of it) to the first free `animath.save.previous` slot (up to 200, where the accidental set-asides keep 20), never over one; with none free the key keeps the old game and this one is not saved in the browser. Picking a starter counts as playing, so an unreadable save waiting in the key goes to `animath.save.unreadable` at once. The lineage the kid left is remembered for the page (`putAway`): the server's copy of that game never replaces the new one (saves jump past it, and the server keeps it in `save_backups`), while any other game the server holds is settled by the usual rule, so a game the title never showed is never put away unseen.
- **Continue** starts the authority from `resumable()`: the save the page carries on from as it is now (the one it loaded, last wrote, or took on from another tab's walk while the title was up), not the game the title first showed.
- **Compare before write.** Every tab keeps the exact text of the save it last read or wrote. Before a write it reads the key again: unchanged → write. Changed → another tab saved. If that save differs from this tab's own only in whereabouts (`sameProgress`: position, facing, steps, visits, lineage, seq), this tab carries on from it (its `seq`, its lineage, and its step and visit counts, which `LocalAuthority.catchUp` raises so encounters and doctor puzzles follow on, with the doctor's card open too, and in a battle, which it re-keys from the new count) and writes on top. Otherwise this tab is behind: it stops writing, locally and to the server. The check is not atomic across tabs: a page's view of `localStorage` is brought up to date only between tasks, so two tabs writing in the same instant both pass it, and the first write is lost. A page that finds itself behind while its own last save is neither the save now in the key, nor played on from (the same lineage, a higher `seq`), nor kept aside anywhere, keeps it in `animath.save.replaced` (`keepOwnSave`). So does a page whose `storage` event carries a save of its game numbered no higher than its own last one, and not it: that save went over this page's unseen, even when the key has moved on since (`overwrittenUnseen`). `behind` says why, from the save that put it behind, whichever page notices: `newer` when a newer build wrote it (checked first, and before every write, a new game's included), `window` when that save is still this tab's game (the same lineage) played on elsewhere, `replaced` when another game has taken its place (a tab that took a bigger game from the server into the browser's save, and every other tab of the old game, as after a new game from the title), `gone` when the key has gone. From then on `main.ts` runs no controller and lets no key reach the game; only the browser's own keys pass (with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd, and F1–F12). It cancels `beforeinput` too, so nothing typed with AltGr or Option, pasted or dropped reaches the name box (only an input method's composition cannot be cancelled). Each frame it asks `behindAction` (`save/behind.ts`) what to do. Hidden, the page waits to be shown. Behind another window, it reloads into the newest game when it is in use (visible and focused); on screen while another window is in use, it shows the behind card and waits for the kid. Behind for another reason, it reloads as soon as it is visible. A page reloads itself at most `RELOADS_PER_MINUTE` (3) times a minute, so no bug can trap a kid in a loop of reloads, and past that it shows the card. Enter, Space outside a text box, or the card's button reload whatever the count, because the kid asked, and do not count. A reload that catches up with another window in the middle of a game leaves a note in `sessionStorage` (`animath.caughtUp`), and the reloaded page skips the title: it does Continue at once and says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!" (`save.caughtUp`) instead of "Welcome back!". A page that was on the title reloads into the title. A `storage` event runs the same check at once, against the key as it is, never the value the event carried. A page that may have missed events runs it again (`recheck`): on `pageshow` back from the back/forward cache, on `resume` after the browser froze it, and when its window gains focus. A key that has gone (site data cleared) is never written back.
- **Start** (`boot()`), whose plan the title shows: its game is Continue's. A readable local save → that game, and `welcome back` once Continue picks it up. A newer build's save (`readSave`'s `newer`: a later version, or a species, or a battle's realm or puzzle kind, this build does not have) → nothing starts, not even the title: the page is behind it (`newer`, `frozen`) and reloads for the new version (`save/behind.ts`). An unreadable save → no game; the new game leaves the key alone until the kid has played (a starter picked, a battle ended, the party changed, or an edit to it was accepted; a doctor visit alone or a refused edit is not playing), then moves the old text to `animath.save.unreadable` and writes (`held`). No readable save but an identity → one `GET` (2.5 s at most): a readable backup becomes the game (and the local save at its first write); an unreadable one gives no game, with that notice; a newer one puts the page behind it, as a newer local save does. Otherwise no game and a fresh lineage, until New game.
- **The backup.** A tab that saves locally also sends its latest document: 1 s after something that matters, 15 s after walking, with `keepalive` on `pagehide` and when the page is hidden; one request at a time. No identity yet → `POST /api/players` first (a second tab that made one first is used instead). With an identity from before, the first thing sent is a `GET`, and the save with the higher `seq` carries on: this tab's is sent; the server's (another game played more, or this game's saves that never reached this browser's storage) is adopted — the local one moved to `animath.save.replaced` — and the page reloads into it. An answer that finds the key changed by another tab whose storage event has not come yet carries on from that tab's walk first, and settles again. A tie between two games goes to the server's, and only between saves: at `seq` 0 neither has one, so nothing is adopted. A `409` settles the same way. An unreadable backup is saved past once the kid has played (the next `seq` beats it; the server keeps it), and a browser that found only that at start says its saved game did not load; a newer build's backup is never written over, and the page that finds it, at start or at a `409`, is behind it (`newer`). A `409` from a server holding a lower `seq` than the one it turned away (a server older than the save it holds) is retried as if the server were out of reach, never in a loop of sends and looks. Out of reach, or a `503` (a server older than the save sent): retries at 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 s, then rest, with no timer, until a battle ends or the party changes. A `400`/`413` is a bug: one `console.error`, and no more backups this visit. `401` or `404 no such player`: the identity moves to the first free slot of `animath.player.previous` (`.2`, `.3`, …, never over one kept before) and a new player is made. Every one of these is decided from the API's own JSON answer; a page from anything in between (a tunnel's warning page, a proxy's error or login page) counts as out of reach, whatever its status.
- **Keys** (`save/storage.ts` `KEYS`): `animath.save`, `animath.player` (`{ id, secret }`), `animath.save.unreadable`, `animath.save.replaced`, `animath.save.previous` (a game left for a new one on the title), `animath.save.upgraded` (an older build's save as it was, kept before this build's first write over it), `animath.player.previous`. `animath.save.replaced` also takes a page's own last save when another tab wrote over it in the same instant, unseen (§ Compare before write). A save set aside goes to the first free slot of its key (`animath.save.unreadable`, then `.2`, `.3`, … up to `.20`; the same for `replaced` and `upgraded`) and is never written over; with no slot free, the save it would have made room for stays where it is and this game is not saved in the browser — except an older build's save, which the upgrade has lost nothing of: without room for its copy, the game is saved over it all the same. The language's `animath.language` belongs to the copy module.
- **`?new`, `?party=`, `?zoo`, `?tokens=` and `?shop`**: no store, no server: a throwaway game that skips the title and reads and writes nothing.
- **Accounts** ([[DECISIONS]] § Accounts). The browser plays the guest game or the game of the account it is logged in to (`account/session.ts`, the `animath.account` pointer), each under keys of its own (`KEYS`, `accountKeys`): one `Autosave` per page, built at start with the game's keys and its server. A guest's server is the anonymous backup, in a development build only; an account's is `accountSaveServer`, behind a `SessionCheck` that asks `/api/account/me` once as the page starts (the first request to the account routes, [[INVARIANTS]] § Server) and lets nothing reach the save routes until the server says the cookie is this account's. With a `session` server the autosave keeps no identity; the same rules as the backup settle the two copies (the higher `seq` both ways, a tie between games to the server's, a `409` answered by asking for the save), with two more for a game played on several devices: a tie within the game goes to the server's when the two saves differ, and after a `409` the server's copy is the game even when the page has saved on since, unless it is the page's own save, sent before and not heard back (`sameJson`); and a newer build's save on the server puts the page behind (`newer`), as it does any page (at start, a page holding the account's game asks the server before anything starts, for no longer than start-up waits), while one that fails this build's checks is saved past once the kid has played, as the backup's is, the server keeping it in `account_save_backups`. A `401` (`unknown-player`) means the session ended: pushes stop (`loggedOut`), the game saves on in the browser, and the page says so once. Registering, logging in and logging out move what they must in storage (`session.ts`), then start the page again (`account/restart.ts`); another tab changing the pointer puts this page behind (`fallBehind`). A page opened by a welcome link (`account/welcome.ts`, the token out of the address first) starts on the title with the welcome card over it, whatever else its start would have done; the password picked there logs in as a login does (`takeAccountGame`, the pointer, `restart('welcome')`). `pushNow` sends the newest save before a logout and waits for it, briefly. A guest's play is counted beside the save (`PlayClock`) for the hourly card, which `main.ts` opens while exploring.

### Data model

- `players` — `id uuid pk`, `secret_hash text` (SHA-256 of the client-held secret; the secret itself is never stored), `display_name text?`, `created_at`, `last_seen_at` (bumped on every authenticated request). One row per anonymous player.
- `saves` — `player_id uuid pk → players (cascade)`, `data jsonb` (the `SaveV2` envelope below), `updated_at`. One save per player: the backup of the save that lives in the player's browser.
- `save_backups` — `id bigserial pk`, `player_id uuid → players (cascade)`, `data jsonb`, `reason text` (`replaced`: a different game took its place; `unreadable`: this build could not read it), `created_at`. A save the server would otherwise lose, copied here before a write replaces it. Nothing reads it; it is for recovering a kid's game by hand ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database).
- `users` — `id uuid pk`, `name text` (as the engine's `checkName` gives it: trimmed, NFC; also the character's name), `name_key text unique` (the engine's `nameKey(name)`, computed at registration: a change to `nameKey`'s rules needs a migration that recomputes every row), `password_hash text` (`passwords.ts`), `created_at`. One row per account. The three tables above are the anonymous backup's; nothing here refers to them.
- `sessions` — `token_hash text pk` (SHA-256 of the cookie's token), `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (a year after the last use; a use more than a day into that year moves it). At most 20 per account: a new session clears that account's expired ones and its least recently used past 20.
- `account_saves` — `user_id uuid pk → users (cascade)`, `data jsonb` (the `SaveV2` envelope below), `seq bigint` (the document's own `seq`), `updated_at`. One save per account, written with the same guard as `saves`.
- `account_save_backups` — `save_backups` for accounts: `id bigserial pk`, `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `data jsonb`, `reason text`, `created_at`; newest first within a budget per account (§ HTTP API, the write guard).
- `welcome_tokens` — `token_hash text pk` (SHA-256 of a welcome link's token), `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (14 days on), `used_at` (null until the link logs in). A link to an account `admin import-save` made with no password (`users.password_hash` is `NO_PASSWORD`, `'!'`); it works only while its account has none.

Hot fields get promoted from `data` to columns when a query needs them (nearby players, leaderboards).

### HTTP API

Errors are JSON `{ error: string }`, except the health check's. All routes are under `/api`.

| Route                         | Auth  | Response                                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/health`             | none  | `200 { ok: true, db: true, sha }`, or `503 { ok: true, db: false, sha }` when the database does not answer. `sha` is the commit the running image was built from (`dev` outside an image); each deploy checks prod reports the one it deployed. |
| any other path under `/api`, and `/ws` | none | `404 { error: 'not found' }`: never the game's page. The client counts it as no answer (`offline`), unlike the players routes' own `404`s. |
| `POST /api/players`           | none  | `201 { id: uuid, secret: string }`. The client stores both; the secret is shown once. |
| `GET /api/players/:id/save`   | owner | `200` the save as stored (`SaveV2`, or an older version stored before an update), readable or not, or `404 { error: 'no save yet' }`. |
| `PUT /api/players/:id/save`   | owner | `200 { ok: true }` after the write; `400` bad JSON, or a document `validateSaveWrite` refuses; `409` when the stored save's `seq` is the same or higher, or a newer build wrote it (`{ error: STORED_FROM_NEWER_BUILD }`, whatever the `seq`); `413` body over 1 MiB (`SAVE_MAX_BYTES`); `503 { error: SAVE_FROM_NEWER_BUILD }` for a document a newer build wrote (`validateSaveWrite`'s `newer`: this server is the older one). |
| `POST /api/account/register`  | none  | `201 { user: { name } }` and the session cookie. Body `{ name, password, save? }`: `save` is the guest game the player brings (checked with `validateSaveWrite`, 1 MiB at most), stored as the account's save. `400 { error: 'bad name', reason }` (the engine's `checkName` reason), `400 { error: 'bad password', reason: 'short' \| 'long' }` (the engine's `checkPassword`), `400 { error: 'bad save', detail }`, `409 { error: 'name taken' }` (by `nameKey`), `413`, `503` for a guest game a newer build wrote. Nothing is made unless all of it is. |
| `POST /api/account/login`     | none  | `200 { user: { name } }` and the session cookie; `401 { error: 'wrong name or password' }` for a wrong password and for a name with no account alike, in the same time. |
| `POST /api/account/logout`    | none  | `200 { ok: true }`: the session is deleted and the cookie cleared, with or without one. With `x-animath-account` (as the game sends it), the session ends only when it is that account's, and no cookie is sent: one sent could land after a login another tab makes at the same moment and clear that login's cookie. `400` for a header that is not a name. |
| `GET /api/account/me`         | none  | `200 { user: { name } }`, or `200 { user: null }` when not logged in. |
| `GET /api/account/save`       | session | `200 SaveV2` as stored, or `404 { error: 'no save yet' }`. |
| `PUT /api/account/save`       | session | `200 { ok: true }`; `409 { error, save }` with the stored save when its `seq` is the same or higher (another device got ahead), or a newer build wrote it (whatever the `seq`); `400 { error: 'bad save', detail }`; `413`; `503` for a document a newer build wrote. |
| `GET /api/account/welcome/:token` | none | `200 { name }` for a live link: its account's name, and nothing else; `410 { error: 'link used' }` (used, or its account has a password by now), `410 { error: 'link expired' }`, `404 { error: 'no such link' }`, which name no account. `Cache-Control: no-store`; never a cookie. |
| `POST /api/account/welcome`   | none  | Body `{ token, password }`: `200 { user: { name }, save }` and the session cookie: the password set, the link used, this browser logged in (its old session ended, as at login), `save` the account's save as stored (or null). `400 { error: 'bad password', reason }` leaves the link as it was; `410` and `404` as the GET says; only a live link costs a password hash. |

**Owner auth** is `Authorization: Bearer <secret>`. Missing or malformed header → `401`; `:id` unknown (or not a uuid) → `404 { error: 'no such player' }`; secret does not match the stored hash → `401`. The client tells the two `404`s apart by their `error` text, which `players.test.ts` pins.

**Session auth** is the `animath_session` cookie and `x-animath-account`, the account the page plays (its name, URI-encoded, which the server keys with `nameKey` by its own Unicode tables), checked against the session as the server answers: the cookie names whichever account the browser logged in to last, in any tab. No live session → `401 { error: 'not logged in' }`; no header, or one that is not a name → `400`; a session that is another account's → `401 { error: 'not logged in' }` as well, since this account is not logged in here. None of these answers sends a cookie.

**`SaveV2`** (engine `src/save.ts`) is a versioned envelope, stored and returned as the engine reads it:

```ts
{
	version: 2,
	home: number,                  // the world the game began in, 1–9999
	world: number,                 // the world the player is in, 1–9999: its seed is worldSeed(world)
	pos: { x: number, y: number },  // integer tile coordinates in `world`, any sign
	party: AnimalInstance[],       // any number, in species bundles, of { id: string, speciesId: string, nickname?: string, hp: number }
	name?: string,                 // 1–40 UTF-16 units; the player's name once chosen (checkName judges it on load)
	// Required in a write (`SaveWrite`):
	facing?: 'up' | 'down' | 'left' | 'right',
	steps?: number,                // whole; the authority's step count, in every world, which keys every encounter
	visits?: number,               // whole; doctor visits opened, which with steps key each visit
	lineage?: string,              // 1–64 characters; the game this save belongs to
	seq?: number,                  // whole ≥ 1; this save's number within its game
	// Optional in a write too.
	tokens?: number,               // whole; the tokens the doctor gave, less what the shop took
	items?: string[],              // ids of 1–64 characters; the tools owned (an id this build doesn't know is kept, and does nothing)
	edits?: string[],              // the tiles cleared in `world`: one "cx,cy:" entry per chunk and its tile indices in hex ("-1,0:0a91"); canonical
	worlds?: { world: number, pos: { x, y }, facing, edits?: string[] }[], // the worlds left behind, most recently left first, each once; all edits together within EDITS_BUDGET (24,000 characters)
	battle?: unknown               // the battle in progress (a BattleState) in `world`, checked on load by readBattle
}
```

`version`, `home`, `world`, `pos` and `party` are validated strictly (`speciesId` shaped as a catalog id, `hp` a whole number ≥ 0, `id` unique within the party, a world a number from 1 to 9999; a species, or in a battle a realm, a species or a puzzle kind, that the engine's catalogs lack makes the document a newer build's, `readSave`'s `newer`, while an item they lack is kept and does nothing); `name`, `facing`, `steps`, `visits`, `lineage`, `seq`, `tokens`, `items`, `edits` (`isEditsText`) and `worlds` (each world once, a world number, a position, a facing, edits) when present, and a write must carry the five named above. `battle` only has to be storable: the client checks it when it loads (`readBattle`) and drops it if it cannot be picked up. Any extra field — top-level or per animal — is stored and returned as sent, so a newer client can add data without a server change; bump `version` only when an old document becomes unreadable. A `PUT` of an older version (a page still open from before an update) is read through the upgrade (`validateSaveWrite`) and stored upgraded; a v1 document (`seed` for `home` and `world`) lands in World 1. "As sent" is JSON semantics: key order and `-0` are not preserved, and a document containing a NUL character, a lone surrogate or a number that overflows to Infinity is a `400`, not a mangled row.

**The write guard** (`canReplace`, `replacesAnotherGame`, `writeSave`). A `PUT` lands only when its `seq` is higher than the stored save's (`saveSeq`: its `seq`, or 0 when it has none or cannot be read), and never over a save a newer build wrote (`isNewerSave`), whatever its `seq`; otherwise `409`. Within one game that puts backups in order however the network delivers them, and the browser's compare-before-write keeps a game's saves in one line (§ Saving). Between two games (two lineages: one started while the other was out of reach), the one saved more often wins. Before a write replaces a save from a different lineage, one `readSave` cannot read, or one an older build wrote (the first backup after an update), the old document goes to `save_backups` as it was. The read, the decision and the write run in one transaction with the player's row locked, so of racing writes one lands and the rest see it. `writeAccountSave` is the same guard for an account's save, with the account's row locked, its set-asides in `account_save_backups`, and the stored save returned with the `409`. An account keeps its set-asides newest first within `ACCOUNT_BACKUP_BYTES` (2 MiB as Postgres stores them, and always the newest): hundreds of a kid's few-kilobyte games, two of a megabyte, so no account can fill the disk.

**The anonymous backup** (`/api/players`) runs only in development: with `production` every route under it answers `410 { error: 'the backup is off here' }`, and its tables stay as they are ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves).

**Accounts.** A session is a random 32-byte token in the cookie `animath_session` (HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, `Path=/`, a year's `Max-Age`; Secure in production), of which the server stores only the SHA-256. Only login, register, a welcome link's POST, `/me` and a logout that names no account send the cookie, and only that logout clears it (the game's logout names its account and sends none: the session it ends leaves a cookie that opens nothing): `/me` moves a session's end a year out once it is 30 days into its year (`SLIDE_AFTER_DAYS`), sending the cookie again; the save routes and the WebSocket upgrade read the session and never answer with a cookie ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server). Every POST and PUT under `/api/account` must declare a JSON body (else `415 { error: 'send JSON' }`) and, when it carries an `Origin`, come from a page of this site (else `403 { error: 'wrong origin' }`): the `Origin`'s host and port must be the request's `Host`, or a proxy's `X-Forwarded-Host`, which is why the dev proxy keeps the browser's `Host` (`vite.config.ts`); a `Host` without a port, as nginx's `$host` passes it, is compared by name alone. Login, register and the save PUT are rate-limited in memory (`ACCOUNT_LIMITS`), per address: every login try, and every registration; per name: failed logins from one address, failed logins from every address together (a higher bar), counted before the password is checked, so tries racing each other cannot slip under a limit, and taken back when it was right, and registrations; and per account: save PUTs, checked before the body is read. A welcome link's GET and POST count together per address, at the login's numbers (`welcomePerIp`). Past a limit, `429 { error: 'too many tries', retryAfter }` with `Retry-After`. The address is the socket's, or, behind a proxy of ours (a loopback or private peer), the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, and an IPv6 address counts as its /64 (`rateKey`).

### Presence

Who is in which world, where, and what they are doing ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer): the server's to decide, in memory only. A restart forgets everyone, and every page says where it is again as its socket comes back.

```
 page (PresenceController)          /api/ws (presence/socket.ts)            PresenceHub
 hello {v, guest, name} ──────────▶ version? origin? account? name rules ─▶ join: one per identity
   ◀── hi {pid, name}  /  refresh  /  bye {reason}
 where {world, x, y, facing, lead, boat, busy} ──────────────────────────▶ into that world's room;
   ◀── peer {pid, name, …} / gone {pid}   (the players near, both ways)       sight both ways
   ◀── roster {world, players}            (everyone else in the world, roughly, every 2 s if it changed)
 find {pid} ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────▶ found {pid, x, y} / lost {pid}
```

- **One socket per page**, at `/api/ws` on the game's own origin (Vite proxies it with `/api` in development). A page opens it once a game is under way and closes it on the title; a throwaway game never opens it.
- **Who.** An account holder by their session cookie, looked up once as the socket opens (`AccountOf`, the accounts' `sessionUserFromCookieHeader`), and shown by their username; anyone else by the guest id their hello carries, shown by the name it carries once the name rules pass it. Nobody sees another's guest id: everyone else knows a socket by its public id, a keyed hash of who it is, the same on every copy of the server and across restarts, so a page that comes back, to this server or the next, is the same player to everyone. A second socket of the same identity takes the first one's place: the first is told `bye: replaced` and closed, and its page shows that the player went on in another window.
- **Rooms are worlds.** A socket is in the room of the world its last `where` named, or in none. Two players in a room see each other's every change while they are within `VIEW_RADIUS` of each other, and keep seeing each other until past `VIEW_KEEP`: the server sends each a `peer` when they come near and on every change, and a `gone` when they part, go to another world or leave. Everyone else in the room is on the roster, nearest first, with a rough bearing and number of steps.
- **Go to.** `find` answers where a player in the asker's world stands, exactly; the page's authority then puts the player beside them (`go-to`, `arrivalSpot`).
- **Guards.** Every message is read through the engine's parsers; a message over 4 KiB closes the socket; a socket may send 10 messages a second on average (20 at once), the rest are dropped, and one that keeps flooding (40 dropped in ten seconds) is closed, as is one that sends 10 that are no message, or no hello within 10 s. An `Origin` that is not the request's own host (or the host a proxy says it came to) is refused. The server holds 1,000 sockets at most, 40 from one address (the one our proxy saw, the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry; an IPv6 /64 is one address; behind a proxy that sets no `X-Forwarded-For`, such as a bare tunnel, everyone is one address), a world 200 players, pings every socket every 20 s and drops one that did not answer the last ping, and stops writing to one that stopped reading.
- **Versions.** A hello of another protocol version is answered `refresh` and closed; a page older than the server reloads itself once, at a calm moment, after saving; one newer waits a minute and tries again.
- **Deploys.** A server told to stop (SIGTERM) tells every socket `bye: restart`, closed with its own code (`byeCloseCode`, 4005), and takes no new socket. A page told so comes straight back (a quarter of a second, not a failure), which reaches the copy taking over (§ Production); the others on its screen and in its list are held while it does (`HOLD_SECONDS`), and the new copy knows every player by the same public id, so they are drawn on where they stood: no one twice, no poof, no note. For the few seconds a deploy runs two copies, each has the players whose sockets it holds.

## Production

The game runs on its own Hetzner Cloud VPS (`mathgame-prod`, a cx23 in nbg1, Ubuntu 24.04, provisioned by `terraform/`), set up as lawcel's is. One Docker Compose project, `mathgame-prod` (`docker-compose.prod.yml`), runs in `~/mathgame`, a checkout of this repo that every deploy resets to the commit it deploys.

```
browser ──443──▶ nginx ──▶ app:3000 ──▶ postgres:5432
        ──80───▶ (ACME challenge, else → 443)    ▲
                                       backup ───┘ every 6 h → ~/mathgame/backups/
                                                 → every 6 h (launchd) → ~/mathgame-backups/ on the Mac
```

| Service    | Image                                            | Holds                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `app`      | `ghcr.io/ulfaslak/mathgame:prod`, the `Dockerfile` | The server bundle (`dist/index.mjs`) and the built client (`../client/dist`), under plain `node` as the `node` user, with tini as PID 1 (`init: true`). Env from `.env.production`. Not published: nginx reaches it as `app` on the compose network. |
| `postgres` | `postgres:18-alpine`                             | The database, in the named volume `mathgame-prod_pgdata` mounted at `/var/lib/postgresql`: postgres 18 keeps its cluster under it (`18/docker`), and a mount at the old `…/data` path would leave the cluster in an anonymous volume. Not published. |
| `nginx`    | `nginx:1.29.8`                                   | The only published ports, 80 and 443. Certificates from Let's Encrypt by its own ACME module over HTTP-01, kept in the volume `mathgame-prod_acme_data`. |
| `backup`   | `postgres:18-alpine`                             | `scripts/backup.sh`: a `pg_dump` at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC, read back whole before it counts, into `~/mathgame/backups/`, 30 days kept. |

**The domain** is one value, `MATHGAME_DOMAIN` in `deploy.env`: nginx's server name and certificate, the deploy workflow's health check, the offsite sync's host. It has an A record only: behind Docker's port proxy every IPv6 visitor would reach nginx from one address, and a limit per address (nginx's, the accounts') would count them all as one.

**nginx** is the `nginx/` folder, mounted whole. At every start the image renders `nginx.conf.template` into `/etc/nginx/nginx.conf` with the domain: HTTP to HTTPS, the ACME issuer, the TLS server with HSTS. `http.conf` and `app.conf` are what it shares with the local stack's `local/nginx.conf.template`, plain HTTP: Docker's DNS asked every 5 s with the app's address in a variable, so a replaced container is found without a reload; an access log without client addresses, and with a welcome link's token `[hidden]` (a `map` over the request line); gzip; a 2 MiB body limit, above the app's 1 MiB save; a flood guard on every POST per address, 60 at once and then one a second, well above the account routes' own limits (keyed on the method, since nginx and the app read a path's spelling differently); the security headers; `/api/` passing WebSocket upgrades on, with an hour's read timeout; `/immutable/` and `/assets/` asking the other app on a 404 (below); and `502.html`, the page shown in the kid's language while the app does not answer. `scripts/lib/nginx-apply.sh` applies nginx at each deploy: it validates the checkout's config, lets compose recreate the service if its definition changed, then reloads, or recreates when the rendered template changed.

**WebSockets** belong under `/api/` (`/api/ws`): only there does nginx pass an upgrade on and keep a quiet socket for an hour. `/ws` is a 404.

**A deploy** (`scripts/deploy.sh`) migrates the database from the new image first (`dist/migrate.mjs`), while the old app serves. It checks the new image in a container without the alias `app`, which nginx cannot reach: healthy, and serving the game's page from the same build. Docker lists a container under an alias from the moment it starts, so only an image that passed becomes a canary, which joins the network as `app` and takes a share of the requests. Compose then recreates the app with the new image, and the canary goes. For those few seconds two builds answer, and a page from one asks for files only its build has: the other app answers `404`, and nginx asks the one that has the file. A stopped app finishes the requests it is answering first (SIGTERM closes the server, 8 s at most; tini, `init: true`, hands it on), and sends its presence sockets on to the other app (§ Presence › Deploys). Two apps can overlap because the app keeps nothing between requests but what is in Postgres and presence, which the pages put back as they come back ([[DEFERRED]] for a friendly match, which they could not). The deploy never recreates Postgres, and warns when the checkout names another image than the one running (`/redeploy` § Changing Postgres). The backup loop runs a copy of `scripts/backup.sh` taken at its start, which the deploy compares with the checkout's to restart it.

**Images.** CI pushes every build to GHCR as `:<commit sha>` and `:prod`; the server keeps the running image, `:prod` and three more, for a quick rollback. It holds no registry credential: each deploy logs in with the workflow's own token.

**Backups**, in three layers as lawcel's: the `backup` service; the launchd agent `com.mathgame.backup-sync` on the Mac, which pulls the dumps and `.env.production` every 6 hours (`scripts/sync-backups.sh`, installed by `scripts/install-backup-sync.sh`); and Hetzner's nightly image of the whole machine (`backups = true`, 7 kept).
