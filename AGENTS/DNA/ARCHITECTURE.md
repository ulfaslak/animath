# Architecture

## Shape

A pnpm workspace with three packages and one rule about the arrows between them:

```
packages/engine   ← pure game rules. Imports nothing.
      ▲      ▲
      │      │
packages/client   packages/server
(Vite, Three.js,  (Hono, ws, Drizzle,
 Svelte overlay)   Postgres)
```

The engine knows the rules of the game and nothing about screens, sockets or databases. The client renders and collects input. The server keeps accounts and their saves, and the pages' error reports for the admin, and is the authority for what two players share: presence and friendly matches ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). Client and server may both import the engine; neither imports the other, and the engine imports neither. Anything that decides an outcome — damage, a catch, an answer's correctness, whether a tile is walkable — is engine code.

To see the current tree:

```bash
tree packages -I 'node_modules|dist' --dirsfirst
```

### The authority seam (how multiplayer slots in)

```
   UI / renderer            Authority                 engine
 ┌──────────────┐  intent  ┌──────────────┐  calls  ┌──────────┐
 │ keyboard,    │ ───────▶ │ validate,    │ ──────▶ │ rules,   │
 │ Svelte panel │          │ apply, emit  │ ◀────── │ formulas │
 │ Three scene  │ ◀─────── │              │         └──────────┘
 └──────────────┘  event   └──────────────┘
```

`Authority` (`protocol.ts`) is `dispatch(intent)` + `subscribe(listener)`. The client has exactly one authority instance and everything above it is a consumer of events. `LocalAuthority` adds the save's hooks, `start`, `snapshot` and `catchUp`, for `main.ts` and the autosave ([[DECISIONS]] § Engine), and one read-only count, `stepsTaken`, for the save card's nudge.

- **Today**: `packages/client/src/authority/local.ts` — `LocalAuthority` runs the engine in-process. Its own randomness (the encounter roll after each step, a battle's seed, a doctor visit's seed) is keyed by the world seed and its count of completed steps (and of visits), never by `Math.random`; see [[INVARIANTS]] § Authority. After a battle it emits `party-changed` (the whole party, HP written back, a caught animal added; after a lost one, as the engine's `knockOut` leaves it: tired where it was fought, or looked after by a witch doctor who came) and the closing `message`; after each heal or hand-over at the doctor, `party-changed` again, and `belongings-changed` (the tokens and items, whole) after tokens are given or an item is bought. Every battle and doctor step goes through the engine's `countSolved`, and one that judged an answer right is followed at once by `solved-changed` (the whole count of puzzles solved). A battle's start and each of its steps go through the engine's `recordBattle`, and when the animal book grows (a kind met for the first time, a kind caught for the first time) `book-changed` follows at once, both lists whole. A friendly match's events, which the server judges, come in as a `match-answers { match, step, side, events }` intent, which the match screen (`match/controller.ts`) dispatches for each step the server sends: this player's own right answers count, and nothing else in the game changes, the animal book included. Each step counts once: the authority keeps the last step it counted of each match (the view's `step`, which the server raises by one with every intent it accepts), and ignores a step at or below it, and a batch that is not one step (a side other than `a` or `b`, a step that is not a whole number from 1, no match id, events that are not a list without holes of at most `MAX_MATCH_EVENTS` objects with a `type`, or more than one `answer-judged`, or one without a side and a yes-or-no `correct`): so one step adds at most one puzzle solved. A `party` intent goes to `applyPartyIntent` with what the player is doing (`battle` while one is in progress, `doctor` during a visit, `flight` in the air, else `explore`) and where they stand, and, while a game is under way, is always answered with `party-edited`: facts only. An `interact` that is not at a tent goes to the engine's `clearTile` with the tile in front: a tree or a rock the player has the tool for is cleared (`tile-cleared`), one they don't answers `tool-needed`, anything else `nothing-to-interact`; the authority keeps the overlay (`WorldEdits`), walks and knocks out through it, and saves it with the game. The party it starts with passes through `normalizeNickname`, as a rename does. It keeps the player's name (`choose-name`, in any mode while a game is under way, through `checkName`: `name-chosen` or `name-refused`), the home world and the world the player is in, and the worlds left behind; It keeps the land the player is in, the lands left behind and the lands unlocked (`unlockLands`, after every hand-over); a `doctor` intent's `flew` (a fare paid) goes through the engine's `fly` once the visit has ended and answers `travelled` (the land reached), `party-changed`, `belongings-changed` and, with no animal of the land, `starter-wanted`, after which only `pick-starter`, `interact` at the tent and `leave-game` are taken until one is picked. `travel`, only while exploring, goes through the engine's `travel` and answers `travelled` (a new seed, spawn, position, facing and overlay; the party, tokens, items, puzzles solved, animal book, name and counts go along) or `travel-refused`; after a clear, `fitWorlds` keeps the worlds left behind within what the budget leaves. The whole game fits in a `SavedGame`: `snapshot()` takes one at any moment, a battle in progress included (its state, never its seed, which is derived again from the step count), and `start({ game })` picks one up, emitting `welcome` and then, for a saved battle, `battle-started`. A doctor visit is not part of it: a reload closes the visit, keeping what it healed. The client saves the game (§ Saving).
- **The title**: until a game is under way (at load, and after `leave-game`) the authority takes one intent, `new-game { speciesId, nickname?, name? }`: the engine's `chooseStarter` checks the starter and `checkName` the name (`not-a-name`), the starter gets a minted id, the game gets a home world picked at random from 2 to 9999 (`homeWorld` in the options pins it; a throwaway game is in World 1), and the answer is `welcome` with `newGame: true` (a game picked up has `newGame: false`), or `new-game-refused { reason }`. Mid-game it is refused (`game-in-progress`). `leave-game` (Quit to title) is accepted only while exploring (in a battle, at the doctor or in the air it does nothing), answers `game-left` and stops the game where it stands: `snapshot()` still holds it. Continue is the host's call, `start({ game })` with the autosave's `resumable()`, because the save is the client's.
- **Words**: events carry what happened, and the client chooses the words for its own screens — the battle narration, everything the witch doctor says, the line under the message while the team needs the witch doctor, who goes first after a party edit (from `party-edited`), the hint for Enter away from a tent (from `nothing-to-interact`, which carries no words). The authority's own lines, a battle's closing line and the one that says a witch doctor came after a go-to or a trip (`doctor.came`), are `message` events carrying a `Line` from the engine's `LINES`: a copy key, with the wild animal by id when the line names it, never a sentence ([[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages).
- **Multiplayer**: every rule of a player's own game still runs in the browser's `LocalAuthority` (which rules: [[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer); the server is the authority only for what two players share ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). Presence, who is where (§ `packages/server`, Presence): the page tells it where the player is and what they are doing, and it tells the page who else is near (`presence/`); a page in a wild battle reports its own battle, which the server only passes on to the players who see its fighter. Friendly matches (§ `packages/server`, Friendly matches): the server plays them in the engine's match reducer with a seed only it holds (`presence/matches.ts`), and the match screen (`match/controller.ts`) sends the kid's choices and shows the views it is sent over the presence socket, not through `Authority`, which hears of a match only its puzzles solved (`match-answers`). Two intents are fed by the server's word: `go-to { near }` (the presence server says where a friend stands, and the authority puts the player beside them, `arrivalSpot`) and `match-answers` (the match server judged the answers; the authority only counts the player's own right ones, at most one a step). Which rules move to the server next is [[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer.

Two things keep a rule cheap to move to the server: intents carry only what the player *chose* (a direction, an attack index, an answer string), never a computed outcome; and events describe what *happened* in enough detail to render without re-running the rules.

### Component reuse

One `PuzzlePanel` component serves the battle and the witch doctor's card, with one `NumberPad` in it on touch (on the witch doctor's card it also carries a token sum's story over the prompt, and Back), and one helper (`input/answer.ts`) turns keys into the typed answer for both. One `BundleCard` draws a card of the party (one animal, or a stack of its kind) and one `BundleAnimals` a card's animals, wherever the team is shown as cards: the HUD's column and its open card, and the pause menu's card; one `stackSummary` (`hp.ts`) words how a card of several is, on the HUD's card and the pause menu's row alike. The witch doctor's list is not cards: its tabs share one grid of rows, the kinds' rows and check boxes on Set free among them, so it draws its own rows with the same `HpBar`. One `HpBar`, which can write a word in its empty track (the witch doctor's "tired", on Heal and Set free), draws slim without numbers for all of a stack's HP together (`thin`), draws chunky with its numbers big (`thick`: the battle's status boxes) and previews a hit (`preview`: the HP it would leave, a lighter segment), with the colour bands of `hpBand`. Every card — the HUD's, the battle panel's, the witch doctor's, the pause menu, the title's — has one look, from the tokens `--panel-bg`, `--radius` and `--hud-shadow` in `styles.css`, not from a shared component. New UI reuses these before inventing.

**The battle's pieces** take their data as props and assume nothing about a wild opponent, so any fight between two animals can be drawn with them: `StatusBox` (a name and a chunky `HpBar`; the paw of the other side, `opponent`; the turn's glow, `acting`; the preview; a hit's `HitBurst` beside it, on the side it is told), `AttackTile` (a name, a level's word and a `HitBadge`), `MoveButton` (a `MoveIcon` — the leash, switch, run, and a door for a match's Leave — its word, an optional hint in words and colour, greyed with `off`), `LevelPicker` (the three levels, each with its word and badge), `TopicChips` (a puzzle's kinds as their glyphs, `kindGlyph`) and `ActionPreview` (the right card before a pick, from an attack's or a move's words and numbers). `menu.ts` takes the row of moves after the attacks as a list (`WILD_MOVES`: Leash, Switch, Run). **A friendly match's screen is the battle's own** ([[UI_SPEC]] § Friendly matches): `MatchController` fills the same `battle` view, with `battle.vs` (the other player) and `battle.moves` (`MATCH_MOVES`, Switch and Leave, through the same `menuKey`, `actionAt` and `rowOf`), so `BattlePanel` draws both: its two status boxes from the view's teams, `acting` from whose turn it is, the attack tiles, the level picker, the badges and the preview (`attackDamage`, `landHit`) as a wild battle's, the other's animal named with its owner, the door for Leave, a `waiting` screen with the other's puzzle (a `ShownPuzzle`, in `PuzzlePanel`'s `watch`) where the preview would be, and the match's own result (`MatchResult`) and notes (`MatchNotes`). The chunky toy button is a look from tokens, not a component: `--press`, `--accent-edge` and `--edge` in `styles.css`, a darker edge under the face that squashes flat when pressed. A list whose rows line up in columns (the switch list, the witch doctor's list) is a grid whose rows are subgrids, sized by its longest name; its edge columns are never a fixed width, because a subgrid's padding is a margin on the items at its edges, and a fixed-width column can't make room for it (the caret slid into the gap against the name).

A component that shows one thing — an animal's HP bar, its name — is keyed by that thing's id wherever a different thing can take its place on screen, so a transition never runs from one animal's value to another's (a newcomer's bar must not drain from the full bar of the animal that left).

### Copy

```
 src/copy/en.yaml ┐  yaml() at build time          ┌ language.current ($state)
 src/copy/da.yaml ┴─────────────▶ plain objects ───┤ t('key', params) ─▶ Svelte markup
                                                   └ language.onChange ─▶ code outside Svelte

 engine event ─▶ Line { key, params: numbers, animals, attacks, players } ─▶ words(line) at draw time
```

- **Where the words live.** Every player-facing line but what the page says before any script runs (`index.html`: [[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages) is in `src/copy/<code>.yaml`, nested by screen (`battle.*`, `doctor.*`, `pause.*`, `explore.*`, …), and every species under its engine id: seven forms (`ANIMAL_FORMS` in `names.ts`: `name`, `a`, `the`, `wild`, `aWild`, `theWild` — "en vild ræv", "et vildt egern" — and `bare`, the noun after a player's name in a friendly match, "Bo's Rabbit"; a bird also has `aGrumpy`) and its attacks' names. Only the client reads the files. Both languages ship in the main bundle.
- **Loading.** `yaml()` in `vite.config.ts` parses a file when Vite builds, serves or tests it; the bundle holds its data and no YAML parser. A syntax error or a repeated key fails the build.
- **The language on screen.** `language.current` starts from `?lang=` (for that visit only), else the choice remembered on this device (`localStorage['animath.language']`), else the first of `navigator.languages` the game speaks (`da-DK` counts as `da`), else English. The Language row, on the title and in the pause menu, calls `language.set()`, which switches, remembers (storage failures are ignored), sets `<html lang>` and calls the `onChange` listeners.
- **`t()` and lines.** `t()` reads `language.current` on every call, so Svelte markup that calls it re-renders the moment the language changes. What the screen says is kept as data until drawn — a `Line` (`lines.ts`: a key, and numbers, animals and attacks by id, and players by name) for the battle narration, a `Said` (`state/hud.svelte.ts`) for the message line, which keeps an authority's `message` as it came, a `DoctorLine` for the witch doctor's card — and `words(line)`, `saidWords` and `doctorWords` word them in the markup. A sentence stored as a string would stay in the language it was made in. Code outside Svelte (the Three.js layer, if it ever draws words) calls `t()` when it draws and redraws on `language.onChange`.
- **Names.** `names.ts` turns an animal or an attack into words (besides it, only `doctor/lines.ts`'s `namesOf`, a list of animals, and the HUD's line for a bird that follows the glider, `aGrumpy`, read a species' form): a nickname is shown as stored and stands for every form; otherwise the species' forms come from the copy files. A sentence picks the form it needs (`{animal.theWild}`), and a form that starts a sentence gets a capital letter; a nickname is never changed.
- **Gaps.** A key missing from Danish shows in English; a key missing from English shows as the key. In development each gap logs one `console.warn`, which makes `scripts/screenshot.mjs` exit non-zero; production is silent. `copy-files.test.ts` fails on both.
- **Dev server.** An edited copy file swaps the words in place: `language.svelte.ts` accepts the hot update of `languages.ts`, so the game on screen and the `language` store survive it.
- **The engine emits no words** ([[DECISIONS]] § Copy and languages). Species and attacks are ids; events say what happened; refusals are codes (`BattleRejection`, `DoctorRejection`, `PartyRejection`); a `message` is a `Line` from `LINES`, typed so no string can ride along. Puzzle prompts are maths notation (`7 × 8 = ?`) and need no words. `no-words.test.ts` and the sweeps' `wordedStrings` check keep it so.

### Pointer input

```
 tap on a button (taps.ts)  ─▶ press(key) ─▶ KeyboardEvent on window ─▶ main.ts ─▶ the screen that is up ─▶ its key handler
 number pad finger down     ─▶ press(key)                                          (explore: keyboard.keydown)
 D-pad finger down / up     ─▶ hold / release of an arrow key
```

- **One path.** A click or a tap never reaches the authority, a view or a controller by a way of its own: the overlay's buttons send key presses (`input/press.ts`), which `main.ts` routes to the screen that is up exactly as a keyboard's (explore's `Keyboard` included, through `keydown`). So every guard a screen keeps on its keys (auto-repeat, busy, the quiet moment before a new choice takes a pick) holds for taps, and what a press means is the screen's key handler's to say, in one place. Components stay readers of their views, as before; a press is not a dispatch.
- **A tap** (`input/taps.ts`) is a pointer that goes down on a button (`data-press` names its key) and comes up on the same button, or hardly moved, on the same screen (`main.ts` counts every change of what takes keys, one animal's options in the pause menu apart from another's), followed by its `pointerId` so each finger is on its own; the key is pressed as it lifts, if the button still names it, however long the pointer rested. A press that lands close to the last tap, soon after it, on a button with another key, while the tapped button has left the spot it was tapped on (a re-sort, a closed screen), presses nothing: the second half of a double click never lands on what slid into the first half's place. The browser's `click` is not used for a pointer: it comes only once every finger has lifted (Talk and Menu under a thumb on the D-pad would never press), and it lands on whatever is under a finger lifted after the screen changed (the battle row under a thumb held on the D-pad into a battle). A click without a pointer (`detail` 0, a screen reader) still presses. The number pad presses as the finger lands, and the D-pad holds and lets go of an arrow key by its own pointer, as the Fly button does Space.
- **The pointer's own keys** name what a pointer touched and a keyboard can't: `row:<i>`, `option:<i>` (a second list beside the rows: in the pause menu an animal's or a card's options and a card's animals, the Worlds screen's buttons, the animal book's cards and Who's here's players; the witch doctor's confirm; and a friendly match's two buttons on its result and on "Leave the match?"; why a list of its own: [[INVARIANTS]] § Input), `level:<n>`, `language:<code>`, `tab:<id>` (the witch doctor's tabs), `reveal` (the account card's show-password button), and the explore HUD's party column, which names a card by its species, never its place, since a drag can re-sort the column under a resting finger: `bundle:<species>` (a card of one animal: it goes first), `open:<species>` (a card of several: open or close it), `animal:<id>` (an animal on an open card: it goes first), `move:<species>:<to>` (a card dropped at place `to`; the HUD presses it itself on the drop, and gives the carried card another key from the moment it is carried until then, so the drag is never also a tap; a card only lifted, never moved, keeps its key and taps). A key handler reads them with `tappedRow` and its siblings beside the keys it knows. A row key on the battle's menus only moves the cursor (`menu.ts`), since a pick there spends the turn, and one on the title's starters only lights that starter, since a pick there starts a game; elsewhere it moves the cursor and then runs the handler's own Enter.
- **Touch controls** are drawing only: `touch.on` decides whether the D-pad, a puzzle's number pad, the tap hints and the finger-sized rows show (the Worlds screen's pad shows either way: it is that screen's own way in) (`:root.touch` in the CSS), never what a press does.
- The pretend key events are untrusted (`isTrusted` false), and `watchInput` ignores them: the page's own presses never switch the touch controls off. They carry no `code`, so the `Keyboard` files a D-pad's held arrow under its key name.

### Sound

```
 explore / battle / doctor / pause / ┐                          ┌ master (MASTER_VOLUME)
 title / match / account / travel    │                          │
 controllers, hud (a new lead)       ┴─ sfx.play(cue, opts) ────┤   → limiter → speakers
 cues.ts (data) ─ scheduleCue ─────────────── WebAudio nodes ───┘
```

- **Where a cue plays.** From the screens, where events already become visuals: a beat of the battle or the doctor plays its cue as it runs, a key that moves a cursor or picks something plays its blip, the HUD dings when a party edit makes a new lead. The authority and the engine never hear of sound ([[DECISIONS]] § Client). The cue table is [[UI_SPEC]] § Sound and juice.
- **When sound can start.** `main.ts` calls `sfx.unlock()` in the capture phase of every `keydown`, `pointerdown`, `pointerup` and `touchend`, before any screen sees the key, so the key that opens a menu can already blip (a finger's tap presses as it lifts, the moment a browser counts a touch as the player's). `unlock` makes the `AudioContext` only on a press the browser counts as the player's (`navigator.userActivation.isActive`): Chrome does not count Escape or the modifier keys, and a context made on one of them logs a warning (which would fail `scripts/screenshot.mjs`).
- **The setting.** `sfx.on`, remembered in `localStorage['animath.sound']` (`'on'` / `'off'`; storage failures ignored), on by default. M is `main.ts` calling `sfx.flip()`, which plays no cue: the sound chip shows what happened. Off disconnects the master gain (whatever was playing ends unheard) and suspends the context; on makes a fresh master and resumes it.
- **Without WebAudio**, or in the screens' tests, which unlock no context, `play` records the cue (`recent`, `onCue`) and schedules nothing; the tests listen with `sfx.onCue`. `test/sfx.test.ts` unlocks an `Sfx` of its own over a fake context, to hear what it schedules.

### Coordinate system

Engine grid `(x, y)` maps to Three `(x, height, z)` with `z = y`; grid "down" is toward the camera, down and to the left on screen (the camera's yaw is 35°: [[DECISIONS]] § Client), the same everywhere because that yaw is fixed. Each tile is a 1 × 1 column centred on integer `x`, `z`, from `y = −0.5` up to its ground top, which is at `y = 0.5 + 0.25·height` for land and lower for water (`groundTop` in `tiles.ts`). Figures stand with their feet on that top face; the player hops 0.15 during a step. A figure faces `+z` (grid "down", toward the camera) at `rotation.y = 0`; `up` is `π`, `right` is `π/2`, `left` is `-π/2`.

A kid never sees the grid's numbers (only `?debug` shows them). The coordinates in the explore corner are `coordinates.ts`'s: `(x − spawn.x, spawn.y − y)`, from the world's `spawnPoint`, so grid "up" is `+y` there.

### Modes

Two modes, one on screen at a time (a friendly match is drawn on the battle's screen, by `MatchController` from the server's messages, never from the authority: § Friendly matches). `battle-started` hands the screen to the battle controller: explore input is switched off, the world keeps drawing for a moment so the step into the grass lands while the iris closes on it, then the battle scene and panel replace it under the iris opening. The authority resolves each battle intent at once (`battle-updated`); the controller plays the events back as beats and only then shows the new state, so the screen lags the authority on purpose. `battle-ended` puts the authority back in explore at once, but the screen stays on the result card until the player leaves it; only then does explore input come back. Walking during a battle and battle intents outside one are ignored by the authority; party intents during one are refused by the engine (`not-exploring`), because the battle holds its own copy of the party and writes it back at the end.

The pause menu is not a mode either: an overlay in explore, opened by Escape, that switches explore input off while it is open and closes itself on `battle-started`, `doctor-visit-started`, `welcome`, `game-left` or `travelled`. The authority does not know it is open; it sees only the party intents the menu sends, and `leave-game` from its Start screen row.

A trip to another world is not a mode either: explore's own overlay (`travel/controller.ts`). The menu's Go hands the world to it; it closes a cover on the trainer, during which no screen takes a key (`keyScreen` gives none; M still flips the sound), sends `travel` only once the cover is shut, and opens it on `travelled`. The authority sees one `travel` intent and answers at once, as it does every intent; the transition round it is the client's alone.

The title is before the modes: no game is under way (`game.mode` is `loading` before the first one and `title` after `game-left`), and while `title.open` the title takes every key (`keyScreen`) but M outside a text box (the sound), unless an account card is over it. Its world is the renderer's, pointed aside and drifting (`lookAt`), with scenery figures that go on `welcome`; its starter stage is drawn instead of the world, as a battle scene is (`setStage`). It closes on `welcome`: Continue's `start({ game })` or a `new-game` the authority accepted.

The doctor is not a mode: a dialogue card over explore, driven by `doctor-visit-started` / `-updated` / `-ended` (the doctor controller plays each update back as beats, like the battle screen). Each doctor event carries its visit's number (a `DoctorState`'s `step` starts at 0 every visit), and the card ignores events of any other visit, as the battle screen ignores other battles'. The world keeps drawing under it; explore input is off while it is open, and the authority ignores walking during a visit and doctor intents outside one. The authority opens a visit on `interact` when `canTalkToDoctor(seed, pos, facing)` holds, so it tracks the facing the client shows: `down` in a new game, then the `dir` of every `move`, walked (`player-moved`) or blocked (`player-blocked`), the `dir` of `player-placed` and of `landed`, and the `facing` of `travelled`. The facing is saved with the game and `welcome` carries it, so a restored player looks the way they did and both sides agree after any `welcome`. The client computes the "Press Enter to talk to the witch doctor" prompt with the same engine function from the same events, and "Press Enter to chop the tree" with `clearableAhead`, the check `clearTile` makes. After a lost battle the authority emits `battle-ended`, `party-changed` (the team tired, where it was fought; with no tent within reach and no glider to fly out on, looked after by a witch doctor who came, `doctor.came`) and the closing `message`, as after any battle: the result card says "Good try!" over it, and back in explore, while the team needs the witch doctor, nobody follows the trainer, the line under the message says to walk to a tent, and `DoctorWay` points the way. `player-placed` is a go-to: the player put down beside another player, turned to them, with a poof, no tween. See [[UI_SPEC]].

### Saving

Local first ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves). The pieces:

```
 LocalAuthority ──events──▶ Autosave.handle ──(one per intent)──▶ commit()
       ▲                        │                                   │
       │ start({ game })        │ snapshot()                        ├─▶ localStorage['animath.save']   the save
 Autosave.boot() ◀── localStorage (instant) · for an account,       │    (an account's: its own keys)
       │               one server GET (2.5 s at most)               └─▶ PUT /api/account/save          an account's
       └─▶ notice (a copy key for the HUD)                               (background, seq must rise)   copy
```

- **The document.** `SaveV4` (engine `save.ts`), since lands (#191: `land`, `lands`, `unlocked`; version 3 brought the book's `freed`); its fields, and what the server checks of each, are in § HTTP API. A field is in every write from the build that brought it on (`solved` with the count, `seen` and `caught` with the book, `freed` with version 3), and a build from before it keeps it as an extra, as it was ([[INVARIANTS]] § "A field this build does not know is written back as it was"). `edits` (`WorldEdits`' canonical text) is written only once something is cleared, and `restoreGame` trims one over `EDITS_BUDGET` round the player, as a clear does; `worlds` is written only once there is one. `steps`, `visits` and the party belong to the player across worlds; `pos`, `facing` and `edits` to the world they are in. A doctor visit is not saved: a reload closes it, and what it healed, set free or sold is already in the party, the tokens and the items. `lineage` is a random id minted when a game starts, the same in every save of that game; `seq` numbers the saves of a game, one higher each time. A v1 document (`version`, `seed`, `pos`, `party`, and the same later fields) is read through `SAVE_UPGRADES[1]`: World 1, its home, everything else as it was (an extra under a name v2 has since taken, and a seed other than World 1's, kept under `v1`). A v2 document (the same as v3 without `freed`) is read through `SAVE_UPGRADES[2]`: every kind in its `seen` or `caught` and not in its party counts as set free, the human's rule, and everything else is as it was (an extra named `freed` kept under `v2`). Older documents lack later fields and `restoreGame` fills them in (facing down, no steps, no visits, no tokens, no items, no puzzle solved, nothing cleared, no world left, no name, and the animal book the save proves: every kind in the party caught, a saved battle's wild animal seen), and stands the player where they were only if they can stand on that tile in the world as they left it, with what they own (ground, or water with the boat), else on the world's spawn tile; `sameProgress` reads a missing `tokens`, `items`, `solved`, `edits` and `worlds` as none, and the book as the book the save proves (its lists compared as sets), so a save from before the shop, the count, the tools, travel or the book and one that writes it out hold the same progress, and a tile cleared, a world travelled to and a name are progress, never whereabouts.
- **When.** `Autosave.handle` marks the save dirty on every event that changes the game (a step, a bump or a go-to, the glider's take-off, each tile glided, a bird following and the landing, a battle's start and turns, a doctor visit, a puzzle solved, the animal book grown; and what counts as playing: a battle ended, the party changed or an edit to it accepted, the tokens or items changed, a tile cleared, a world travelled to, a name chosen) and writes once, in a microtask, after everything one intent caused. `flush()` writes at once on `pagehide` and when the page is hidden. Events emitted before `begin()` are the authority starting from the plan and write nothing. The title writes and sends nothing: saving starts with `begin()` (Continue) or with a new game's `welcome`, and `game-left` writes what is dirty, sends an account's save and stops it.
- **A new game** (`welcome` with `newGame: true`, from the title) is a game of its own: a fresh lineage, no extras, its `seq` numbered on past every save the page has seen and past whatever the key holds when it first writes. That first write moves the key's text (the game the kid left, or another tab's later save of it) to the first free `animath.save.previous` slot (up to 200, where the accidental set-asides keep 20), never over one; with none free the key keeps the old game and this one is not saved in the browser. Picking a starter counts as playing, so an unreadable save waiting in the key goes to `animath.save.unreadable` at once. The lineage the kid left is remembered for the page (`putAway`): the server's copy of that game (an account's) never replaces the new one (saves jump past it, and the server keeps it in `account_save_backups`), while any other game the server holds is settled by the usual rule, so a game the title never showed is never put away unseen.
- **Continue** starts the authority from `resumable()`: the save the page carries on from as it is now (the one it loaded, last wrote, or took on from another tab's walk while the title was up), not the game the title first showed.
- **Compare before write.** Every tab keeps the exact text of the save it last read or wrote. Before a write it reads the key again: unchanged → write. Changed → another tab saved. If that save differs from this tab's own only in whereabouts (`sameProgress`: position, facing, steps, visits, lineage, seq), this tab carries on from it (its `seq`, its lineage, and its step and visit counts, which `LocalAuthority.catchUp` raises so encounters and doctor puzzles follow on, with the witch doctor's card open too, and in a battle, which it re-keys from the new count) and writes on top. Otherwise this tab is behind: it stops writing, locally and to the server. The check is not atomic across tabs: a page's view of `localStorage` is brought up to date only between tasks, so two tabs writing in the same instant both pass it, and the first write is lost. A page that finds itself behind while its own last save is neither the save now in the key, nor played on from (the same lineage, a higher `seq`), nor kept aside anywhere, keeps it in `animath.save.replaced` (`keepOwnSave`). So does a page whose `storage` event carries a save of its game numbered no higher than its own last one, and not it: that save went over this page's unseen, even when the key has moved on since (`overwrittenUnseen`). `behind` says why, from the save that put it behind, whichever page notices: `newer` when a newer build wrote it (checked first, and before every write, a new game's included), `window` when that save is still this tab's game (the same lineage) played on elsewhere, `replaced` when another game has taken its place (a tab that took a bigger game from the server into the browser's save, and every other tab of the old game, as after a new game from the title), `gone` when the key has gone. From then on `main.ts` runs no controller and lets no key reach the game; only the browser's own keys pass (with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd, and F1–F12). It cancels `beforeinput` too, so nothing typed with AltGr or Option, pasted or dropped reaches the name box (only an input method's composition cannot be cancelled). Each frame it asks `behindAction` (`save/behind.ts`) what to do. Hidden, the page waits to be shown. Behind another window, it reloads into the newest game when it is in use (visible and focused); on screen while another window is in use, it shows the behind card and waits for the kid. Behind for another reason, it reloads as soon as it is visible. A page reloads itself at most `RELOADS_PER_MINUTE` (3) times a minute, so no bug can trap a kid in a loop of reloads, and past that it shows the card. Enter, Space outside a text box, or the card's button reload whatever the count, because the kid asked, and do not count. A reload that catches up with another window in the middle of a game leaves a note in `sessionStorage` (`animath.caughtUp`), and the reloaded page skips the title for a game with a name (one without goes through the title, which asks for it): it does Continue at once and says "You were playing in another window. Here's your newest game!" (`save.caughtUp`) instead of "Welcome back!". A page that was on the title reloads into the title. A `storage` event runs the same check at once, against the key as it is, never the value the event carried. A page that may have missed events runs it again (`recheck`): on `pageshow` back from the back/forward cache, on `resume` after the browser froze it, and when its window gains focus. A key that has gone (site data cleared) is never written back.
- **Start** (`boot()`), whose plan the title shows: its game is Continue's. A readable local save → that game, and `save.welcomeBack` ("Welcome back!") once Continue picks it up. A newer build's save (`readSave`'s `newer`: a later version, or a species, or a battle's realm or puzzle kind, this build does not have) → nothing starts, not even the title: the page is behind it (`newer`, `frozen`) and reloads for the new version (`save/behind.ts`). An unreadable save → no game; the new game leaves the key alone until the kid has played (a starter picked, or anything § When counts as playing; a doctor visit alone or a refused edit is not playing), then moves the old text to `animath.save.unreadable` and writes (`held`). An account's game with no readable save here → one `GET` (2.5 s at most, the session check included): a readable server copy becomes the game (and the local save at its first write); an unreadable one gives no game, with that notice; a newer one puts the page behind it, as a newer local save does. Otherwise no game and a fresh lineage, until New game. A guest's start never waits: nothing but this browser keeps a guest's game.
- **The server's copy**, an account's only (§ Accounts below; a guest's game has had none since the anonymous backup was retired, [[DECISIONS]] § Saves). A tab that saves an account's game locally also sends its latest document: 1 s after the kid played (§ When), 15 s after anything else (a step, a battle's turn), with `keepalive` on `pagehide` and when the page is hidden; one request at a time. The first thing sent is a `GET`, and the save with the higher `seq` carries on: this tab's is sent; the server's (another device played more, or this game's saves that never reached this browser's storage) is adopted — the local one moved to the account's `replaced` key — and the page reloads into it. An answer that finds the key changed by another tab whose storage event has not come yet carries on from that tab's walk first, and settles again. A tie between two games goes to the server's, and only between saves: at `seq` 0 neither has one, so nothing is adopted. A `409` settles the same way. An unreadable server copy is saved past once the kid has played (the next `seq` beats it; the server keeps it), and a browser that found only that at start says its saved game did not load; a newer build's copy is never written over, and the page that finds it, at start or at a `409`, is behind it (`newer`). A `409` from a server holding a lower `seq` than the one it turned away (a server older than the save it holds) is retried as if the server were out of reach, never in a loop of sends and looks. Out of reach, a `503` (a server older than the save sent) or a `429` (too many saves a minute): retries at 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 s, then rest, with no timer, until the kid plays again (§ When). A `400`/`413` is a bug: one `console.error`, and nothing more sent this visit. A `401` (`logged-out`) is a session that ended (§ Accounts below). Every one of these is decided from the API's own JSON answer; a page from anything in between (a tunnel's warning page, a proxy's error or login page) counts as out of reach, whatever its status.
- **Keys** (`save/storage.ts` `KEYS`): `animath.save`, `animath.save.unreadable`, `animath.save.replaced`, `animath.save.previous` (a game left for a new one on the title, and the guest game's last save once registering has moved it into the account, `moveGuestGameIn`), `animath.save.upgraded` (an older build's save as it was, kept before this build's first write over it). A browser that played before the anonymous backup was retired also holds `animath.player` (`{ id, secret }`) and maybe `animath.player.previous`, that backup's identity: the game never reads or writes them, and they stay for the export ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Moving a kid's game to production). `animath.save.replaced` also takes a page's own last save when another tab wrote over it in the same instant, unseen (§ Compare before write), and, for an account's game, the browser's copy when a newer one came from the server (an account's keys have the same set-asides). A save set aside goes to the first free slot of its key (`animath.save.unreadable`, then `.2`, `.3`, … up to `.20`; the same for `replaced` and `upgraded`) and is never written over; with no slot free, the save it would have made room for stays where it is and this game is not saved in the browser — except an older build's save, which the upgrade has lost nothing of: without room for its copy, the game is saved over it all the same. The language's `animath.language` belongs to the copy module.
- **A throwaway switch** (the `flags.ts` row lists them): no store, no server: a throwaway game that skips the title and reads and writes nothing.
- **Accounts** ([[DECISIONS]] § Accounts). The browser plays the guest game or the game of the account it is logged in to (`account/session.ts`, the `animath.account` pointer), each under keys of its own (`KEYS`, `accountKeys`): one `Autosave` per page, built at start with the game's keys and its server. A guest's game has no server; an account's is `accountSaveServer`, behind a `SessionCheck` that asks `/api/account/me` once as the page starts (the first request to the account routes that reads the session: only `/ready`, which reads no cookie and sends none, may go before it; [[INVARIANTS]] § Server) and lets nothing reach the save routes until the server says the cookie is this account's. The rules of § The server's copy settle the two copies (the higher `seq` both ways, a tie between games to the server's, a `409` answered by asking for the save), with two more for a game played on several devices: a tie within the game goes to the server's when the two saves differ, and after a `409` the server's copy is the game even when the page has saved on since, unless it is the page's own save, sent before and not heard back (`sameJson`). A newer build's save on the server puts the page behind (`newer`), as it does any page: at start, a page holding the account's game asks the server before anything starts, for no longer than start-up waits; one that fails this build's checks is saved past once the kid has played, the server keeping it in `account_save_backups`. A `401` (`logged-out`) means the session ended: pushes stop (`loggedOut`), the game saves on in the browser, and the page says so once. Registering, logging in and logging out move what they must in storage (`session.ts`), then start the page again (`account/restart.ts`); another tab changing the pointer puts this page behind (`fallBehind`). A page opened by a welcome link (`account/welcome.ts`, the token out of the address first, or kept by the tab from an earlier start) starts on the title with the welcome card over it, whatever else its start would have done, unless a newer build's save puts it behind (then nothing starts) or it plays a throwaway game (which drops the token); the password picked there logs in as a login does (`takeAccountGame`, the pointer, `restart('welcome')`). `pushNow` sends the newest save before a logout and waits for it, briefly. A guest game's next save card is kept beside the save (`SaveNudge`), due by the game's own steps, and `main.ts` opens it while exploring.

### Data model

- `players`, `saves` and `save_backups` — the retired anonymous backup's ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves), written by nothing since it was retired, and kept, rows and all ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server; dropping them is a [[DEFERRED]] item). `players`: `id uuid pk`, `secret_hash text` (SHA-256 of the secret the browser held), `display_name text?`, `created_at`, `last_seen_at`; one row per browser that played as a guest on the development server. `saves`: `player_id uuid pk → players (cascade)`, `data jsonb` (a save document of its day, version 1 or 2: § HTTP API), `updated_at`; the last save that browser sent, which `admin export-local-save` reads. `save_backups`: `id bigserial pk`, `player_id uuid → players (cascade)`, `data jsonb`, `reason text` (`replaced`, `unreadable`), `created_at`; the saves it kept aside, for recovering a game by hand ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Database). The human's local database holds the tunnel's games in them; production's are empty.
- `users` — `id uuid pk`, `name text` (as the engine's `checkName` gives it: trimmed, NFC; also the character's name), `name_key text unique` (the engine's `nameKey(name)`, computed when the account is made, at registration or by `admin import-save`: a change to `nameKey`'s rules needs a migration that recomputes every row), `password_hash text` (`passwords.ts`), `created_at`. One row per account. Nothing here refers to the retired backup's tables.
- `sessions` — `token_hash text pk` (SHA-256 of the cookie's token), `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (a year on; `/me` moves it a year out again once it is 30 days into that year, `SLIDE_AFTER_DAYS`). At most 20 per account: a new session clears that account's expired ones and its least recently used past 20.
- `account_saves` — `user_id uuid pk → users (cascade)`, `data jsonb` (the `SaveV4` envelope below), `seq bigint` (the document's own `seq`), `updated_at`. One save per account, written by the write guard (§ HTTP API).
- `account_save_backups` — `id bigserial pk`, `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `data jsonb`, `reason text` (`replaced`: a different game took its place, or this build's first save over an older build's save of the same game; `unreadable`: this build could not read it), `created_at`. A save the server would otherwise lose, copied here before a write replaces it, newest first within a budget per account (§ HTTP API, the write guard). Nothing reads it; it is for recovering a kid's game by hand ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Accounts).
- `welcome_tokens` — `token_hash text pk` (SHA-256 of a welcome link's token), `user_id uuid → users (cascade)`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (14 days on), `used_at` (null until the link logs in). A link to an account `admin import-save` made with no password (`users.password_hash` is `NO_PASSWORD`, `'!'`); it works only while its account has none.
- `client_errors` — `id bigserial pk`, `created_at`, `message text`, `stack text`, `build text`, `mode text`, `browser text`, `screen text`, `test boolean` (default false). One row per error report a page sent (`client-errors.ts`), and nothing about who sent it: no player, no account, no address. At most 30 days old and 5,000 rows, the oldest let go as a report comes in; indexed by `created_at` (`client_errors_created_at_idx`). Refers to no other table; only `admin errors` reads it.

Every reference that is not its table's key has an index of its own (`save_backups_player_id_idx`, `sessions_user_id_idx`, `account_save_backups_user_id_idx`, `welcome_tokens_user_id_idx`), and `name_key`'s uniqueness is the constraint `users_name_key_unique`.

Hot fields get promoted from `data` to columns when a query needs them: `account_saves.seq` is one, which the admin's list reads (`listAccounts`; the write guard reads the document itself). A leaderboard would be next; who is near whom is presence's, in memory (§ Presence).

### HTTP API

Errors are JSON `{ error: string }`, except the health check's. All routes are under `/api`. A route that reads a body answers `400 { error: 'body is not valid JSON' }` to one that is not JSON, and a `400` to one without the fields it needs; every POST and PUT has a body limit, past which it answers `413 { error: 'body is bigger than <n> bytes' }`: 4 KiB for login, logout and the welcome link's POST, a save's 1 MiB and 4 KiB more for register, 1 MiB (`SAVE_MAX_BYTES`) for the save's PUT, 8 KiB (`REPORT_MAX_BYTES`) for an error report.

| Route                         | Auth  | Response                                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/health`             | none  | `200 { ok: true, db: true, sha }`, or `503 { ok: true, db: false, sha }` when the database does not answer. `sha` is the commit the running image was built from (`dev` outside an image); each deploy checks prod reports the one it deployed. |
| any other path under `/api`, and `/ws` and every path under it | none | `404 { error: 'not found' }`: never the game's page. The retired anonymous backup's `/api/players` routes are among them, in every environment. The client counts it as no answer (`offline`), unlike the save route's own `404` (`no save yet`). |
| `POST /api/account/register`  | none  | `201 { user: { name } }` and the session cookie. Body `{ name, password, save? }`: `save` is the guest game the player brings (checked with `validateSaveWrite`, 1 MiB at most), stored as the account's save. `400 { error: 'bad name', reason }` (the engine's `checkName` reason), `400 { error: 'bad password', reason: 'short' \| 'long' }` (the engine's `checkPassword`), `400 { error: 'bad save', detail }`, `409 { error: 'name taken' }` (by `nameKey`), `413 { error: 'save is bigger than 1048576 bytes' }` for a `save` over 1 MiB (the body's own limit is the line above's), `503` for a guest game a newer build wrote. Nothing is made unless all of it is. |
| `POST /api/account/login`     | none  | `200 { user: { name } }` and the session cookie; `401 { error: 'wrong name or password' }` for a wrong password and for a name with no account alike, in the same time. |
| `POST /api/account/logout`    | none  | `200 { ok: true }`: the session is deleted and the cookie cleared, with or without one. With `x-animath-account` (as the game sends it), the session ends only when it is that account's, and no cookie is sent: one sent could land after a login another tab makes at the same moment and clear that login's cookie. `400` for a header that is not a name. |
| `GET /api/account/ready`      | none  | `200 { ready: boolean }`, `Cache-Control: no-store`: whether accounts work here now, that is the database answers and has the accounts' tables (`AccountsReady`). `false` on a server whose database `0003_accounts` has not reached, or that does not answer. The game offers an account only on `true` ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server). |
| `GET /api/account/me`         | none  | `200 { user: { name } }`, or `200 { user: null }` when not logged in. |
| `GET /api/account/save`       | session | `200` the save as stored (`SaveV4`, or an older version stored before an update), readable or not, or `404 { error: 'no save yet' }`. |
| `PUT /api/account/save`       | session | `200 { ok: true }`; `409 { error, save }` with the stored save when its `seq` is the same or higher (another device got ahead), or a newer build wrote it (`STORED_FROM_NEWER_BUILD`, whatever the `seq`); `400 { error: 'body is not valid JSON' }`, or `400 { error: 'bad save', detail }` for a document `validateSaveWrite` refuses; `413` for a body over 1 MiB (`SAVE_MAX_BYTES`); `503 { error: SAVE_FROM_NEWER_BUILD }` for a document a newer build wrote (`validateSaveWrite`'s `newer`: this server is the older one); `401 { error: 'not logged in' }` when the account was deleted while the request was on its way (`writeAccountSave`'s `gone`). |
| `GET /api/account/welcome`    | none  | The token in `x-animath-welcome`, never in the path (`WELCOME_HEADER`): a request line is what nginx's logs keep: the access log every one, the error log only when nginx runs out of memory. `200 { name }` for a live link: its account's name, and nothing else; `410 { error: 'link used' }` (used, or its account has a password by now), `410 { error: 'link expired' }`, `404 { error: 'no such link' }`, which name no account; `400` without the header. `Cache-Control: no-store`; never a cookie. |
| `POST /api/account/welcome`   | none  | Body `{ token, password }`: `200 { user: { name }, save }` and the session cookie: the password set, the link used, this browser logged in (its old session ended, as at login), `save` the account's save as stored (or null). `400 { error: 'bad password', reason }` leaves the link as it was; `410` and `404` as the GET says; only a live link costs a password hash. |
| `POST /api/client-errors`     | none  | An error report from a page (`client-errors.ts`). Body `{ message, stack?, build, mode, browser, screen, test? }`: `message` a string, `stack` a string, `build` 7 to 40 lower-case hex digits or `dev`, `mode` lower-case letters and dashes (24 at most), `browser` a letter then letters, digits, spaces and `._()-` (60 at most), `screen` `<width>x<height>`, `test` a boolean (a report sent by hand). `204`, kept cleaned and cut to its bounds; a field it does not know is dropped. `400 { error: 'bad report', detail }` naming the first field that is wrong; `415`, `403` and `413` as every POST; `429 { error: 'too many reports', retryAfter }` with `Retry-After` past `REPORT_LIMITS` (30 per address in 10 minutes; nothing shared by every address); `503 { error: 'not kept' }` when the database does not take it. It reads no session, sends no cookie, and keeps no address. Any other method is the API's `404`. |

**Session auth** is the `animath_session` cookie and `x-animath-account`, the account the page plays (its name, URI-encoded, which the server keys with `nameKey` by its own Unicode tables), checked against the session as the server answers: the cookie names whichever account the browser logged in to last, in any tab. No live session → `401 { error: 'not logged in' }`; no header, or one that is not a name → `400`; a session that is another account's → `401 { error: 'not logged in' }` as well, since this account is not logged in here. None of these answers sends a cookie.

**`SaveV4`** (engine `src/save.ts`) is a versioned envelope, stored and returned as the engine reads it:

```ts
{
	version: 4,
	home: number,                  // the world the game began in, 1–9999
	world: number,                 // the world the player is in, 1–9999: its seed is landSeed(land, world)
	land?: string,                 // the land the player is in (written in every save; none is Nordland): a land this build lacks makes the save newer
	pos: { x: number, y: number },  // integer tile coordinates in `world`, any sign
	party: AnimalInstance[],       // any number, in species bundles, of { id: string, speciesId: string, nickname?: string, hp: number }
	name?: string,                 // 1–40 UTF-16 units; the player's name once chosen (checkName judges it on load)
	// Required in a write (`SaveWrite`):
	facing?: 'up' | 'down' | 'left' | 'right',
	steps?: number,                // whole; the authority's step count, in every world, which keys every encounter
	visits?: number,               // whole; doctor visits opened, which with steps key each visit
	lineage?: string,              // 1–64 characters; the game this save belongs to
	seq?: number,                  // whole ≥ 1; this save's number within its game
	// Optional in a write too.
	tokens?: number,               // whole; the tokens the witch doctor gave, less what the shop took
	items?: string[],              // ids of 1–64 characters; the tools owned (an id this build doesn't know is kept, and does nothing)
	solved?: number,               // whole; the puzzles the player has solved
	seen?: string[],               // the animal book: the species met, by id, in the order first met
	caught?: string[],             // the animal book: the species caught, by id, in the order first caught
	freed?: string[],              // the animal book: the species set free at the witch doctor's, by id, in the order first set free
	edits?: string[],              // the tiles cleared in `world`: one "cx,cy:" entry per chunk and its tile indices in hex ("-1,0:0a91"); canonical
	worlds?: { world: number, pos: { x, y }, facing, edits?: string[] }[], // the worlds left behind, most recently left first, each once; all edits together within EDITS_BUDGET (24,000 characters)
	battle?: unknown,              // the battle in progress (a BattleState) in `world`, checked on load by readBattle
	// Since lands (#191): what above is the land's (pos, facing, edits, worlds, party, tokens, items, battle) is `land`'s.
	lands?: { land: string, party: AnimalInstance[], tokens?: number, items?: string[], worlds?: …[] }[], // the lands left behind, most recently left first, each once and never `land`; written once there is one
	unlocked?: string[]            // the lands unlocked, once one past Nordland is; an id this build lacks is kept
}
```

`version`, `home`, `world`, `pos` and `party` are validated strictly (`speciesId` shaped as a catalog id, `hp` a whole number ≥ 0, `id` unique within the party, a world a number from 1 to 9999; a species in the party or the animal book, or in a battle a realm, a species or a puzzle kind, that the engine's catalogs lack makes the document a newer build's, `readSave`'s `newer`, while an item they lack is kept and does nothing); `name`, `facing`, `steps`, `visits`, `lineage`, `seq`, `tokens`, `items`, `solved`, `seen`, `caught` and `freed` (lists of species-shaped ids), `edits` (`isEditsText`) and `worlds` (each world once, a world number, a position, a facing, edits) when present, and a write must carry the five named above. `battle` only has to be storable: the client checks it when it loads (`readBattle`) and drops it if it cannot be picked up. Any extra field — top-level or per animal — is stored and returned as sent, so a newer client can add data without a server change; bump `version` when an old document becomes unreadable, or when an older build must not carry on with a save it cannot read all of ([[DECISIONS]] § Saves). A `PUT` of an older version (a page still open from before an update) is read through the upgrade (`validateSaveWrite`) and stored upgraded; a v1 document (`seed` for `home` and `world`) lands in World 1, and a v2 one gets its kinds set free. "As sent" is JSON semantics: key order and `-0` are not preserved, and a document containing a NUL character, a lone surrogate or a number that overflows to Infinity is a `400`, not a mangled row. So is one nested more than `MAX_SAVE_DEPTH` (64) deep, where a game's save is 4, refused before anything walks it ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server).

**The write guard** (`canReplace`, `replacesAnotherGame`, `writeAccountSave`). A `PUT` lands only when its `seq` is higher than the stored save's (`saveSeq`: its `seq`, or 0 when it has none or cannot be read), and never over a save a newer build wrote (`isNewerSave`), whatever its `seq`; otherwise `409`, with the stored save. Within one game that puts saves in order however the network delivers them, and the browser's compare-before-write keeps a game's saves in one line (§ Saving). Between two games (two lineages: one started while the other was out of reach), the one saved more often wins. Before a write replaces a save from a different lineage, one `readSave` cannot read, or one an older build wrote (the first save after an update), the old document goes to `account_save_backups` as it was. The read, the decision and the write run in one transaction with the account's row locked, so of racing writes one lands and the rest see it. An account keeps its set-asides newest first within `ACCOUNT_BACKUP_BYTES` (2 MiB as Postgres stores them, and always the newest): hundreds of a kid's few-kilobyte games, two of a megabyte, so no account can fill the disk.

**The retired anonymous backup** (`/api/players`, a guest's save under an id and a secret the browser held) ran in development only, until the human's kid's game moved to production; its routes are gone, and its tables stay (§ Data model, [[DECISIONS]] § Saves).

**Accounts.** A session is a random 32-byte token in the cookie `animath_session` (HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, `Path=/`, a year's `Max-Age`; Secure in production), of which the server stores only the SHA-256. Only login, register, a welcome link's POST, `/me` and a logout that names no account send the cookie, and only that logout clears it (the game's logout names its account and sends none: the session it ends leaves a cookie that opens nothing): `/me` moves a session's end a year out once it is 30 days into its year (`SLIDE_AFTER_DAYS`), sending the cookie again; the save routes and the WebSocket upgrade read the session and never answer with a cookie ([[INVARIANTS]] § Server). Every POST and PUT under `/api/account` must declare a JSON body (else `415 { error: 'send JSON' }`) and, when it carries an `Origin`, come from a page of this site (else `403 { error: 'wrong origin' }`): the `Origin`'s host and port must be the request's `Host`, or a proxy's `X-Forwarded-Host`, which is why the dev proxy keeps the browser's `Host` (`vite.config.ts`); a `Host` without a port, as nginx's `$host` passes it, is compared by name alone. Login, register and the save PUT are rate-limited in memory (`ACCOUNT_LIMITS`), per address: every login try, and every registration; per name: failed logins from one address, failed logins from every address together (a higher bar), counted before the password is checked, so tries racing each other cannot slip under a limit, and taken back when it was right, and registrations; and per account: save PUTs, checked before the body is read. A welcome link's GET and POST count together per address, at the login's numbers (`welcomePerIp`). Past a limit, `429 { error: 'too many tries', retryAfter }` with `Retry-After`. The address is the socket's, or, behind a proxy of ours (a loopback or private peer), the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, and an IPv6 address counts as its /64 (`rateKey`).

### Presence

Who is in which world, where, and what they are doing ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer): the server's to decide, in memory only. A restart forgets everyone, and every page says where it is again as its socket comes back.

```
 page (PresenceController)          /api/ws (presence/socket.ts)            PresenceHub
 hello {v, guest, name} ──────────▶ version? origin? account? name rules ─▶ join: one per identity
   ◀── hi {v, pid, name, match, boot}  /  refresh {v}  /  bye {reason}
 where {world, land, x, y, facing, lead, boat, harness?, busy} ──────────▶ into that land's room of the world;
   ◀── peer {pid, name, …} / gone {pid}   (the players near, both ways)       sight both ways
   ◀── roster {world, land, players}      (the 50 nearest others in the room, roughly, every 2 s if it changed)
 find {pid} ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────▶ found {pid, x, y} / lost {pid}
 battle {view, events}   (its own, while its where says battle) ─────────▶ kept on the player, passed on
   ◀── fight {pid, vs, view, events}       (a battle near you: someone's with a wild animal, or a match)
```

- **One socket per page**, at `/api/ws` on the game's own origin (Vite proxies it with `/api` in development). A page opens it once a game is under way and closes it on the title; a throwaway game never opens it.
- **Who.** An account holder by their session cookie, looked up once as the socket opens (`AccountOf`, the accounts' `sessionUserFromCookieHeader`), and shown by their username; anyone else by the guest id their hello carries, shown by the name it carries once the name rules pass it. Nobody sees another's guest id: everyone else knows a socket by its public id, a keyed hash of who it is, the same on every copy of the server and across restarts, so a page that comes back, to this server or the next, is the same player to everyone. A second socket of the same identity takes the first one's place: the first is told `bye: replaced` and closed, and its page shows that the player went on in another window.
- **Rooms are lands of worlds.** A socket is in the room of the land and world its last `where` named (`placeKey`: `nordland:42`, since #191), or in none; a roster names both, and a page reads only its own's. Two players in a room see each other's every change while they are within `VIEW_RADIUS` of each other, and keep seeing each other until past `VIEW_KEEP`: the server sends each a `peer` when they come near and on every change, and a `gone` when they part, go to another world or leave. Everyone else in the room is on the roster, nearest first, with a rough bearing and number of steps.
- **Go to.** `find` answers where a player in the asker's world stands, exactly; the page's authority then puts the player beside them (`go-to`, `arrivalSpot`).
- **Battles seen from outside.** A page in a battle with a wild animal says so in its `where`, then reports each step of it (`battle`: the engine's `FightView` after the step and the step's `FightEvent`s, `net/fight.ts`), at most a few a second (`battleRatePerSecond`). The hub takes it only from a page whose last `where` says `battle`, keeps the view on the player, and sends it to everyone who sees them (`fight`); one who comes near later is sent the view as it stands, with no events, right after the player's `peer`. A friendly match's steps come from `matches.ts`, built from the server's own match state, and go once to each player who sees either of its two, never to the two. A battle's view goes when it ends, when its page says it does anything else, or when it leaves the world; nothing is kept past a restart (the page tells its battle again to the next server). What crosses is numbers and species, never a nickname: a puzzle travels as its numbers and the watching page writes it out with the engine's `facePrompt`, so no word and no answer reaches another page.
- **Guards.** Every message is read through the engine's parsers; a message over 4 KiB closes the socket; a socket may send 10 messages a second on average (20 at once), the rest are dropped, and one that keeps flooding (40 dropped in ten seconds) is closed, as is one that sends 10 that are no message, or no hello within 10 s. An `Origin` that is not the request's own host and port (or the host a proxy says it came to) is refused; only where that host is this machine's own name with no port (`localhost`, a loopback address: the local stack, whose nginx passes `$host`, which drops the port) is the name enough (#164). The server holds 1,000 sockets at most, 40 from one address (the one our proxy saw, the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry; an IPv6 /64 is one address; behind a proxy that sets no `X-Forwarded-For`, such as a bare tunnel, everyone is one address), a world 200 players, pings every socket every 20 s and drops one that did not answer the last ping, and cuts off one that stopped reading (256 KiB waiting to be sent, `maxBuffered`).
- **Versions.** A hello of another protocol version is answered `refresh` and closed; a page older than the server reloads itself once, at a calm moment, after saving; one newer waits a minute and tries again.
- **Deploys.** A server told to stop (SIGTERM) tells every socket `bye: restart`, closed with its own code (`byeCloseCode`, 4005), and takes no new socket. A page told so comes straight back (a quarter of a second, not a failure), which reaches the copy taking over (§ Production); the others on its screen and in its list are held while it does (`HOLD_SECONDS`), and the new copy knows every player by the same public id, so they are drawn on where they stood: no one twice, no poof, no note. For the few seconds a deploy runs two copies, each has the players whose sockets it holds.

### Friendly matches

A match is the server's (`presence/matches.ts`), on the presence socket ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). The page asks, answers and plays by message; the server judges every rule it can with the engine and keeps the rest (who is asking, who is in a match) in memory, like presence.

```
 Ada's page                         server (Matches)                          Bo's page
 challenge {pid, team} ───────────▶ challengeRefusal on both last `where`s,
   ◀── asking {pid, ms}              one invite each, the waits ─────────────▶ invite {pid, name, ms}
                                    ◀──────────────────────────────────────── accept {pid, team} / decline {pid}
 withdraw ────────────────────────▶ or no match after all (a No, 20 s, a withdraw, out of reach, busy…):
   ◀── uninvite {pid, reason}        each side told why (`INVITE_ENDS`) ─────▶ uninvite {pid, reason}
                                    matchTeam ×2, startMatch(crypto seed)
   ◀── match {id, view(a), events}  ──────────────────────────────────────▶ match {id, view(b), events}
 play {id, intent} ───────────────▶ applyMatchIntent → both views + events ─▶ (a refusal: `rejected`, to Ada alone)
 here {id} (keys on her turn)       turn clock: nudge 90 s, timeout 3 min
   ◀── nudge {id} ("Still there?")
                                    a socket closed: away 30 s, then timeout; back in time: hi {match} + match
 rematch {id, team} ──────────────▶ both said yes: a new match               ◀── rematch-wish {side, yes}
 done {id} (Back to exploring) ───▶ the rematch is off for both
   (again after a hi that still names the match: it went while the socket was away)
   (after a rematch Bo's Rematch? started: called off ── match {calledOff} ──▶ his result back up)
 play {id, leave} ────────────────▶ the leaver is free at once, with no rematch
```

- **Who, where.** A player is known by who they are (a guest id or an account), so a second window, or a page back after a reload, is the same player in the same match. The server reads positions and busy states from presence (`hub.present`); the team a page sends goes through `matchTeam`.
- **No answer on the wire.** Every match message is built in one place from `matchView` and the step's events; a refusal is a code. The pages' frames are read in a test (`match-socket.test.ts`).
- **Stopping.** `restart()` (SIGTERM) stops the matches before telling the sockets to come back: a match does not survive a server, and the pages say the game is updating ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). A server that stops without a word (a crash) tells nobody: a page back to another run (`hi.boot`) than its match began on says the game restarted, the same card; back to the same run with no match, the match ended while it was away.
- **Busy.** Only a match going on keeps its players busy for an invite, and a finished one a page has yet to put up (its `where` has not said `match` since it was sent it: a hidden tab, a page just back). A match that ended stays for its rematch (`lingerMs`) only for the pages still on its result; the one who left it is let go of at once, and a player asking or asked for another lets go of theirs (#139).

## Production

The game runs on its own Hetzner Cloud VPS (`mathgame-prod`, a cx23 in nbg1, Ubuntu 24.04, provisioned by `terraform/`), set up as lawcel's is. One Docker Compose project, `mathgame-prod` (`docker-compose.prod.yml`), runs in `~/mathgame`, a checkout of this repo that every deploy resets to the commit it deploys.

```
browser ──443──▶ nginx ──▶ app:3000 ──▶ postgres:5432
        ──80───▶ (ACME challenge, else → 443)    ▲
                                       backup ───┘ every 6 h → ~/mathgame/backups/
                                                 → every 6 h (launchd) → ~/mathgame-backups/ on the Mac
```

| Service    | Image                                            | Holds                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `app`      | `ghcr.io/ulfaslak/mathgame:prod`, the `Dockerfile` | The server bundle (`dist/index.mjs`) and the built client (`../client/dist`), under plain `node` as the `node` user, with tini as PID 1 (`init: true`). Env from `.env.production`. Not published: nginx reaches it as `app` on the compose network. |
| `postgres` | `postgres:18-alpine`                             | The database, in the named volume `mathgame-prod_pgdata` mounted at `/var/lib/postgresql`: postgres 18 keeps its cluster under it (`18/docker`), and a mount at the old `…/data` path would leave the cluster in an anonymous volume. Not published. |
| `nginx`    | `nginx:1.29.8`                                   | The only published ports, 80 and 443. Certificates from Let's Encrypt by its own ACME module over HTTP-01, kept in the volume `mathgame-prod_acme_data`. |
| `backup`   | `postgres:18-alpine`                             | `scripts/backup.sh`: a `pg_dump` at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC, read back whole before it counts, into `~/mathgame/backups/`, 30 days kept. |

**The domain** is one value, `MATHGAME_DOMAIN` in `deploy.env` (`animath.xyz`): nginx's server name and certificate, the deploy workflow's health check, the offsite sync's host, and, in the client, the link preview's URLs and the address a page on an old address points home to (`VITE_GAME_DOMAIN`, `moved.ts`; the workflow passes it to the image's build). It has an A record only, and `www` a CNAME to it: behind Docker's port proxy every IPv6 visitor would reach nginx from one address, and a limit per address (nginx's, the accounts') would count them all as one. `www.<domain>` only redirects to the domain, never serves the game: a kid's game is saved under the address the page came from, so a second address would be a second, empty game.

**nginx** is the `nginx/` folder, mounted whole. At every start the image renders `nginx.conf.template` into `/etc/nginx/nginx.conf` with the domain: HTTP to HTTPS on the domain, the ACME issuer, the TLS server with HSTS, and a TLS server for `www.<domain>` with a certificate of its own that answers every request with a 301 to the domain. `http.conf` and `app.conf` are what it shares with the local stack's `local/nginx.conf.template`, plain HTTP: Docker's DNS asked every 5 s with the app's address in a variable, so a replaced container is found without a reload; a short `proxy_connect_timeout` for the app to take a connection before nginx tries its other address, since an address whose container has left answers nothing ([[INVARIANTS]] § Serving has its length); logs that name no client (an access log without addresses, which gives the app addresses each request tried and what each answered, and hides a welcome link's token as `[hidden]` with a `map` over the request line; an error log that writes lines about requests and connections only at `emerg`, and nginx's own lines (starting, reloading, the certificate module) at `notice`: [[DECISIONS]] § Deployment); gzip; a 2 MiB body limit, above the app's 1 MiB save; a flood guard on every POST per address, 60 at once and then one a second, well above the account routes' own limits and the three error reports a tab sends in an hour (keyed on the method, since nginx and the app read a path's spelling differently; [[DEFERRED]] "Error reports share nginx's guard on POSTs with logging in"); the security headers; `/api/` passing WebSocket upgrades on, with an hour's read timeout; `/immutable/` and `/assets/` asking the other app on a 404 (below); and `502.html`, the page shown in the kid's language while the app does not answer. `scripts/lib/nginx-apply.sh` applies nginx at each deploy, before the swap: it validates the checkout's config and lets compose recreate the service if its definition changed. Otherwise it reloads, and when the template changed, the running nginx first renders it again with the image's own script, as at a start, into a folder of its own; only a render that is the checked config replaces `nginx.conf`, in one rename, and one that is not fails the deploy with nginx untouched. A reload counts once nginx runs new workers: nginx can refuse, as it applies it, a config `nginx -T` passed, and then goes on with its old ones, so the deploy fails there. A reload refuses no connection, while a recreate refuses every one until nginx is back, for up to ~12 s while presence sockets are open (nginx's graceful stop waits for them until Docker kills it).

**WebSockets** belong under `/api/` (`/api/ws`): only there does nginx pass an upgrade on and keep a quiet socket for an hour. `/ws` is a 404.

**A deploy** (`scripts/deploy.sh`) takes a lock first, one deploy at a time, and stops before it touches a container while a canary of an earlier deploy is still running (a stopped one serves nothing, and it removes it); the checkout is on the new commit by then. It migrates the database from the new image (`dist/migrate.mjs`), while the old app serves. It checks the new image in a container without the alias `app`, which nginx cannot reach: healthy, and serving the game's page from the same build. Docker lists a container under an alias from the moment it starts, so only an image that passed becomes a canary, which joins the network as `app` and takes a share of the requests. Compose then recreates the app with the new image, and the canary goes; nginx is reloaded as it goes, so that none of its workers keeps the canary's address, which no container answers on any more ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § What a request meets during a swap). For those few seconds two builds answer, and a page from one asks for files only its build has: the other app answers `404`, and nginx asks the one that has the file. A stopped app finishes the requests it is answering first (SIGTERM closes the server, 8 s at most; tini, `init: true`, hands it on), and sends its presence sockets on to the other app (§ Presence › Deploys). Two apps can overlap because the app keeps nothing between requests but what is in Postgres and presence, which the pages put back as they come back, and its rate limits, which each copy counts for itself ([[DEFERRED]]); a friendly match under way ends when its app stops, and its pages offer to play again on the next ([[DECISIONS]] § Multiplayer). The deploy never recreates Postgres, and warns when the checkout names another image than the one running (`/redeploy` § Changing Postgres). The backup loop runs a copy of `scripts/backup.sh` taken at its start, which the deploy compares with the checkout's to restart it, as it does when the service's definition changed. If the recreated app never turns healthy, the canary keeps serving and the deploy fails, rather than removing the only healthy app. A first deploy starts the whole stack, and an image that did not change gets no swap; both still apply nginx and reconcile the backup service.

**Images.** CI pushes every build to GHCR as `:<commit sha>` and `:prod`; the server keeps the running image, `:prod` and three more, for a quick rollback. It holds no registry credential: each deploy logs in with the workflow's own token.

**Backups**, in three layers as lawcel's: the `backup` service; the launchd agent `com.mathgame.backup-sync` on the Mac, which pulls the dumps and `.env.production` into `~/mathgame-backups/` every 6 hours (`scripts/sync-backups.sh`, installed by `scripts/install-backup-sync.sh`, logging to `~/Library/Logs/mathgame-backup-sync.log`); and Hetzner's nightly image of the whole machine (`backups = true`, 7 kept).

**Watching**, from the Mac ([[DECISIONS]] § Deployment): the launchd agent `com.mathgame.health-watch` runs a copy of `scripts/health-watch.sh` every 10 minutes (installed into `~/Library/Application Support/mathgame/` by `scripts/install-health-watch.sh`, logging to `~/Library/Logs/mathgame-health-watch.log`, its memory in `health-watch.state` beside the copy). It asks `https://<domain>/api/health`, reads the dates of the domain's and `www.`'s certificates off a TLS handshake (`curl -v`), and reads the error groups new since its last window over SSH, as the deploy user, from the admin CLI in the app's container (`errors --new --json`); it says what is wrong with `osascript`'s `display notification`, the words passed as arguments and never a report's text, new errors once an hour at most, and to Slack too with the backups' webhook ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Errors and health). The error reports it reads come from the pages to the app (`POST /api/client-errors`) and live in the `client_errors` table, 30 days and 5,000 rows at most.
