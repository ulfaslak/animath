# Human TODO

Tasks that genuinely require human action — accounts, consents, decisions that need human judgment. Running servers, migrations, and database operations are NOT human tasks; agents do those.

One numbered list: product questions first, then checks only a person can make, then guardrail edits waiting for approval. Each item says what the game does today.

1. **Should a wrong answer show the right one?** Today it never does, in battle or at the doctor: the answer box shakes red and says "Not quite!", and [[UI_SPEC]] keeps the answer hidden on purpose, since the kid meets the sum again.
2. **Losing is a free full heal.** A lost battle puts the kid beside the nearest tent with the whole team healed, for nothing, while the doctor asks a puzzle per kind of animal hurt, a hard one for a big animal (difficulty 6 for a wolf, 8 for a bear), so kids may learn to lose on purpose. Tokens don't change this: healing costs puzzles, not tokens, and losing gives or takes none. If healing ever costs tokens, losing on purpose becomes a way round paying.
3. **Times tables on every starter.** All three starter cards promise times tables: the frog asks them on its easy Tongue Flick, the squirrel and the rabbit on their second attack at hard ("7 × ? = 56"). Right for a seven-year-old's first animal?
4. **The switching loophole.** A kid can lead with a small animal and switch in a big one on the first turn: the small one never gets tired, so it stays the lead and keeps the encounters small (in PR #18's simulation a squirrel leading a squirrel and a bear was hurt in 0% of its battles, against 96–99% without switching). Keep it, or size encounters by the strongest animal standing, or by whoever fought last?
5. **A big lead meets nothing, or only smaller animals.** Nothing two or more tiers below the lead comes out, so a bear in front meets nothing in the meadow or at the river (64% of the tall grass within 32 tiles of the start), and where only one-tier-smaller animals live every battle is one of them at the full 1 step in 10: a deer at the river meets only otters, a wolf in the meadow only deer. Keep both, cut the rate where only smaller animals live, or give those biomes visitors of the lead's tier; and after play, is 1/10 the right weight for one tier below?
6. **Keep the frog?** An agent added it (PR #21) for the planned choice of three starters: a third tier-1 animal, living in the river reeds. No message from the human asks for a frog. Removing it now takes a save upgrade that turns caught frogs into another animal (`SAVE_UPGRADES`); the squirrels and rabbits that visit the river near home go with one line (`'river'` in `VISITED_BIOMES`).
7. **Is a bear's Crush on medium too strong?** Since #32, Crush hits for 45 on medium at difficulty 9 and 28 on easy at 8, and Roar, Maul and Crush all ask 8, 9 and 10, where Crush hits hardest every time. Keep it, give Maul and Crush less power, or give the bear three attacks so its ladders spread out?
8. **How strongly animals keep to their ground?** Since PR #64 each animal favours water, trees, rocks or open grass, and with plenty of its ground within 3 tiles comes out 4 times as often as an animal of its size with none; near home the ground only picks within a size, so there the share of bigger animals is what it was, on every tile. What a kid will feel: the open meadow near home is now 3 rabbits in 4 of its tier-1 animals (the strongest tier-1 animal), so a squirrel starter on the easiest puzzle wins 69% of those fights always right and 37% right 7 times in 10 (it was 77% and 45%; near home overall 78% and 46%); the reed by the start is 10 frogs in 16. Keep 4, or weaken it (2 makes the meadow 2 rabbits in 3)? One invariant is restated by the request itself: far from home the animals from the lead's size up are equal only on the biome's table, and the ground then sorts them ([[INVARIANTS]] § Encounters).
