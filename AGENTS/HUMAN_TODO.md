# Human TODO

Tasks that genuinely require human action — accounts, consents, decisions that need human judgment. Running servers, migrations, and database operations are NOT human tasks; agents do those.

One numbered list: product questions first, then checks only a person can make, then guardrail edits waiting for approval. Each item says what the game does today.

1. **Should a wrong answer show the right one?** Today it never does, in battle or at the doctor: the answer box shakes red and says "Not quite!", and [[UI_SPEC]] keeps the answer hidden on purpose, since the kid meets the sum again.
2. **Losing is a free full heal.** A lost battle puts the kid beside the nearest tent with the whole team healed, for nothing, while the doctor asks a puzzle per kind of animal hurt, a hard one for a big animal (difficulty 6 for a wolf, 8 for a bear), so kids may learn to lose on purpose. Tokens don't change this: healing costs puzzles, not tokens, and losing gives or takes none. If healing ever costs tokens, losing on purpose becomes a way round paying.
3. **Times tables on every starter.** All three starter cards promise times tables: the frog asks them on its easy Tongue Flick, the squirrel and the rabbit on their second attack at hard ("7 × ? = 56"). Right for a seven-year-old's first animal?
4. **The switching loophole.** A kid can lead with a small animal and switch in a big one on the first turn: the small one never gets tired, so it stays the lead and keeps the encounters small (in PR #18's simulation a squirrel leading a squirrel and a bear was hurt in 0% of its battles, against 96–99% without switching). Keep it, or size encounters by the strongest animal standing, or by whoever fought last?
5. **A big lead meets nothing, or only smaller animals.** Nothing two or more tiers below the lead comes out, so a bear in front meets nothing in the meadow or at the river (64% of the tall grass within 32 tiles of the start), and where only one-tier-smaller animals live every battle is one of them at the full 1 step in 10: a deer at the river meets only the river's tier-2 animals (otters, herons, raccoons and beavers since #89), a wolf in the meadow only deer. Keep both, cut the rate where only smaller animals live, or give those biomes visitors of the lead's tier; and after play, is 1/10 the right weight for one tier below?
6. **Keep the frog?** An agent added it (PR #21) for the planned choice of three starters: a third tier-1 animal, living in the river reeds. No message from the human asks for a frog. Removing it now takes a save upgrade that turns caught frogs into another animal (`SAVE_UPGRADES`); the squirrels and rabbits that visit the river near home go with one line (`'river'` in `VISITED_BIOMES`).
7. **Is a bear's Crush on medium too strong?** Since #32, Crush hits for 45 on medium at difficulty 9 and 28 on easy at 8, and Roar, Maul and Crush all ask 8, 9 and 10, where Crush hits hardest every time. Keep it, give Maul and Crush less power, or give the bear three attacks so its ladders spread out?
8. **How strongly animals keep to their ground?** Since PR #64 each animal favours water, trees, rocks or open grass, and with plenty of its ground within 3 tiles comes out 4 times as often as an animal of its size with none; near home the ground only picks within a size, so there the share of bigger animals is what it was, on every tile. What a kid will feel, since #89's small animals: the open meadow near home is rabbits, shrews, hedgehogs and moles, 18% each of its tier-1 animals, so a squirrel starter on the easiest puzzle wins 68% of those fights always right and 36% right 7 times in 10 (71% and 40% if the ground did not pull; near home overall 72% and 40%, against 73% and 41%); the reed by the start is frogs, brown rats and toads, 5 in 28 each. With the prototype's three small animals the pull was stronger: the meadow was 3 rabbits in 4, the squirrel's 69% and 37% there had been 77% and 45%, and the reed was 10 frogs in 16. Keep 4, or weaken it (2 makes the meadow 2 rabbits in 3)? One invariant is restated by the request itself: far from home the animals from the lead's size up are equal only on the biome's table, and the ground then sorts them ([[INVARIANTS]] § Encounters).

15. **Add the account tables to your local database, when you want accounts there.** Your dev API (the tunnel's) has the account routes since PR #85, but the `mathgame` database doesn't have their tables until migration `0003_accounts` runs on it, so logging in or registering there fails kindly as "can't reach". 16. **Put the game on its own server.** The deploy is built and tried on this Mac ([[DEVELOPMENT]] § Deployment); no server exists yet, so nothing deploys and every merge stays green. In this order:
    1. **Let the deploy workflow in.** In a terminal: `gh auth refresh -h github.com -s workflow`. The workflow file can then be pushed ([[ENVIRONMENT_NOTES]]).
    2. **A Hetzner API token.** In the Hetzner Cloud Console, a new project ("mathgame"), then Security → API tokens → Generate API token, Read & Write. Put it in `~/git/mathgame/terraform/terraform.tfvars` (copy `terraform.tfvars.example` beside it; the file is gitignored), as `hcloud_token = "…"`.
    3. **The server.** Tell an agent the token is in place: it runs `/redeploy`, which applies the Terraform (a cx23 in Nuremberg, lawcel's size, with Hetzner's backups on) and sets the server up. Or yourself: `brew tap hashicorp/tap && brew install hashicorp/tap/terraform`, then `cd ~/git/mathgame/terraform && terraform init && terraform apply`.
    4. **The domain.** Pick one, point its A record at the `server_ip` Terraform prints (and no AAAA record: `/redeploy` says why), and tell the agent, which puts it in `deploy.env`. The certificate comes by itself.
    5. **The GitHub secrets** `VPS_HOST`, `VPS_USER` and `VPS_SSH_KEY`: the agent sets them during `/redeploy`, straight from the server, with `gh secret set VPS_HOST -R ulfaslak/mathgame --body <server_ip>`, `gh secret set VPS_USER -R ulfaslak/mathgame --body deploy`, and the server's CI key piped into `gh secret set VPS_SSH_KEY -R ulfaslak/mathgame`. Nothing to paste. The first deploy follows.

    Each deploy then costs GitHub Actions minutes: about 5 for the checks and 2 to 4 for the image, out of the 2,000 a month the free plan gives private repos, shared with lawcel. A merge with `[skip deploy]` in its subject, or one touching only docs, costs a few seconds or nothing.
